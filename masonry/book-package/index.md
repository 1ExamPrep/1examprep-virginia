### Exam Details

The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination is associated with the Masonry Contracting specialty designation, abbreviated BRK, under the Virginia Board for Contractors. Virginia contractor licenses consist of a license class and one or more classifications or specialty designations. The specialty identifies the type of contracting work the licensed business may perform.

The scope of Masonry Contracting includes work involving brick, concrete block, stone, marble, slate, and other units and products commonly used in the masonry industry, including mortarless masonry products. The specialty also encompasses related masonry activities such as grout installation, caulking, tuck pointing, mortar washing, parging, cleaning, and reinforcement associated with masonry construction.

The examination content is organized around major masonry knowledge areas. Candidates should be prepared to work with subjects such as estimating and plan reading, unit masonry, mortar and grout, accessories and specialties, stone, masonry tools, restoration and cleaning, reinforced masonry, and safety. These areas combine practical field knowledge with information that can be found in the examination references.

Virginia requires Qualified Individuals for the Masonry specialty to satisfy the applicable experience requirements and successfully complete the required technical examination. Candidates must receive the required approval before taking the specialty examination.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia contractor specialty examinations administered through PSI are open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because reference materials are not supplied at the testing site.

Open book does not mean that every type of study material can be brought into the examination. PSI establishes rules governing the condition and preparation of examination references. Loose papers and unbound references are not permitted. Downloaded materials must be properly bound in an acceptable format. Study guides are not permitted as substitutes for approved examination references.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination according to PSI testing rules. Permanent tabs may be used for navigation, while removable tabs are prohibited. References containing writing that does not meet testing rules may be rejected. Candidates should prepare their books carefully before exam day and become comfortable navigating them without relying on handwritten study notes.

An open-book examination rewards familiarity. Searching an unfamiliar book for every question can consume valuable testing time. Candidates should know which reference is most likely to contain information about a particular subject. For example, a construction safety question should direct attention toward OSHA regulations, while a code-based masonry requirement may require the International Building Code. Questions concerning limestone construction can point toward the *Indiana Limestone Handbook*, while historic mortar-joint repair may be addressed by the preservation publication on repointing.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. The contractor license is issued to the business entity or sole proprietorship rather than simply functioning as an individual trade credential.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class and specialty.** Applicants should select the license class appropriate for their contracting business and identify Masonry Contracting, BRK, when that specialty matches the work the business intends to perform.
2. **Identify the required business personnel.** Virginia contractor applications involve responsible management and a Qualified Individual for each applicable classification or specialty. Class A and Class B businesses also use a Designated Employee for applicable business examination requirements.
3. **Meet the Qualified Individual experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must meet the experience requirement associated with the class of contractor license being pursued and satisfy the requirements for the Masonry specialty.
4. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants are subject to an eight-hour pre-license education requirement. This business-focused education is separate from the Masonry technical examination.
5. **Obtain examination approval when required.** The Masonry specialty is among the Virginia specialties that require the Qualified Individual to receive approval and successfully complete the applicable technical examination.
6. **Prepare for and complete the required examination.** Candidates should study the examination topics, organize permitted reference books, practice reference navigation, and follow current PSI testing procedures.
7. **Complete the contractor license application requirements.** The business must satisfy the requirements applying to its chosen license class, responsible management, Qualified Individual, examinations, documentation, and other Board requirements.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses operating under Class A, Class B, and Class C licenses. The license class determines the level of contracting activity authorized under Virginia requirements, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the contractor is authorized to perform.

Each licensed contractor must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty appearing on the license. For current applications, the Qualified Individual experience requirement is at least one year for a Class C business, three years for a Class B business, and five years for a Class A business. Masonry is one of the specialties for which the Qualified Individual must successfully complete the appropriate technical examination after meeting the applicable approval requirements.

The Qualified Individual must also have the required relationship with the contracting business. Virginia application requirements provide for the Qualified Individual to be a member of Responsible Management or a qualifying full-time employee meeting the state's employment criteria.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education may be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of Responsible Management completes the requirement. The course covers business and regulatory subjects and is separate from preparation for the Masonry specialty examination.

## How does the Masonry Contractor exam work?

The Masonry Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
