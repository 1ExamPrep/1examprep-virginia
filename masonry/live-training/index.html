Exam Details

The Virginia Masonry Contracting specialty is identified by the abbreviation BRK. The scope includes the installation of brick, concrete block, stone, marble, slate, and other products common to the masonry industry. It also includes related work such as grout installation, caulking, tuck pointing, sand blasting, mortar washing, parging, cleaning, and welding of reinforcement steel related to masonry construction.

The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination contains 60 questions. Candidates must achieve a minimum score of 70 percent, which equals 42 correct answers. The examination allows 150 minutes.

The examination content is divided among the following major subject areas:

  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 5 questions
  • Unit Masonry: 12 questions
  • Mortar and Grout: 11 questions
  • Accessories and Specialties: 10 questions
  • Stone: 3 questions
  • Tools: 4 questions
  • Restoration and Cleaning: 4 questions
  • Reinforced Masonry: 6 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

This distribution makes balanced preparation important. Unit masonry, mortar and grout, and accessories and specialties account for a significant portion of the examination, but candidates should also prepare for questions involving estimating, plans, stone, tools, restoration, reinforced masonry, and construction safety.

The Masonry specialty requires the applicable Qualified Individual to satisfy Virginia qualification requirements and successfully complete the required technical examination. BRK is among the Virginia contractor specialties requiring pre-approval for the examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination is open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing their permitted reference materials to the testing location because the examination center does not supply the books.

An open-book examination still requires preparation. Candidates have 150 minutes to answer 60 questions, so searching unfamiliar books for every answer can use valuable testing time. A stronger approach is to understand the major trade concepts and know which reference is most likely to contain the information needed for a particular question.

Permitted examination references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the test. Permanent tabs may be used in accordance with PSI testing requirements. Removable tabs are not permitted, and candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination.

Reference-navigation practice should therefore be part of the study process. Students should learn how each book is organized and practice locating information by chapter, section, table, index term, and subject. Repeated use helps build familiarity that can reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

For example, construction-safety questions may direct the candidate toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Building-code questions may involve the 2021 International Building Code. General masonry construction questions may require Modern Masonry, while stone and historic restoration questions may direct candidates toward the Indiana Limestone Handbook or the publication on repointing historic masonry.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to businesses and include a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Select the appropriate license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the Masonry Contracting specialty. Masonry Contracting is identified as BRK.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
  4. Meet the required experience level. The amount of experience required for the Qualified Individual depends on the contractor license class being pursued.
  5. Complete the required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor license applicants must satisfy the current Board-approved pre-license education requirement.
  6. Obtain approval for the BRK examination. Masonry is one of the specialties requiring pre-approval before the applicable technical examination.
  7. Prepare for and complete the required examination. Candidates should study the examination content areas and become familiar with the technical references permitted for testing.
  8. Complete the contractor license application requirements. The business must satisfy the applicable requirements for its license class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, examination, documentation, and application.

Virginia requires the applicable examination to be successfully completed before the contractor license application is submitted. Candidates should treat BRK exam preparation as one part of the larger contractor licensing process.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses. A Virginia contractor license consists of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties. The class determines the level of contracting activity authorized, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the business may perform.

A business seeking the Masonry specialty must designate a Qualified Individual. The Qualified Individual must be a member of Responsible Management or a qualifying full-time employee of the business working at least 30 hours per week.

The minimum experience requirement for a Qualified Individual depends on the license class. Virginia requires at least one year of experience for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A in the applicable classification or specialty.

BRK Masonry is among the classifications and specialties for which the Qualified Individual must be pre-approved and pass the applicable examination. An Experience Verification Form is required for Qualified Individuals requesting a designation that requires examination pre-approval.

Virginia also requires Board-approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. This education focuses on business principles, regulations, and requirements of contractor licensure. It is separate from the BRK Masonry technical examination and from trade-focused Masonry exam preparation.

Reference Books

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    This federal construction-safety reference contains OSHA standards applicable to construction workplaces. Candidates can use it to review safety subjects involving personal protective equipment, fall protection, scaffolds, ladders, tools, material handling, and other construction hazards.
  • International Building Code, 2021
    The 2021 International Building Code contains building requirements used for applicable code-based questions. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the code, including chapters, definitions, tables, indexes, and cross-references that can assist with locating masonry-related requirements.
  • Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone, Clois E. Kicklighter, 10th Edition
    This trade-focused masonry reference covers practical concepts involving brick, block, stone, mortar, grout, tools, layout, construction methods, wall systems, reinforcing, and other masonry topics. It provides a useful foundation for many of the trade subjects represented on the BRK examination.
  • Indiana Limestone Handbook, 22nd Edition, 2007
    This specialized reference addresses Indiana limestone and includes information associated with natural stone characteristics, detailing, fabrication, joints, anchorage, handling, and installation. Candidates should become familiar with its organization when preparing for stone-related questions.
  • Repointing Mortar Joints in Historic Masonry Buildings, Robert C. Mack and John P. Speweik, October 1998
    This preservation publication addresses repair of deteriorated mortar joints in historic masonry. Subjects include mortar selection, removal of deteriorated mortar, joint preparation, repointing procedures, tooling, curing, and cleaning.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should connect each examination category with the reference material and trade knowledge most likely to support it. Instead of reading every book from beginning to end without a plan, candidates can divide preparation into focused subject areas and practice locating information as they study.

For estimating and plan reading, review the types of dimensions, quantities, and construction information used to evaluate masonry work. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting information and determining what is needed to complete masonry-related calculations or decisions.

Unit masonry should receive substantial attention because it represents 12 examination questions. Review brick and concrete masonry units, bonding, layout, construction practices, materials, walls, openings, and terminology. Understanding the vocabulary of masonry construction also makes it easier to recognize useful index terms in the reference books.

Mortar and grout represent another major part of the examination. Study material properties, appropriate uses, mixing and placement concepts, joints, curing, and how mortar and grout function within masonry assemblies.

Accessories and specialties account for 10 questions. Candidates should understand components used with masonry construction and how those components interact with masonry assemblies. Reinforced masonry should also receive focused review because it represents six additional questions.

Construction-safety preparation should include direct practice with 29 CFR Part 1926. Rather than attempting to memorize the entire federal safety standard, become familiar with its structure and learn how to locate information about major construction hazards.

Code preparation should involve regular use of the 2021 International Building Code. Practice moving from an index term to a chapter, section, table, or related provision. Learning how the code is organized can make code-based questions easier to manage during an open-book examination.

Stone preparation should incorporate the Indiana Limestone Handbook, while restoration and cleaning preparation should include Repointing Mortar Joints in Historic Masonry Buildings. Candidates should know which specialized reference applies to a question before spending time searching.

Timed practice can also improve preparation. With 150 minutes for 60 questions, candidates should develop a consistent pace and learn when a reference lookup is necessary. Practice questions can be used not only to test knowledge but also to improve the process of identifying the subject, selecting the correct reference, locating the information, and confirming the answer.

Is the Virginia Masonry Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials and follow current PSI rules for book preparation and use.

How many questions are on the Virginia Masonry Contractor exam?

The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination contains 60 questions, and candidates have 150 minutes to complete the test.

What score is required to pass the Virginia Masonry exam?

The minimum passing score is 70 percent, which requires 42 correct answers out of 60 questions.

What subjects are covered on the BRK exam?

The examination covers estimating and plan reading, unit masonry, mortar and grout, accessories and specialties, stone, tools, restoration and cleaning, reinforced masonry, and safety.

What does BRK mean in Virginia contractor licensing?

BRK is the abbreviation used for the Virginia Masonry Contracting specialty.

What is the benefit of combining live virtual and self-study training?

Live virtual training provides scheduled, instructor-led structure, while self-study allows candidates to continue reviewing trade topics and practicing reference navigation between live sessions. Together, they create a preparation format that combines guidance with independent study.

Do I still need to study the reference books?

Yes. Because the examination is open book, familiarity with the technical references is an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice locating information in the books instead of waiting until exam day to become familiar with them.

Can I use highlighted and tabbed references during the examination?

PSI permits references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used, while removable tabs are prohibited. Candidates must follow current testing rules governing examination reference materials.

Does Virginia require experience for the Masonry Qualified Individual?

Yes. The minimum experience requirement is one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A in the applicable classification or specialty.

Is Virginia contractor pre-license education the same as BRK exam preparation?

No. Virginia's Board-approved contractor pre-license education is a separate business and regulatory requirement. BRK exam preparation focuses on the technical knowledge and reference-navigation skills associated with the Masonry Contracting examination.

How does the Masonry Contractor exam work?

The Masonry Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
