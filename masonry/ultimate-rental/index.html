Exam Details

The Virginia Masonry Contracting specialty is identified as BRK. It applies to masonry contracting work involving materials and construction methods associated with brick, concrete block, stone, marble, slate, and other masonry products, together with related masonry activities within the defined specialty.

The Masonry technical examination evaluates knowledge connected with the work performed by masonry contractors. Preparation should include subjects such as estimating and plan reading, unit masonry, mortar and grout, accessories and specialties, stone, masonry tools, restoration and cleaning, reinforced masonry, and construction safety.

Candidates preparing for the examination should avoid concentrating on only one part of the trade. Masonry work combines material knowledge, construction practices, code requirements, safety responsibilities, tools, structural concepts, and repair methods. A balanced preparation plan should provide regular review across these areas while giving additional study time to weaker subjects.

Reference-book familiarity is also an important part of technical exam preparation. Candidates should learn which book is most appropriate for different types of questions and practice locating information using tables of contents, indexes, chapters, code sections, tables, and technical terminology.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination is an open-book examination. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials and follow the testing requirements governing how those references may be prepared and used.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for study. Searching through unfamiliar technical books during a timed examination can make answering questions significantly more difficult. Candidates should practice with their references throughout the preparation period so that choosing and navigating the correct book becomes part of their normal test-taking process.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors and combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Determine the appropriate license class. Virginia contractor businesses may pursue a Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license depending on the level of contracting activity.
  2. Select the Masonry Contracting specialty. Candidates pursuing masonry contracting work should identify the BRK specialty when it corresponds with the services the business intends to provide.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for each requested classification or specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirement associated with the selected contractor license class.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  6. Obtain approval for the BRK examination. Masonry is among the specialties requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and successfully complete an examination.
  7. Prepare for the required examinations. Candidates should study the applicable technical and business material and become familiar with the references connected with their examinations.
  8. Complete the contractor application requirements. The business must satisfy the requirements applying to its license class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, examinations, experience documentation, education, and application.

State Requirements

Virginia contractor licenses include both a license class and a classification or specialty. The license class determines the level of contracting activity authorized for the business, while the classification or specialty determines the type of contracting work the company may perform.

Each contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the applicable classification or specialty. The Qualified Individual must have the required relationship with the business and satisfy the applicable experience and prerequisite requirements.

Current Virginia requirements provide a minimum of one year of experience for a Qualified Individual associated with a Class C business, three years for a Class B business, and five years for a Class A business in the applicable classification or specialty.

Masonry, identified as BRK, is among the specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the applicable technical examination. Qualified Individuals applying for designations requiring examination pre-approval must submit the required experience verification documentation.

New Virginia contractor applicants must also satisfy the eight-hour pre-license education requirement. The pre-license education course addresses general contractor business, regulatory, and statutory topics and is separate from preparation for the Masonry technical examination.

Class A and Class B contractor businesses also have applicable business examination requirements for the Designated Employee. The included Virginia business and law reference materials can support preparation for the broader contractor licensing process in addition to the Masonry specialty.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should combine trade review, reference navigation, and practice-oriented preparation. Candidates can begin by dividing the Masonry examination into individual subject areas and working through each area systematically rather than attempting to study every book at once.

Unit masonry preparation should include brick and concrete masonry units, terminology, layout concepts, construction procedures, wall components, openings, and related materials. Understanding the language used in masonry references also makes it easier to identify useful index terms during an open-book examination.

Mortar and grout preparation should focus on their purposes, properties, placement, construction procedures, joints, curing considerations, and relationship to completed masonry assemblies. Candidates should practice locating supporting information in their references rather than relying only on memorization.

Construction-safety preparation should include regular work with 29 CFR Part 1926. Learning how federal construction safety standards are organized can help candidates locate information involving scaffolds, fall protection, ladders, equipment, tools, and personal protective equipment more efficiently.

For building-code preparation, candidates should spend time working directly with the 2021 International Building Code. Practice moving between the index, definitions, chapters, sections, and tables so that code navigation becomes familiar before the examination.

Stone preparation should incorporate the Indiana Limestone Handbook. Restoration study should incorporate Repointing Mortar Joints in Historic Masonry Buildings. These specialized references may be narrower than the primary code and trade books, but candidates should still understand their organization and know when they are likely to contain relevant information.

How does the Masonry Contractor exam work?
