### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors and combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate license class.** Virginia contractor businesses may pursue a Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license depending on the level of contracting activity.
2. **Select the Masonry Contracting specialty.** Candidates pursuing masonry contracting work should identify the BRK specialty when it corresponds with the services the business intends to provide.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for each requested classification or specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirement associated with the selected contractor license class.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
6. **Obtain approval for the BRK examination.** Masonry is among the specialties requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and successfully complete an examination.
7. **Prepare for the required examinations.** Candidates should study the applicable technical and business material and become familiar with the references connected with their examinations.
8. **Complete the contractor application requirements.** The business must satisfy the requirements applying to its license class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, examinations, experience documentation, education, and application.

### State Requirements

Virginia contractor licenses include both a license class and a classification or specialty. The license class determines the level of contracting activity authorized for the business, while the classification or specialty determines the type of contracting work the company may perform.

Each contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the applicable classification or specialty. The Qualified Individual must have the required relationship with the business and satisfy the applicable experience and prerequisite requirements.

Current Virginia requirements provide a minimum of one year of experience for a Qualified Individual associated with a Class C business, three years for a Class B business, and five years for a Class A business in the applicable classification or specialty.

Masonry, identified as BRK, is among the specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the applicable technical examination. Qualified Individuals applying for designations requiring examination pre-approval must submit the required experience verification documentation.

New Virginia contractor applicants must also satisfy the eight-hour pre-license education requirement. The pre-license education course addresses general contractor business, regulatory, and statutory topics and is separate from preparation for the Masonry technical examination.

Class A and Class B contractor businesses also have applicable business examination requirements for the Designated Employee. The included Virginia business and law reference materials can support preparation for the broader contractor licensing process in addition to the Masonry specialty.

## How does the Masonry Contractor exam work?

The Masonry Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
