Exam Details

The Virginia Masonry Contracting specialty is identified as BRK. Virginia contractor licenses consist of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialties. The specialty determines the type of contracting work the licensed business is authorized to perform.

Masonry Contracting involves work associated with masonry materials and construction methods, including brick, concrete block, stone, marble, slate, and other products commonly used in masonry construction. Candidates preparing for the BRK specialty examination should understand both practical masonry methods and the technical information found in the applicable reference materials.

Important areas of preparation include estimating and plan reading, unit masonry, mortar and grout, accessories and specialties, stone, tools, restoration and cleaning, reinforced masonry, and construction safety. Each of these subjects represents a different part of the knowledge expected from a professional masonry contractor.

Exam preparation should combine trade understanding with reference-navigation practice. Candidates should know which reference applies to a particular subject and become familiar with the organization, terminology, index, chapters, tables, and sections of each book.

Virginia requires successful completion of applicable contractor examinations before applying for licensure. BRK Masonry is among the specialty designations requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and successfully complete the appropriate technical examination.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors rather than functioning only as personal trade credentials.

  1. Select the appropriate license class. Virginia contractor licenses are available as Class A, Class B, or Class C licenses.
  2. Select the Masonry Contracting specialty. Masonry Contracting is identified by the BRK specialty designation.
  3. Establish the business structure. The 1 Package includes Business Formation assistance for an LLC or Corporation to help establish the customer’s contracting business entity.
  4. Obtain an EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included to help establish the federal tax identification used for important business functions.
  5. Identify the Qualified Individual. Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested on the contractor application.
  6. Meet the experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the minimum experience requirement associated with the selected license class.
  7. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants must complete eight hours of approved pre-license education.
  8. Obtain exam approval. BRK Masonry requires pre-approval for the applicable Qualified Individual specialty examination.
  9. Prepare for and complete the required examination. Candidates should use the technical references and course materials to prepare for the required masonry examination.
  10. Complete the contractor license application. Application Service is included with The 1 Package to support the application stage.
  11. Address ongoing contractor compliance. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included to help customers understand important compliance considerations associated with operating the business.

State Requirements

Virginia contractor licenses contain two important components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class determines the level of contracting activity authorized, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of contracting work the business can perform.

Each contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty requested. The Qualified Individual must be a member of Responsible Management or a qualifying full-time employee of the business.

The minimum experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. Virginia currently requires at least one year of applicable experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

BRK Masonry is one of the specialty designations requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the applicable examination. An Experience Verification Form is required for a Qualified Individual requesting a designation that requires examination pre-approval.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The pre-license course is a general business course covering regulations, statutes, and requirements applicable to contractor business owners. It is separate from the technical BRK Masonry examination.

Class A and Class B businesses also have Designated Employee examination requirements. The business examination covers general business knowledge and Virginia contractor laws and regulations, with the applicable examination structure depending on the license class.

Test Information and Study Materials

An effective Virginia Masonry Contractor study plan should combine technical review, book navigation, and repeated practice. Candidates should divide the material into specific subject areas instead of attempting to study the entire reference collection at once.

Unit masonry preparation should cover terminology, brick and concrete masonry units, layout, bonding concepts, walls, openings, materials, and installation practices. Understanding trade terminology is especially helpful during an open-book exam because the correct terminology can lead directly to useful index entries and reference sections.

Mortar and grout preparation should include their properties, functions, mixing and placement concepts, joints, curing, and use within masonry construction. Reinforced masonry should also receive dedicated study because reinforcement introduces additional construction considerations and technical requirements.

Safety preparation should include direct use of 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates should learn how OSHA’s construction standards are organized and practice locating information related to common construction hazards and work practices.

Building-code study should include frequent navigation of the 2021 International Building Code. Practice moving between the index, definitions, chapters, tables, sections, and cross-references. Familiarity with the structure of the code can make reference-based questions easier to approach.

Stone preparation should incorporate the Indiana Limestone Handbook, while restoration study should include Repointing Mortar Joints in Historic Masonry Buildings. These specialized references should become familiar enough that candidates know when to use them.
