### Exam Details

The Virginia Masonry Contracting specialty is identified as BRK. Virginia contractor licenses consist of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialties. The specialty determines the type of contracting work the licensed business is authorized to perform.

Masonry Contracting involves work associated with masonry materials and construction methods, including brick, concrete block, stone, marble, slate, and other products commonly used in masonry construction. Candidates preparing for the BRK specialty examination should understand both practical masonry methods and the technical information found in the applicable reference materials.

### State Requirements

Virginia contractor licenses contain two important components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class determines the level of contracting activity authorized, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of contracting work the business can perform.

Each contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty requested. The Qualified Individual must be a member of Responsible Management or a qualifying full-time employee of the business.

The minimum experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. Virginia currently requires at least one year of applicable experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

BRK Masonry is one of the specialty designations requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the applicable examination. An Experience Verification Form is required for a Qualified Individual requesting a designation that requires examination pre-approval.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The pre-license course is a general business course covering regulations, statutes, and requirements applicable to contractor business owners. It is separate from the technical BRK Masonry examination.

Class A and Class B businesses also have Designated Employee examination requirements. The business examination covers general business knowledge and Virginia contractor laws and regulations, with the applicable examination structure depending on the license class.

## How does the Masonry Contractor exam work?

The Masonry Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
