Exam Details

The Virginia Masonry Contracting specialty is identified as BRK by the Virginia Board for Contractors. The specialty covers masonry services involving brick, concrete block, stone, marble, slate, and other units and products commonly used in the masonry industry. Its scope also includes related work involving grout, caulking, tuck pointing, sand blasting, mortar washing, parging, cleaning, and reinforcement steel associated with masonry construction.

The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination contains 60 questions. Candidates must answer at least 42 questions correctly for a 70 percent passing score, and the examination allows 150 minutes.

The official examination outline divides the test into several subject areas. These include estimating and plan reading, unit masonry, mortar and grout, accessories and specialties, stone, tools, restoration and cleaning, reinforced masonry, and safety. Unit masonry, mortar and grout, and accessories and specialties represent significant portions of the examination, making organized study across the complete reference set particularly important.

  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 5 questions
  • Unit Masonry: 12 questions
  • Mortar and Grout: 11 questions
  • Accessories and Specialties: 10 questions
  • Stone: 3 questions
  • Tools: 4 questions
  • Restoration and Cleaning: 4 questions
  • Reinforced Masonry: 6 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Virginia requires the Qualified Individual for the Masonry specialty to meet applicable qualification requirements and successfully complete a Board-approved technical examination. Masonry is specifically listed among the specialties requiring examination approval and successful completion of the applicable technical exam.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination is open book. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted references to the testing site because the examination center does not provide the books.

PSI allows reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References may also be tabbed using permanent tabs. Removable tabs, including temporary flag-style tabs, are not permitted. References containing writing are not allowed, and candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination session.

That makes preparation with properly organized books an important part of studying for the Virginia Masonry Contractor exam. An open-book exam still requires candidates to work efficiently. Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. Virginia contractor licenses are issued to contractor businesses, and each license consists of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations that determine the type of contracting work the business is authorized to perform.

  1. Select the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C licenses.
  2. Select the Masonry Contracting specialty. Masonry Contracting is identified by the BRK specialty designation.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must identify a Qualified Individual for the specialty requested on the contractor license.
  4. Meet the required experience. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirement applicable to the chosen contractor license class.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants must satisfy the state's eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
  6. Obtain approval for the specialty examination. BRK Masonry is among the specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the applicable technical examination.
  7. Prepare for and pass the required examination. Candidates should study the examination content areas and become comfortable using the reference materials within PSI testing rules.
  8. Complete the contractor license application. After satisfying the applicable examination and licensing requirements, the business can complete the required application process with the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The Virginia Board for Contractors requires successful completion of the applicable examination before contractor licensure is issued.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses businesses performing construction, removal, repair, and improvement work on property owned by others. A contractor license consists of two major components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class determines the level of contracting activity permitted, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of contracting services the business may perform.

Each contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the classification or specialty requested. Under current Virginia application requirements, the Qualified Individual must be a member of Responsible Management or a qualifying full-time employee of the business.

The minimum experience requirement for the Qualified Individual depends on the contractor license class. Virginia currently requires at least one year of experience for a Class C license, three years for a Class B license, and five years for a Class A license.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the course may be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For a Class C applicant, a member of Responsible Management completes the education requirement. This course is a general business and regulatory course and is separate from the Masonry technical examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective preparation for the Virginia Masonry Contractor exam involves more than reading the references. Candidates should develop a repeatable method for identifying the subject of a question, selecting the correct reference, locating the appropriate section, and confirming the answer efficiently.

For safety questions, begin by becoming familiar with the organization of 29 CFR Part 1926. Learn how major OSHA construction topics are separated and practice using the table of contents and index. Because safety represents a defined portion of the masonry examination, familiarity with this reference can reduce unnecessary searching.

For building-code questions, spend time navigating the 2021 International Building Code. Learn how its chapters, definitions, tables, index, and cross-references are organized so you can move efficiently through code-based questions.

Trade preparation should include consistent work with Modern Masonry. Review terminology and concepts involving masonry units, mortar, grout, tools, layout, reinforced masonry, construction procedures, and related field practices. Rather than trying to memorize every page, use practice sessions to learn where important subjects are located.

Stone-related study should include the Indiana Limestone Handbook, while restoration preparation should include Repointing Mortar Joints in Historic Masonry Buildings. These narrower references can become much easier to use when candidates understand their structure before the examination.

The highlighted and tabbed format is especially useful when paired with practice questions. When you miss a question, locate the supporting information in the correct book. Review the surrounding material, identify the relevant tab or section, and practice returning to it again later. This creates familiarity that can improve book-navigation efficiency.

Candidates should also practice under timed conditions. The Virginia Masonry Contracting examination allows 150 minutes for 60 questions, so study sessions should include a balance between answering from trade knowledge and confirming answers through the references.

Because PSI controls what reference materials may be brought into the examination room, candidates should preserve the condition of the books after receiving them. Do not add handwritten notes to examination references. Permanent tabs and highlighting are permitted, but removable tabs and written material are not allowed under the published examination rules.

What is BRK in Virginia contractor licensing?

No. Virginia's eight-hour pre-license education requirement is separate from the Masonry technical examination. The pre-license course covers business, regulatory, statutory, and contractor licensing subjects rather than serving as the BRK technical exam.
