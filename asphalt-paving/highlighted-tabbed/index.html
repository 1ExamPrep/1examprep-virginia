Exam Details

The Virginia Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors and administered through PSI.

The examination contains 40 questions. Candidates are allowed 100 minutes to complete the test, and the minimum passing score is 70%, or 28 correct answers.

The official examination content outline divides the test into five primary subject areas:

  • Asphalt – 7 questions
  • Paving and Sealcoating – 13 questions
  • Excavation, Compaction, and Grading – 13 questions
  • Traffic Control – 3 questions
  • Safety – 4 questions

Paving and Sealcoating and Excavation, Compaction, and Grading are the two largest sections of the examination, with 13 questions in each category. Together, these subjects make up a substantial portion of the exam and deserve significant attention during preparation.

The Asphalt category contains 7 questions and focuses attention on knowledge related to asphalt materials and paving operations. Candidates should develop a working understanding of the materials, equipment, procedures, and terminology commonly associated with asphalt paving work.

Traffic Control accounts for 3 questions. Candidates should be familiar with traffic-control principles applicable to work areas where construction operations interact with vehicles, pedestrians, and roadway users.

Safety accounts for 4 questions. OSHA construction requirements are especially relevant because asphalt paving, excavation, grading, and equipment operations may involve moving machinery, excavation hazards, personal protective equipment, traffic exposure, tools, and other jobsite safety concerns.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their permitted reference materials to the testing location because references are not provided at the test site.

PSI permits reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References containing written notes are not allowed into the examination. Candidates are also prohibited from writing in their reference materials during the testing session.

Permanent tabs are allowed. Removable tabs and temporary flags are not permitted. The highlighted and tabbed format of this package is intended to support the type of organized book navigation that is especially useful for an open-book contractor examination.

With 100 minutes available for 40 questions, candidates have an average of approximately two and one-half minutes per question. Strong reference navigation can therefore be an important part of preparation. Knowing which book is most likely to contain information about excavation, asphalt operations, construction equipment, traffic control, or safety can reduce unnecessary searching.

Candidates should spend time using the books before exam day rather than relying on the open-book format alone. Practice identifying the subject of a question first, selecting the most appropriate reference second, and then using the table of contents, index, highlighted material, and tabs to locate the applicable information.

State Requirements

Virginia identifies Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contracting with the specialty abbreviation PAV. The specialty applies to services involving asphalt paving and/or sealcoating on several common types of surfaces and facilities.

The defined scope includes work on subdivision streets and adjacent intersections, driveways, parking lots, tennis courts, running tracks, and play areas, using materials and accessories common to asphalt paving and sealcoating operations.

The specialty also includes the height adjustment of existing sewer manholes, storm drains, water valves, sewer cleanouts, and drain grates when associated with the work.

Necessary excavation and grading are included within the PAV specialty scope. This helps explain why excavation, compaction, and grading receive significant attention on the technical examination.

Virginia also identifies the Highway/Heavy classification as providing for this type of work. Contractors should operate only within the classifications and specialties authorized by their Virginia contractor license.

The technical PAV examination is only one component of contractor licensing. The contractor business must satisfy the applicable requirements established by the Virginia Board for Contractors for the license and specialty being pursued.

Test Information and Study Materials

A focused study plan for the Virginia Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contractor Exam should closely follow the examination content outline. Because Paving and Sealcoating and Excavation, Compaction, and Grading each contain 13 questions, these two categories should receive a large portion of preparation time.

For paving and sealcoating, candidates should build familiarity with asphalt paving operations, surface preparation, paving procedures, equipment use, material placement, and general industry practices. Understanding how paving work progresses from preparation through finished surface can help candidates connect technical questions to actual field operations.

Excavation, compaction, and grading preparation should include the principles of earthwork, preparation of the paving base, equipment applications, grade control, excavation procedures, and compaction. Proper grading and compaction are central to successful paving work because the condition of the supporting materials influences the finished pavement.

The Asphalt section should be studied as a distinct subject. Candidates should understand terminology, material characteristics, asphalt-related production concepts, and the relationship between asphalt materials and paving operations.

Traffic control should not be overlooked simply because it represents only 3 questions. Asphalt paving contractors frequently perform work near roadways, intersections, parking areas, and other locations where controlling the movement of vehicles and pedestrians is an important part of safe construction operations.

Use the Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices for Streets and Highways to become familiar with the organization and terminology used for traffic-control devices and work-zone applications.

Safety preparation should focus on the construction requirements contained in 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates should know how to locate relevant OSHA information rather than attempting to memorize the entire regulation.

Construction equipment is another important area to review. Asphalt paving and grading operations involve specialized machinery as well as common earthmoving and compaction equipment. Understanding the purpose and application of equipment helps candidates interpret questions dealing with planning, productivity, excavation, hauling, grading, and compaction.

Estimating and field calculations may also require careful reading and practical construction knowledge. When working through practice material, write out calculations clearly and make sure units remain consistent.

Open-book practice should become part of every study session. Use the highlighted sections and tabs to develop a mental map of the books. A candidate who knows that excavation information is in one reference, traffic-control information is in another, and safety requirements are in OSHA can begin a lookup much faster.

Practice in timed conditions as the exam date approaches. The test allows 100 minutes for 40 questions, so candidates should avoid spending excessive time on a single difficult lookup. Answer familiar questions efficiently, mark more difficult questions for additional attention when permitted by the testing system, and use the remaining time strategically.

The highlighted and tabbed books are preparation tools, but candidates should still read and work with the references before taking the examination. Tabs are most effective when the candidate understands why a section matters and what type of information is located there.
