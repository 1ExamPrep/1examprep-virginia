Exam Details

The Virginia Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contracting examination is administered through PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors. The technical specialty examination contains 40 questions, requires a minimum score of 70%, or 28 correct answers, and allows 100 minutes for completion.

The official examination content is divided into five principal subject areas:

  • Asphalt – 7 questions
  • Paving and Sealcoating – 13 questions
  • Excavation, Compaction, and Grading – 13 questions
  • Traffic Control – 3 questions
  • Safety – 4 questions

Paving and Sealcoating and Excavation, Compaction, and Grading are the two largest examination areas, with 13 questions assigned to each. Together, they represent more than half of the scored exam. Candidates should therefore devote substantial study time to pavement preparation, paving procedures, sealcoating, excavation operations, soil and aggregate handling, grading, and compaction.

The Asphalt portion tests knowledge related to asphalt materials and paving applications. Candidates should understand the principles and terminology used in hot-mix asphalt production, handling, placement, and pavement construction.

The Traffic Control portion addresses work-zone traffic-control practices. The Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices provides the package's dedicated reference for signs, markings, channelizing devices, and temporary traffic-control concepts.

The Safety portion draws attention to construction hazards encountered during paving, excavation, equipment operation, and related field activities. OSHA's construction regulations in 29 CFR Part 1926 are therefore an important part of this package.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contractor technical examination is an open book test. Candidates should prepare not only by reading the reference materials but also by learning how information is organized within each book.

With 40 questions and 100 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Efficient reference navigation can make a significant difference. Knowing which book addresses a particular topic reduces the amount of time spent searching for information.

An OSHA question should direct the candidate toward 29 CFR Part 1926. A temporary traffic-control question should point toward the MUTCD. Questions involving earthwork, excavation, grade calculations, or compaction may require the excavation and construction-method references. Asphalt placement and pavement-construction questions should direct attention toward the Hot Mix Asphalt Paving Handbook.

Preparing for an open-book exam does not mean every answer can be found quickly without prior study. Candidates should develop familiarity with terminology, chapters, indexes, tables, and subject organization before exam day. Practice locating information under time limits so that reference books function as efficient tools rather than unfamiliar resources.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contracting (PAV) as work involving the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of asphalt paving or sealcoating on specified surfaces such as subdivision streets and adjacent intersections, driveways, parking lots, tennis courts, running tracks, and play areas.

The PAV scope also includes necessary excavation and grading associated with that work. Virginia's specialty definition additionally includes height adjustment of existing sewer manholes, storm drains, water valves, sewer cleanouts, and drain grates associated with paving and sealcoating work.

The Virginia Board for Contractors requires a technical examination for the Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating specialty. Applicants must also satisfy the requirements associated with the contractor license class being pursued. Contractor licensing and the PAV specialty examination are related, but the specialty examination is only one component of the overall Virginia licensing process.

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to qualifying business entities after the Board determines that applicable licensing requirements have been met. The Qualified Individual is associated with the specialty qualification, while additional requirements can apply depending on the contractor license class.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should reflect the exam's question distribution. The two largest areas are Paving and Sealcoating and Excavation, Compaction, and Grading, so candidates should begin with these subjects and then build outward into asphalt materials, traffic control, and construction safety.

For Asphalt, focus on fundamental paving-material concepts and the terminology used in hot-mix asphalt construction. Study how asphalt mixtures are produced, transported, placed, and compacted, and understand the factors that influence pavement quality.

The Hot Mix Asphalt Paving Handbook should be a central resource for this portion of preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the stages of a paving operation rather than studying asphalt as an isolated material. Production, delivery, placement, rolling, compaction, joints, and quality considerations all contribute to the completed pavement.

For Paving and Sealcoating, review surface preparation, paving operations, pavement placement, compaction, joints, equipment, and sealcoating concepts. Candidates should understand the sequence of operations required to produce a durable finished surface.

Preparation should also include recognizing how existing conditions affect the paving process. Surface condition, grade, drainage, base preparation, equipment selection, and weather can influence how work is performed.

For Excavation, Compaction, and Grading, use Pipe and Excavation Contracting, Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods, and the Excavation and Grading Handbook together. These resources provide complementary coverage of field operations and equipment.

Excavation study should include jobsite preparation, trench and earthwork concepts, safe excavation practices, equipment selection, and handling of excavated material. Candidates should understand that proper pavement construction begins below the asphalt surface.

Grading establishes the elevations and slopes required for the finished paving work. Candidates should review cut and fill concepts, grade control, site layout, drainage, and methods used to prepare a stable base before paving begins.

Compaction should also receive substantial attention. Asphalt and underlying materials depend on proper compaction to achieve the required stability and performance. Review equipment selection, rolling concepts, material condition, lift considerations, and the role of compaction in paving and earthwork.

The construction-equipment reference is valuable for understanding the machinery used throughout sitework. Excavators, loaders, graders, rollers, trucks, and paving equipment perform different functions, and candidates should recognize their appropriate applications.

For Traffic Control, use the Manual on Uniform Traffic Control Devices. Paving work frequently takes place near moving traffic, pedestrians, equipment, and temporary changes in normal roadway conditions. Candidates should understand basic work-zone traffic-control principles and the role of signs, channelizing devices, and other traffic-control measures.

For Safety, review 29 CFR Part 1926. Paving and excavation projects can involve heavy mobile equipment, trenches, traffic exposure, hazardous materials, personal protective equipment, and other construction hazards.

OSHA preparation should focus on locating applicable construction-safety requirements quickly. Become familiar with the organization of Part 1926 so that questions involving excavation, personal protection, equipment, or other jobsite hazards can be directed to the appropriate section.

An effective open-book preparation strategy is to associate every content area with the most useful reference. When a practice problem mentions traffic-control devices, immediately think MUTCD. For OSHA hazards, move to 29 CFR Part 1926. For asphalt placement, begin with the paving handbook. For grading and excavation, use the earthwork references.

Candidates should also practice with a timer. The 100-minute examination period allows about two and a half minutes per question. Some questions may be answered quickly from trade knowledge, leaving more time for questions that require a table, calculation, or detailed book lookup.

When reviewing a missed practice question, determine why the answer was missed. It may be a knowledge gap, a terminology issue, an unfamiliar reference location, or a failure to identify the correct subject. Correcting the cause of the mistake is more useful than memorizing the individual answer.

Repeated navigation is particularly valuable with a multi-book exam package. The more frequently candidates use the references during preparation, the easier it becomes to select the right book quickly. This helps turn a six-book collection into an organized examination tool.

How does the Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contractor exam work?

The Asphalt Paving and Sealcoating Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
