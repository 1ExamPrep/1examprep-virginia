### Exam Details

The Virginia Roofing Contractor examination is the Qualified Individual technical examination associated with the **ROC** specialty. Virginia requires Qualified Individuals for Roofing Contracting to meet the applicable experience requirements, obtain pre-approval, and successfully complete the technical examination.

The current Virginia Roofing Contractor examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates receive **120 minutes** to complete the examination and must achieve a minimum passing score of **75%**.

The current examination content includes:

- **Estimating and Plan Reading:** 5 questions
- **Surface Preparation and Planning:** 5 questions
- **Steep and Low Slope Roofing, including Built-Up Roofing and Waterproofing:** 13 questions
- **Asphalt, Wood, and Hard Tile Shingles and Wood Shakes:** 5 questions
- **Roofing Components:** 10 questions
- **Repairs and Reroofing:** 6 questions
- **OSHA Safety:** 6 questions

Steep- and low-slope roofing represents the largest technical category. Candidates should understand roof assemblies, waterproofing, built-up roofing, membranes, underlayments, insulation, flashing, drainage, attachment methods, penetrations, and related installation practices.

Roofing Components is also a major portion of the exam. Candidates should be comfortable with the individual parts that make a complete roofing assembly work, including roof decks, flashing, insulation, fasteners, drainage components, underlayments, edges, transitions, and penetrations.

Estimating and plan reading require candidates to interpret roof dimensions, slopes, material quantities, drawings, project specifications, and other construction information. Repairs, reroofing, surface preparation, and OSHA safety are additional areas that should receive dedicated study time.

Virginia uses PSI to administer Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. The current Roofing Contractor specialty exam fee is **$85**, paid directly to PSI and separate from the Ultimate package price.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses combine a contractor license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Roofing Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation ROC.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select Roofing Contracting.** The applicable specialty designation is ROC.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The business must designate a Qualified Individual for the Roofing specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must have roofing experience appropriate to the requested contractor class.
5. **Complete experience verification.** ROC requires pre-approval for the technical examination, so the applicable experience documentation must be submitted.
6. **Receive examination approval.** The Qualified Individual must be approved before scheduling the ROC technical examination.
7. **Prepare for the examination.** Use the course, technical roofing references, practice preparation, and structured study plan.
8. **Complete the ROC technical examination.** Successful completion is required for the Qualified Individual.
9. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the Board-approved pre-license education requirement.
10. **Complete applicable Class A or Class B business requirements.** These are separate from the ROC technical examination.
11. **Complete the contractor license application.** Application Service is included with this Ultimate package.

**Application Service is included** to help support the licensing application portion of the process. Passing the ROC examination alone does not create a contractor license. The business must also satisfy the applicable contractor class, Qualified Individual, business, education, and application requirements.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses. Each license includes a contractor class and one or more classifications or specialties. The Qualified Individual is the person who qualifies the business for the requested specialty.

The Qualified Individual must be either a full-time employee working at least 30 hours per week with the business or a member of Responsible Management. Current minimum experience requirements are **one year for Class C**, **three years for Class B**, and **five years for Class A**.

Roofing Contracting is one of the Virginia specialties requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and successfully complete a Board-approved technical examination. An Experience Verification Form is required for a Qualified Individual seeking a designation that requires examination pre-approval.

The ROC specialty covers installation, repair, removal, and improvement of roofing materials used to create watertight and weather-resistant roof and deck surfaces. Its defined scope also includes related roofing components, dampproofing, waterproofing, and roof insulation systems installed above the roof deck.

New contractor applicants must complete **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education**. The course is a business-focused requirement and is separate from technical Roofing Contractor exam preparation.

Class A and Class B applicants also have separate Designated Employee business examination requirements. The additional NASCLA Virginia guide and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code included in this package provide broader study resources for applicable business, regulatory, and contractor preparation.

## How does the Roofing Contractor exam work?

The Roofing Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
