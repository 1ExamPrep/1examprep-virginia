Exam Details

The Virginia Roofing Contractor examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the ROC specialty. Virginia requires Qualified Individuals for Roofing Contracting to meet applicable experience requirements and successfully complete the required technical examination.

Virginia uses PSI as the examination vendor for Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. Candidates must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the ROC examination.

The current Virginia Roofing Contractor examination contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the examination. The current minimum passing score is 75%.

The current examination content is organized into the following areas:

  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 5 questions
  • Surface Preparation and Planning: 5 questions
  • Steep and Low Slope Roofing, including Built-Up Roofing and Waterproofing: 13 questions
  • Asphalt, Wood, and Hard Tile Shingles and Wood Shakes: 5 questions
  • Roofing Components: 10 questions
  • Repairs and Reroofing: 6 questions
  • OSHA Safety: 6 questions

The examination requires preparation across both roofing systems and general contractor skills. Candidates should understand estimating, plans, surface preparation, steep- and low-slope systems, built-up roofing, waterproofing, shingles, shakes, roofing components, repair work, reroofing, and OSHA safety.

Steep- and low-slope roofing represents the largest technical category. Roofing Components is another major portion of the exam. Candidates should therefore understand not only roof coverings but also the flashing, insulation, drainage, attachment, edges, penetrations, substrates, and related components that make a roofing system perform properly.

The current Virginia Qualified Individual specialty examination fee is $85, paid directly to PSI. That testing fee is separate from the Book & Courses Rental Package pricing.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing combines a contractor license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Roofing Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation ROC.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Roofing Contracting. The applicable specialty designation is ROC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the Roofing specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must have qualifying experience within the Roofing specialty.
  5. Submit required experience verification. The applicable documentation must be completed for specialties requiring examination approval.
  6. Receive approval for the ROC examination. Candidates must complete the required qualification process before scheduling.
  7. Prepare for the technical examination. Use the course and rental references to study roofing systems, estimating, safety, repairs, and related technical subjects.
  8. Successfully complete the Roofing Contractor examination. Examination completion is required for the Qualified Individual.
  9. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education.
  10. Complete Class A or Class B business requirements when applicable. These requirements are separate from the ROC technical examination.
  11. Submit the contractor license application. The business must satisfy the remaining requirements for its selected class and specialty.

The ROC technical exam is only one part of the contractor licensing process. Passing the examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The contractor business must also meet the applicable class, business, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, and application requirements.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses business entities and sole proprietors engaged in contracting. A contractor license includes both a license class and one or more classifications or specialties.

The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management. Current minimum experience requirements are one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Roofing is among the contractor specialties requiring the Qualified Individual to successfully complete a technical examination. An Experience Verification Form is required for Qualified Individuals requesting a designation that requires examination pre-approval.

The Virginia ROC specialty covers the installation, repair, removal, and improvement of materials commonly used in the roofing industry to form watertight and weather-resistant surfaces for roofs and decks. The defined scope also includes roofing components installed in conjunction with a roofing project, dampproofing, waterproofing, and above-deck roof insulation systems.

New contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This business course is separate from preparation for the ROC technical examination.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants also have Designated Employee examination requirements. Candidates using this package for broader contractor preparation can use the included NASCLA Virginia guide and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code as study references for applicable business and regulatory material.

Test Information and Study Materials

The package includes 6 months of course access, allowing candidates to study the ROC examination content in stages. Because the examination is closed book, preparation should gradually move from reference-supported learning toward independent recall and application.

Steep and Low Slope Roofing should receive substantial study time. Review roof assemblies, membrane systems, built-up roofing, flashing, insulation, waterproofing, drainage, fastening, penetration details, and installation principles.

Shingles and Shakes should include asphalt shingles, wood shingles and shakes, hard tile materials, underlayments, attachment methods, flashing, slopes, and installation considerations.

Roofing Components can include decks, insulation, drainage, flashing, fasteners, edges, penetrations, underlayments, transitions, and related materials that contribute to complete roof-system performance.

Estimating and Plan Reading preparation should include roof areas, slopes, dimensions, material quantities, waste, drawings, and specifications. Candidates should practice translating project information into practical roofing calculations.

Repairs and Reroofing should include evaluation of existing systems, substrate conditions, removal, repair procedures, flashing, replacement materials, and integration of new roofing with existing construction.

OSHA Safety should be incorporated throughout preparation. Roofing professionals routinely work around fall hazards, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, and elevated work surfaces.

Practice questions are especially important for a closed-book examination. Candidates should first attempt questions without consulting the references. Afterward, use the appropriate book to confirm the answer, understand the underlying principle, and reinforce the topic.

As exam readiness improves, candidates can complete larger groups of practice questions without books and reserve reference use for reviewing missed answers. This helps strengthen recall while continuing to benefit from the technical depth of the books.
