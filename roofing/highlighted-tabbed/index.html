Exam Details

The Virginia Roofing Contractor examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the ROC specialty. Candidates seeking the Roofing specialty must satisfy the applicable Virginia qualification requirements and receive approval before scheduling the examination.

The Virginia ROC examination contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the test. The examination is designed to evaluate knowledge associated with roofing contracting and can include both reference-based questions and general trade knowledge.

Major areas of preparation include:

  • Estimating and Plan Reading
  • Surface Preparation and Planning
  • Steep-Slope Roofing
  • Low-Slope Roofing
  • Built-Up Roofing
  • Waterproofing
  • Roofing Components
  • Safety
  • Repairs and Reroofing

Candidates should understand common roofing materials, roof decks, insulation, underlayments, flashing, drainage, fasteners, attachment methods, roof penetrations, edge conditions, repair procedures, and reroofing considerations. Preparation should include both residential and nonresidential roofing because the included references cover different types of construction.

Estimating and plan reading are also important. Roofing contractors regularly work with roof dimensions, slopes, plans, specifications, quantities, labor requirements, and material calculations. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting construction information and applying it to roofing work.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity with the references. Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Roofing Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation ROC.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Roofing Contracting. The applicable specialty designation is ROC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the Roofing specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must have experience relevant to Roofing Contracting and the contractor class being requested.
  5. Complete the applicable experience verification. Qualified Individuals seeking specialties that require examination pre-approval must submit the required experience documentation.
  6. Receive approval for the ROC examination. Candidates must obtain the applicable approval before scheduling the technical test.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Study roofing systems, safety, estimating, building codes, waterproofing, repairs, reroofing, and open-book navigation.
  8. Complete the Roofing Contractor examination. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable technical exam requirement.
  9. Complete pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education.
  10. Complete applicable Class A or Class B business requirements. Class A and Class B businesses have additional Designated Employee examination requirements.
  11. Submit the contractor license application. The business must complete the licensing process for the selected class and Roofing specialty.

Passing the Roofing Contractor examination does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. The technical examination is one part of the overall process, which also includes the business entity, license class, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, application, and other applicable requirements.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses. Every contractor license includes a class and one or more classifications or specialties. The class determines the monetary limits of the contractor's work, while the specialty determines the type of work the business may perform.

Virginia currently defines Class C contractors as businesses performing projects over $1,000 but under $30,000, with annual contracting activity not exceeding $250,000. Class B applies to projects from $30,000 to under $150,000, or annual contracting activity from $250,000 to under $1 million. Class A applies when a single project is $150,000 or more or annual contracting activity reaches $1 million or more.

The Qualified Individual for a contractor specialty must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management and must satisfy the applicable experience requirement. Current minimum experience requirements are one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Virginia defines Roofing Contracting as work involving installation, repair, removal, or improvement of materials that form watertight, weather-resistant surfaces for roofs and decks. The scope includes related roofing components, dampproofing, waterproofing, and roof insulation systems installed above the roof deck.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. Class A and Class B applicants must also satisfy applicable Designated Employee business examination requirements, which are separate from the ROC technical exam.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Roofing Contractor study plan should begin with the major exam categories rather than reading all six references from beginning to end. Organizing preparation by subject can make the material more manageable and help candidates use the highlighted and tabbed books effectively.

Estimating and Plan Reading should include roof dimensions, slope, material quantities, waste allowances, labor considerations, plans, specifications, and practical job calculations. Candidates should understand how roofing measurements translate into materials and installation requirements.

Surface Preparation and Planning should include evaluation of roof decks, substrates, existing roofing, moisture conditions, removal requirements, sequencing, and preparation before installation begins.

Steep-Slope Roofing preparation should include underlayments, roof coverings, flashing, valleys, ridges, penetrations, attachment methods, ventilation considerations, and weather protection. Use both the applicable building code and NRCA steep-slope manual during study.

Low-Slope and Membrane Roofing should include built-up systems, membrane materials, flashing, insulation, drainage, penetrations, attachment, terminations, and waterproofing. The NRCA membrane manual is particularly useful for this area.

Roofing Components can include insulation, fasteners, flashing, drainage features, roof decks, penetrations, edges, transitions, and related materials that function together as a roofing assembly.

Safety should be incorporated throughout preparation. Roofing contractors work at elevation and encounter fall hazards, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, and other conditions governed by OSHA construction requirements.

Repairs and Reroofing should include assessment of existing conditions, code considerations, substrate preparation, removal, integration of new materials, flashing, and repair techniques.

Use the permanent tabs actively during study. Learn what each tab represents and practice locating related information without relying solely on highlighted passages. An exam question may involve an exception, table, definition, or nearby provision rather than the exact highlighted sentence.

Timed reference practice can also improve preparation. Select a roofing question, identify the subject, choose the appropriate book, and locate the supporting information within a limited amount of time. Repeat this exercise across code, safety, estimating, steep-slope, and membrane topics.
