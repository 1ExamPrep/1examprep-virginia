### Exam Details

The Virginia Roofing Contractor examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the **ROC** specialty. Virginia requires the Qualified Individual for applicable contractor specialties to satisfy the experience requirements and complete the appropriate technical examination before the specialty can be approved for the contractor business.

Virginia uses PSI as its examination vendor for Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. Candidates must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the Roofing Contractor examination.

The examination evaluates knowledge associated with professional roofing work. Candidates should prepare across several major subject areas, including:

- Estimating and plan reading
- Surface preparation and planning
- Steep-slope roofing
- Low-slope roofing
- Built-up roofing
- Waterproofing
- Roofing components
- Construction safety
- Repairs and reroofing

Roofing-system questions can require candidates to understand how individual components work together to create a complete weather-resistant assembly. This may include roof decks, insulation, underlayments, membranes, roof coverings, flashing, drainage, attachment, penetrations, edges, transitions, and related materials.

Estimating and plan-reading preparation is also important. Roofing contractors routinely work with roof areas, dimensions, slopes, quantities, materials, plans, specifications, and project requirements. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting written and graphical construction information and applying it to roofing work.

Safety preparation should remain part of the entire study process. Roofing contractors commonly work at elevation and encounter ladders, scaffolds, fall hazards, material handling, tools, and changing work surfaces. OSHA construction requirements are therefore an important part of exam preparation.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Roofing Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted references because the books are not supplied at the testing center.

The open-book format makes efficient reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should know which reference is most appropriate for each subject instead of attempting to search every book whenever they encounter a difficult question.

A construction-safety question may require **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**. A building-code question may require the International Building Code or International Residential Code depending on the type of construction involved.

Questions involving measurements, estimating, quantities, or general roofing practices may be supported by **Roofing Construction and Estimating**. Technical steep-slope questions can be reviewed through the **NRCA Roofing Manual: Steep Slope Roof Systems**, while membrane and low-slope subjects may require the **NRCA Roofing Manual: Membrane Roofing Systems**.

Reference books permitted under the testing rules may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used within applicable examination requirements, while removable tabs are not permitted.

Live Virtual training and Self Study preparation should both include repeated use of the references. The objective is to learn the roofing material and develop enough familiarity with the books to find detailed information efficiently when it is needed.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Roofing Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation ROC.

1. **Determine the contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select Roofing Contracting.** The applicable specialty abbreviation is ROC.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for Roofing Contracting.
4. **Document applicable roofing experience.** The Qualified Individual must meet the minimum experience requirement associated with the license class.
5. **Complete the required experience verification.** Candidates seeking a specialty requiring examination pre-approval must provide the applicable documentation.
6. **Receive approval for the ROC examination.** The Qualified Individual must be approved before scheduling the technical examination.
7. **Prepare for the examination.** Use Live Virtual instruction, Self Study preparation, practice review, and the applicable roofing reference materials.
8. **Complete the required technical examination.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the examination requirement for the ROC specialty.
9. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must complete the applicable Board-approved pre-license education.
10. **Complete applicable Class A or Class B business requirements.** These requirements are separate from the ROC technical examination.
11. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must complete the application process for its selected class and Roofing specialty.

Passing the Roofing Contractor technical examination does not by itself issue a contractor license. The contractor business must also meet the requirements associated with its license class, Qualified Individual, Responsible Management, pre-license education, and other applicable licensing requirements.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses and establishes the requirements for contractor classes, classifications, specialties, Qualified Individuals, and Responsible Management.

Every business requesting the Roofing specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable eligibility requirements. The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management.

The current minimum work-experience requirements for Qualified Individuals are **one year for Class C**, **three years for Class B**, and **five years for Class A**. The experience must be within the scope of the specialty for which the Qualified Individual is qualifying the business.

An Experience Verification Form is required when a Qualified Individual is requesting a designation that requires pre-approval for an examination. Candidates pursuing Roofing Contracting should therefore organize their applicable roofing experience as part of the licensing process.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive ROC study strategy should follow the examination categories rather than treating all six references as one large reading assignment. Candidates can divide preparation among estimating, preparation, roofing systems, components, safety, and repair or reroofing topics.

**Steep-Slope Roofing** preparation can include roof coverings, underlayments, flashing, valleys, ridges, penetrations, drainage, attachment, and weather-resistant construction. Candidates should connect code requirements with the technical information contained in the NRCA steep-slope manual.

**Low-Slope and Membrane Roofing** should include membrane systems, built-up roofing, insulation, waterproofing, flashing, drainage, penetrations, attachment, and terminations. Candidates should become familiar with terminology and construction methods that may differ from steep-slope roofing.

**Estimating and Plan Reading** preparation should include roof areas, slope, material quantities, waste, plans, specifications, and practical estimating procedures. Candidates should understand how drawings and measurements are translated into roofing material requirements.

**Roofing Components** can include roof decks, insulation, flashing, fasteners, underlayments, drainage features, penetrations, edges, transitions, and other materials that function together as a roof assembly.

**Surface Preparation and Planning** can include existing roof conditions, substrate evaluation, deck preparation, moisture concerns, removal requirements, material compatibility, sequencing, and job planning.

**Repairs and Reroofing** should include evaluation of existing systems, removal, repair methods, substrate conditions, flashing, integration of new materials, and applicable code considerations.

**Safety** should remain part of every study phase. Candidates should understand how OSHA construction requirements apply to fall exposure, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, and other roofing operations.
