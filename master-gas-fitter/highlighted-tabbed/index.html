Exam Details

The Virginia Master Gasfitter examination is administered through PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation and the Board for Contractors. Pre-approval from the Board is required before a candidate can sit for the examination.

The Journeyman and Master Gasfitter examination portion contains 50 items. Master Gasfitter candidates must answer at least 37 questions correctly. Candidates are allowed 150 minutes to complete the examination.

The current examination outline includes the following subject areas:

  • Standards of Practice: 3 items
  • Standards of Conduct: 2 items
  • Renewal and Reinstatement: 2 items
  • Definitions: 1 item
  • Qualification for Licensure: 1 item
  • Revocation of Licensure: 1 item
  • Natural and LP Gas Piping: 12 items
  • Pipe Sizing: 11 items
  • LP-Gas Tanks: 9 items
  • Gas Piping Controls: 5 items
  • Testing: 3 items

The distribution shows why candidates should prepare across both regulatory and technical material. Natural and LP gas piping, pipe sizing, and LP-gas tanks represent a significant part of the examination, but licensing regulations, controls, testing, and professional requirements also contribute to the overall score.

The current PSI examination fee for the Master Gasfitter examination is $125. Exam fees are paid directly to PSI after the candidate has satisfied the applicable education and work experience requirements and received approval from the Board.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Master Gasfitter examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because reference materials are not provided at the testing site.

PSI permits examination references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed. Reference materials containing writing are not permitted, and candidates may not write in reference books during the examination session.

Study guides are not allowed in the examination room. Loose papers and unbound reference materials are also prohibited. Downloaded references must meet PSI binding requirements before they can be used during testing.

Candidates should repeatedly practice with the prepared books so they understand why specific sections are important and how to locate related information quickly.

Open-book testing still requires preparation. With 50 questions and 150 minutes, candidates have an average of three minutes per scored item. Some questions may be answered directly from trade knowledge, while others may require checking a code section, sizing table, LP-gas requirement, or Virginia regulation. Efficient navigation can help candidates manage their testing time more effectively.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Master Gasfitter credential is an individual tradesman license regulated through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates pursuing this credential should follow the application and examination process established by the Board.

  1. Review Virginia Master Gasfitter requirements. Candidates should determine which current education and experience pathway applies to their background before submitting an application.
  2. Complete the applicable education and work experience requirements. Virginia requires candidates to satisfy the Board’s qualifications before examination approval is issued.
  3. Submit the appropriate exam and license application. The Board reviews applications and determines eligibility to test.
  4. Receive Board approval. Pre-approval is required before the Master Gasfitter examination can be scheduled.
  5. Schedule the examination with PSI. After receiving approval, candidates may proceed with PSI registration and examination scheduling.
  6. Prepare with the permitted references. Candidates should study the current exam topics and become comfortable navigating the Virginia regulations, 2021 International Fuel Gas Code, and NFPA 58.
  7. Take the Master Gasfitter examination. Candidates must meet the required passing score for the Master level.
  8. Complete applicable licensing requirements. After successfully completing the required examination, candidates must satisfy the remaining Board requirements for license issuance.

A valid Master Gas-Fitter Tradesman License also has importance for contractor-business licensing. Virginia identifies a valid Master Gas-Fitter Tradesman License as the Qualified Individual requirement for the Gas Fitting contractor specialty, identified as GFC.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen working in gas fitting along with other regulated trades. The Master Gasfitter credential is an individual license rather than the contractor business license itself.

Businesses seeking a Virginia contractor license for Gas Fitting must separately meet the requirements that apply to the contractor business license. Virginia contractor businesses are licensed as Class A, Class B, or Class C and must hold the appropriate classification or specialty for the work they perform.

For the Gas Fitting contractor specialty, Virginia requires the Qualified Individual to hold a valid Master Gas-Fitter Tradesman License. This makes the individual Master Gasfitter credential an important qualification for contractors pursuing the GFC specialty.

Individual tradesmen and contractor businesses follow different application structures. Candidates should distinguish between earning the individual Master Gasfitter license and obtaining a contractor business license. Holding the Master Gasfitter credential does not by itself replace the separate business licensing requirements that apply to a contractor firm.

Virginia also requires continuing education for licensed gas fitters. Current Board requirements call for one hour of approved continuing education for Gas Fitters during each three-year renewal period. The continuing education course must cover the International Fuel Gas Code and must be provided through a Board-approved education provider.

  • International Fuel Gas Code, 2021

  • NFPA 58 - Standard for Liquid Petroleum Gas Code, 2014
    NFPA 58 provides requirements for liquefied petroleum gas systems. It addresses topics involving LP-gas containers, tanks, storage, piping, transfer operations, location and installation requirements, and safety considerations. This reference is particularly important for the LP-gas tank and piping topics included in the Master Gasfitter examination.

How does the Master Gas Fitter (LP) exam work?

The Master Gas Fitter (LP) exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
