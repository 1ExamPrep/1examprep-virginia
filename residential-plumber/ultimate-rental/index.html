Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Plumber examination is part of Virginia's individual tradesman licensing program. Candidates must receive the applicable approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Residential Plumber portion contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the examination and need at least 35 correct answers to meet the passing requirement.

The examination combines Virginia regulatory subjects with technical residential plumbing material. The current content outline includes:

  • Standards of Practice: 3 questions
  • Standards of Conduct: 2 questions
  • Renewal and Reinstatement: 1 question
  • Definitions: 1 question
  • Qualification for Licensure: 1 question
  • Drainage, Waste and Vents: 12 questions
  • Water Supply Systems: 8 questions
  • Backflow Prevention: 6 questions
  • Storm Drainage Systems: 4 questions
  • Plumbing Fixtures: 4 questions
  • General Regulations: 4 questions
  • Interceptors and Traps: 4 questions

Drainage, Waste and Vents is the largest individual technical section of the examination. Water Supply Systems and Backflow Prevention are also significant areas. Candidates should devote preparation time according to the examination outline rather than studying every portion of the plumbing code equally.

The regulatory portion also matters. Questions involving professional standards, licensing qualifications, renewal, reinstatement, and definitions require familiarity with the Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations.

Virginia requires Board pre-approval before candidates sit for the tradesman examination. PSI administers the examination after approval has been received.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their permitted reference materials because the testing center does not provide the books.

Open-book testing makes reference-navigation practice an important part of preparation. With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Candidates who spend excessive time searching an unfamiliar code book may have less time available for later questions.

The International Plumbing Code, 2021 serves as the principal technical reference for plumbing preparation. Candidates should become familiar with its index, chapters, definitions, tables, and individual code sections.

The Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations supports preparation for the state-specific regulatory portion of the examination. Questions involving licensing qualifications, professional conduct, renewal, reinstatement, or Standards of Practice may require this reference instead of the plumbing code.

A productive open-book strategy begins with identifying the subject of the question before reaching for a reference. A drainage, venting, water-supply, backflow, fixture, or trap question will typically lead to the plumbing code, while a licensing or conduct question may lead to the Virginia regulations.

Candidates should practice using the table of contents and index rather than scanning pages one at a time. Learning alternate terminology is also helpful because the wording used in a question may not exactly match the index entry a candidate initially expects.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman credential is issued to an individual through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Applicants must meet the applicable experience and training requirements, receive examination approval, complete the test, and satisfy the remaining licensing requirements.

  1. Review the Residential Plumber qualification pathways. Virginia provides several combinations of practical plumbing experience and formal vocational training.
  2. Select the appropriate qualification pathway. Determine which combination matches your documented work experience and training.
  3. Document practical plumbing experience. Applicants must provide the required experience verification for the qualification pathway being used.
  4. Provide vocational training documentation when applicable. Candidates using formal training must submit the required supporting records.
  5. Submit the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application. Select the Residential Plumber Tradesman designation.
  6. Receive Board approval. Pre-approval is required before scheduling the examination.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Use the one-year course access and reference books to review technical and regulatory material.
  8. Schedule and complete the PSI examination. Candidates can proceed through the testing process after approval.
  9. Complete the remaining licensing requirements. Candidates proceed toward issuance of the Residential Plumber Tradesman credential after satisfying the applicable requirements.

State Requirements

Virginia introduced Residential Plumber and Residential HVAC tradesman credentials as additional residential licensing pathways beginning April 1, 2025. The Residential Plumber credential is specifically intended for plumbing work within the residential scope and does not permit commercial plumbing work outside that scope.

Applicants must meet an approved combination of practical plumbing experience and formal vocational training. Current pathways include:

  • 2 years of practical experience plus 160 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 3 years of practical experience plus 120 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 4 years of practical experience plus 80 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 5 years of practical experience plus 40 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 6 years of practical experience without using formal vocational training toward the qualification pathway.

Applicants using practical experience must provide the required verification. Candidates who use vocational education toward their qualification must also provide documentation supporting those training hours.

Virginia provides the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application specifically for individuals. The current application lists a $150 application fee. This state application fee is separate from the Ultimate package price and the applicable PSI examination fee.

The Virginia tradesmen program also requires Board pre-approval before an applicant can sit for the examination. Candidates should therefore complete the application and qualification process before attempting to schedule testing.

The Residential Plumber credential applies to the individual tradesman. Business owners should understand that forming or operating a plumbing contracting company can involve separate Virginia contractor licensing obligations.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Ultimate package includes 1 year of course access, allowing candidates to divide preparation into manageable stages. A productive approach begins with the examination outline and gives additional study time to the subjects containing the greatest number of questions.

Drainage, Waste and Vents contains 12 questions and should receive substantial attention. Candidates should review sanitary drainage, vent systems, drainage connections, pipe sizing, fixture-unit concepts, slopes, cleanouts, and related code requirements.

Water Supply Systems contains eight questions. Study potable water distribution, water-service piping, sizing principles, valves, materials, pressure-related provisions, and installation requirements.

Backflow Prevention contains six questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of protecting potable water systems from contamination and become familiar with the devices, arrangements, and code provisions associated with backflow protection.

Storm Drainage Systems, Plumbing Fixtures, General Regulations, and Interceptors and Traps each account for four questions. Together, these categories make up a significant part of the technical examination.

Virginia regulatory subjects should be studied separately from technical plumbing material. Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, qualifications, renewal, reinstatement, and definitions can affect the final exam result even though they are not plumbing-installation questions.

Reference navigation should be incorporated into regular course study. When a practice question is answered, candidates should locate the supporting code or regulation and review the surrounding material. This helps reinforce knowledge and builds familiarity with where information appears in the books.

When a question is answered incorrectly, avoid memorizing only the correct response. Locate the applicable provision, determine why it applies, and review neighboring sections or exceptions. This creates a stronger understanding of the rule and makes preparation more adaptable to differently worded questions.

Timed practice can help candidates develop a realistic examination rhythm. The goal is to balance plumbing knowledge with efficient reference use. Questions that can be answered confidently may not require a lookup, while detailed code or regulatory questions may benefit from confirmation.
