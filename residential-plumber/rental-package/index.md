### State Requirements

Virginia's Residential Plumber license is intended for residential plumbing work and does not authorize commercial plumbing outside the residential scope. The Virginia Tradesmen Program regulates the individual credential, while contractor business licensing is handled separately through the Board for Contractors.

Residential Plumber applicants must satisfy the applicable combination of practical experience and vocational education established for the residential tradesman pathway. The current pathways include:

- **2 years of practical experience** plus **160 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **3 years of practical experience** plus **120 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **4 years of practical experience** plus **80 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **5 years of practical experience** plus **40 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **6 years of practical experience** without using formal vocational training toward the qualification pathway.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old and provide the documentation required for their selected qualification pathway. Practical experience must be relevant to plumbing work, and vocational education must be documented when it is being used to satisfy part of the eligibility requirement.

The Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application is for individuals, not business entities. Virginia also states that an applicant performing contracting work over the applicable contractor licensing threshold must address contractor licensing requirements separately.

## How does the Residential Plumber Tradesman exam work?

The Residential Plumber Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
