Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Plumber examination is administered through Virginia's individual tradesman licensing program. Applicants must receive the appropriate approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before proceeding with the examination process through PSI.

The Residential Plumber portion contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the examination and need at least 35 correct answers to meet the passing requirement.

The examination content is divided between Virginia regulatory material and technical plumbing subjects. The current content outline includes:

  • Standards of Practice: 3 questions
  • Standards of Conduct: 2 questions
  • Renewal and Reinstatement: 1 question
  • Definitions: 1 question
  • Qualification for Licensure: 1 question
  • Drainage, Waste and Vents: 12 questions
  • Water Supply Systems: 8 questions
  • Backflow Prevention: 6 questions
  • Storm Drainage Systems: 4 questions
  • Plumbing Fixtures: 4 questions
  • General Regulations: 4 questions
  • Interceptors and Traps: 4 questions

Drainage, waste and vent systems make up the largest single technical section of the examination. Water supply systems and backflow prevention are also major areas, so candidates should dedicate significant study time to these subjects.

The Virginia regulatory questions should not be overlooked. Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, Renewal and Reinstatement, Definitions, and Qualification for Licensure are all part of the examination and are supported by the Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations.

With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Efficient use of the highlighted and tabbed references can help candidates avoid spending unnecessary time searching through unfamiliar material.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted reference materials because books are not supplied at the testing center.

This makes reference preparation an important part of exam readiness. A candidate may have years of practical plumbing experience but still benefit from learning how the written code is organized and where specific requirements appear.

The highlighted and permanently tabbed books in this package are designed to support that preparation. Permanent tabs can help candidates move directly to important subjects, while highlighting can make selected provisions easier to identify once the correct section has been located.

Candidates should still learn how to use the index, table of contents, chapter organization, definitions, code tables, and individual sections. Not every question will use the same wording as a tab or highlighted passage, so understanding the structure of the book remains essential.

A question involving drainage sizing, venting, water distribution, fixtures, backflow, or traps will generally lead candidates to the International Plumbing Code. A question about licensing qualifications, standards of conduct, renewal, or professional responsibilities may require the Virginia regulations instead.

Practice identifying the subject of each question before opening a reference. This simple step can reduce the tendency to search both books unnecessarily and can improve overall testing efficiency.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman credential is issued to an individual through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Applicants must qualify for examination approval before proceeding through the testing and licensing process.

  1. Review the Residential Plumber qualification requirements. Virginia provides several pathways based on practical plumbing experience and formal vocational training.
  2. Select the applicable qualification pathway. Determine which combination of practical experience and vocational training matches your background.
  3. Document plumbing experience. Applicants must provide the required experience verification for the qualification pathway being used.
  4. Provide vocational training documentation when applicable. Candidates using formal education toward qualification must submit the appropriate records.
  5. Submit the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application. Select the Residential Plumber Tradesman designation.
  6. Receive examination approval. Approval is required before scheduling the examination.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Study the highlighted and tabbed Virginia regulations and 2021 International Plumbing Code.
  8. Schedule and complete the examination. Candidates proceed through the approved PSI testing process.
  9. Complete the remaining licensing requirements. Once the applicable qualifications and examination requirements are satisfied, the individual can proceed toward issuance of the Residential Plumber Tradesman credential.

The Residential Plumber Tradesman license is an individual credential. It should not be confused with a Virginia contractor business license. A business performing plumbing contracting work may have separate contractor licensing responsibilities depending on the nature and value of the work being performed.

State Requirements

Virginia's Residential Plumber Tradesman licensing pathway became effective April 1, 2025. The credential was created as an additional route for individuals seeking to perform plumbing work in residential structures while gaining experience that can support future advancement in the trade.

Virginia defines a Residential Plumber Tradesman as a person possessing the ability, proficiency, and qualifications to perform plumbing work using sufficient working knowledge to comply with applicable provisions of the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code for dwellings and townhouses and structures annexed to those dwellings or townhouses.

The credential does not include commercial, industrial, institutional, or government-use structures outside the defined residential scope.

Current Virginia regulations provide the following qualification pathways:

  • 160 hours of formal vocational training plus 2 years of practical experience.
  • 120 hours of formal vocational training plus 3 years of practical experience.
  • 80 hours of formal vocational training plus 4 years of practical experience.
  • 40 hours of formal vocational training plus 5 years of practical experience.
  • No formal vocational training plus 6 years of practical experience.

The experience must be practical experience in the plumbing trade. Applicants using formal vocational training must provide documentation of the applicable education along with required experience verification.

Residential Plumber Tradesman licensing can also serve as part of a longer professional pathway. Virginia regulations recognize experience as a licensed Residential Plumber Tradesman when establishing certain future qualifications for higher tradesman licensing.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Residential Plumber study plan should begin with the examination outline. Candidates can use the number of questions assigned to each subject to determine where to spend the most preparation time.

Drainage, Waste and Vents contains 12 questions and is the largest section. Candidates should review sanitary drainage systems, venting methods, pipe sizing, drainage fixture units, cleanouts, slopes, connections, and related code requirements.

Water Supply Systems contains eight questions. Study potable water distribution, sizing, materials, valves, service piping, pressure-related requirements, and installation provisions.

Backflow Prevention contains six questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of protecting potable water systems from contamination and become familiar with the methods and devices used to provide the required protection.

Storm Drainage Systems, Plumbing Fixtures, General Regulations, and Interceptors and Traps each contain four questions. Taken together, these areas account for a substantial portion of the technical exam and deserve regular review.

Virginia regulatory material should be studied separately. Learn the Standards of Practice and Standards of Conduct that apply to licensed tradesmen, along with qualification, renewal, reinstatement, and definition sections.

The highlighted and tabbed books can make this study process more organized. Review the tabs before working practice questions and learn what subject each tab represents. When a question is answered incorrectly, use the relevant tab or index entry to locate the supporting code section and review the surrounding material.

Do not rely only on highlighted lines. Examination questions may test a related provision, exception, table, or definition located near the highlighted section. Reading the surrounding code can strengthen understanding and make the reference more useful when the question is worded differently.

Timed navigation exercises can also improve preparation. Select a plumbing topic, open the correct reference, and practice finding the applicable section within a limited amount of time. Repeat this process with drainage, venting, fixtures, backflow, water supply, and regulatory questions.

How does the Residential Plumber Tradesman exam work?

The Residential Plumber Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
