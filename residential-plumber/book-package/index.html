About this Residential Plumber Tradesman package

Prepare for the Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman examination with a focused two-book reference package built around the regulatory and plumbing code materials used for the exam. The Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman Book Package includes the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations and the International Plumbing Code, 2021, giving candidates the primary references needed to study Virginia licensing requirements and residential plumbing code topics.

Virginia's Residential Plumber Tradesman credential is an individual license for qualified professionals performing plumbing work within the residential scope established by the Commonwealth. The residential designation is intended for plumbing work involving dwellings and townhouses, including structures annexed to those residential buildings. It does not authorize plumbing work in commercial, industrial, institutional, or government-use structures outside the residential scope.

This book package is designed for plumbers, plumbing apprentices, experienced tradespeople, residential plumbing professionals, and other candidates preparing to enter Virginia's residential tradesman licensing pathway. It can also be useful for candidates who have practical plumbing experience but need to strengthen their familiarity with formal code requirements and Virginia regulatory material before taking the examination.

The Residential Plumber exam tests more than field experience alone. Candidates should be prepared to work with subjects involving drainage, waste and vent systems, water supply systems, backflow prevention, storm drainage, plumbing fixtures, general plumbing regulations, interceptors and traps, and Virginia tradesman licensing requirements.

Because the examination is open book, candidates should also develop a strong reference-navigation strategy. Knowing how plumbing systems work is important, but candidates should be able to locate exact code provisions, definitions, tables, and regulatory requirements efficiently when additional confirmation is needed during the examination.

Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Plumber examination is administered through Virginia's individual tradesman licensing program. Candidates must receive approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Residential Plumber portion contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the examination and must answer at least 35 questions correctly to achieve the required passing score.

The current examination content outline includes:

  • Standards of Practice: 3 questions
  • Standards of Conduct: 2 questions
  • Renewal and Reinstatement: 1 question
  • Definitions: 1 question
  • Qualification for Licensure: 1 question
  • Drainage, Waste and Vents: 12 questions
  • Water Supply Systems: 8 questions
  • Backflow Prevention: 6 questions
  • Storm Drainage Systems: 4 questions
  • Plumbing Fixtures: 4 questions
  • General Regulations: 4 questions
  • Interceptors and Traps: 4 questions

Drainage, waste and vent systems represent the largest individual technical category on the examination. Water supply systems and backflow prevention are also significant areas of preparation. Candidates should therefore spend substantial study time learning how these systems are designed, installed, vented, protected, and regulated under the applicable plumbing code.

The examination also includes questions about Virginia licensing requirements. Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, Renewal and Reinstatement, Definitions, and Qualification for Licensure are covered through the Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations.

The current PSI examination fee for the Virginia Residential Plumber examination is $100. The examination fee is paid through the testing process and is separate from the purchase of this book package.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted reference materials because books are not supplied at the testing site.

The current examination rules allow references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted, while removable tabs are not allowed. Reference materials containing handwriting are not permitted, and candidates may not write in the references during the examination session.

No loose papers or unbound reference material may be used. Candidates should make sure their references comply with the applicable testing requirements before arriving for the examination.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. A candidate who searches through the plumbing code from beginning to end for every answer can quickly lose valuable testing time.

A stronger approach is to learn the major plumbing subjects first and then use the books selectively. If a question asks about drainage or venting, candidates should recognize the subject immediately and know where to begin searching in the International Plumbing Code. Questions involving licensing conduct, qualifications, or renewal may instead require the Virginia regulations.

Practice should include regular use of the table of contents, index, chapter headings, definitions, code sections, and tables. The more familiar candidates become with the structure of each reference, the easier it becomes to select the correct book and locate the relevant information efficiently.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman credential is issued to an individual through the Virginia Board for Contractors. It is separate from a Virginia contractor business license.

  1. Review the Residential Plumber qualification requirements. Virginia provides approved combinations of practical plumbing experience and formal vocational training.
  2. Determine the qualification pathway that applies to your background. Candidates may qualify through varying combinations of vocational education and practical plumbing experience or through the experience-only pathway.
  3. Document practical plumbing experience. Applicants must provide the required experience verification for the pathway being used.
  4. Provide vocational training records when applicable. Applicants relying on formal vocational education must submit the required documentation.
  5. Submit the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application. Select Residential Plumber Tradesman as the requested designation.
  6. Receive approval from the Board. Candidates must be approved before scheduling the examination.
  7. Prepare for the Residential Plumber examination. Study the Virginia regulations and 2021 International Plumbing Code while practicing open-book navigation.
  8. Schedule and complete the PSI examination. Once approved, candidates can proceed through the testing process.
  9. Complete the remaining licensing requirements. After satisfying the applicable qualifications and examination requirement, candidates can proceed toward issuance of the Residential Plumber Tradesman credential.

The current Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application lists a $150 application fee. Candidates should distinguish this state application fee from the separate PSI examination fee.

The individual tradesman credential should also be distinguished from contractor business licensing. Virginia states that individuals performing contracting work over the applicable contractor licensing threshold must address contractor licensing requirements separately.

State Requirements

Virginia's Residential Plumber Tradesman license became available as part of the residential tradesman licensing pathway effective April 1, 2025. The credential gives qualified individuals a residential plumbing licensing option with requirements different from those for the traditional journeyman plumber credential.

A Residential Plumber Tradesman is defined as a person possessing the ability, proficiency, and qualifications to perform plumbing work using working knowledge sufficient to comply with the applicable provisions of the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code for dwellings and townhouses and structures annexed to those residential buildings.

The credential does not extend to commercial, industrial, institutional, or government-use structures outside the defined residential scope.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old and satisfy one of Virginia's approved experience and education combinations. The current qualification pathways include:

  • 160 hours of formal vocational training plus 2 years of practical experience in plumbing.
  • 120 hours of formal vocational training plus 3 years of practical experience.
  • 80 hours of formal vocational training plus 4 years of practical experience.
  • 40 hours of formal vocational training plus 5 years of practical experience.
  • 6 years of practical experience without using formal vocational training toward the qualification pathway.

Applicants using vocational training must provide the applicable documentation. Practical experience must also be verified according to the requirements of the Virginia Board for Contractors.

Virginia also provides licensing pathways for certain applicants who hold qualifying residential tradesman credentials from other jurisdictions, subject to the applicable application and documentation requirements.

Residential Plumber Tradesman licensing is intended for individuals. A plumbing company or other business performing contracting work may also need a separate Virginia contractor license depending on the nature and value of the work.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Residential Plumber study plan should begin with the exam content outline. The number of questions assigned to each subject can help candidates decide where to devote their preparation time.

Drainage, Waste and Vents is the largest section, with 12 questions. Candidates should understand sanitary drainage systems, venting principles, drainage connections, sizing concepts, slope requirements, cleanouts, and other provisions governing the movement of wastewater and air through plumbing systems.

Water Supply Systems accounts for eight questions. Candidates should review potable water distribution, piping requirements, valves, sizing concepts, water service provisions, and applicable installation requirements.

Backflow Prevention contributes six questions. Candidates should understand the purpose of protecting potable water supplies from contamination and become familiar with applicable backflow protection methods and code provisions.

Storm Drainage Systems, Plumbing Fixtures, General Regulations, and Interceptors and Traps each account for four questions. Combined, these areas make up a significant portion of the technical examination and should not be treated as minor topics.

Virginia regulatory questions should be studied separately from technical plumbing material. Learn the Standards of Practice and Standards of Conduct that apply to tradesmen and review definitions, qualification requirements, renewal, and reinstatement provisions.

Reference-navigation practice should be incorporated into every study session. After answering a practice question, locate the supporting code section even when you already know the answer. This helps build familiarity with where information appears within the International Plumbing Code.

Use the index strategically. Plumbing terminology can vary, so practice searching by the main subject and by related terms. Once a likely code section is found, read the surrounding provisions instead of focusing on a single isolated sentence.

Timed practice can also help candidates develop an efficient testing rhythm. Because the examination provides 120 minutes for 50 questions, candidates should learn when a quick reference lookup is useful and when it makes more sense to answer from established knowledge and move forward.

When a practice question is answered incorrectly, use the error as a study opportunity. Find the correct provision, review the surrounding section, and understand why the other answer choices do not fit. This builds stronger code knowledge than memorizing individual practice answers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What books are included in the Virginia Residential Plumber Tradesman Book Package?

The package includes the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations and the International Plumbing Code, 2021.

Is the Virginia Residential Plumber examination open book?

Yes. The examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the testing site.

How many questions are on the Residential Plumber exam?

The Residential Plumber examination contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes and must answer at least 35 questions correctly to pass.

What is the largest subject area on the exam?

Drainage, Waste and Vents is the largest individual section with 12 questions. Water Supply Systems and Backflow Prevention are also major areas of the examination.

Can I highlight and tab my books?

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed. Removable tabs and handwritten material are not permitted under the current examination rules.

Who administers the Virginia Residential Plumber examination?

PSI administers the examination after the candidate receives the required approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors.

What experience is required for Residential Plumber licensing?

Virginia offers several pathways combining practical plumbing experience and formal vocational education, ranging from two years of experience with 160 hours of training to six years of practical experience without formal vocational training.

Does the Residential Plumber license permit commercial plumbing work?

No. The Residential Plumber Tradesman credential is limited to the residential scope established by Virginia and does not include commercial, industrial, institutional, or government-use structures outside that scope.

Is a Residential Plumber Tradesman license the same as a Virginia contractor license?

No. The Residential Plumber Tradesman credential is issued to an individual. A contractor license applies separately to a business when its contracting activity falls under Virginia's contractor licensing requirements.

Why is the Virginia regulations book included?

The examination includes questions involving Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, renewal and reinstatement, definitions, and qualifications for licensure. These subjects are covered by the Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations.

How does the Residential Plumber Tradesman exam work?

The Residential Plumber Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
