### Open Book Test

The Virginia Master Natural Gasfitter exam is an **open-book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own approved reference books to the examination. Reference books are not provided at the test site.

Open-book testing requires preparation before exam day. Candidates should not expect to search through unfamiliar books for the first time during the test. The exam allows limited time per question, so students should practice finding information quickly, recognizing key terms, and knowing which book applies to each type of question.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. Candidates must follow the testing program’s rules for book preparation. Study guides are not permitted in the examination room. Reference materials containing writing are not allowed, and candidates may not write in their reference materials during the exam. Permanent tabs are allowed, but removable tabs are not allowed. Loose papers and unbound references are not permitted.

Because the exam is open book, students should build a system for using the books. Good preparation includes learning each reference’s table of contents, index, chapter structure, definitions, tables, and common topic locations. Pipe sizing, testing, gas piping controls, and regulatory questions often require candidates to know where to look and how to interpret the information once they find it.

## How does the Master Natural Gas Fitter exam work?

The Master Natural Gas Fitter exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
