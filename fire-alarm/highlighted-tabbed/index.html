Exam Details

The Virginia Fire Alarm Systems Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Qualified Individual requirements administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. PSI administers the examination program for the Commonwealth of Virginia.

  • Examination: Fire Alarm Systems Contracting Examination
  • Specialty Abbreviation: FAS
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 35 correct answers
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Exam Administrator: PSI
  • Current QI Specialty Exam Fee: $85 paid directly to PSI

The published examination content outline divides the 50 questions among several major knowledge areas. General Electrical Knowledge accounts for 5 questions, Wiring for 8 questions, Grounding for 3 questions, Power Supply for 5 questions, Detectors for 12 questions, Alarm Notification for 12 questions, and General Regulations for 5 questions.

The largest portions of the examination are Detectors and Alarm Notification, with 12 questions assigned to each area. Candidates should still prepare across the complete content outline because electrical fundamentals, wiring methods, grounding, power requirements, and regulatory knowledge are also represented on the exam.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Fire Alarm Systems Contracting examination is an open-book examination. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted references because reference books are not supplied at the testing site.

PSI examination instructions allow references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs, including removable flags, are not permitted. Reference materials containing writing are not allowed, and candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination session.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to businesses and sole proprietors and consist of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

A general path toward a Virginia contractor license with the Fire Alarm Systems specialty includes the following steps:

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia licenses contractors as Class A, Class B, or Class C businesses. The license class and the specialty designation are separate components of the contractor license.
  2. Complete the required pre-license education. New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must satisfy Virginia's eight-hour pre-license education requirement through an approved course.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contracting business must identify a Qualified Individual for the Fire Alarm Systems specialty. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience and examination requirements applicable to the requested license class and specialty.
  4. Complete the required Fire Alarm Systems technical examination. Fire Alarm Systems is among the contractor specialties for which the Qualified Individual must successfully complete a board-approved technical examination.
  5. Complete additional examinations when required by the license class. Class A and Class B applicants also have designated business examination requirements for the business's Designated Employee.
  6. Submit the contractor license application and required supporting documentation. The application is submitted to the Virginia Board for Contractors with the documentation applicable to the requested class and specialty.

Exam completion alone does not create a contractor license. The business must meet the Board for Contractors requirements for the license class and Fire Alarm Systems specialty and complete the applicable licensing process.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Fire Alarm Systems Contracting as the service that provides for the installation, repair, or improvement of fire alarm systems operating at 50 volts or less. The Electrical classification also provides for this function. A firm holding the Fire Alarm Systems specialty is responsible for meeting applicable tradesman licensure standards.

The Virginia Board for Contractors requires a licensed contracting firm to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty designation appearing on the license. The experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. Current Virginia requirements call for a minimum of one year of applicable experience for the Qualified Individual associated with a Class C business, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

New contractor license applicants also must satisfy Virginia's eight-hour pre-license education requirement. For Class A and Class B applicants, the course must be completed by a member of responsible management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the course. This pre-license education is a business-focused requirement and is separate from the Fire Alarm Systems technical specialty examination.

Class A and Class B applications also involve additional requirements that are separate from the FAS specialty examination. Applicants should complete each requirement associated with their selected license class as part of the overall contractor licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study strategy for the Virginia Fire Alarm Systems Contractor exam combines subject knowledge with strong book-navigation skills. With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. Spending too long searching for a single provision can quickly reduce the time available for the rest of the exam.

Begin by learning the structure of both books. Review the table of contents, indexes, major chapters, commonly referenced articles, and the permanent tabs. Rather than simply reading from front to back, practice starting with a question or topic and locating the relevant requirement using the index, tabs, headings, and highlighted material.

For the electrical portion, build familiarity with the sections that relate to general electrical principles, wiring, grounding, and power supply requirements. Fire alarm systems combine specialized signaling requirements with electrical installation rules, so candidates should understand how the NEC is organized and how to move quickly between applicable provisions.

NFPA 72 deserves substantial attention because detectors and alarm notification together account for 24 of the 50 published examination questions. Study the organization of the code and practice locating requirements involving initiating devices, notification functions, system performance, installation, testing, and related fire alarm provisions.

Use the highlighted material as a navigation aid rather than attempting to memorize every highlighted passage. Read enough surrounding text to understand definitions, exceptions, conditions, and related requirements. Code questions can turn on a small qualification or exception, so locating the correct section is only the first step; careful reading remains essential.

During practice sessions, give yourself time limits. Look up sample topics using only the books you intend to use during the examination. Repeating this process helps build familiarity with the tabs and creates a mental map of each reference. The more consistently you practice with your books, the less time you may need to spend deciding where to search during the actual exam.
