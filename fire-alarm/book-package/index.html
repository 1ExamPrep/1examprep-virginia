Exam Details

The Virginia Fire Alarm Systems Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the FAS designation under the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates seeking the FAS specialty must receive the required Board approval and successfully complete the applicable examination.

Important technical areas associated with the examination include:

  • General Electrical Knowledge
  • Wiring
  • Grounding
  • Power Supply
  • Detectors and Initiating Devices
  • Alarm Notification
  • General Fire Alarm Regulations

General Electrical Knowledge provides the foundation for understanding low-voltage fire alarm circuits. Candidates should be comfortable with basic electrical terminology, voltage, current, resistance, power, conductor characteristics, circuit behavior, and electrical safety principles.

Wiring is an important part of fire alarm installation. Candidates should understand conductor installation, cable routing, wiring methods, protection from physical damage, circuit identification, separation, and the electrical-code requirements that apply to fire alarm circuits.

Grounding requires candidates to understand the relationship between fire alarm equipment and the electrical grounding system. Grounding and bonding provisions can affect equipment installation, circuit protection, and overall system reliability.

Power Supply includes understanding how fire alarm systems receive normal and secondary power. Fire alarm systems must continue to perform important life-safety functions under conditions anticipated by the applicable code and standard.

Detectors and Initiating Devices involve the equipment used to detect fire-related conditions or manually initiate an alarm. Candidates should understand the purpose, placement concepts, application, and operation of common initiating devices.

Alarm Notification involves devices and systems used to alert building occupants and other parties when an alarm condition occurs. Candidates should be familiar with audible, visible, and other notification concepts addressed by NFPA 72.

General Regulations can include system installation, inspection, testing, maintenance, documentation, and other requirements governing fire alarm system performance and reliability.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Fire Alarm Systems Contracting examination is an open book test. The permitted references include the National Electrical Code, NFPA 70, 2011 and NFPA 72 - National Fire Alarm and Signaling Code, 2013.

Approved examination references may be prepared according to current PSI rules. Candidates should become familiar with the testing requirements governing highlighting, underlining, indexing, and permanent tabs before preparing books for exam day.

An open-book exam still requires significant preparation. Fire alarm questions can involve terminology that appears in both electrical and fire alarm standards. Candidates should know whether a question is primarily asking about an electrical wiring requirement or a specialized fire alarm requirement before deciding which reference to open.

For example, a question involving the installation of fire alarm circuit conductors may require the National Electrical Code. A question involving detector application, notification appliances, supervising stations, emergency communications, inspection, testing, or maintenance is more likely to require NFPA 72.

Practice navigating both books under timed conditions. Learn the table of contents, index, major Articles and Chapters, defined terms, tables, and frequently used sections. Efficient reference use allows candidates to spend more time analyzing technical scenarios and less time searching through unfamiliar material.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Fire Alarm Systems Contracting as the service that provides for the installation, repair, or improvement of fire alarm systems operating at 50 volts or less. The specialty abbreviation is FAS.

The Virginia Electrical Contractor classification also provides for fire alarm systems contracting. A business holding the FAS specialty is authorized within the scope defined for that specialty and must comply with all applicable Virginia contractor licensing requirements.

Virginia requires businesses to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on the contractor license. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience and prerequisite requirements associated with the requested designation.

The current Virginia contractor application materials identify FAS as a specialty requiring pre-approval and successful completion of an examination. Experience verification is also required for Qualified Individuals requesting designations subject to pre-approval.

For these designations, Virginia requires the Qualified Individual to meet the minimum experience associated with the license class. Current Board materials identify at least one year of experience for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Passing the Fire Alarm Systems technical examination does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. The business must complete the full licensing process and satisfy the requirements associated with the selected license class and FAS specialty.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia Fire Alarm Contractor study plan should use both references together rather than treating them as unrelated books. The National Electrical Code establishes many of the electrical installation requirements, while NFPA 72 addresses the specialized performance and operation of fire alarm and signaling systems.

Begin with General Electrical Knowledge. Review voltage, current, resistance, power, electrical circuits, conductor concepts, and electrical terminology. Understanding these fundamentals makes it easier to interpret wiring and power-supply questions.

Next, study fire alarm wiring. Become familiar with the NEC provisions specifically addressing fire alarm circuits. Candidates should understand that different circuit types and installation conditions can affect the wiring methods and protection requirements that apply.

Conductor protection should receive focused review. Wiring can pass through walls, ceilings, framing, mechanical spaces, and other portions of a building where physical conditions influence installation requirements.

Practice identifying whether a question relates to cable type, conductor routing, protection, support, separation, circuit classification, or termination. Identifying the specific issue before looking up the code can significantly improve navigation speed.

Grounding should be studied as part of the complete electrical installation. Candidates should understand basic grounding and bonding concepts and how equipment grounding relates to the reliability and safety of installed systems.

Power supplies are critical to fire alarm operation. Review the concepts of primary and secondary power and understand why a fire alarm system must have the power needed to perform its required functions.

NFPA 72 should be a central part of study for initiating devices. Review smoke detection, heat detection, manual fire alarm boxes, waterflow-related initiation, supervisory devices, and other equipment used to initiate or report system conditions.

Candidates should understand the difference between alarm, supervisory, and trouble conditions. These terms have specific meanings in fire alarm systems and can affect how signals are processed and annunciated.

Detector questions can involve application and installation. Rather than memorizing individual statements, study how detector type, environment, location, ceiling configuration, and system design influence placement and performance.

Notification appliances are another important study area. Review audible and visible notification concepts and understand how fire alarm systems communicate emergency information to occupants.

Visible notification requires familiarity with the purpose and application of visual signaling devices. Audible notification requires understanding of sound-based occupant notification and the performance concepts addressed by NFPA 72.

Candidates should also review notification appliance circuits and how field devices relate to the fire alarm control equipment and power supplies.

Fire alarm control units serve as a central part of many systems. Candidates should understand the general relationship among initiating devices, control equipment, notification appliances, power supplies, and monitoring functions.

Study supervising station systems and the basic purposes of off-premises monitoring. Candidates should understand how signals can be transmitted from protected premises to locations responsible for receiving and processing those signals.

Emergency communications systems are also addressed by NFPA 72. Candidates should become familiar with the terminology and organization of these provisions so they can locate the appropriate material when a question involves emergency messages or mass notification concepts.

Inspection, testing, and maintenance should receive substantial attention. Fire alarm systems must remain operational throughout their service life, and NFPA 72 includes requirements addressing how systems and components are inspected, tested, documented, and maintained.

Practice recognizing whether a question concerns initial acceptance, periodic inspection, functional testing, maintenance, recordkeeping, or system impairment. Each of these topics has a different purpose within the overall fire alarm lifecycle.

System documentation should also be included in preparation. Fire alarm work can involve drawings, specifications, equipment information, test records, and other documentation necessary to demonstrate system design and performance.

A useful open-book preparation strategy is to assign each major subject to its most likely reference. General electrical installation, conductors, wiring methods, grounding, and fire alarm circuit wiring should direct attention toward the NEC. Detection, notification, system operation, signaling, testing, and maintenance should generally direct attention toward NFPA 72.

Permanent tabs can help organize frequently used areas when prepared in accordance with current PSI rules. Useful tab categories may include fire alarm circuits, definitions, power supplies, initiating devices, notification appliances, supervising stations, emergency communications, and inspection/testing subjects.

Do not over-tab the references. Too many labels can make navigation slower rather than faster. Organize the books around broad subject areas that are easy to recognize under examination conditions.

When practicing a question, first identify the key noun or system component. If the question is about conductor installation, look toward the NEC. If it is about smoke detectors or notification appliances, move toward NFPA 72. This method helps reduce random searching.

Missed questions should become study opportunities. Determine whether the mistake resulted from an electrical-theory gap, misunderstanding of fire alarm terminology, incorrect code interpretation, or slow reference navigation.

A strong Virginia Fire Alarm Contractor preparation routine combines electrical fundamentals, specialized fire alarm knowledge, repeated code navigation, system-level understanding, and familiarity with both NFPA references.
