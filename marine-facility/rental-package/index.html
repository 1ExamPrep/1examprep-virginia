Exam Details

The Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the MCC designation. Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors, and MCC is among the specialties requiring the applicable Qualified Individual to receive approval and successfully complete a technical examination.

Marine facility construction involves a combination of technical disciplines. Candidates should develop familiarity with structural concrete, reinforcing steel, concrete materials, construction equipment, construction methods, project operations, and workplace safety.

These subjects often overlap on actual marine construction projects. Reinforced concrete work, for example, can require an understanding of reinforcement placement, concrete mixture characteristics, structural requirements, construction sequencing, equipment needs, and OSHA safety practices.

The examination should therefore be approached as a construction-knowledge test rather than a simple exercise in memorizing isolated facts. Candidates benefit from understanding why materials and methods are used and how different construction activities work together.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing involves both the contracting business and the Qualified Individual associated with the requested specialty. Passing the Marine Facility Contracting technical examination is one component of the complete process.

  1. Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
  2. Select Marine Facility Contracting. MCC is the specialty designation associated with qualifying marine facility work.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate the individual responsible for satisfying the technical qualification requirements for the specialty.
  4. Meet applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the requested license class and specialty.
  5. Complete applicable pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must complete the required contractor pre-license education applicable to their license application.
  6. Receive approval for the MCC examination. Marine Facility Contracting is among the Virginia specialties requiring successful completion of a technical examination.
  7. Prepare for the technical exam. Study OSHA safety, construction planning and equipment, reinforcing steel, concrete mixtures, and structural concrete requirements.
  8. Complete applicable business preparation. Use the NASCLA Virginia contractor guide for business, law, and project-management material associated with broader contractor licensing preparation.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. Complete the application process and provide the required supporting information for the requested class and specialty.

Successful completion of the technical examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The contractor business must satisfy all requirements applicable to its selected license class and specialty.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Marine Facility Contracting as the service that provides for the construction, repair, improvement, or removal of a structure whose purpose is to provide access to, impede, or alter a body of surface water.

The Commercial Building Contractor and Highway/Heavy classifications also provide for this function within their authorized scopes.

The MCC specialty does not provide for construction of accessory structures or for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing functions. Work outside the defined Marine Facility Contracting scope requires the appropriate contractor authorization.

Virginia contractor licenses consist of two primary components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class determines the monetary level of contracting work the licensed business may undertake, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work authorized.

The contracting business must also identify a Qualified Individual for the MCC specialty and satisfy the applicable experience, examination, education, and application requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Marine Facility Contractor study plan should begin by assigning a clear purpose to every reference. When candidates understand which book addresses each subject, the complete reference collection becomes easier to navigate.

Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 for construction-safety preparation. Marine facility projects can involve heavy equipment, lifting operations, reinforcing steel, concrete placement, elevated work, excavation, electrical hazards, and changing jobsite conditions. Safety should be incorporated throughout technical preparation.

Use Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods for equipment and construction operations. Study equipment capabilities, productivity, site conditions, construction sequencing, lifting, earthmoving, and material handling.

Equipment questions may involve more than recognizing the name of a machine. Candidates should understand how the work being performed, site access, loads, production needs, and construction conditions can influence equipment selection and use.

For reinforcing steel, work with Placing Reinforcing Bars. Learn reinforcing terminology and understand how bars are positioned and supported within reinforced-concrete construction.

For concrete materials, use Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Review the ingredients used in concrete and the factors that influence strength, workability, durability, placement, curing, and long-term performance.

Concrete used in demanding environments requires careful attention to material selection and construction practices. Understanding the relationship between mixture characteristics and performance can help candidates interpret technical concrete questions more effectively.

For structural concrete requirements, use ACI 318-14. This is a substantial technical reference, so candidates should focus on understanding its organization rather than attempting to memorize the complete document.

Review the table of contents, major chapters, terminology, and index. Permanent tabs and highlighting can help identify important areas, but candidates should understand what each tab represents and how the surrounding technical provisions are organized.

Use the NASCLA Virginia contractor guide for business, law, and project-management preparation. Keeping business material associated with its dedicated reference can help candidates distinguish it from trade-specific construction subjects.

The Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code provides a separate source for Virginia-specific code material. Candidates should practice recognizing when a question involves state code rather than general construction or contractor-business information.

Reference navigation should be incorporated into regular course study. Take a practice topic, classify the subject, choose the appropriate reference, locate the applicable tab or chapter, and read the supporting information in context.

When an answer is missed, determine whether the problem was technical understanding or navigation. If the construction concept was unfamiliar, review the subject more deeply. If the information was difficult to find, practice locating the relevant section again until the reference becomes easier to use.

The included 6 months of course access provides time to repeat this process across concrete, reinforcement, equipment, safety, business, and code subjects while developing a more organized preparation routine.
