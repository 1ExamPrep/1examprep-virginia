Exam Details

The Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the MCC designation. The examination program is administered through PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates must receive eligibility approval before scheduling the specialty examination. Open Book Test

The Virginia contractor specialty examination is an open book test. PSI allows candidates to bring reference books. References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination, but they must otherwise remain unmarked. Study guides are not permitted as exam-room references. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

The open-book format makes the preparation of these references especially useful. Permanent tabs can provide quick access to major chapters or technical subjects, while highlighting can help important information stand out once the correct section has been located.

Open book does not mean candidates should expect to look up every answer from the beginning. Effective preparation includes developing enough familiarity with marine construction concepts to recognize where information is likely to be located.

For example, a workplace-safety question should direct attention toward 29 CFR Part 1926. A question involving equipment productivity or construction operations may be better addressed through Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods. Reinforcing-steel subjects may require Placing Reinforcing Bars, while concrete materials may point toward Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures.

Questions involving structural concrete requirements may require navigation of ACI 318-14. Practicing these subject-to-reference associations before test day can help candidates approach an open-book examination more systematically.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors. A contractor license consists of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations. Virginia uses Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses, while the classification or specialty determines the type of contracting work authorized. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

  1. Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. The business must determine whether it is pursuing Class A, Class B, or Class C licensing.
  2. Select Marine Facility Contracting. MCC is the Virginia specialty designation for qualifying marine facility work.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate a Qualified Individual for the requested specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirements. Virginia requires applicable experience for the Qualified Individual based on the contractor license class being pursued.
  5. Complete pre-license education. Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}
  6. Obtain approval for the MCC specialty examination. Marine Facility Contracting is a specialty requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical exam. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="6"}
  7. Prepare for the technical examination. Study structural concrete, reinforcing steel, construction methods, equipment, and safety using the included references.
  8. Complete additional requirements for the selected license class. Business examination and documentation requirements vary according to the license class.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. Successful examination completion is part of the process; the business must also complete the Virginia Board for Contractors application requirements.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Marine Facility Contracting as the service providing for the construction, repair, improvement, or removal of a structure whose purpose is to provide access to, impede, or alter a body of surface water. The Commercial Building Contractor and Highway/Heavy classifications also provide for this function. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="7"}

The MCC specialty does not provide for construction of accessory structures or for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing functions. Contractors performing work outside the scope of the MCC specialty must hold the appropriate authorization for those functions. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="8"}

The business must also satisfy the general Virginia contractor licensing requirements associated with its selected license class. This includes the Qualified Individual requirements, applicable experience, pre-license education, examination requirements, and completion of the contractor license application.

The technical examination should therefore be viewed as one part of the overall licensing process rather than the entire requirement.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should begin by assigning each reference a clear purpose. This prevents unnecessary searching and helps candidates build a mental map of the book package.

Use 29 CFR Part 1926 for construction safety. Marine facility projects can involve work around water, heavy equipment, lifting operations, concrete construction, excavation, elevated work, and changing jobsite conditions. Safety preparation should be incorporated into regular study rather than treated as a separate final topic.

Use Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods to review how construction work is organized and how equipment is selected for specific tasks. Equipment choice can influence production, safety, access, excavation, lifting, material placement, and overall project efficiency.

Use Placing Reinforcing Bars when studying reinforcement. Focus on understanding the terminology associated with reinforcing steel and how bars are handled, supported, positioned, and incorporated into concrete construction.

For concrete-material questions, turn to Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Study the ingredients of concrete, the role of water, cementitious materials, aggregates, admixtures, curing, strength, and durability. Marine environments can place significant demands on concrete, making a solid understanding of concrete properties useful for technical preparation.

Use ACI 318-14 for structural concrete code requirements. Rather than attempting to memorize the complete code, learn how it is organized. Become familiar with chapter headings and the terminology used to describe structural concrete requirements.

Practice under realistic conditions. Start with a technical topic and identify which reference should be used before opening a book. Once the correct book is selected, use the permanent tabs, table of contents, index, and highlighted material to locate the relevant section.

Read enough surrounding material to understand the requirement in context. Technical questions can depend on qualifications, conditions, exceptions, dimensions, or construction circumstances that are not obvious from a single highlighted sentence.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The problem may involve unfamiliar terminology, confusion between references, misunderstanding a concrete principle, a reinforcement detail, or difficulty navigating the code. Identifying the source of the mistake creates a more useful study routine than simply memorizing the correct answer.
