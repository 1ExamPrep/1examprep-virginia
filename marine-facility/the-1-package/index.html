Exam Details

The Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the MCC designation. Marine Facility Contracting is one of the Virginia contractor specialties requiring the applicable Qualified Individual to receive approval and successfully complete a technical examination.

  • Specialty: Marine Facility Contracting
  • Specialty Abbreviation: MCC
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 35 correct answers

The published MCC examination content is divided into four major technical areas:

  • Piles and Pile Driving: 20 questions
  • Concrete: 15 questions
  • Concrete Reinforcing Steel: 9 questions
  • Safety: 6 questions

Piles and Pile Driving represents the largest portion of the examination. Candidates should spend significant preparation time learning pile-driving concepts, equipment, construction methods, operations, and terminology.

Concrete is another major subject. Candidates should understand concrete materials, mixture characteristics, placement, curing, strength, durability, quality, and structural requirements.

Concrete Reinforcing Steel requires familiarity with reinforcing bars, placement practices, positioning, supports, handling, and the role of reinforcement within concrete construction.

Safety requires familiarity with construction-site hazards and federal construction safety requirements. Candidates should be prepared to navigate OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and understand how safety rules apply to construction equipment, material handling, concrete work, elevated work, and other site conditions.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates should prepare to use allowable reference materials efficiently while working within the exam's time limit.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need for technical knowledge. Candidates who understand the subject of a question can select the appropriate reference more quickly instead of searching randomly through several books.

For pile-driving and construction equipment questions, Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods is an important reference. Candidates should become familiar with equipment terminology, lifting methods, construction operations, productivity concepts, and pile-driving information.

For reinforcing-steel questions, Placing Reinforcing Bars provides a focused source for placement practices, positioning, supports, handling, and related terminology.

For concrete-material questions, Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures provides information involving cementitious materials, aggregates, water, admixtures, mixture proportioning, durability, strength, placement, and curing.

For structural concrete requirements, ACI 318-14 provides the code requirements and commentary candidates should learn to navigate.

Safety questions may direct candidates toward OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Business, law, and project-management topics are supported by the NASCLA Virginia contractor reference, while the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code provides Virginia-specific code material.

The included 1 year of course access gives candidates time to practice using the references repeatedly. Developing familiarity with the table of contents, index, terminology, chapters, and technical organization of each book can help create a more efficient open-book exam strategy.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Marine Facility Contractor examination is one part of the broader contractor licensing process. The contractor license is issued to the business, and the business must satisfy the requirements applicable to its selected license class and specialty.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Marine Facility Contracting. MCC is the specialty designation associated with qualifying marine facility work.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate a Qualified Individual for the MCC specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirement associated with the requested contractor license class.
  5. Complete applicable pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable contractor pre-license education requirements.
  6. Receive approval for the technical examination. Marine Facility Contracting requires successful completion of an approved technical examination.
  7. Prepare for the MCC examination. Focus on piles and pile driving, concrete, reinforcing steel, safety, and open-book reference navigation.
  8. Complete applicable business and licensing requirements. Additional requirements depend on the contractor license class being pursued.
  9. Complete the contractor application. Application Service is included with The 1 Package.
  10. Establish the contracting business. Business Formation, EIN Filing, and Contractor Compliance Guidance are included with this package.

Passing the technical examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The business must complete the full licensing process and satisfy the requirements applicable to the requested class and specialty.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Marine Facility Contracting as the service providing for the construction, repair, improvement, or removal of a structure whose purpose is to provide access to, impede, or alter a body of surface water.

The Commercial Building Contractor and Highway/Heavy classifications also provide for this function within their applicable scopes.

The MCC specialty does not provide for the construction of accessory structures or for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing functions. Contractors performing work outside the MCC scope must hold the appropriate authorization for those functions.

The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the requested specialty. For designations requiring examination approval, the Qualified Individual must also satisfy the applicable experience requirements and submit the required experience documentation.

Virginia currently requires at least one year of applicable experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

The contractor license class and specialty serve different purposes. The license class determines the monetary level of contracting work the business may perform, while the specialty determines the type of work authorized.

Test Information and Study Materials

Because Piles and Pile Driving account for 20 of the 50 examination questions, candidates should give this subject substantial attention. Study pile-driving methods, construction equipment, terminology, lifting operations, and the practical considerations associated with pile installation.

Use Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods to connect equipment capabilities with actual construction operations. Understanding what equipment is designed to do can be more useful than memorizing isolated equipment names.

The Concrete portion accounts for 15 questions. Candidates should understand concrete ingredients, mixture design principles, durability, strength, curing, placement, and other performance considerations.

Use Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures for material-related topics and ACI 318-14 when the question involves structural concrete requirements. Learning the difference between those two reference roles can improve navigation efficiency.

Concrete Reinforcing Steel accounts for nine questions. Use Placing Reinforcing Bars to become familiar with reinforcing terminology, placement practices, bar supports, positioning, handling, and related field concepts.

Safety accounts for six questions. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 should be part of regular study. Practice locating applicable safety provisions instead of relying solely on memorization.

A productive open-book study session should include both technical review and reference navigation. Start with a topic, identify the correct book, locate the relevant chapter or section, and carefully read the surrounding material.

When a practice question is missed, identify why. The cause might be unfamiliar technical terminology, selecting the wrong reference, misunderstanding a concrete concept, overlooking a reinforcing detail, or difficulty navigating a code requirement.

The included 1 year of course access provides time to revisit weaker areas and practice repeatedly with the books. The objective is to make both the technical concepts and the reference structure more familiar before exam day.
