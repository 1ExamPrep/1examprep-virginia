Exam Details

The Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the MCC designation. The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor licensing, and PSI administers the specialty examination program.

  • Specialty: Marine Facility Contracting
  • Specialty Abbreviation: MCC
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 35 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination requires familiarity with the technical principles associated with marine construction. Candidates should prepare for subjects involving construction methods, concrete, reinforcement, equipment, and safety. These subjects often overlap in actual construction work, so preparation should focus on understanding complete construction processes rather than isolated terms.

For example, a reinforced concrete structure may involve concrete mixture design, reinforcing-bar placement, structural requirements, equipment selection, material handling, sequencing, and jobsite safety. Understanding how these topics connect can make technical questions easier to analyze.

With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Strong reference-navigation habits can help candidates make better use of that time when a technical provision needs to be located.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring allowable reference materials into the examination, subject to the current PSI rules governing those materials.

Allowable references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. Permanent tabs are permitted, while temporary or removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates should become familiar with the examination reference rules before test day and prepare their books accordingly.

The open-book format should not be treated as a substitute for study. Searching through several unfamiliar books for every question can use too much time. A stronger strategy is to identify the technical subject first and then choose the reference most likely to contain the relevant information.

A construction-safety question may direct attention toward 29 CFR Part 1926. A question involving equipment or project operations may point toward Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods. Reinforcing-bar questions can require Placing Reinforcing Bars, while concrete-material questions may be supported by Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures.

When a question involves structural concrete requirements, candidates should be prepared to navigate ACI 318-14. Learning where major subjects appear in the code can make the book much easier to use during an exam.

Reference navigation should be practiced throughout the course. Start with a topic, identify the correct book, use the table of contents or index, locate the section, and read enough surrounding material to understand the requirement in context.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing involves both the contracting business and the Qualified Individual associated with the requested specialty. Passing the MCC technical exam is one component of the overall licensing process.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Marine Facility Contracting. MCC is the specialty designation for qualifying marine facility construction work.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contracting business must designate a Qualified Individual for the requested specialty.
  4. Meet applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the license class being pursued.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must complete eight hours of approved pre-license education.
  6. Receive eligibility approval for the MCC examination. Marine Facility Contracting requires successful completion of the applicable technical specialty exam.
  7. Prepare for the technical examination. Study construction safety, equipment, methods, reinforcement, concrete materials, and structural concrete requirements.
  8. Complete additional requirements associated with the selected license class. Business examination and other licensing requirements may apply depending on the class being pursued.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. Complete the Virginia Board for Contractors application process and provide the required supporting documentation.

Successful completion of the technical examination does not by itself issue a contractor license. The business must satisfy the complete requirements applicable to its selected license class and Marine Facility Contracting specialty.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Marine Facility Contracting as the service that provides for the construction, repair, improvement, or removal of a structure whose purpose is to provide access to, impede, or alter a body of surface water.

The Commercial Building Contractor and Highway/Heavy classifications also provide for the marine facility function within their authorized scopes.

The MCC specialty does not provide for construction of accessory structures or for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing functions. Work falling outside the defined MCC scope requires the appropriate authorization.

The Virginia contractor license itself consists of two major components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The license class addresses the monetary level of work the business may perform, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work authorized.

The contractor business must also designate a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable requirements for the requested specialty. Candidates preparing for the MCC exam should therefore understand that technical exam completion is part of a broader licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should begin by understanding the role of each reference. The goal is to develop clear associations between technical subjects and the books most likely to contain the supporting information.

For construction safety, work regularly with OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Marine construction can involve work around water, elevated areas, concrete operations, equipment, reinforcement, material handling, excavation, and electrical hazards. Safety should therefore be integrated into technical preparation rather than treated as a separate last-minute subject.

For construction planning and equipment, use Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods. Review how equipment is selected and how site conditions, access, production needs, material handling, lifting, excavation, and construction sequencing affect project operations.

Equipment-related questions often require more than recognizing the name of a machine. Candidates should understand what the equipment is designed to do, how its capabilities relate to project conditions, and how construction planning affects productivity and safety.

For reinforcing steel, use Placing Reinforcing Bars. Study the terminology used for reinforcing bars, positioning, supports, and placement. Reinforced concrete depends on both the concrete and the reinforcement working together, making reinforcement an important subject for marine facility preparation.

For concrete materials, use Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Review the ingredients of concrete and the factors that influence workability, strength, durability, curing, and quality.

Understanding water-cement relationships, aggregates, cementitious materials, admixtures, curing, and placement concepts can help candidates analyze questions involving concrete performance instead of relying only on memorized facts.

Marine facility environments can place significant demands on concrete. Candidates should therefore pay attention to durability concepts and how mixture design and construction practices can influence long-term performance.

For structural concrete, use ACI 318-14. This type of code-style reference is extensive, so navigation practice is essential. Learn the overall organization rather than attempting to memorize every requirement.

Review the table of contents and major chapter headings. Become familiar with the vocabulary used by ACI 318-14, because knowing the correct technical term can make the index and section structure much easier to use.

During practice sessions, begin with the technical subject rather than randomly opening a book. Decide whether the question involves OSHA safety, construction methods, reinforcement, concrete materials, or structural concrete requirements. Then select the appropriate reference.

Use the index and table of contents before scanning page by page. Once you locate the relevant section, read enough surrounding material to understand its conditions and limitations.

Technical provisions frequently include exceptions, dimensions, qualifications, or specific construction circumstances. A correct answer can depend on details beyond a single sentence or highlighted phrase.

When a practice question is missed, identify the cause. If the concept itself is unfamiliar, return to the technical subject. If the information was difficult to locate, practice navigating the reference until the correct section becomes easier to find.

This process turns missed questions into targeted study opportunities. Over time, candidates can build stronger construction knowledge and a clearer mental map of the references.
