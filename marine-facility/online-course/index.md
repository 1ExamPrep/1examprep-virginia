### Open Book Test

The Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination is an **open book test**. Candidates may bring allowable reference materials into the examination, subject to the current PSI rules governing those materials.

Allowable references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. Permanent tabs are permitted, while temporary or removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates should become familiar with the examination reference rules before test day and prepare their books accordingly.

The open-book format should not be treated as a substitute for study. Searching through several unfamiliar books for every question can use too much time. A stronger strategy is to identify the technical subject first and then choose the reference most likely to contain the relevant information.

A construction-safety question may direct attention toward **29 CFR Part 1926**. A question involving equipment or project operations may point toward **Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods**. Reinforcing-bar questions can require **Placing Reinforcing Bars**, while concrete-material questions may be supported by **Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures**.

When a question involves structural concrete requirements, candidates should be prepared to navigate **ACI 318-14**. Learning where major subjects appear in the code can make the book much easier to use during an exam.

Reference navigation should be practiced throughout the course. Start with a topic, identify the correct book, use the table of contents or index, locate the section, and read enough surrounding material to understand the requirement in context.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing involves both the contracting business and the Qualified Individual associated with the requested specialty. Passing the MCC technical exam is one component of the overall licensing process.

5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must complete eight hours of approved pre-license education.

Successful completion of the technical examination does not by itself issue a contractor license. The business must satisfy the complete requirements applicable to its selected license class and Marine Facility Contracting specialty.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Marine Facility Contracting** as the service that provides for the construction, repair, improvement, or removal of a structure whose purpose is to provide access to, impede, or alter a body of surface water.

The Commercial Building Contractor and Highway/Heavy classifications also provide for the marine facility function within their authorized scopes.

The MCC specialty does not provide for construction of accessory structures or for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing functions. Work falling outside the defined MCC scope requires the appropriate authorization.

The Virginia contractor license itself consists of two major components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The license class addresses the monetary level of work the business may perform, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work authorized.

The contractor business must also designate a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable requirements for the requested specialty. Candidates preparing for the MCC exam should therefore understand that technical exam completion is part of a broader licensing process.

## How does the Marine Facility Contractor exam work?

The Marine Facility Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
