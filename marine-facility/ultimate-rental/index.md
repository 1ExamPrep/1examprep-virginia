### Exam Details

The **Virginia Marine Facility Contracting examination** is the technical specialty examination associated with the MCC designation. The examination contains **50 questions**, allows **120 minutes**, and requires a minimum passing score of **70%, or 35 correct answers**.

- **Specialty:** Marine Facility Contracting
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** MCC
- **Number of Questions:** 50
- **Time Allowed:** 120 minutes
- **Passing Score:** 70%, or 35 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The technical content is concentrated in four major areas:

- **Piles and Pile Driving:** 20 questions
- **Concrete:** 15 questions
- **Concrete Reinforcing Steel:** 9 questions
- **Safety:** 6 questions

Piles and pile driving represent the largest portion of the examination, so candidates should devote substantial preparation time to construction methods, equipment, operations, and related marine construction concepts.

Concrete is also a major examination area. Candidates should understand concrete materials, placement considerations, durability, quality, curing, mixture characteristics, and structural concrete requirements.

Concrete reinforcing steel accounts for another significant part of the exam. Candidates should be familiar with reinforcing-bar terminology, placement, positioning, supports, and related construction practices.

Safety questions require familiarity with OSHA construction requirements and the hazards associated with construction operations, equipment, concrete work, material handling, elevated work, and other jobsite conditions.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Marine Facility Contracting** as work involving the construction, repair, improvement, or removal of a structure whose purpose is to provide access to, impede, or alter a body of surface water.

The Commercial Building Contractor and Highway/Heavy classifications also provide for this function within their applicable scopes.

The MCC specialty does not provide for construction of accessory structures or for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing functions. Work outside the Marine Facility Contracting scope requires the appropriate contractor authorization.

The Virginia contractor license consists of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties. The license class establishes the monetary level of contracting activity permitted, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the business is authorized to perform.

The contractor business must also identify the appropriate Qualified Individual and satisfy the applicable experience, examination, education, application, and other licensing requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan should reflect the examination's emphasis on **piles and pile driving**. With 20 questions in this area, candidates should develop familiarity with pile-driving methods, construction equipment, operations, and the terminology associated with this type of work.

Use **Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods** regularly while studying this subject. Focus on understanding what different equipment is designed to do and how construction conditions influence method and equipment selection.

The **Concrete** portion contains 15 questions. Use Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures to study materials and performance, and use ACI 318-14 to develop familiarity with structural concrete requirements.

Rather than treating these references as interchangeable, learn the role of each one. A question about concrete ingredients, mixture characteristics, curing, or durability may lead to a different reference than a question involving structural concrete requirements.

**Concrete Reinforcing Steel** accounts for nine questions. Use Placing Reinforcing Bars to review terminology, positioning, supports, handling, and placement practices. Understanding how reinforcing steel functions within concrete construction can help candidates interpret these questions more effectively.

**Safety** accounts for six questions. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and practice locating construction requirements efficiently. Safety preparation should include regular interaction with the reference rather than a single review at the end of the study period.

Build reference-navigation practice into each study session. Begin with a technical topic, determine which book applies, locate the applicable section, and read enough surrounding material to understand the complete requirement.

When a practice question is answered incorrectly, identify whether the problem involved technical knowledge or difficulty finding the information. Review the underlying concept when necessary, then practice navigating back to the correct section.

## How does the Marine Facility Contractor exam work?

The Marine Facility Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
