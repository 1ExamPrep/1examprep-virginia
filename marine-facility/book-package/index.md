### Open Book Test

The Virginia contractor specialty examination is an **open book test**. Candidates may bring permitted reference books into the examination, subject to the testing rules established by PSI.

Allowable references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam, but they must otherwise remain unmarked. Candidates should therefore prepare their references before test day and become familiar with the organization of each book.

An open-book examination still requires technical knowledge. Searching through several unfamiliar references for every question can consume valuable time. A better approach is to identify the subject first and select the reference most likely to contain the applicable information.

A construction-safety question may direct candidates toward **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926**. Questions involving equipment selection, construction operations, production, or methods may point toward **Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods**.

Questions involving reinforcing steel can direct candidates toward **Placing Reinforcing Bars**. Concrete materials and mixture-related subjects may be supported by **Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures**, while structural concrete requirements can require navigation of **ACI 318-14**.

Practice making these subject-to-reference connections throughout preparation. Review tables of contents, indexes, chapter headings, terminology, and major technical sections so each reference becomes a familiar working tool.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to businesses and sole proprietors. The license contains both a license class and one or more classifications or specialty designations. Marine Facility Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation MCC.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select Marine Facility Contracting.** MCC is the specialty designation associated with qualifying marine facility construction work.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the requested specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the contractor license class being pursued.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must complete eight hours of approved pre-license education.
6. **Receive eligibility approval for the MCC examination.** Marine Facility Contracting is one of the Virginia specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.
7. **Prepare for the technical examination.** Review construction safety, equipment and methods, reinforcing steel, concrete materials, and structural concrete requirements using the applicable references.
8. **Complete additional requirements for the selected license class.** Separate business examination and licensing requirements may apply depending on whether the applicant is pursuing Class A, Class B, or Class C licensing.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** Complete the Virginia Board for Contractors application process and provide the required documentation for the selected license class and specialty.

Successful completion of the MCC technical exam does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The business must satisfy the complete licensing requirements associated with its selected class and Marine Facility Contracting specialty.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Marine Facility Contracting** as the service providing for the construction, repair, improvement, or removal of a structure whose purpose is to provide access to, impede, or alter a body of surface water.

The Commercial Building Contractor and Highway/Heavy classifications also provide for this function within their authorized scopes.

The MCC specialty does not provide for construction of accessory structures or for electrical, HVAC, or plumbing functions. Contractors performing work that falls outside the defined MCC scope must hold the appropriate authorization for those activities.

Every licensed Virginia contractor must identify a Qualified Individual for each applicable classification or specialty. Marine Facility Contracting is among the specialties for which the Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience criteria and successfully complete the approved technical examination.

Virginia's contractor licensing requirements also distinguish the license class from the specialty. The license class determines the monetary level of contracting activity the business may perform, while the MCC specialty establishes the type of work authorized.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should begin by assigning a clear purpose to each reference. Knowing what each book covers can reduce the amount of time spent deciding where to search for technical information.

Use **OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926** for construction-safety preparation. Marine facility projects can involve work around water, heavy equipment, reinforcing steel, concrete placement, elevated work areas, lifting operations, and changing jobsite conditions. Safety should therefore be incorporated throughout preparation rather than treated as an isolated topic.

For construction operations and equipment, use **Construction Planning, Equipment, and Methods**. Review how equipment is selected based on the work being performed and how factors such as access, production requirements, site conditions, lifting needs, excavation, and material movement affect construction planning.

Use **Placing Reinforcing Bars** to strengthen understanding of reinforcement. Study reinforcing-bar terminology and how reinforcement is positioned and supported within concrete construction. Understanding the purpose and placement of reinforcement can make structural-concrete questions easier to interpret.

For concrete materials, use **Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures**. Review the different ingredients used in concrete and the factors that affect strength, durability, workability, curing, and long-term performance.

Concrete exposed to demanding environments requires careful control of materials and construction practices. Understanding the relationship between mixture characteristics and performance can provide useful technical context when working through concrete-related questions.

Use **ACI 318-14** when studying structural concrete requirements. Instead of attempting to memorize the entire document, develop a working knowledge of its organization. Practice locating subjects through the table of contents, index, chapter headings, and defined terminology.

Reference navigation should become part of every study session. Start with a technical question or topic, identify the subject area, choose the reference most likely to contain the information, and then locate the applicable section.

Read enough surrounding material to understand the information in context. Technical requirements can depend on specific conditions, exceptions, dimensions, materials, or construction situations. Finding a familiar word on a page does not necessarily mean the surrounding provision answers the question being asked.

Practice moving between the books as the subject changes. A concrete-mixture question may require a different source from a structural concrete question. Reinforcement may require another reference, while equipment planning and OSHA safety have their own dedicated books.

When a practice question is missed, determine whether the issue was technical knowledge or reference navigation. If the concept was unfamiliar, study the underlying subject. If the answer was difficult to find, practice using the index and chapter structure until that part of the reference becomes easier to navigate.

## How does the Marine Facility Contractor exam work?

The Marine Facility Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
