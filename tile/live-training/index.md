### Exam Details

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the **TMC** specialty. A contractor business requesting this specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable experience requirements and completes the required technical examination.

The current TMC examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates receive **120 minutes** to complete the test and must answer at least **35 questions correctly** to achieve the required 70% passing score.

The exam subject areas include:

- Plan Reading and Estimating
- General Tile Knowledge
- Grouting and Finishing
- Adhesives
- Tile Installation
- Terrazzo
- Safety

General Tile Knowledge and Tile Installation are central areas of preparation because they relate directly to the day-to-day work performed under the TMC specialty. Candidates should understand tile types, substrates, setting materials, surface preparation, movement accommodation, layout, installation procedures, curing, and accepted installation practices.

Terrazzo should receive dedicated attention even for candidates whose primary field experience is ceramic, porcelain, stone, or glass tile. Terrazzo materials, system design, installation procedures, finishing, and specifications represent a distinct part of the specialty.

Plan Reading and Estimating requires candidates to work with dimensions, layouts, quantities, project plans, and specifications. Grouting, finishing, adhesives, and OSHA safety are additional subjects that can affect overall exam performance and should be included in a balanced study plan.

PSI administers Virginia Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. Candidates must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the TMC examination. The current Qualified Individual specialty exam fee is **$85**, paid directly to PSI.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates should become familiar with the permitted references before examination day and practice locating information efficiently.

The open-book format does not eliminate the need to understand the material. With 50 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Searching several unfamiliar references for every answer can quickly consume the available testing time.

Reference books used for the examination may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed in accordance with applicable testing rules. Permanent tabs are permitted, while removable tabs are not allowed.

A safety question may lead to **29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**. Questions involving formal tile installation specifications, material requirements, mortars, adhesives, or grouts may require **ANSI A108/A118/A136.1:2017**.

The **Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation, 2017** supports preparation involving recognized tile installation methods and systems. **Setting Tile, 1995** supports practical installation topics, while the **Terrazzo Specification and Design Guide** provides focused information for terrazzo-related questions.

Live Virtual training and Self Study preparation should both include reference-navigation practice. Candidates can select a practice question, identify the subject, choose the appropriate book, and locate the supporting material using indexes, headings, tables, standards, diagrams, or permanent tabs.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation TMC.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the TMC specialty.** The business requests Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting as the applicable specialty.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate an individual who qualifies the company for the TMC specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must document trade experience appropriate to the requested contractor class.
5. **Submit experience verification.** TMC requires the applicable qualification and examination approval process.
6. **Receive approval for testing.** The candidate must be approved before scheduling the TMC specialty examination.
7. **Prepare for the technical exam.** Use Live Virtual instruction, Self Study preparation, practice-oriented review, and the approved references.
8. **Complete the TMC examination.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the technical examination requirement.
9. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license business education.
10. **Complete additional business requirements when applicable.** Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
11. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must satisfy the remaining requirements for the requested class and TMC specialty.

Passing the TMC technical examination does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. The examination is one part of a broader process involving the contractor business, license class, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, and other applicable requirements.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses engaged in construction, repair, removal, and improvement work on property owned by others. Each contractor license includes a class and one or more classifications or specialty designations.

The Qualified Individual for the TMC specialty must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management and must satisfy the experience requirements applicable to the contractor class.

Current minimum Qualified Individual experience requirements are **one year for Class C**, **three years for Class B**, and **five years for Class A**.

Virginia's TMC specialty covers preparation, fabrication, construction, and installation of artificial marble, burned clay tile, ceramic, terrazzo, encaustic, faience, quarry, semi-vitreous, cementitious board, and other tile. Hollow or structural partition tile is excluded.

The Residential Building and Commercial Building classifications and certain improvement specialties can also provide authority for tile, marble, ceramic, and terrazzo work within their respective scopes.

Virginia requires **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education** for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. This business course is separate from technical TMC exam preparation.

Class A and Class B applicants also have separate Designated Employee business examination requirements. Those requirements are different from the TMC technical examination taken by the Qualified Individual.

### Reference Books

- **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**
 This construction-safety reference supports preparation involving personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, silica exposure, work areas, and other OSHA requirements relevant to tile and terrazzo construction.
- **ANSI A108/A118/A136.1:2017**
 This standards reference contains specifications for ceramic tile installation and performance requirements for related materials. It supports study of mortars, adhesives, grouts, substrates, installation methods, and standardized tile practices.
- **Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation, 2017**
 This industry handbook supports review of recognized ceramic, glass, and stone tile installation methods. Candidates can use it to study substrates, assemblies, movement joints, materials, installation conditions, and system selection.
- **Setting Tile, 1995 (USED)**
 This practical trade reference supports preparation involving layout, cutting, surface preparation, materials, installation methods, setting procedures, and finishing. The listed reference is a used book.
- **Terrazzo Specification and Design Guide**
 This terrazzo-focused reference supports preparation involving terrazzo materials, systems, specifications, installation considerations, design concepts, construction details, and finishing.

The references should be used together rather than treated as interchangeable. OSHA is the logical starting point for safety. ANSI supports standards and installation-material requirements. The installation handbook addresses recognized tile systems, Setting Tile provides practical trade information, and the terrazzo guide supports terrazzo-specific preparation.
