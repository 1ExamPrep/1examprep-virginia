Exam Details

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the TMC specialty. Virginia contractor businesses requesting this specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable experience requirements and completes the required technical examination.

The TMC examination contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the test and must answer at least 35 questions correctly to meet the 70% passing requirement.

Major subjects represented on the examination include:

  • Plan Reading and Estimating
  • General Tile Knowledge
  • Grouting and Finishing
  • Adhesives
  • Tile Installation
  • Terrazzo
  • Safety

General Tile Knowledge and Tile Installation are important areas because they address the core technical work performed under the TMC specialty. Candidates should understand tile types, substrates, surface preparation, setting methods, movement accommodation, installation procedures, material selection, and recognized industry practices.

Terrazzo requires dedicated preparation rather than being treated simply as another form of tile installation. Candidates should understand terrazzo systems, materials, specifications, preparation, installation concepts, and finishing considerations.

Virginia uses PSI as the examination vendor for Qualified Individual specialty examinations. Candidates must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the test. The current Virginia Qualified Individual specialty examination fee is $85, paid directly to PSI.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates should prepare to work efficiently with the permitted technical references rather than assuming that having the books available will make every question easy to answer.

The listed references may be highlighted and organized with permanent index tabs when prepared in accordance with applicable testing rules. Candidates should become familiar with each book before examination day and understand what type of information is most likely to be found in each reference.

A safety question may direct the candidate to 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA). A question involving formal tile installation specifications, mortars, adhesives, grouts, or installation-material requirements may require ANSI A108/A118/A136.1:2017.

Questions involving recognized ceramic, glass, and stone installation systems may lead to the Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation, 2017. Practical tile-setting subjects can be reinforced through Setting Tile, 1995, while terrazzo-specific material may require the Terrazzo Specification and Design Guide.

With 50 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. A candidate who spends several minutes searching the wrong book can quickly lose valuable testing time. The online course therefore emphasizes identifying the type of question first and then selecting the reference most likely to contain the answer.

Repeated navigation practice can help candidates become more comfortable using indexes, section headings, tables of contents, standards, installation-method listings, permanent tabs, and other reference features.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to businesses and combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation TMC.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the TMC specialty. The business requests Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting as the specialty under which it intends to operate.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate an individual who qualifies the company for the TMC specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must document experience appropriate to the requested contractor class and specialty.
  5. Submit experience verification. TMC requires the applicable qualification and pre-approval process before the technical examination.
  6. Receive approval for testing. The Qualified Individual must be approved before scheduling the PSI specialty examination.
  7. Prepare for the TMC exam. Use the online course alongside the listed references to study tile systems, standards, terrazzo, estimating, adhesives, grouting, and safety.
  8. Complete the technical examination. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the TMC exam requirement.
  9. Complete Virginia pre-license education. New contractor applicants must complete the required eight-hour Board-approved pre-license business course.
  10. Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable. Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee requirements.
  11. Submit the contractor license application. The business must satisfy the remaining requirements for the requested license class and specialty.

Passing the TMC technical examination does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. The contractor business must also complete the applicable licensing, business, education, and qualification requirements.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses businesses engaged in construction, repair, removal, and improvement work on property owned by others. A contractor license consists of a class and one or more classifications or specialties.

The Qualified Individual for a specialty must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management and must meet the applicable experience requirement for the license class being requested.

Current minimum Qualified Individual experience requirements are one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

The TMC specialty covers preparation, fabrication, construction, and installation of artificial marble, burned clay tile, ceramic, terrazzo, encaustic, faience, quarry, semi-vitreous, cementitious board, and other tile. Hollow or structural partition tile is excluded.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. The pre-license course is a business requirement and is separate from the technical TMC exam preparation provided by this online course.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants also have business examination requirements for a Designated Employee. Those business examinations are separate from the Qualified Individual's TMC specialty examination.

Reference Books

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    This construction-safety reference supports study involving personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, tools, material handling, silica exposure, work areas, and other federal safety requirements that can affect tile and terrazzo contractors.
  • ANSI A108/A118/A136.1:2017
    This standards compilation addresses ceramic tile installation specifications and performance requirements for installation materials. Candidates can use it to study mortars, adhesives, grouts, substrates, installation methods, and technical requirements associated with ceramic tile systems.
  • Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation, 2017
    This industry handbook supports preparation involving recognized ceramic, glass, and stone tile installation methods. It provides information involving substrates, assemblies, movement accommodation, installation conditions, materials, and system selection.
  • Setting Tile, 1995 (USED)
    This practical trade reference supports preparation involving layout, cutting, surface preparation, setting techniques, materials, installation methods, and finishing. The listed reference is a used copy.
  • Terrazzo Specification and Design Guide
    This terrazzo-focused reference supports study of terrazzo materials, systems, specifications, design considerations, installation practices, construction details, and finishing concepts.

The online course works best when candidates actively use these books during their preparation. Reading a course explanation and then locating the supporting material in the appropriate reference can reinforce both technical understanding and book-navigation skills.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive TMC study plan should begin by dividing the examination into major trade subjects. Candidates can work through general tile knowledge, installation, terrazzo, estimating, grouting, adhesives, and safety while using practice questions to measure progress.

General Tile Knowledge should include tile materials, classifications, substrates, surface preparation, moisture considerations, terminology, installation conditions, and material compatibility.

Tile Installation preparation should include layout, substrate evaluation, setting methods, movement joints, installation procedures, mortar use, curing, joint design, and common field conditions. Candidates should connect practical experience with the terminology used in ANSI standards and industry handbooks.

Adhesives and Setting Materials should be reviewed carefully through the ANSI reference. Candidates should understand that different tile types, substrates, exposure conditions, and installations can require different material characteristics.

Grouting and Finishing preparation should include grout selection, joint preparation, application, cleaning, curing, finishing procedures, and compatibility with the overall installation system.

Terrazzo should receive dedicated study time. Candidates whose primary experience is ceramic, porcelain, glass, or stone tile should avoid overlooking terrazzo-specific materials, specifications, preparation, construction, and finishing practices.

Plan Reading and Estimating preparation should include dimensions, areas, layouts, material quantities, waste, construction drawings, and specifications. Candidates should be comfortable converting plan information into practical tile or terrazzo requirements.

Safety should include cutting equipment, silica exposure, personal protective equipment, ladders, scaffolds, electrical tools, material handling, housekeeping, and other OSHA construction requirements relevant to the trade.

The course's practice-oriented structure can help candidates identify subjects that require more attention. When a practice question is missed, candidates should locate the answer in the appropriate reference, review the surrounding material, and understand why the correct answer applies.

For open-book navigation practice, candidates should identify the subject before opening a book. Selecting the correct reference first can save more time than trying to search several books for the same question.

As candidates progress, timed practice can help develop exam pacing. The goal is to answer familiar trade questions efficiently while reserving enough time for standards-based or technical questions that require reference confirmation.
