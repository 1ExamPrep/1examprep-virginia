### Exam Details

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the **TMC** specialty. A contractor business requesting this specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable experience requirements and completes the required technical examination.

The current TMC examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates receive **120 minutes** to complete the test and need at least **35 correct answers** to meet the 70% passing requirement.

The examination content outline includes:

- **Plan Reading and Estimating:** 5 questions
- **General Tile Knowledge:** 12 questions
- **Grouting and Finishing:** 4 questions
- **Adhesives:** 4 questions
- **Tile Installation:** 12 questions
- **Terrazzo:** 8 questions
- **Safety:** 5 questions

General Tile Knowledge and Tile Installation are the two largest exam categories, each containing 12 questions. Terrazzo is another substantial section with eight questions. Candidates should therefore devote significant preparation time to tile systems, installation practices, substrates, materials, layout, standards, setting methods, and terrazzo construction.

Plan Reading and Estimating requires candidates to interpret project information, dimensions, quantities, layouts, and specifications. Grouting, finishing, adhesives, and safety are smaller categories individually, but together they account for a meaningful portion of the examination and should not be neglected.

The Virginia Board for Contractors uses PSI to administer Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. The current specialty examination fee is **$85**, paid directly to PSI.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the examination center.

Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed, while removable tabs are not permitted. Reference materials containing handwritten notes are not allowed, and study guides are not permitted in the examination room.

The open-book format makes efficient reference navigation an important exam skill. With 50 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Searching through an unfamiliar reference for every answer can quickly consume the available testing time.

Candidates should practice identifying the subject before selecting a book. Safety questions may lead to OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Installation standards, methods, and material specifications may require ANSI A108/A118/A136.1. General tile installation questions can be supported by the Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation or Setting Tile. Terrazzo questions may require the Terrazzo Specification and Design Guide.

The objective is not to depend on books for every question. Strong preparation combines trade knowledge with the ability to confirm exact specifications, standards, procedures, or safety requirements when needed.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation **TMC**.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the TMC specialty.** The business must request Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting when completing the applicable contractor licensing process.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a person who has the experience needed to qualify the company for the TMC specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The amount of required experience depends on the contractor license class being requested.
5. **Submit the required experience verification.** TMC is among the specialties for which the Qualified Individual must provide the applicable experience documentation.
6. **Receive approval for the technical examination.** Candidates must complete the applicable qualification process before scheduling the TMC test.

11. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must satisfy the remaining requirements for the selected contractor class and TMC specialty.

Passing the TMC examination does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. The exam is one part of the overall licensing process for the contractor business.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses businesses performing construction, repair, removal, and improvement work on property owned by others. Each contractor license includes a class and one or more classifications or specialties.

A business applying for the TMC specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable experience requirements. Current minimum experience requirements are **one year for Class C**, **three years for Class B**, and **five years for Class A**.

The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee of the contractor business or a member of Responsible Management. The individual must have experience within the scope of the classification or specialty being requested.

The TMC specialty covers preparation, fabrication, construction, and installation of artificial marble, burned clay tile, ceramic, terrazzo, encaustic, faience, quarry, semi-vitreous, cementitious board, and other tile. Hollow or structural partition tile is excluded from the specialty.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. This business-focused requirement is separate from technical TMC exam preparation.

Class A and Class B businesses have additional Designated Employee examination requirements involving business and Virginia contractor knowledge. Those requirements are separate from the technical TMC specialty examination taken by the Qualified Individual.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive TMC study strategy should follow the examination outline rather than reading every reference from beginning to end. Candidates can organize preparation around general tile knowledge, installation, terrazzo, estimating, adhesives, grouting, finishing, and safety.

**General Tile Knowledge** should receive substantial attention. Review tile types, materials, substrates, preparation, terminology, moisture considerations, installation conditions, setting materials, and common construction practices.

**Tile Installation** is another major category. Candidates should understand layout, surface preparation, installation methods, joint placement, setting procedures, material selection, substrate requirements, curing, and accepted installation practices.

**Terrazzo** represents eight questions, making it an important study area even for candidates whose primary field experience is ceramic or stone tile. Review terrazzo materials, systems, specifications, installation procedures, preparation, and finishing considerations.

**Plan Reading and Estimating** preparation should include dimensions, areas, material quantities, waste considerations, plans, layouts, and specifications. Tile contractors should be comfortable translating construction drawings into practical material requirements.

**Grouting and Finishing** should include grout selection, application, joint preparation, curing, cleaning, finishing procedures, and material compatibility. Candidates should understand how finishing affects both appearance and long-term installation performance.

**Adhesives** preparation should include the types and uses of setting materials, substrate compatibility, installation conditions, and relevant performance requirements contained in the ANSI reference.

**Safety** should remain part of regular study. Tile and terrazzo contractors work with cutting equipment, power tools, heavy materials, ladders, scaffolds, chemicals, dust, and other construction hazards. OSHA requirements should be connected to actual field situations.

Reference-navigation practice is especially important for the open-book examination. Candidates should choose a practice question, identify the topic, decide which reference is most likely to contain the information, and locate the relevant section using the index, table of contents, headings, standards, or permanent tabs.

When a question is answered incorrectly, locate the supporting information and review the surrounding material. This helps candidates understand the underlying standard or installation principle rather than simply memorizing the correct answer.

Timed practice can also improve exam pacing. Candidates should become comfortable moving past a difficult lookup and returning to it after completing questions they can answer more efficiently.
