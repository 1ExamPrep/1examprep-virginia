Exam Details

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor examination is the Qualified Individual technical examination associated with the TMC specialty. The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable experience requirements and completes the required technical examination.

The TMC examination contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the test and must answer at least 35 questions correctly to meet the 70% passing requirement.

Examination subjects include:

  • Plan Reading and Estimating
  • General Tile Knowledge
  • Grouting and Finishing
  • Adhesives
  • Tile Installation
  • Terrazzo
  • Safety

General Tile Knowledge and Tile Installation are major areas of preparation. Candidates should understand tile types, substrates, surface preparation, setting materials, layout, installation methods, movement accommodation, curing, and accepted trade practices.

Terrazzo is also an important part of the TMC specialty and deserves dedicated preparation. Candidates whose daily work centers on ceramic, porcelain, stone, or glass tile should also study terrazzo materials, systems, specifications, installation procedures, and finishing.

Virginia uses PSI as the examination vendor for Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. Candidates must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the TMC examination. The current Qualified Individual specialty examination fee is $85, paid directly to PSI and separate from this rental package.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because the books are not supplied at the examination center.

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed in accordance with testing rules. Permanent tabs may be used, while temporary or removable tabs are not permitted. References must otherwise comply with the examination rules.

Some books in this rental package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. These features can support faster study and more organized reference navigation, but candidates should still learn what each book covers and where major subjects are located.

With 50 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Efficient reference selection is therefore important. Searching every book for every question can consume testing time quickly.

Safety questions may lead to 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA). Questions involving formal tile installation specifications, mortars, adhesives, grouts, or material performance requirements may require ANSI A108/A118/A136.1:2017.

The Handbook for Ceramic, Glass, and Stone Tile Installation supports questions involving recognized installation systems and methods. Setting Tile provides practical installation information, while the Terrazzo Specification and Design Guide supports terrazzo-specific preparation.

The six-month course period can be used to practice this reference-selection process repeatedly before test day.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing combines a contractor license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting is identified by the abbreviation TMC.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the TMC specialty. Request Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting as the applicable specialty.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate the person qualifying it for the TMC specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must have experience within the scope of the specialty.
  5. Submit the required experience verification. The appropriate documentation is required for specialties subject to examination approval.
  6. Receive approval for the examination. The Qualified Individual must be approved before scheduling the TMC test.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Use the course and rental references to review tile systems, terrazzo, standards, adhesives, grouting, estimating, safety, and reference navigation.
  8. Complete the TMC technical examination. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the specialty examination requirement.
  9. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable Board-approved pre-license education requirement.
  10. Complete Class A or Class B business requirements when applicable. These requirements are separate from the TMC technical examination.
  11. Submit the contractor license application. The business must complete the remaining requirements for the requested class and specialty.

Passing the TMC technical examination does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. The examination is one component of the overall contractor licensing process.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses and requires a Qualified Individual for each applicable classification or specialty designation.

The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee of the contractor business or a member of Responsible Management. Current Virginia requirements establish minimum experience of one year for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

An Experience Verification Form is required for Qualified Individuals requesting designations that require examination approval. The TMC specialty requires the applicable technical qualification process.

Virginia defines Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting as preparation, fabrication, construction, and installation of artificial marble, burned clay tile, ceramic, terrazzo, encaustic, faience, quarry, semi-vitreous, cementitious board, and other tile. Hollow or structural partition tile is excluded.

The Commercial Building and Residential Building classifications and the Home Improvement and Commercial Improvement specialties can also provide authority for this work within their respective scopes.

New contractor applicants must also complete the applicable Board-approved pre-license education. Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee business examination requirements in addition to the Qualified Individual specialty examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

The package includes 6 months of course access, allowing candidates to divide preparation into manageable trade subjects and repeat difficult material as needed.

General Tile Knowledge preparation should include tile classifications, substrates, preparation, terminology, moisture considerations, setting materials, environmental conditions, and material compatibility.

Tile Installation should include layout, substrate evaluation, setting methods, movement accommodation, joints, installation procedures, mortar use, curing, and recognized trade practices.

Adhesives and Setting Materials should receive dedicated study using the ANSI reference. Candidates should understand how tile type, substrate, environment, and installation conditions affect material selection and performance requirements.

Grouting and Finishing preparation should include joint preparation, grout selection, application, cleaning, curing, finishing, and compatibility with the installation system.

Terrazzo should be studied independently. Review terrazzo systems, materials, specifications, preparation, installation methods, construction details, and finishing concepts.

Plan Reading and Estimating should include dimensions, areas, layouts, material quantities, waste considerations, project drawings, and specifications. Candidates should practice converting plans into practical material requirements.

Safety should include OSHA requirements involving tools, silica exposure, PPE, ladders, scaffolds, material handling, housekeeping, and other hazards encountered during tile, marble, ceramic, and terrazzo work.

Open-book practice should be part of regular course study. Candidates can choose a practice question, identify the subject, select the correct reference, and locate the supporting information. This develops technical knowledge and book-navigation speed at the same time.

When a question is answered incorrectly, candidates should review the underlying standard or technical section rather than memorizing one answer. Installation standards often contain conditions, exceptions, and related requirements that can appear in different forms on the examination.
