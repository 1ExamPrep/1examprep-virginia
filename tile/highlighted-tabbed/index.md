The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the **TMC** specialty. The Virginia Board for Contractors requires pre-approval for the TMC technical examination. Candidates must complete the applicable qualification and experience documentation before proceeding to testing. PSI administers Virginia contractor specialty examinations for approved candidates.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors and include both a license class and one or more classifications or specialties. Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation TMC.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the TMC specialty.** The business requests Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting as the applicable specialty.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The business must designate a person who qualifies the company for the TMC specialty.
4. **Meet the required experience level.** The Qualified Individual must document experience appropriate to the requested contractor class.
5. **Submit experience verification.** TMC is a specialty requiring examination pre-approval, so applicable experience documentation must be provided.
6. **Receive approval for testing.** The candidate must be approved before scheduling the TMC technical examination.
7. **Prepare for the examination.** Study installation standards, tile systems, terrazzo, adhesives, grouting, estimating, and OSHA safety using the highlighted and tabbed references.
8. **Complete the technical examination.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the TMC examination requirement.
9. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must complete the applicable Board-approved business education.
10. **Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable.** Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee requirements.
11. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must satisfy the remaining requirements for its selected class and specialty.

Passing the TMC technical examination is one part of the Virginia contractor licensing process. Examination completion alone does not create a contractor license.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses performing construction, repair, removal, and improvement work on property owned by others. Contractor licenses consist of a class and one or more classifications or specialty designations.

The Qualified Individual for the TMC specialty must either be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management. Virginia's current contractor application materials require the Qualified Individual to document the minimum experience applicable to the requested license class.

Current minimum Qualified Individual experience requirements are **one year for Class C**, **three years for Class B**, and **five years for Class A**.

Virginia defines TMC work as preparation, fabrication, construction, and installation of artificial marble, burned clay tile, ceramic, terrazzo, encaustic, faience, quarry, semi-vitreous, cementitious board, and other tile. Hollow or structural partition tile is excluded.

The Residential Building and Commercial Building classifications and certain improvement specialties can also provide authority for this work within their respective scopes. The TMC designation specifically identifies tile, marble, ceramic, and terrazzo work as the business's contractor specialty.

New contractor applicants must also complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This business-focused education is separate from the TMC technical examination.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive TMC exam study plan should organize preparation by trade subject rather than simply reading each reference from beginning to end. Candidates can begin with tile fundamentals and installation methods, then move into adhesives, grouting, terrazzo, estimating, and safety.

**General Tile Knowledge** should include tile types, substrates, preparation, moisture considerations, installation terminology, setting materials, and common field conditions. Candidates should become comfortable connecting practical jobsite experience to the terminology used in the standards.

**Tile Installation** preparation should include layout, substrate preparation, installation methods, setting-bed requirements, joint placement, movement accommodation, curing, and material compatibility.

**Adhesives and Setting Materials** should receive specific review through the ANSI reference. Candidates should understand that different installation conditions and materials can require different mortar or adhesive properties.

**Grouting and Finishing** preparation should include joint preparation, grout selection, application, curing, cleaning, finishing, and compatibility with the installation system.

**Terrazzo** should be studied independently rather than treated as an extension of ceramic tile. Review terrazzo materials, specifications, installation procedures, system components, preparation, and finishing.

**Plan Reading and Estimating** preparation should include dimensions, areas, layouts, quantities, waste considerations, plans, and specifications. Candidates should practice converting project information into material and installation requirements.

**Safety** should include PPE, ladders, scaffolding, cutting equipment, dust exposure, power tools, material handling, and other OSHA construction requirements relevant to tile and terrazzo work.
