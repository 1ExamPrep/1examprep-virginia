Exam Details

The Virginia Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo examination is the technical Qualified Individual examination associated with the TMC specialty. A Virginia contractor business requesting this specialty must designate a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable experience requirements and successfully completes the required technical examination.

The Virginia Board for Contractors requires pre-approval for the TMC technical examination. Candidates must complete the applicable qualification and experience documentation before proceeding to testing. PSI administers Virginia contractor specialty examinations for approved candidates.

The current Qualified Individual specialty examination fee is $85, paid directly to PSI. That examination fee is separate from the cost of the highlighted and tabbed book package.

Preparation for the TMC examination should address several important areas of trade knowledge, including:

  • Tile materials and terminology
  • Surface and substrate preparation
  • Tile layout and installation methods
  • Mortars, adhesives, and setting materials
  • Grouting and finishing
  • Ceramic, glass, and stone tile systems
  • Terrazzo materials and installation concepts
  • Plan reading and estimating
  • Construction safety and OSHA requirements

Field experience is valuable, but the technical exam can also require familiarity with written standards and formal terminology. A contractor who performs tile work every day may still benefit from reviewing ANSI specifications, system requirements, material limitations, and safety rules that are presented differently from everyday jobsite language.

Open Book Test

The Virginia contractor specialty testing program permits candidates to bring approved reference books into applicable technical examinations. Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before testing, provided they remain otherwise unmarked and comply with PSI examination-security requirements.

This makes prepared references especially useful for TMC exam study. Permanent tabs can help candidates move quickly between major subjects, while highlighting can draw attention to selected standards, installation requirements, definitions, tables, and technical material.

Open-book testing should not be approached as permission to search every book for every question. Efficient candidates learn to identify the subject first and then choose the most appropriate reference. A safety question may lead to OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. A question involving tile installation standards, setting materials, or performance requirements may lead to ANSI A108/A118/A136.1.

Questions involving ceramic, glass, and stone assemblies can be supported by the installation handbook. Practical tile-setting topics may lead to Setting Tile, while terrazzo-specific questions can require the Terrazzo Specification and Design Guide.

During preparation, candidates should practice navigating the books under time pressure. Choose a topic, locate the relevant permanent tab, identify the correct section, and confirm the information. Repeating this process builds familiarity with both the content and the physical organization of the reference library.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors and include both a license class and one or more classifications or specialties. Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation TMC.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the TMC specialty. The business requests Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contracting as the applicable specialty.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate a person who qualifies the company for the TMC specialty.
  4. Meet the required experience level. The Qualified Individual must document experience appropriate to the requested contractor class.
  5. Submit experience verification. TMC is a specialty requiring examination pre-approval, so applicable experience documentation must be provided.
  6. Receive approval for testing. The candidate must be approved before scheduling the TMC technical examination.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Study installation standards, tile systems, terrazzo, adhesives, grouting, estimating, and OSHA safety using the highlighted and tabbed references.
  8. Complete the technical examination. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the TMC examination requirement.
  9. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must complete the applicable Board-approved business education.
  10. Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable. Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee requirements.
  11. Submit the contractor license application. The business must satisfy the remaining requirements for its selected class and specialty.

Passing the TMC technical examination is one part of the Virginia contractor licensing process. Examination completion alone does not create a contractor license.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses performing construction, repair, removal, and improvement work on property owned by others. Contractor licenses consist of a class and one or more classifications or specialty designations.

The Qualified Individual for the TMC specialty must either be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management. Virginia's current contractor application materials require the Qualified Individual to document the minimum experience applicable to the requested license class.

Current minimum Qualified Individual experience requirements are one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Virginia defines TMC work as preparation, fabrication, construction, and installation of artificial marble, burned clay tile, ceramic, terrazzo, encaustic, faience, quarry, semi-vitreous, cementitious board, and other tile. Hollow or structural partition tile is excluded.

The Residential Building and Commercial Building classifications and certain improvement specialties can also provide authority for this work within their respective scopes. The TMC designation specifically identifies tile, marble, ceramic, and terrazzo work as the business's contractor specialty.

New contractor applicants must also complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This business-focused education is separate from the TMC technical examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive TMC exam study plan should organize preparation by trade subject rather than simply reading each reference from beginning to end. Candidates can begin with tile fundamentals and installation methods, then move into adhesives, grouting, terrazzo, estimating, and safety.

General Tile Knowledge should include tile types, substrates, preparation, moisture considerations, installation terminology, setting materials, and common field conditions. Candidates should become comfortable connecting practical jobsite experience to the terminology used in the standards.

Tile Installation preparation should include layout, substrate preparation, installation methods, setting-bed requirements, joint placement, movement accommodation, curing, and material compatibility.

Adhesives and Setting Materials should receive specific review through the ANSI reference. Candidates should understand that different installation conditions and materials can require different mortar or adhesive properties.

Grouting and Finishing preparation should include joint preparation, grout selection, application, curing, cleaning, finishing, and compatibility with the installation system.

Terrazzo should be studied independently rather than treated as an extension of ceramic tile. Review terrazzo materials, specifications, installation procedures, system components, preparation, and finishing.

Plan Reading and Estimating preparation should include dimensions, areas, layouts, quantities, waste considerations, plans, and specifications. Candidates should practice converting project information into material and installation requirements.

Safety should include PPE, ladders, scaffolding, cutting equipment, dust exposure, power tools, material handling, and other OSHA construction requirements relevant to tile and terrazzo work.

The highlighted and tabbed books can help candidates develop a repeatable navigation strategy. During practice, identify the question category first, select the correct reference, use the applicable tab, and then locate the supporting section.

When a practice question is answered incorrectly, review the surrounding section rather than memorizing a single answer. Technical standards often contain conditions, limitations, and exceptions that are easier to understand when read in context.

What does the Virginia TMC specialty cover?

Who administers the Virginia contractor specialty exam?

Can reference books be highlighted for the exam?

Virginia's contractor specialty examination rules permit reference books to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed when otherwise compliant with PSI examination-security requirements.

Why is ANSI A108/A118/A136.1 included?

The ANSI reference contains important installation specifications and material-performance requirements involving tile installation, mortars, adhesives, grouts, and related systems.

Why is OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 included?

Tile and terrazzo work involves construction hazards such as tools, cutting operations, silica exposure, ladders, scaffolds, heavy materials, and personal protective equipment. OSHA standards support preparation for these safety topics.

Is Setting Tile a new book?

Does passing the TMC exam automatically issue a Virginia contractor license?

No. Successful completion of the technical examination is one part of the licensing process. The contractor business must also satisfy applicable license class, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, application, and other Virginia requirements.

How does the Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor exam work?

The Tile, Marble, Ceramic and Terrazzo Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
