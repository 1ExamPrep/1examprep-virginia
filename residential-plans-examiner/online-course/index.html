Exam Details

The ICC Residential Plans Examiner R3 exam measures a candidate’s ability to apply the 2021 International Residential Code to residential plan review scenarios. The exam is based on the job tasks of a residential plans examiner and requires students to locate code requirements related to residential construction documents, building layout, structural elements, fire and life safety, energy efficiency, mechanical systems, plumbing systems, electrical systems, and administrative provisions.

The exam is focused on plan review, not field inspection alone. A residential plans examiner must be able to review drawings, schedules, specifications, and construction details before the project is built. Questions may describe what is shown on a plan, what is missing from a submittal, which code requirement applies, or what correction should be required before approval. The correct answer must come from the code reference used for the exam.

Major study areas for the ICC R3 exam include Administrative, Site, Architectural and Life Safety, Structural, Energy Efficiency, Mechanical, Plumbing, and Electrical. These areas reflect the broad scope of residential plan review. Students should be prepared to move throughout the 2021 International Residential Code because the R3 exam may test requirements from several different parts of the book.

Administrative topics include the scope of the code, permit requirements, construction documents, code official authority, plan submittal expectations, approval responsibilities, inspections, notices, and related administrative provisions. These topics help define the plan review process and the authority used to evaluate submitted documents.

Site topics may include lot conditions, building location, drainage, flood-resistant construction when applicable, foundation-related site information, setbacks when addressed through the submitted plan review materials, and other site conditions that may affect residential code compliance. Students should understand how site information supports a complete residential plan review.

Architectural and Life Safety topics include building planning, emergency escape and rescue openings, light and ventilation, room dimensions, glazing, doors, stairways, guards, handrails, smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, fire separation, townhouse provisions, and related life safety requirements. These topics are common in residential plan review because they affect the overall safety and usability of the dwelling.

Structural topics include foundations, wall construction, floor systems, roof-ceiling construction, bracing, fastening, design assumptions, loads, span tables, headers, beams, joists, rafters, trusses, and related structural provisions found in the 2021 International Residential Code. Students should be comfortable using tables and reading the conditions attached to those tables because structural questions often depend on exact limits, spans, materials, spacing, and footnotes.

Energy Efficiency topics include residential energy provisions addressed by the 2021 International Residential Code. Students should be ready to locate insulation, fenestration, air sealing, mechanical system, duct, and other energy-related provisions when they are part of the plan review question.

Mechanical, Plumbing, and Electrical topics require students to understand how the residential code addresses building service systems. Plan review questions may involve equipment location, combustion air, exhaust, ventilation, water distribution, drainage, vents, water heaters, electrical service, branch circuits, receptacles, lighting outlets, grounding, bonding, and other residential system requirements.

Open Book Test

The ICC R3 Residential Plans Examiner exam is an open-book exam. The approved reference for this product is the 2021 International Residential Code. Students should still prepare carefully because open book does not mean the answers will be easy to find without practice. Candidates must know how the code is organized, where major topics are located, and how to apply the code language to plan review questions.

A strong open-book strategy begins with familiarity. Students should study the chapter layout, definitions, tables, figures, exceptions, and indexes before exam day. The R3 exam may require a candidate to move from a question about building planning to a question about structural spans, then to a question about energy efficiency, then to a question about plumbing or electrical requirements. Speed comes from knowing where to look before the clock starts.

Students should also practice reading the question carefully. ICC-style questions may include important words such as minimum, maximum, except, required, not permitted, approved, listed, or where provided. A small word can change the answer. Good preparation includes slowing down enough to understand the question while still moving efficiently through the exam.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Virginia Residential Plans Examiner (ICC - R3) Online Exam Prep course is designed to help students study the 2021 International Residential Code in a focused and practical way. The course should be used with the code book so students can build the habit of finding answers directly in the approved reference. The strongest preparation comes from combining code reading, guided review, and repeated question practice.

Students should begin by learning the layout of the 2021 International Residential Code. The R3 exam is broad because the residential plans examiner reviews more than one trade area. A single plan review may involve building layout, structural details, energy compliance, mechanical equipment, plumbing fixtures, electrical layouts, and administrative completeness. Knowing the code’s organization helps students avoid wasting time during the exam.

Definitions are an important part of R3 preparation. Residential plan review questions may turn on the meaning of a specific term, and using the wrong definition can lead to the wrong answer. Students should become familiar with definitions related to dwellings, townhouses, habitable spaces, story height, means of egress, emergency escape and rescue openings, fire separation, light and ventilation, structural elements, mechanical systems, plumbing systems, and electrical components.

Tables and figures also deserve careful study. Structural questions may require span tables, footing tables, fastener schedules, bracing provisions, and load-related information. Energy questions may require insulation or fenestration values. Plumbing and mechanical questions may require sizing, venting, clearances, ventilation, or equipment installation requirements. Electrical questions may require outlet, circuit, service, grounding, bonding, or safety-related provisions. Students should practice reading table headings, footnotes, limitations, and conditions.

Because this is a plans examiner exam, students should practice thinking like a reviewer. A plans examiner asks whether the submitted documents show enough information to demonstrate compliance. A question may describe a missing detail, an incomplete schedule, an incorrect structural member, an unsafe stair layout, an improper opening requirement, or a system design that does not match the code. The student’s job is to identify the code issue and locate the requirement that supports the correct answer.

Architectural and life safety topics should receive steady attention. Students should review room sizes, ceiling heights, light and ventilation, stairways, guards, handrails, smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, glazing, emergency escape and rescue openings, fire separation, dwelling-garage separation, and townhouse-related provisions. These subjects are frequently connected to residential plan review because they are visible on the plans and affect occupant safety.

Structural topics should be studied with patience and repetition. Students should practice locating requirements for foundations, footing sizes, wood framing, floor joists, rafters, headers, beams, wall bracing, roof construction, and fasteners. Many students find structural table questions challenging because the answer depends on multiple conditions. A good study routine includes identifying the material, spacing, span, loading condition, and notes before choosing an answer.

Mechanical, plumbing, and electrical topics should not be ignored. Even when a student’s background is mainly building plan review, the R3 exam covers the residential code as a complete reference. Students should review basic requirements for mechanical equipment, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, water heaters, water supply, drainage, vents, plumbing fixtures, electrical service, receptacles, lighting outlets, GFCI protection, AFCI protection, grounding, and bonding.

A steady study routine can make the R3 exam more manageable. Students should review one subject area at a time, answer practice questions, check every answer against the code, and return to weak areas before moving forward. This approach helps students build both knowledge and speed, which are important for an open-book exam.

What is the difference between the B1 and R3 exams?

The ICC B1 Residential Building Inspector exam focuses on inspection of residential construction. The ICC R3 Residential Plans Examiner exam focuses on reviewing residential construction documents and plans before construction or installation work is performed.

How does the Residential Plans Examiner (R3) exam work?

The Residential Plans Examiner (R3) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
