Exam Details

The ICC R3 Residential Plans Examiner exam is a national certification exam offered by the International Code Council. It is associated with residential plan review and evaluates a candidate’s ability to review residential construction documents for compliance with the International Residential Code.

The ICC R3 exam is listed as a 60-question multiple-choice exam with a 2-hour time limit. Candidates should expect questions that require code lookup, interpretation, and practical application. The 2021 version of the exam is connected to the 2021 International Residential Code.

Major study areas for the ICC R3 Residential Plans Examiner exam include code administration, building planning, footings and foundations, floor construction, wall construction and coverings, roof and ceiling construction, public safety, and special construction. Candidates should be prepared to apply code requirements to submitted residential construction documents rather than simply recall isolated code facts.

Residential plan review questions may require candidates to evaluate building layouts, verify construction document information, review foundation details, check floor framing, review wall bracing or wall construction provisions, evaluate roof and ceiling framing, confirm stairway requirements, review guard and handrail provisions, check emergency escape and rescue openings, evaluate fire separation details, and apply public safety provisions.

The 2021 International Residential Code is the central reference for this exam. Candidates should study the book directly and become familiar with how residential building provisions are arranged. Plan review questions may involve definitions, administrative requirements, building planning provisions, foundation rules, floor construction, wall construction, roof-ceiling construction, energy-related construction notes, and other residential code provisions.

The exam is designed to measure code application, not memorization alone. A candidate may need to locate a specific section, read an exception, compare a table value, apply a definition, review a construction detail, or decide whether a submitted plan condition meets the code. Strong preparation includes both residential construction knowledge and repeated practice using the reference book under timed conditions.

Open Book Test

The ICC R3 Residential Plans Examiner exam is an open book test. Candidates preparing for the 2021 version should study with the 2021 International Residential Code. Open book testing does not mean the exam is easy. It means the code book is part of the exam strategy, and candidates must know how to use it quickly and accurately.

For an open book residential plans examiner exam, speed and familiarity matter. Candidates should be comfortable using the table of contents, chapter layout, definitions, index, tables, figures, notes, and exceptions. Candidates should know where to look for building planning, foundation requirements, floor construction, wall construction, roof-ceiling construction, stairways, guards, handrails, emergency escape and rescue openings, fire separation, and other residential plan review provisions.

Open book preparation should include timed code lookup practice. A useful method is to read the question carefully, identify the subject, move to the correct section of the International Residential Code, confirm the applicable requirement, and then select the best answer. This process should be practiced repeatedly before exam day.

Many R3 exam questions include details that affect the answer. A question may depend on the building type, occupancy scope, story height, opening location, stair geometry, framing span, foundation condition, wall construction detail, roof assembly, or plan documentation issue. Careful reading and accurate code navigation work together.

Candidates should also practice pacing. With 60 questions and a 2-hour time limit, spending too long on one question can create pressure later in the exam. A practical approach is to answer questions you can locate confidently, mark longer lookup questions for review, and return to them after completing faster questions.

Test Information and Study Materials

The best preparation for the ICC R3 Residential Plans Examiner exam begins with direct use of the 2021 International Residential Code. Candidates should study from the same reference used for exam preparation so the book becomes familiar before test day.

Start by learning the structure of the International Residential Code. Review the table of contents, definitions, chapter titles, key tables, figures, and index. Residential plans examiner candidates should understand where to find code administration, building planning, footings and foundations, floor construction, wall construction, roof-ceiling construction, public safety, and special construction provisions.

Code administration is a useful starting point. Candidates should understand construction documents, permits, inspections, alternative materials and methods, duties of the code official, and general enforcement language. These provisions help connect exam questions to the actual role of a residential plans examiner.

Building planning should be studied carefully. Candidates should review occupancy scope, dwelling unit provisions, room requirements, light and ventilation, ceiling heights, sanitation, glazing, stairways, ramps, handrails, guards, emergency escape and rescue openings, smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, fire separation, and general safety requirements. These topics are commonly connected to plan review questions.

Footings and foundations should receive focused review. Candidates should study footing size, foundation walls, concrete and masonry requirements, frost protection, foundation drainage, anchor bolts, sill plates, reinforcement, slab construction, and conditions shown on foundation plans. Plan review questions may require candidates to evaluate whether a proposed detail meets the code.

Floor construction is another important study area. Candidates should review floor framing, joists, beams, girders, bearing, spans, notching and boring, fasteners, subflooring, and related structural provisions. Plan review questions may require candidates to use tables, interpret framing details, or identify missing information.

Wall construction and coverings should be reviewed in detail. Candidates should study wall framing, braced wall lines, wall coverings, sheathing, weather protection, flashing, exterior walls, interior walls, fireblocking, draftstopping, and related construction details. These topics may appear in plan review questions because they affect structural performance, weather protection, and safety.

Roof and ceiling construction should be part of regular preparation. Candidates should review rafters, ceiling joists, trusses, roof assemblies, roof coverings, attic access, ventilation, roof drainage, fasteners, sheathing, and framing details. Candidates should understand how roof and ceiling provisions appear on plans, details, and specifications.

Public safety and special construction topics should also be reviewed. Candidates should study safety glazing, smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, fire-resistant construction where applicable, swimming pool barrier provisions where applicable, emergency openings, fire separation, and other provisions that protect occupants. These topics often require careful reading because details such as location, height, clearance, or use can change the answer.

Definitions should be reviewed throughout preparation. Many exam questions turn on the exact meaning of a term. When a question uses words related to dwelling, townhouse, story, habitable space, emergency escape and rescue opening, guard, handrail, braced wall line, footing, foundation, roof assembly, or approved construction documents, definitions may help guide the correct answer.
