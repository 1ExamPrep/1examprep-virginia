### Exam Details

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. PSI administers the examination for candidates pursuing the DRY specialty.

- **Exam Name:** Drywall Contracting Examination
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** DRY
- **Number of Questions:** 40
- **Time Allowed:** 90 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 28 correct answers
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

The published examination outline divides the 40 questions into five major subject areas:

- **Estimating and Plan Reading:** 6 questions
- **Materials and Installation:** 18 questions
- **Taping:** 4 questions
- **Special Applications:** 6 questions
- **Safety:** 6 questions

Materials and Installation is the largest section, accounting for 18 of the 40 questions. Candidates should devote substantial study time to gypsum products, panels, framing systems, installation methods, fasteners, walls, ceilings, sheathing, and other construction practices associated with drywall work.

Estimating and Plan Reading, Special Applications, and Safety each account for six questions, making them significant parts of the examination. Taping accounts for four questions and should include preparation involving joint treatment, compounds, tape, finishing procedures, and related drywall terminology.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because the books are not supplied at the testing center.

PSI permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed. References containing written notes are not permitted, and candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination.

With 90 minutes available for 40 questions, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient reference navigation is therefore an important part of preparation. Candidates should practice deciding which reference applies before beginning a detailed search.

A question involving gypsum panels, metal framing, finishing, or wall and ceiling systems may point toward the *Gypsum Construction Handbook*. Commercial building-code questions may require the *International Building Code, 2021*. Residential code questions may require the *International Residential Code, 2021*, while workplace safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

A useful open-book strategy is to identify the subject, select the appropriate reference, locate the applicable section using the index or table of contents, and then evaluate the question using the technical information found there.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor businesses operate under a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
2. **Select the Drywall Contracting specialty.** Virginia identifies this specialty with the abbreviation DRY.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for the specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** Virginia requires minimum qualifying experience based on the selected contractor license class.
5. **Complete the applicable experience verification.** Drywall Contracting is one of the specialties requiring a technical examination approved by the Board.
6. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor license applicants must satisfy the state's eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
7. **Complete the required technical examination.** The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the DRY examination.
8. **Complete any additional examination requirements associated with the selected contractor license class.**
9. **Complete and submit the Virginia contractor license application and supporting documentation.**

Passing the Drywall Contracting examination is one component of the Virginia contractor licensing process. Successful completion of the technical examination does not by itself issue a contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia's current contractor regulations define Drywall Contracting as work involving the installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, panels and assemblies of gypsum wallboard, sheathing, and cementitious board, along with installation of sheet-metal studs for framing ceilings and nonstructural partitions.

The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty designation. The Qualified Individual must meet Virginia's applicable employment, experience, examination, and qualification requirements.

Virginia currently identifies the minimum experience requirement for a Qualified Individual based on the contractor license class:

- **Class C:** Minimum of 1 year of qualifying experience
- **Class B:** Minimum of 3 years of qualifying experience
- **Class A:** Minimum of 5 years of qualifying experience

The Virginia Board for Contractors requires a technical examination for Qualified Individuals seeking the DRY specialty. Contractor applicants must also satisfy any applicable business, education, examination, and application requirements associated with their requested license class.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia DRY study plan should follow the examination content distribution. Materials and Installation represents almost half of the exam, so candidates should spend substantial time reviewing gypsum products, panel types, framing systems, ceilings, partitions, fasteners, installation methods, sheathing, and cementitious board.

The *Gypsum Construction Handbook* should become a familiar working reference. Rather than reading it once and setting it aside, candidates should repeatedly locate information about wall systems, ceilings, panel installation, framing, finishing, and special applications.

Estimating and Plan Reading requires both construction knowledge and calculation practice. Candidates should become comfortable interpreting dimensions, recognizing construction information on plans, calculating wall and ceiling areas, accounting for openings, and determining quantities of drywall-related materials.

Taping preparation should include familiarity with joint tape, joint compound, corner treatment, finishing sequences, application practices, and terminology. Candidates should understand how the finishing process progresses rather than memorizing isolated definitions.

Special Applications can involve construction conditions that differ from standard wallboard installation. Study specialized gypsum assemblies, fire-related conditions, moisture considerations, code requirements, and other applications covered by the technical references.

Safety represents six questions and should remain part of the study plan from beginning to end. Drywall work often involves ladders, scaffolds, overhead installation, lifting and handling panels, powered tools, and other construction hazards. Candidates should become familiar with how the OSHA reference is organized and practice locating applicable requirements.

Code navigation should also be practiced deliberately. Since PSI now bases applicable ICC questions on the 2021 editions, candidates should use the 2021 IBC and 2021 IRC during preparation. Learn how the chapters are organized and practice recognizing whether a question involves commercial building requirements or residential construction requirements.

A strong online preparation routine combines trade review with reference practice. After studying a topic, answer practice-oriented questions and then locate the supporting information in the appropriate reference. This approach reinforces both technical understanding and open-book navigation.

When a question is answered incorrectly, review the reason for the mistake. Was the construction concept misunderstood? Was the wrong code book selected? Was an important word in the question overlooked? Was too much time spent looking in the wrong section? Identifying the cause helps make future study more productive.

Timed practice is useful as the examination approaches. Work toward maintaining a steady pace without rushing every question. Some questions may be answered directly from trade knowledge, while others require careful reference work or calculations.

The objective of preparation is not to memorize every page of four technical books. It is to build enough subject familiarity that candidates can understand the question, recognize the correct reference, locate detailed information when necessary, and work through the examination efficiently.
