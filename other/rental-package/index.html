Exam Details

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. Virginia identifies the specialty as DRY, and a Qualified Individual seeking this designation must successfully complete the approved technical examination as part of the applicable licensing process.

  • Exam Name: Drywall Contracting Examination
  • Specialty Abbreviation: DRY
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Allowed: 90 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 28 correct answers
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The published examination outline divides the 40 questions among five principal content areas:

  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 6 questions
  • Materials and Installation: 18 questions
  • Taping: 4 questions
  • Special Applications: 6 questions
  • Safety: 6 questions

Materials and Installation is the largest section, representing 18 of the 40 examination questions. Candidates should give substantial attention to gypsum products, drywall panels, framing, fastening, ceilings, partitions, wall assemblies, installation methods, sheathing, cementitious board, and other construction practices related to the DRY specialty.

Estimating and Plan Reading, Special Applications, and Safety each contribute six questions. Taping accounts for four questions. A balanced preparation schedule should cover all five subject areas while allowing additional time for Materials and Installation because of its larger share of the examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted reference materials to the test center.

The published examination rules allow references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and references containing written notes are prohibited.

This package provides highlighted and permanently tabbed rental books to support organized preparation. Candidates should use the prepared references regularly during the 6 months of course access so the tabs, chapters, indexes, and major subject areas become familiar before examination day.

With 40 questions and 90 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. That makes efficient navigation important. Searching randomly through multiple books can quickly consume time that could otherwise be used for estimating calculations, plan-reading questions, or more detailed code lookups.

A question involving drywall panels, framing, joint treatment, or gypsum assemblies may lead to the Gypsum Construction Handbook. A commercial code question may require the International Building Code, 2021, while a residential code question may require the International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021. Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the Drywall Contracting specialty. The specialty abbreviation is DRY.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the specialty.
  4. Document applicable experience. Qualified Individuals seeking DRY must satisfy Virginia's experience requirements and provide the applicable experience verification.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor license applicants must complete the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
  6. Complete the DRY technical examination. Virginia requires successful completion of a Board-approved technical examination for the Drywall Contracting designation.
  7. Complete any additional examinations required for the selected contractor license class.
  8. Complete the contractor license application and supporting documentation.
  9. Submit the applicable licensing materials to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

Successful completion of the DRY technical examination is one component of the licensing process. Passing the examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license.

State Requirements

The Virginia Drywall Contracting specialty covers the installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, panels and assemblies of gypsum wallboard, sheathing, and cementitious board. It also includes installation of sheet-metal studs used for framing ceilings and nonstructural partitions.

Virginia regulations also recognize other contractor classifications and specialties as providing authority for drywall work within their respective scopes, including Commercial Building, Residential Building, Commercial Improvement, and Home Improvement.

The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable requirements for the DRY designation. Current Virginia contractor application materials require Qualified Individuals for DRY and numerous other technical designations to submit an Experience Verification Form.

Virginia also requires successful completion of a technical examination approved by the Board for the Drywall Contracting designation. The contractor applicant must satisfy the remaining requirements for its selected license class, business organization, education, examinations, and application before licensure is granted.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive preparation plan should reflect the exam's subject distribution. Materials and Installation should receive the greatest amount of study time because it represents 18 questions. Candidates should understand gypsum products, panels, board applications, sheet-metal framing, fasteners, ceilings, partitions, installation methods, and common trade procedures.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook should become a familiar working reference. Practice locating information about gypsum board installation, wall assemblies, ceiling systems, framing, joint treatment, finishing, fire-related assemblies, and specialized applications. Repeated navigation can help candidates move directly toward the appropriate chapter instead of searching the book from the beginning.

Estimating and Plan Reading requires practical work with dimensions and construction information. Candidates should practice calculating quantities and interpreting information relevant to drywall projects, including wall areas, ceiling areas, openings, and material requirements.

Taping preparation should include joint tape, compounds, finishing procedures, corner treatment, application methods, and terminology. Understanding the sequence of the finishing process helps candidates evaluate questions that describe practical field situations.

Special Applications should include review of drywall conditions that extend beyond basic standard wall installation. Candidates should be familiar with specialized assemblies, fire-related conditions, moisture considerations, and applications addressed by the gypsum and code references.

Safety represents six questions. Drywall installation often involves overhead work, ladders, scaffolds, powered tools, panel handling, and other construction hazards. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of 29 CFR Part 1926 and practice locating the sections that apply to different jobsite conditions.

Code navigation deserves dedicated practice. The IBC and IRC contain extensive construction requirements, so candidates should learn how chapters and sections are organized rather than trying to memorize entire code books. Recognizing whether a question involves a commercial or residential condition is an important first step.

The included 6 months of course access provides time to combine online study with direct book practice. Candidates can review a subject in the course, work through practice-oriented material, and then locate related information in the applicable rental reference.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. Review whether the problem resulted from a trade concept, calculation, terminology, code navigation, or selecting the wrong reference. Then locate the supporting material and use that error to guide additional study.

Timed practice can help candidates adjust to the 90-minute examination period. Some questions may be answered quickly through trade knowledge, while others may require a reference lookup. Developing judgment about when to search a book is an important part of open-book preparation.
