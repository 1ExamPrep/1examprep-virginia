Exam Details

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. The specialty is identified by the abbreviation DRY, and the technical examination is administered through PSI.

  • Exam Name: Drywall Contracting Examination
  • Specialty Abbreviation: DRY
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Allowed: 90 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 28 correct answers
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The published examination outline divides the test among five subject areas:

  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 6 questions
  • Materials and Installation: 18 questions
  • Taping: 4 questions
  • Special Applications: 6 questions
  • Safety: 6 questions

Materials and Installation is the largest examination category, accounting for 18 of the 40 questions. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to gypsum products, panels, framing, fasteners, walls, ceilings, sheathing, cementitious board, installation procedures, and other construction practices associated with the DRY specialty.

Estimating and Plan Reading, Special Applications, and Safety each account for six questions. Taping accounts for four questions. A balanced study plan should include every category while placing additional emphasis on Materials and Installation because of its larger share of the examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials to the examination.

Open-book testing still requires preparation. Candidates have 90 minutes to complete 40 questions, giving them an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Searching through unfamiliar books without knowing which source applies can use valuable examination time.

The highlighted and permanently tabbed references included with The 1 Package support organized navigation. Candidates should work with these books regularly throughout the preparation period so the tabs, chapters, indexes, and major subject areas become familiar.

Questions involving gypsum panels, wall systems, ceilings, framing, taping, or finishing may point toward the Gypsum Construction Handbook. Commercial building-code questions may require the International Building Code, 2021. Residential code questions may require the International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021, while construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

An effective approach is to identify the subject of the question first, select the appropriate reference, and then use the index, table of contents, permanent tabs, headings, and technical terminology to locate detailed information efficiently.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to qualifying businesses and combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Establish the business entity. The 1 Package includes Business Formation assistance for an LLC or Corporation.
  2. Obtain an EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included with the package.
  3. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia licenses contractors under Class A, Class B, or Class C.
  4. Select the Drywall Contracting specialty. Virginia identifies the specialty by the abbreviation DRY.
  5. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contracting business must designate an individual meeting Virginia's applicable requirements for the specialty.
  6. Document qualifying experience. The Qualified Individual must provide the applicable experience verification for the DRY designation.
  7. Complete required pre-license education. Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor license applicants.
  8. Complete the required technical examination. The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the Virginia Drywall Contracting examination.
  9. Complete additional examination requirements applicable to the selected license class.
  10. Prepare the contractor licensing application. Application Service is included with The 1 Package.
  11. Address contractor compliance requirements. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included to support understanding of requirements relevant to operating the business.

Passing the DRY technical examination is one component of the Virginia contractor licensing process. The business must satisfy all applicable licensing requirements before the requested license and specialty are issued.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Drywall Contracting as the installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, panels and assemblies of gypsum wallboard, sheathing, and cementitious board. The specialty also includes installation of studs made of sheet metal for framing ceilings and nonstructural partitioning.

The Virginia regulations also recognize the Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications and the Commercial Improvement and Home Improvement specialties as providing authority for this work within their respective scopes.

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses business entities and sole proprietors under Class A, Class B, or Class C licenses. The license class and the classification or specialty serve different purposes: the class determines the applicable level of contracting authority, while the classification or specialty establishes the type of work the contractor may perform.

New Virginia contractor applicants must complete eight hours of approved pre-license education. Qualified Individuals for the DRY designation must also satisfy the applicable experience and technical examination requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive DRY preparation plan should reflect the examination content distribution. Materials and Installation represents nearly half of the test, making it a logical starting point. Candidates should understand gypsum board types, framing, ceilings, partitions, fastening methods, wall systems, sheathing, cementitious board, and installation practices.

The Gypsum Construction Handbook should become a familiar working reference. Candidates should repeatedly locate sections covering gypsum panels, metal framing, walls, ceilings, joint treatment, finishing, and specialized systems. Repeated navigation helps reduce the time needed to identify information during an open-book examination.

Estimating and Plan Reading requires practical preparation. Candidates should work with dimensions and construction information and practice determining quantities associated with drywall installation, including wall areas, ceiling areas, openings, and material requirements.

Taping preparation should include joint tape, compounds, corner treatments, finishing procedures, and the sequence used to prepare gypsum surfaces. Understanding the purpose of each stage can help when questions are presented as practical field scenarios.

Special Applications should include review of conditions that differ from ordinary wallboard installation. Fire-related assemblies, moisture considerations, specialized panels, and code-driven conditions may require candidates to move between the Gypsum Construction Handbook and the applicable building codes.

Safety represents six questions and should remain part of the study schedule throughout preparation. Drywall operations may involve overhead work, scaffolds, ladders, powered tools, panel handling, and other construction hazards. Practice locating applicable information in 29 CFR Part 1926.
