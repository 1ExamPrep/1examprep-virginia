### Exam Details

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. Virginia identifies the specialty by the abbreviation **DRY**, and PSI administers the technical examination.

- **Exam Name:** Drywall Contracting Examination
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** DRY
- **Number of Questions:** 40
- **Time Allowed:** 90 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 28 correct answers
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

The published examination outline divides the 40 questions among five subject areas:

- **Estimating and Plan Reading:** 6 questions
- **Materials and Installation:** 18 questions
- **Taping:** 4 questions
- **Special Applications:** 6 questions
- **Safety:** 6 questions

Materials and Installation is the largest portion of the examination, accounting for 18 of the 40 questions. Candidates should devote significant preparation time to gypsum products, panels, fasteners, metal framing, wall and ceiling systems, installation procedures, sheathing, cementitious board, and related drywall construction practices.

Estimating and Plan Reading, Special Applications, and Safety each account for six questions. Taping accounts for four questions. A balanced preparation plan should cover all five areas, while placing additional emphasis on Materials and Installation because of its larger share of the examination.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Drywall Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because the books are not supplied by the testing center.

Virginia's published PSI examination information permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and references containing written notes are prohibited.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia licenses contractors as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
2. **Select the Drywall Contracting specialty.** Virginia identifies this specialty as DRY.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the specialty.
4. **Document qualifying experience.** Qualified Individuals seeking the DRY designation must provide the applicable experience verification.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the state's approved pre-license education requirement.
6. **Complete the required technical examination.** The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the Virginia Drywall Contracting examination.
7. **Complete any additional examination requirements associated with the selected contractor license class.**
8. **Prepare the contractor license application and supporting documentation.**
9. **Submit the licensing application through the applicable Virginia Board for Contractors process.Application Service is included** with this Ultimate package, providing support with the contractor application portion of the process. Passing the technical examination remains only one component of Virginia contractor licensing and does not by itself result in issuance of a contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia currently defines Drywall Contracting as the installation, taping, and finishing of drywall, panels and assemblies of gypsum wallboard, sheathing, and cementitious board. It also includes installation of studs made of sheet metal for framing ceilings and nonstructural partitions.

Virginia regulations also recognize the Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications and the Commercial Improvement and Home Improvement specialties as providing authority for drywall work within their respective scopes.

The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the DRY specialty. Current Virginia contractor licensing materials require Qualified Individuals for DRY to provide the applicable Experience Verification Form as part of the licensing process.

Applicants must also satisfy the requirements associated with their selected license class, business organization, education, examinations, and contractor application. The DRY technical examination is only one element of the complete licensing process.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive DRY study plan should follow the published examination outline. Materials and Installation represents nearly half of the technical exam, making it the logical starting point for preparation. Candidates should understand gypsum panels, board types, metal framing, fasteners, partitions, ceilings, sheathing, cementitious board, and standard installation procedures.

The *Gypsum Construction Handbook* should become a familiar working reference. Candidates should repeatedly locate material involving wall assemblies, ceilings, panel installation, framing, fastening, finishing, and special applications. Regular use helps transform the book from an unfamiliar technical manual into a practical exam reference.

Estimating and Plan Reading requires a different type of preparation. Candidates should practice interpreting dimensions and construction information and determining quantities associated with drywall work. This can include wall and ceiling areas, openings, panel requirements, and other project measurements.

Taping preparation should include familiarity with joint tape, compounds, joint treatment, corner systems, finishing procedures, and related terminology. Understanding the sequence and purpose of each step can help candidates answer questions that describe actual field conditions.

Special Applications should include study of assemblies and conditions that differ from standard wallboard installation. Fire-related systems, moisture considerations, specialized board products, and code-related requirements may require candidates to move between the Gypsum Construction Handbook and building-code references.

Safety accounts for six questions and should remain part of the study routine throughout preparation. Drywall installation can involve ladders, scaffolds, overhead work, powered tools, material handling, and other construction hazards. Candidates should learn how 29 CFR Part 1926 is organized and practice locating the sections applicable to different work conditions.

Code navigation also deserves dedicated practice. The International Building Code and International Residential Code contain extensive requirements, so candidates should become familiar with their chapter structure and terminology. Recognizing whether a question involves commercial or residential construction can help narrow the reference search quickly.
