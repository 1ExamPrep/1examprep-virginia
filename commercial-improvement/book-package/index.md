### Exam Details

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is a specialty examination associated with the Qualified Individual for the CIC designation. The Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors uses PSI as its contractor examination vendor.

The Commercial Improvement Contracting examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates are allowed **120 minutes** to complete the examination and must achieve a minimum score of **70 percent**, which equals 35 correct answers.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because books are not provided at the test site.

PSI allows permitted reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References containing writing are not permitted. Candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination session.

Permanent tabs are permitted under PSI's reference-material rules. Removable tabs are not allowed. Loose papers and unbound references are also prohibited. Candidates using downloaded reference material must follow PSI requirements for properly binding the material before bringing it to the examination.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an essential exam skill. Candidates should avoid treating the books as resources that can simply be searched from beginning to end during each question. The examination allows 120 minutes for 50 questions, so efficient navigation can make an important difference in time management.

During preparation, learn the purpose and organization of each reference. Become familiar with indexes, tables of contents, chapters, code sections, definitions, technical tables, diagrams, and major topic areas. When practicing questions, first identify the subject and then select the reference most likely to contain the answer.

### Licensing Steps

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses and sole proprietorships. A Virginia contractor license consists of a license class and one or more classifications or specialty designations. Commercial Improvement Contracting is a specialty designation identified as CIC.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia contractor applicants select the class that applies to the business and its planned contracting activity.
2. **Select the Commercial Improvement Contracting specialty.** Applicants seeking authority to perform work within the CIC scope identify Commercial Improvement Contracting as the requested specialty.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The business must have a Qualified Individual for the CIC specialty. The Qualified Individual must satisfy Virginia's applicable qualification and experience requirements.
4. **Complete the experience verification process.** Commercial Improvement Contracting requires the applicable experience documentation for the Qualified Individual as part of the examination and licensing process.
5. **Complete the required specialty examination.** Successful completion of the applicable examination is required before applying for the contractor license.
6. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must satisfy the state's eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
7. **Complete additional Class A or Class B requirements when applicable.** Class A and Class B applicants have Designated Employee and contractor business examination requirements separate from the CIC technical specialty examination.
8. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business submits the current application and required supporting documentation to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The CIC technical examination is only one component of the contractor licensing process. Candidates should distinguish between qualification for the Commercial Improvement Contracting specialty and the separate requirements associated with the contractor license class.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates businesses performing construction, removal, repair, and improvement work on property owned by others. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietorships rather than as individual trade licenses.

The license class establishes the level of contracting activity authorized under Virginia's licensing structure, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of work the contractor is permitted to perform. CIC is the specialty designation for Commercial Improvement Contracting.

Each contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty requested on the license. The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management and must satisfy the experience and qualification requirements applicable to the requested license class and specialty.

Commercial Improvement Contracting requires successful completion of the applicable technical examination. Virginia's Board for Contractors states that required examinations must be successfully completed before applying for licensure.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the pre-license course must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of Responsible Management completes the course. This education requirement is separate from the Class A and Class B business examinations and the CIC specialty examination.

The official CIC scope includes commercial repair and improvement work but excludes construction of new buildings and accessory buildings. The specialty also excludes electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and gas work. Masonry Contracting, Painting and Wallcovering Contracting, and Roofing Contracting are included within the CIC scope and do not have to be separately listed when operating within that specialty.

## How does the Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam work?

The Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
