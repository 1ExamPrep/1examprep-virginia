### Exam Details

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is a technical contractor specialty examination administered through PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Commercial Improvement Contracting is one of the Virginia specialties for which the Qualified Individual must receive the applicable approval and successfully complete a technical examination.

The current Commercial Improvement Contracting examination consists of **50 questions**. Candidates receive **120 minutes** to complete the examination and must earn a minimum score of **70 percent**, which corresponds to 35 correct answers.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates may bring permitted reference materials into the examination center, but the testing center does not provide the books. Open-book testing makes familiarity with the physical organization of each reference especially important.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. PSI rules require reference materials to remain otherwise unmarked, meaning written notes are not permitted in the books. Candidates may not write in the reference materials during the examination session.

Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates preparing for the test should therefore become accustomed to using permanent tabs, indexes, tables of contents, chapter headings, and code organization to find information efficiently.

Having reference books available does not eliminate the need to study. With 50 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have limited time to identify the subject of each question, select the correct reference, locate the applicable information, and choose an answer. Candidates who have practiced with their books before the examination are better positioned to use the open-book format effectively.

The highlighted and tabbed preparation included with this package is intended to support that navigation process. Spend time working directly with the books during study sessions so the tabs and highlighted sections become familiar rather than seeing them for the first time on exam day.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietorships rather than directly to an individual worker. The license has two major components: the class of license and the classification or specialty. Commercial Improvement Contracting is a specialty designation identified by the abbreviation CIC.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The class determines the level of contracting activity permitted under Virginia's licensing structure.
2. **Select Commercial Improvement Contracting.** Candidates pursuing commercial repair and improvement work within the CIC scope identify Commercial Improvement Contracting as the requested specialty.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** Each classification or specialty requires a Qualified Individual who meets Virginia's applicable criteria.
4. **Meet the experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must demonstrate the required amount of experience associated with the license class being requested.
5. **Complete the examination approval process.** Commercial Improvement Contracting is one of the Virginia specialties requiring examination pre-approval and an Experience Verification Form for the Qualified Individual.
6. **Pass the CIC technical examination.** Successful completion of the approved Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is required for the applicable Qualified Individual.
7. **Complete pre-license education.** Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants.
8. **Complete any Class A or Class B business requirements.** Class A and Class B businesses have additional Designated Employee and business examination requirements separate from the CIC trade examination.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business submits the required licensing information and supporting documentation to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses engaged in construction, repair, removal, or improvement work on property owned by others. Contractor licenses consist of a license class and the classification or specialty that establishes the type of work the contractor may perform.

Each contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty requested. The Qualified Individual must be either a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management and must satisfy the applicable experience and qualification requirements.

Virginia's current contractor application identifies minimum Qualified Individual experience requirements of at least one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A. Commercial Improvement Contracting requires pre-approval for the specialty examination, and an Experience Verification Form must be submitted for the Qualified Individual seeking CIC qualification.

Virginia also requires eight hours of pre-license education for all new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of Responsible Management completes the requirement.

Class A and Class B businesses have business examination requirements in addition to specialty qualification. This product is a Commercial Improvement Contracting book package focused on the CIC technical trade examination references.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Effective CIC exam preparation should combine construction knowledge with frequent book-navigation practice. Because the examination includes several technical disciplines, begin by learning which reference is most useful for each major content area.

For safety questions, become comfortable using 29 CFR Part 1926. For concrete questions, practice finding information in Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Masonry questions should lead naturally to Modern Masonry, while drywall subjects may require the Gypsum Construction Handbook. Rough carpentry and framing topics may involve Carpentry and Building Construction or applicable code requirements.

Code navigation deserves its own study time. Learn how definitions, chapters, sections, exceptions, tables, and indexes are organized. A code question may require more than finding a keyword; candidates may need to understand how a general rule, exception, or table applies to a specific situation.

Practice under timed conditions. With 120 minutes for 50 questions, the average available time is limited. During practice sessions, identify the likely reference before opening a book. Then use tabs, indexes, and highlighted sections to locate the information and confirm the answer.

It is also useful to prioritize larger content areas without ignoring smaller ones. Rough carpentry and metal framing, masonry, concrete, and roofing and exterior siding account for a substantial portion of the published exam outline. Windows and doors, drywall, general code requirements, and safety remain important and should be included in a complete study schedule.

## How does the Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam work?

The Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
