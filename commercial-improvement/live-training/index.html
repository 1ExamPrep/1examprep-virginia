Exam Details

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination taken by the Qualified Individual seeking to qualify a contracting business for the CIC specialty.

The examination contains 50 questions. Candidates must earn a minimum score of 70%, or 35 correct answers, and are allowed 120 minutes to complete the examination.

The examination content outline includes:

  • Windows and Doors: 4 questions
  • Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing: 9 questions
  • General Code Requirements: 5 questions
  • Roofing and Exterior Siding: 7 questions
  • Drywall: 5 questions
  • Concrete: 7 questions
  • Masonry: 8 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Rough carpentry and metal framing represent the largest individual category, followed by masonry. Concrete and roofing and exterior siding are also significant portions of the examination. Candidates should prepare across the entire outline rather than concentrating on only one construction trade.

Code questions on the current examination use the applicable editions specified by PSI. The examination uses the 2021 editions of the International Building Code and International Residential Code for applicable ICC code questions.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because examination references are not supplied at the testing center.

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used to organize important sections. Removable tabs and temporary flags are not permitted.

Reference materials containing written notes are not allowed in the examination center, and candidates may not write in their reference books during the testing session. Loose papers and unbound references are also restricted under the PSI examination rules.

Because the exam contains 50 questions with 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and 24 seconds per question. Efficient reference navigation can therefore be an important part of preparation.

A productive study method is to identify the construction subject first and then determine which reference is most likely to contain the answer. For example, a safety question may direct you to 29 CFR Part 1926, a building-code question may require the International Building Code, a concrete question may point toward Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures, and a masonry question may require Modern Masonry.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia contractor licensing process is administered by the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. A contractor license consists of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties.

  1. Select the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia uses Class A, Class B, and Class C licenses. The class establishes the monetary limits of the contracting work the business may perform.
  2. Select Commercial Improvement Contracting. CIC is the specialty that authorizes qualifying repair and improvement work on structures that are not defined as dwellings and townhouses under the USBC.
  3. Identify a Qualified Individual. The contracting business must designate an individual who meets the applicable qualification requirements for the CIC specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must document the amount of trade experience required for the class of contractor license being requested.
  5. Complete the required technical examination. Commercial Improvement Contracting is among the Virginia specialties that require the Qualified Individual to successfully complete a Board-approved technical examination.
  6. Complete Pre-License Education. New Virginia contractor businesses must satisfy the eight-hour Board-approved Pre-License Education requirement.
  7. Complete Class A/B Business requirements when applicable. Class A and Class B contractor businesses also require a Designated Employee who meets the applicable business examination requirements.
  8. Submit the contractor license application. Complete the required Board for Contractors application and submit applicable business, experience, examination, and supporting information.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Commercial Improvement Contracting as the service of providing repair or improvement work to structures that are not defined as dwellings and townhouses in the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

The Commercial Building Contractor classification also provides authority for commercial improvement work. However, the CIC specialty is narrower than the CBC classification and focuses specifically on repair and improvement rather than new commercial building construction.

The CIC specialty does not authorize construction of new buildings or accessory buildings. It also does not independently authorize electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas work.

Commercial Improvement Contracting includes the following specialties within its authorized scope:

  • Masonry Contracting
  • Painting and Wallcovering Contracting
  • Roofing Contracting

These specialties do not need to be listed separately on the contractor license when the business holds the CIC specialty and the work falls within its authorized scope.

The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience requirements for the contractor license class. Virginia generally requires two years of experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

New contractor applicants must also complete eight hours of Board-approved Pre-License Education. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the Designated Employee. Pre-License Education is separate from the CIC technical examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Commercial Improvement Contracting examination rewards broad construction knowledge. Rough carpentry and metal framing account for nine questions, making framing and structural building concepts an important study area. Candidates should review framing terminology, wall construction, roof systems, openings, doors, windows, and related building procedures.

Masonry accounts for eight questions. Preparation should include masonry units, mortar, layout, installation methods, brick and block construction, and other fundamental masonry practices. Modern Masonry provides a focused reference for this portion of the exam.

Concrete accounts for seven questions. Candidates should study concrete ingredients, mixture properties, placement, consolidation, curing, finishing, durability, and common construction practices using Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures.

Roofing and exterior siding account for seven questions. Review building-envelope concepts, roof construction, exterior protection, weather resistance, and applicable code requirements.

General code requirements account for five questions. Candidates should become familiar with how the International Building Code and applicable Virginia requirements are organized so that code provisions can be located efficiently during the examination.

Drywall accounts for five questions. The Gypsum Construction Handbook supports preparation in gypsum products, wall and ceiling systems, installation methods, finishing, and associated framing practices.

Safety also accounts for five questions. Candidates should use 29 CFR Part 1926 regularly during preparation and become familiar with the organization of OSHA construction standards. Knowing where to locate requirements can be more useful than attempting to memorize every regulation.

Windows and doors account for four questions. Candidates should review terminology, installation concepts, framing around openings, applicable code considerations, and general construction practices.

Timed practice can help candidates develop a balanced exam strategy. Questions that can be answered confidently from trade knowledge may be completed quickly, leaving additional time for questions requiring detailed reference searches.

How does the Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam work?
