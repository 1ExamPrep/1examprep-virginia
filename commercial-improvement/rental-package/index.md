### Exam Details

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is a contractor specialty examination taken by the Qualified Individual associated with the contractor license. The Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors requires CIC Qualified Individuals to receive approval and successfully complete the applicable technical examination.

The Commercial Improvement Contracting exam is administered through PSI as part of Virginia's contractor examination program. Candidates must receive eligibility approval before scheduling the specialty examination.

The CIC examination contains 50 questions and allows 120 minutes for completion. A minimum score of 70 percent is required, which means answering at least 35 questions correctly.

The examination content covers several major areas of commercial improvement construction. Published subject areas include:

- **Windows and Doors** — installation, components, and related construction knowledge.
- **Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing** — framing systems, structural components, materials, and construction practices.
- **General Code Requirements** — building-code concepts and requirements applicable to construction work.
- **Roofing and Exterior Siding** — roof systems, exterior coverings, installation practices, and related requirements.
- **Drywall** — gypsum materials, assemblies, installation, and construction practices.
- **Concrete** — concrete materials, placement, mixtures, properties, and construction principles.
- **Masonry** — brick, block, stone, mortar, materials, and masonry construction methods.
- **Safety** — construction safety requirements and OSHA-related workplace practices.

The breadth of the examination makes reference familiarity particularly important. A candidate may move from a building-code question to a concrete question, then to masonry, framing, drywall, or OSHA safety. Study sessions should therefore include regular practice identifying which reference is most appropriate for each type of question.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is an open-book examination. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because reference books are not supplied at the testing center.

PSI permits candidates to bring reference books, but study guides are not permitted in the examination room. Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. The books must otherwise remain unmarked, meaning written notes are not permitted. Loose papers and unbound references are also prohibited.

This makes preparation with the actual reference books especially useful. Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. In a timed examination, searching an unfamiliar book from beginning to end can consume valuable minutes. Candidates benefit from knowing which book covers each subject and how the chapters, indexes, tables, sections, and code provisions are organized.

The highlighted and tabbed books included with this package are prepared to support reference navigation. The objective is to develop a repeatable process: identify the subject of the question, select the appropriate reference, navigate to the relevant section, and confirm the answer efficiently.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietorships through the Virginia Board for Contractors. A contractor license consists of a license class and one or more classifications or specialty designations. Commercial Improvement Contracting is a specialty designation identified by the code CIC.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia contractor businesses are licensed as Class A, Class B, or Class C depending on the applicable licensing requirements and the level of contracting activity the business intends to perform.
2. **Select Commercial Improvement Contracting as the specialty.** Applicants seeking authority within the CIC scope must request the Commercial Improvement Contracting specialty designation.
3. **Identify a Qualified Individual.** Each contractor classification or specialty must have a Qualified Individual who meets Virginia's applicable experience and qualification requirements.
4. **Submit the required experience documentation.** CIC is among the specialties requiring examination pre-approval, and the Qualified Individual must submit the required experience verification documentation.
5. **Receive examination eligibility.** The Qualified Individual must be approved before taking the required Commercial Improvement Contracting specialty examination.
6. **Schedule and complete the CIC examination.** After eligibility is established, the candidate schedules the examination through PSI and completes the technical exam.
7. **Complete Virginia's other licensing requirements.** Contractor applicants must satisfy the requirements associated with the selected license class, including pre-license education and, for Class A or Class B firms, the applicable business examination requirements for the Designated Employee.
8. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business applies to the Virginia Board for Contractors with the required information and supporting documentation.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses operating in the Commonwealth. The contractor license class determines the level of work the business may perform, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work authorized.

Every contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on the license. For specialties such as Commercial Improvement Contracting, the Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience requirements and successfully complete the board-approved technical examination.

Virginia's current contractor application requirements identify minimum experience based on license class. A Qualified Individual associated with a Class A applicant must have at least five years of applicable experience. A Qualified Individual for a Class B applicant must have at least three years of applicable experience, and a Qualified Individual for a Class C applicant must have at least one year of applicable experience.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. For a Class A or Class B application, the pre-license education may be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For a Class C application, a member of Responsible Management completes the requirement.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants have additional business examination requirements separate from the Commercial Improvement Contracting trade examination. ### Test Information and Study Materials

A useful study plan for the Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting exam should combine technical review with frequent book-navigation practice. Because the test draws from several different references, candidates should avoid relying on a single book for every question.

Begin by becoming familiar with the purpose of each reference. OSHA regulations should immediately suggest safety questions. Concrete questions should direct you toward the concrete reference. Masonry topics should point you toward the masonry book, while framing and carpentry questions may require the carpentry reference or applicable building code. Drywall questions may lead to the gypsum handbook. Code questions require familiarity with the appropriate Virginia, international building, or residential code material.

Next, practice finding information under time pressure. The CIC exam provides 120 minutes for 50 questions, so candidates need to manage their time carefully. Practice questions can be used not only to test construction knowledge but also to develop efficient lookup habits.

When studying, pay attention to terminology. Exam questions may use a technical term that closely matches an index entry, chapter title, table heading, or code section. Recognizing those terms can reduce the amount of time spent searching.

Tabs are most valuable when you already understand what they represent. Spend time working with the prepared books before the examination so the tab locations become familiar. Likewise, highlighted material should be used as a navigation aid and review tool rather than a substitute for understanding the surrounding section.

A strong study routine may rotate between major content areas. Review framing and carpentry, then switch to masonry, concrete, roofing, drywall, building-code requirements, and OSHA safety. Revisit weaker subjects more frequently while continuing to practice the full range of CIC material.

## How does the Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam work?

The Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.
