Exam Details

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is administered by PSI as part of the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors examination program. Candidates must receive eligibility approval before taking the applicable contractor specialty examination.

The current CIC examination contains 50 questions. Candidates are allowed 120 minutes to complete the exam. The minimum passing score is 70 percent, which requires 35 correct answers.

The published examination content outline is divided among the following areas:

  • Windows and Doors: 4 questions.
  • Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing: 9 questions.
  • General Code Requirements: 5 questions.
  • Roofing and Exterior Siding: 7 questions.
  • Drywall: 5 questions.
  • Concrete: 7 questions.
  • Masonry: 8 questions.
  • Safety: 5 questions.

Rough carpentry and metal framing represent the largest individual category on the examination, followed by masonry. Concrete and roofing and exterior siding also account for significant portions of the test. Candidates should nevertheless prepare for every published subject because the examination includes questions from the complete outline.

For applicable ICC code questions, the CIC examination is based on the 2021 code editions. This includes the 2021 International Building Code and the 2021 International Residential Code listed among the reference materials for this course.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the examination center.

PSI allows permitted references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Reference materials containing handwritten notes are not permitted. Candidates are also prohibited from writing in their reference books during the examination session.

Permanent tabs are permitted for organizing examination references. Removable or temporary tabs are not allowed. This makes advance book preparation and familiarity with reference organization particularly useful for CIC candidates.

An open-book examination still requires substantial preparation. With 50 questions to complete in 120 minutes, candidates cannot depend on searching every book from beginning to end. They need to identify the subject of a question, choose the most relevant reference, navigate to the correct section, and confirm the information efficiently.

The online course places emphasis on this reference-navigation process. Highlighting and tabbing locations help students organize their books, while practice questions encourage repeated use of the references. Over time, candidates can become more familiar with where major subjects are located and which book is most likely to contain a particular answer.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietorships by the Virginia Board for Contractors. Commercial Improvement Contracting is a specialty designation that may be added to the appropriate contractor license when the applicable requirements are satisfied.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia recognizes Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The business must determine which class is appropriate for its intended contracting activity.
  2. Select Commercial Improvement Contracting. The contractor application identifies the classification or specialty the business intends to hold. Commercial Improvement Contracting uses the CIC designation.
  3. Identify a Qualified Individual. The business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty included on the application.
  4. Satisfy the applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must have the experience required for the selected contractor license class and specialty.
  5. Receive examination eligibility. Candidates pursuing CIC must complete the applicable approval process before taking the required specialty examination.
  6. Prepare for and complete the CIC exam. The Qualified Individual completes the Commercial Improvement Contracting examination administered through PSI.
  7. Complete pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the state's eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
  8. Complete additional Class A or Class B requirements when applicable. Class A and Class B contractor businesses have separate Designated Employee and business examination requirements.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. After satisfying the applicable requirements, the business submits its licensing application and supporting documentation to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

State Requirements

Virginia's Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses operating within the Commonwealth. The contractor business holds the license, and the license identifies both the class and the classifications or specialties under which the contractor is authorized to operate.

Commercial Improvement Contracting is defined as repair or improvement to structures not defined as dwellings and townhouses in the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code. The Commercial Building Classification also provides for this function. CIC does not provide authority for construction of new buildings or accessory buildings and does not include electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas work.

Every contractor business must have a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty appearing on its application. The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee or a member of Responsible Management and must have the required level of experience in the specialty.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor license applicants. The pre-license education is a separate licensing requirement and should not be confused with this Commercial Improvement Contracting technical exam prep course.

Class A and Class B contractor businesses have additional examination requirements for a Designated Employee. Those business examinations are separate from the CIC specialty examination taken by the Qualified Individual. This online course is focused specifically on preparation for the Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting technical exam.

Reference Books

The following books are used as reference materials for Virginia Commercial Improvement Contracting preparation. Students should become familiar with these resources while progressing through the course and practicing open-book navigation.

  • Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)
    A federal construction safety reference containing OSHA standards for the construction industry. It supports preparation for workplace safety, employee protection, jobsite hazards, equipment, fall protection, scaffolding, and other construction safety subjects.
  • Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2018
    A Virginia-specific reference addressing statewide building code requirements and providing important context for construction regulations applicable within the Commonwealth.
  • International Building Code, 2021
    A commercial construction code reference covering building requirements, occupancy, construction types, fire-resistance, means of egress, structural provisions, materials, and other code-related subjects.
  • International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021
    A code reference addressing construction requirements for one- and two-family dwellings, including framing, foundations, materials, building components, and related construction provisions.
  • Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016
    A practical construction reference covering rough carpentry, framing, tools, materials, building components, and general construction techniques applicable to CIC preparation.
  • Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures, 17th Edition
    A technical concrete reference covering concrete materials, mixtures, properties, production, placement, curing, durability, and quality considerations.
  • Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone, Clois E. Kicklighter, 10th Edition
    A masonry reference covering brick, concrete block, stone, mortar, tools, materials, layout, and masonry construction methods. Masonry represents eight questions on the published CIC examination outline.
  • Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition
    A technical reference covering gypsum products, drywall construction, wall and ceiling assemblies, installation procedures, and related construction systems.
