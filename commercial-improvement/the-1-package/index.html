Exam Details

Commercial Improvement Contracting, identified as CIC, is a Virginia contractor specialty regulated by the Virginia Board for Contractors within the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Virginia contractor licensing combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. The license class establishes the monetary level of work the business is authorized to perform, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of contracting work the business may perform.

The Commercial Improvement Contracting specialty is associated with alteration, repair, improvement, and related construction work. Candidates pursuing this specialty must satisfy the requirements applicable to the Qualified Individual, including the technical examination requirement.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses operating in the Commonwealth. A contractor license consists of a class together with one or more classifications or specialties. Commercial Improvement Contracting is identified by the CIC specialty designation.

The contractor business must have a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on its license. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience and qualification requirements for Commercial Improvement Contracting, including the required technical examination.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. This education requirement is separate from the technical and Class A/B business examinations.

Class A and Class B applicants have additional requirements involving a Designated Employee. The business examination requirement for the Designated Employee is separate from the technical specialty examination associated with the Qualified Individual.

Business formation is another important consideration because the Virginia contractor license is connected to the business entity. The 1 Package includes formation of an LLC or corporation, allowing the customer to establish the business entity that will be used for contracting operations.

After formation, EIN Filing with the IRS provides the business with an Employer Identification Number. An EIN can be used to open business bank accounts, manage federal tax matters, hire employees, and support professional business operations.

How does the Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam work?

The Commercial Improvement Contractor (CIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
