Exam Details

The Virginia Master Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The Board approves applications for testing. Candidates must satisfy the education and work experience requirements set by the Board for Contractors before applying for the examination. After the application is approved, the candidate receives notification and may schedule the exam through PSI.

The current Virginia PSI candidate bulletin lists the Master Electrician portion with 90 items, a passing score of 63, and 270 minutes to complete the exam. The exam is designed to confirm that the candidate has reached the required level of knowledge in electrical principles, practices, statutes, regulations, and trade subjects.

The Master Electrician exam outline includes Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, Renewal and Reinstatement, Definitions, Qualification for Licensure, Revocation of Licensure, General Electrical Knowledge, Requirements for Electrical Installations, Electrical-Electronic Fundamentals, Services, Feeders, and Branch Circuits, Service and Feeder Requirements, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, Electrical Signs and Outline Lighting, Motors and Controls, Utilization Equipment, Lighting, General Low Voltage Requirements, Communication Systems, and Fire Detection and Alarm Systems.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Master Electrician exam is an open book test. PSI’s Virginia individual license and certification candidate bulletin states that all examinations are open book and that candidates must bring their own references because references are not provided at the test site.

Virginia’s reference rules are specific. No study guides are permitted. No loose papers or unbound references are permitted. Downloaded references must be spiral bound, hole-punched and placed in a binder, or stapled in the left-hand corner. References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Reference materials containing writing are not allowed into the exam, and candidates may not write in references during the exam session.

References may be tabbed with permanent tabs only. Removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates should prepare their exam materials carefully and follow PSI’s testing-room rules for reference format, tabs, markings, and allowed materials. A candidate who writes in reference materials during the examination may have the references confiscated and may be reported to the Department.

The included National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback with Tabs is especially useful during study because it helps students practice moving through the code book efficiently. Tabs can make it easier to locate major chapters, definitions, wiring and protection, wiring methods, equipment for general use, special occupancies, special equipment, communication systems, fire alarm topics, and Chapter 9 tables.

Open book testing still requires serious preparation. The NEC is large, detailed, and technical. Students who have not practiced with the code book may lose time searching for basic sections, using the wrong table, overlooking an exception, or missing an article-specific requirement. The strongest candidates understand how to move from a question topic to the correct article, read the code language carefully, check the proper table, and apply the rule accurately.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia master electrician licensing path begins with meeting the Board for Contractors’ eligibility requirements. Applicants must satisfy the education and work experience requirements before applying for the examination. Virginia tradesman licenses are issued to individuals, not companies, and applicants must meet the individual licensing requirements at the time the completed application package is received by the Board.

The applicant completes the Tradesman Exam and License Application through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation process. The application requires the applicant to select the trade designation and method of licensure being pursued. Applicants must provide identity information, address information, licensing history when applicable, employer information, and any required documentation requested by the Board.

After the Board reviews the application and approves the applicant for testing, the candidate receives notification. The candidate then schedules the Virginia Master Electrician examination through PSI. PSI offers scheduling through its registration system, and the candidate must follow the identification, scheduling, exam fee, testing format, and reference material rules in the current candidate bulletin.

After passing the examination, the applicant completes the licensing process through DPOR and the Board for Contractors. The Master Electrician license is an individual tradesman license. It establishes individual qualification but does not automatically replace the need for a contractor business license when contracting requirements apply.

After licensure, the electrician must continue to follow Virginia Board for Contractors individual license and certification regulations, renewal requirements, continuing education requirements, standards of practice, standards of conduct, and the scope of work authorized by the license held. Electricians who operate or qualify a contracting business should also understand Virginia contractor licensing requirements, business classifications, and local permit responsibilities.

How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
