### Exam Details

Virginia tradesman licensing is regulated by the **Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR)** through the **Board for Contractors**. The tradesman examinations are administered by **PSI**. PSI’s Virginia Individual License and Certification Candidate Information Bulletin lists the **Master Electrician** portion as:

- **90 items**
- **270 minutes** to complete
- **Passing score:** 63
- **Exam fee:** $125 (fee is valid for one year from the date of payment; non-refundable and non-transferable)

The bulletin also provides a topic breakdown that includes both professional standards and technical electrical knowledge. Master-level candidates should be ready for questions that cover ethics and professional responsibility (standards of practice, standards of conduct, renewal and reinstatement, definitions, qualification for licensure, revocation of licensure) as well as broad electrical competencies (services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, motors and controls, utilization equipment, lighting, and more).

Because the exam includes both rule-based and application-based questions, your study plan should train two skills at the same time: (1) recognizing what the question is really asking, and (2) confirming the correct rule quickly in your references.

### Open Book Test

Virginia tradesman examinations are **open book**. PSI’s bulletin states that all examinations are open book and that you must bring your own references because they are not provided at the test site.

**Open-book rules that matter on exam day:**

- **No study guides are permitted.**
- **No loose papers** or unbound references are permitted.
- If a reference is downloaded from the internet, it must be **spiral bound** or **hole-punched and placed in a binder**, or **stapled in the left-hand corner**.
- References may be **highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed** prior to the exam.
- **Reference materials containing any writing are not allowed** into the examination.
- You may **not write** in reference materials during the exam session.
- References may be tabbed with **permanent tabs only**; **no removable tabs** are allowed.

Open book doesn’t remove the pressure—it shifts it. The test rewards the electrician who can find and apply the correct requirement efficiently. That’s why timed practice is so effective: it forces you to develop a reliable method for locating answers, confirming exceptions, and moving forward without losing momentum.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia’s Board for Contractors oversees the licensing process for individual tradesmen. The general pathway to a Virginia Master Electrician license includes meeting eligibility requirements, applying for examination approval, completing the PSI exam, and maintaining your license through renewal.

1. **Confirm you meet master eligibility.** Virginia regulations list experience standards used for examination approval (see State Requirements below).
2. **Prepare documentation.** DPOR requires applicants to submit documentation of identification, vocational training, and experience.
3. **Submit your application to the Board.** The Board approves applications for testing, and PSI notes a notification is sent after your application is approved.
4. **Register and schedule through PSI.** Once approved, you pay and schedule your exam through PSI’s registration process.
5. **Take and pass the Master Electrician exam.** Use an open-book strategy with strong reference navigation and time management.
6. **Renew on time.** Electrician licenses in Virginia expire on a three-year cycle and have continuing education requirements tied to renewal.

### State Requirements

Virginia’s tradesman program is administered by DPOR’s Board for Contractors. Regulations include general application requirements and specific experience standards for master examination approval.

**General application requirements** include:

- Being at least **18 years of age**
- Submitting documentation of **identification, vocational training, and experienceMaster examination approval (experience standards):** Virginia regulations state that an applicant for examination to be licensed as a master in a trade must furnish evidence that one of the listed experience standards has been attained. These standards include:

- **Having held a valid journeyman license** in the trade for which licensure is desired for a minimum of **one year**; or
- **Nine years of practical experience** in the trade for which licensure is desired; or
- **Having held a valid residential HVAC tradesman or residential plumber tradesman license** in the trade for which licensure is desired for **three years** (a pathway that applies to those specific residential trade licenses).

**Renewal and continuing education:** Virginia regulations state that electrician licenses expire **three years** from the last day of the month in which they were issued. As a condition of renewal or reinstatement, individuals holding tradesman licenses with the electrical designation must complete **three hours of continuing education** for each designation from a provider approved by the Board. Renewal fees for a tradesman license are listed as **$160**.

- **Save the 2 full final exams for the end.** Treat them like dress rehearsals: timed, distraction-free, and as close to exam conditions as possible.

Open-book exams reward a calm, methodical approach. When you’ve practiced enough, you stop “hoping you’ll find it” and start knowing exactly how you’ll handle each question type.

## How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
