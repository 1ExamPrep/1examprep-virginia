### Exam Details

Virginia tradesman licensing is regulated by the **Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR)** through the **Board for Contractors**. The tradesman examinations are administered by **PSI**.

According to PSI’s Virginia Individual License and Certification Candidate Information Bulletin, the **Master Electrician** portion is:

- **90 items**
- **270 minutes** to complete
- **Passing score:** 63

The same bulletin explains that PSI provides exam scheduling through test centers and also offers a remote proctored option for certain exams (when available under PSI’s process). Your name must match your government-issued ID exactly when you register, and the Board approves applications for testing before PSI schedules the exam.

PSI also lists the **Master Electrician examination fee as $125**. Examination fees are described as non-refundable and non-transferable, and PSI notes the fee is valid for one year from the date of payment.

While the exact question mix varies from form to form, the exam outline structure emphasizes both technical and regulatory knowledge. The Master Electrician exam blueprint includes core “rules and professional standards” areas (such as standards of practice, standards of conduct, renewal and reinstatement, definitions, qualification for licensure, and revocation of licensure), along with broad electrical knowledge areas (including service equipment, grounding and bonding, conductors and wiring methods, overcurrent protection, motors and controls, utilization equipment, and more). Your practice should prepare you to respond confidently across all these areas—not only the technical installations you do every day, but also the licensing and professional responsibility topics that appear on the exam.

### Open Book Test

Yes—Virginia tradesman examinations are **open book**. PSI’s bulletin states that **all examinations are open book** and that you must bring your own references because they are not provided at the test site.

That open-book format changes how you should prepare. Success is not about memorizing entire chapters—it’s about being able to locate and apply the correct rule quickly, especially when you’re under a long time limit and the questions keep coming.

**Key open-book rules and reference handling (from PSI’s bulletin):**

- **No study guides are permitted.**
- **No loose papers** or unbound references are permitted.
- If a reference is downloaded from the internet, it must be **spiral bound** or **hole-punched and placed in a binder**, or **stapled in the left-hand corner**.
- References may be **highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed** prior to the exam.
- **Reference materials containing any writing are not allowed** into the examination.
- You may **not write** in reference materials during the exam session.
- References may be tabbed with **permanent tabs only**; **no removable tabs** are allowed.

**Why that matters for your prep:** the fastest test-takers aren’t the ones who “know everything” from memory. They’re the ones who can recognize what the question is really asking, identify where the rule lives, and confirm the answer efficiently without getting lost in the book.

### Licensing Steps

4. **Receive approval to test.** PSI’s bulletin states the Board approves applications for testing and that notification is sent upon approval.
5. **Schedule your exam with PSI.** Once approved, you register and schedule through PSI using the exam scheduling process.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines “master” in a trade as a person who can plan and lay out installation details and supervise the work, using sufficient working knowledge to comply with the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code. That definition aligns with the Master Electrician scope: it’s not just doing the work—it’s directing it and ensuring compliance.

**General requirements for licensure or certification** in Virginia regulations include items such as:

- Being at least **18 years of age**
- Submitting documentation of **identification, vocational training, and experience**
- Following Board and testing organization rules for examination conduct (failure to comply can be grounds for denial)

**Master eligibility standards (experience):** Virginia regulations state that an applicant for examination to be licensed as a master in a trade must meet *one* of the following experience standards:

- **Held a valid journeyman license** in the trade for a minimum of **one year**; or
- **Nine years of practical experience** in the trade; or
- **Held a valid residential HVAC tradesman or residential plumber tradesman license** in the trade for which licensure is desired for **three years** (this pathway applies to those specific residential trade licenses).

**License term, renewal, and continuing education:** Virginia regulations state that licenses issued to electricians expire **three years** from the last day of the month in which they were issued (as shown on the license). As a condition of renewal or reinstatement, individuals holding tradesman licenses with the trade designation of electrical must satisfactorily complete **three hours of continuing education** for each designation, and that continuing education must be from a provider approved by the Board.

**Fees (selected items from Virginia regulations and PSI):**

- **Original tradesman license by examination:** $150.00 (Virginia regulations)
- **Tradesman renewal fee:** $160.00 (Virginia regulations)
- **Master Electrician exam fee:** $125 (PSI bulletin)

## How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
