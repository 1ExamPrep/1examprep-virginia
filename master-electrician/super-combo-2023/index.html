Exam Details

Virginia’s DPOR tradesmen testing is administered by PSI. For the Virginia Master Electrician exam portion, PSI’s Candidate Information Bulletin lists:

  • Number of items: 90
  • Time allowed: 270 minutes
  • Passing score: 63

The bulletin also provides a topic outline for the Master Electrician portion, reflecting the kind of knowledge tested for Master-level work. The outline includes areas such as services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, motors and controls, special occupancies, special equipment, utilization equipment, lighting, and more—exactly the areas where code navigation and accurate interpretation become critical.

DPOR’s Tradesmen program information also lists the Master Tradesman exam fee as $125 (paid directly to PSI), and notes that the tradesmen exams are offered on a “daily” testing frequency (typically Monday through Friday), with multiple test locations listed for Virginia and nearby regional sites.

Because your exam performance depends heavily on speed and accuracy, the most effective plan is to practice the way you’ll test: timed work, code lookups, and calculations that are set up cleanly and checked for reasonableness.

Open Book Test

Virginia’s DPOR tradesmen exams are open book. PSI’s Candidate Information Bulletin states that all examinations are open book, that you must bring your own references (they are not provided at the test site), and that no study guides are permitted.

The bulletin also allows reference preparation that helps you move quickly while keeping the materials clean and compliant. Key rules include:

  • You must bring your own references (they are not provided at the test site).
  • No study guides are permitted in the exam room.
  • No loose papers or unbound references are permitted.
  • References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed prior to the exam.
  • References must be otherwise unmarked (no writing in the books), and you may not write in them during the session.
  • Permanent tabs are allowed; removable tabs are not allowed.

This is exactly why a tabbed NEC can be a major advantage. Open-book testing rewards candidates who can: (1) recognize what the question is asking, (2) go directly to the correct NEC area, and (3) confirm the rule quickly without second-guessing.

A simple open-book strategy that works well for many Master candidates:

  • First pass: Answer the questions you can solve quickly with direct lookups.
  • Second pass: Work the questions that require longer navigation or calculations.
  • Final check: Review flagged questions, verify code references, and confirm calculation results.

Your tabs help with speed, but your practice is what makes that speed reliable.

Licensing Steps

Virginia’s individual tradesmen electrician licensing is managed by DPOR’s Board for Contractors. The state’s Tradesmen program information explains that pre-approval from the Board is required to sit for the exam, and that once you receive approval notification, you then contact PSI to schedule your exam.

  1. Choose the correct license level.
    Virginia issues both journeyman and master tradesman licenses for electricians. Make sure your application matches the credential you’re seeking.
  2. Meet the qualification standard for Master Electrician.
    Virginia’s regulations provide multiple ways to qualify for Master examination approval (explained in the State Requirements section below).
  3. Submit your application for examination approval.
    DPOR requires approval before you can test. Gather experience verification and any required documentation before you apply.
  4. After approval, schedule your exam with PSI.
    DPOR’s Tradesmen program directs candidates to schedule with PSI once Board approval is issued.
  5. Take the Master Electrician exam under open-book rules.
    Bring permitted references, use permanent tabs, and follow the rules for clean, unmarked materials (no writing).
  6. Maintain your license and stay current.
    Virginia regulates renewal and reinstatement requirements for tradesmen licenses. Staying current keeps your credential active and avoids unnecessary reinstatement steps.

State Requirements

Virginia’s Board for Contractors regulations outline how applicants can qualify for examination approval. These requirements are structured differently for journeyman and master levels, and they provide multiple pathways based on education, formal vocational training, and practical experience.

Master Electrician examination approval (Virginia regulations): An applicant for examination to be licensed as a master in a trade must meet one of these experience standards:

  • Hold a valid journeyman license in the trade for at least one year, or
  • Have nine years of practical experience in the trade.

Journeyman Electrician examination approval (Virginia regulations): Virginia also provides an experience-and-education matrix for journeyman electrician exam approval. Examples include pathways such as:

  • Bachelor’s degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade + one year of practical experience,
  • Associate degree or a certificate from at least a two-year tradesman-related program + two years of practical experience,
  • Formal vocational training (with different hour totals) paired with progressively higher experience requirements,
  • No formal training paired with a higher experience requirement.

Because eligibility and documentation matter, successful applicants typically keep their records organized—experience verification, training documentation, and the details needed for Board review. Once approved, you move into the test-prep phase where the right study system can save you weeks of wasted effort.

FAQ

Is the Virginia Master Electrician exam open book?

Yes. Virginia’s PSI Candidate Information Bulletin states that all examinations are open book. You must bring your own references, and study guides are not permitted.

Are tabs allowed on the Virginia Master Electrician exam?

Yes—per PSI’s bulletin, references may be tabbed with permanent tabs only. Removable tabs are not allowed.

Can I highlight or underline my NEC and references?

Yes. PSI’s bulletin allows references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed prior to the exam, as long as the materials do not contain writing and remain otherwise unmarked.

How many questions are on the Virginia Master Electrician exam portion?

PSI’s Candidate Information Bulletin lists the Master Electrician portion as 90 items.

How much time do I get for the Master Electrician portion?

The PSI bulletin lists 270 minutes to complete the Master Electrician portion.

What score do I need to pass the Master Electrician portion?

PSI’s bulletin lists a passing score of 63 for the Master Electrician portion.

What are the Virginia eligibility pathways for Master Electrician exam approval?

Virginia’s Board for Contractors regulations state that an applicant may qualify for master examination approval by holding a valid journeyman license in the trade for at least one year, or by having nine years of practical experience in the trade.

Does this Super Combo include the NEC book?

Yes. This bundle includes the National Electrical Code (NEC) 2023 paperback with tabs, along with the Virginia Master Electrician Study Guide, Electrician Calculations Study Guide, and Master Electrician Flash Cards.

How should I use the flash cards during prep?

Use them for short, consistent review—definitions, core concepts, and key electrical knowledge. Ten minutes a day can keep your momentum strong between longer study sessions.

How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
