### Exam Details

The Virginia Master Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation and the Board for Contractors. Candidates must satisfy the Board’s qualification requirements and receive approval before scheduling the examination.

The current Virginia Master Electrician examination contains 90 items. Candidates are allowed 270 minutes, or four hours and 30 minutes, to complete the test. A passing score requires 63 correct answers.

The examination covers both Virginia regulatory material and technical electrical subjects. Regulatory topics include standards of practice, standards of conduct, renewal and reinstatement, definitions, qualifications for licensure, and circumstances involving revocation of licensure.

Technical subjects include general electrical knowledge, requirements for electrical installations, electrical and electronic fundamentals, branch circuits, services and feeders, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies, special equipment, motors and controls, utilization equipment, lighting, low-voltage systems, communication systems, fire detection and alarm systems, and safety.

Services and feeders account for eight questions, while grounding and bonding account for another eight. Candidates should be prepared to work with service conductors, service equipment, feeder sizing, grounding electrode systems, equipment grounding conductors, bonding requirements, and associated calculations.

Branch circuits account for seven questions. Preparation should include conductor requirements, overcurrent protection, receptacles, lighting outlets, required branch circuits, loads, GFCI and AFCI concepts, and other branch-circuit provisions.

Conductors and cables account for seven questions, and raceways and boxes account for seven questions. Candidates should understand conductor ampacity, insulation, temperature limitations, adjustment factors, correction factors, cable uses, raceway requirements, box fill, raceway fill, support, and physical protection.

Motors and controls account for eight questions. Candidates should practice motor conductor sizing, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, disconnecting means, controllers, and related calculations.

Electrical-electronic fundamentals account for five questions. Preparation should include voltage, current, resistance, power, Ohm’s Law, Watt’s Law, series and parallel circuits, and electrical relationships used in professional electrical work.

The simulated practice exams included with this package help candidates apply these subjects under exam-style conditions. Practice testing can reveal weaknesses in theory, calculations, code navigation, services, grounding, motors, wiring methods, or time management.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Master Electrician examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own approved references because reference materials are not provided at the testing location.

The current Virginia examination bulletin identifies the NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, 2020 Edition, as the code reference used to develop electrician code questions. The examination also identifies the Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations as a regulatory reference and NFPA 70E as an electrician reference.

The 2023 NEC included with this package is used throughout the course and provides advanced NEC study. Candidates taking the current Virginia examination should also prepare with the 2020 NEC because official code questions are based on that listed edition.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References containing handwritten material are not permitted. Candidates may not write in reference books during the examination session.

Permanent tabs are permitted in approved examination references. Removable tabs are not allowed. The included NEC tabs support navigation practice during preparation, and candidates should make sure any tabs used in the official exam-room reference comply with PSI’s current permanent-tab requirements.

Study guides are not allowed as exam-room references. Loose papers and unbound references are also prohibited. The Master Electrician Study Guide, Calculations Study Guide, flash cards, videos, and simulated exams in this package should therefore be used before test day to build the knowledge needed for the official examination.

An open-book exam still requires substantial preparation. With 90 questions and 270 minutes available, candidates need to recognize the subject of a question quickly and identify the most likely reference section without searching through the entire code book.

Practice should include the NEC table of contents, index, Article 100 definitions, chapter organization, tables, exceptions, cross-references, and equipment-specific articles. Candidates should also become familiar with the organization of Virginia’s Individual License and Certification Regulations.

### Licensing Steps

Begin by reviewing the Virginia Board for Contractors qualifications for examination approval as a Master Electrician. Applicants must be at least 18 years old and satisfy the experience requirements established by Virginia regulation.

One qualification pathway is to hold a valid journeyman license in the electrical trade for at least one year. This provides a direct progression from Virginia Journeyman Electrician to Master Electrician.

Virginia also permits an applicant to qualify for a master examination by documenting nine years of practical experience in the trade. Applicants should prepare complete experience documentation for the pathway they are using.

Complete the Virginia Tradesman Exam and License Application for the Master Electrician designation and submit the required identification, experience verification, and supporting documents to the Board for Contractors.

The Board reviews the application and determines whether the applicant satisfies the qualifications for examination. Candidates approved for testing receive authorization and may proceed with PSI examination registration.

Use the preparation period to develop a structured study routine. Begin with the NEC overview, organize the included 2023 NEC with the supplied tabs, and work through the Master Electrician Study Guide by examination topic.

Complete the Calculations Study Guide with a calculator and written work available. Practice services, feeders, conductor sizing, grounding and bonding, motors, transformers, voltage drop, raceway fill, box fill, and other mathematical subjects.

Use the flash cards for regular active recall. Attend live instructor-led sessions and use recorded videos to revisit difficult electrical concepts. Complete simulated practice exams under a 270-minute time limit to develop realistic pacing.

Prepare the 2020 NEC and other official references required for the current Virginia examination. Make sure the books are properly bound and prepared according to PSI’s highlighting, indexing, and tabbing rules.

After passing the examination and satisfying the remaining Board requirements, the applicant can receive the Virginia Master Electrician license. The individual credential is separate from a Virginia contractor business license.

### State Requirements

Virginia Master Electrician licensing is administered through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation and the Board for Contractors. The license is issued to an individual rather than to a company.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old and provide the identification, experience, and other documentation required by the Board. Examination approval depends on meeting the applicable regulatory qualifications before testing.

For a Master Electrician applicant, the current regulation provides qualification through at least one year holding a valid journeyman license in the electrical trade or through nine years of practical experience in the trade.

Virginia also recognizes certain pathways for individuals licensed or certified by regulatory bodies outside the Commonwealth. Applicants with qualifying out-of-state credentials must follow the applicable Virginia application process before performing work under a Virginia license.

Virginia distinguishes the individual Master Electrician license from contractor licensure. Electrical contractor businesses must meet the Board for Contractors requirements applicable to the company, classification, qualified individual, and contractor license class.

Virginia law requires a master tradesman license as a condition associated with electrical contractor licensure. Electricians planning to operate or qualify an electrical contracting business should therefore understand both the individual tradesman requirements and the separate contractor licensing structure.

Master Electricians must complete continuing education as a condition of renewal or reinstatement. Virginia currently requires three hours of Board-approved electrical continuing education addressing applicable code changes for the electrical trade designation.

Licensed electricians remain responsible for complying with applicable Virginia construction codes, electrical requirements, permits, inspections, safety rules, and local administrative procedures when performing regulated work.

## How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
