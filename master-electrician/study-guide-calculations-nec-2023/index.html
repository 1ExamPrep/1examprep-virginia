Exam Details

Virginia’s tradesman licensing is regulated by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR), Board for Contractors. DPOR requires pre-approval before you can sit for the tradesman exam, and PSI is listed as the exam administrator for tradesmen exams.

For the Master Electrician portion, the PSI Candidate Information Bulletin for Virginia’s Individual License and Certification exams lists the following exam structure:

  • Exam portion: Master Electrician
  • Number of items: 90
  • Time allowed: 270 minutes
  • Passing score: 63
  • Exam fee: $125 (paid directly to PSI)

DPOR also lists tradesmen exam locations and notes that examinations are offered daily (typically Monday through Friday). The DPOR tradesmen page includes test locations such as Charlottesville, Richmond, Roanoke, Vienna, Virginia Beach, and additional out-of-state sites listed for testing availability.

Open Book Test

The Virginia PSI Candidate Information Bulletin states that all examinations are open book. It also explains key reference rules you should prepare around, including that you must bring your own references, no study guides are permitted, and no loose papers or unbound references are permitted. It further notes that references may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam, and that permanent tabs are allowed while removable tabs are not allowed.

Open-book is not a shortcut—it’s a skill test. Your results often come down to how efficiently you can do three things:

  • Identify the question type (definition, installation requirement, protection rule, equipment requirement, calculation, etc.).
  • Get to the right place fast using the index and the NEC’s structure instead of hunting page by page.
  • Verify the fine print (exceptions, conditions, table columns, and notes that change the correct answer).

This is exactly why pairing a Master study guide with a calculations guide and a physical NEC book works so well. You’re training the two skills an open-book exam rewards most: lookup speed and calculation reliability.

Licensing Steps

Virginia DPOR (Board for Contractors) outlines the tradesmen program structure and emphasizes that pre-approval from the Board is required before you can sit for the exam. The PSI bulletin also states that candidates must have satisfactorily completed all education and work experience set by the Board for Contractors prior to applying for the examination, and that an approval notification is sent upon application approval.

In practical terms, the process typically follows this path:

  1. Choose your license level (Master Electrician) and review the current DPOR Tradesmen Program instructions and requirements.
  2. Submit your application to DPOR with the required documentation for education and work experience as required by the Board.
  3. Receive approval to test from the Board (pre-approval is required before scheduling).
  4. Register and pay PSI for the exam fee once you are eligible to schedule.
  5. Take the exam under PSI testing rules (including reference and calculator rules).
  6. Move forward with licensing based on DPOR’s licensing process once you have passed the required exam portion.

This combo supports the stage you control most: preparation. When your study time is structured and realistic, you’re far less likely to lose minutes on test day due to slow lookups, avoidable calculation errors, or uncertainty about where the answer “lives” in the code.

State Requirements

Virginia’s DPOR Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen who engage in the electrical trade, along with other trades. DPOR also notes that individuals may be subject to local ordinances or additional requirements depending on where they work, and it provides the official Tradesmen Program page for applications, exam information, and updates.

For examinations, DPOR states that:

  • Pre-approval is required before you can sit for the tradesman exam.
  • PSI is the exam administrator for tradesmen examinations.
  • Exam fees are paid directly to PSI (DPOR lists Journeyman Tradesman and Master Tradesman fees, including the Master Tradesman fee level).

The PSI bulletin reinforces that candidates must complete the education and work experience set by the Board for Contractors before applying for the examination. Because your eligibility is reviewed before approval to test, it’s smart to organize your documentation early—so your exam timeline doesn’t get delayed by missing information.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Virginia Master Electrician exam rewards a very specific kind of preparation. Reading the NEC is helpful, but it’s not the same as practicing the exam skill: finding the right rule fast, interpreting it correctly, and moving on without second-guessing.

This combo helps you train three high-impact areas:

  • Code navigation speed — using the index well, recognizing where topics typically appear, and locating sections quickly.
  • Calculation confidence — identifying which inputs matter, choosing the correct path, and solving consistently without wasting time.
  • Accuracy habits — reading carefully, catching exceptions, and verifying your selection with the code language and tables.

How to practice NEC navigation (the way open-book exams are actually won):

  • Practice “index first” instead of flipping. On test day, flipping is how time disappears.
  • Build key-term instincts so you can search the index using the words the NEC uses (not just jobsite slang).
  • Train table discipline by slowing down on table questions in practice until you stop making column/condition mistakes.
  • Always check exceptions before locking in an answer. Many questions are designed around the exception.

How to use the calculations guide effectively: The goal is to reduce hesitation. Most missed calculation questions happen because the setup is unclear or the process changes from one problem to the next. The calculations study guide supports a consistent method:

  • Translate the question: what is the final value or selection being requested?
  • Pull the conditions: voltage, phase, distance, continuous load conditions, conductor type, and other constraints.
  • Choose the path: which rule/table/process applies to this type of calculation?
  • Compute cleanly: solve the math with good unit awareness and calculator discipline.
  • Sanity-check: confirm the result makes sense and matches the conditions in the problem.

A weekly study structure that fits a working schedule:

  • 3 sessions/week (NEC navigation): do timed lookups and mixed code questions. Focus on speed without sacrificing accuracy.
  • 2 sessions/week (calculations): drill the same calculation types repeatedly until the setup becomes automatic.
  • 1 session/week (review): redo missed questions only. Track the code areas and calculation types that slow you down.

This approach builds momentum because it’s simple: practice, grade yourself honestly, then focus on what actually needs work. Over time, the NEC becomes easier to navigate, calculations become predictable, and your pace improves naturally.

How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
