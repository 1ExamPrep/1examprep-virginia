Exam Details

Virginia’s Master Electrician examination program is administered through PSI for the Commonwealth of Virginia (DPOR / Board for Contractors). PSI publishes the exam content outline for the Master Electrician portion, including question count, passing score, and time allowed.

For the Master Electrician portion, PSI lists:

  • 90 items
  • Passing score: 63
  • Time allowed: 270 minutes

PSI also provides a topic-by-topic outline so you can see what’s on the exam and how many items appear in each category. That outline shows a balanced focus across trade knowledge, core electrical theory, and Code-based installation requirements. Master topics include:

  • Raceways and boxes

Open Book Test

PSI states that all examinations are open book for Virginia tradesman testing. It also states that you must bring your own references (they are not provided at the test site), and that no study guides are permitted in the exam area.

Open-book success is not about searching harder—it’s about searching smarter. The most effective approach is a repeatable workflow you can rely on under a clock:

  • Step 1: Identify the topic before you touch the book.
    Is the question really about grounding and bonding? A service rule? A conductor requirement? A wiring method detail? Special occupancy rules?
  • Step 2: Choose your “first stop.”
    Before flipping pages, decide where the answer should live—definitions, a specific Code area, or a table.
  • Step 3: Find the governing rule and read carefully.
    Many wrong answers come from stopping at a general rule and missing the condition that changes it.
  • Step 4: Check exceptions, notes, and table conditions.
    Master-level questions frequently hinge on exceptions and table notes.

PSI also publishes key reference rules that matter when you prepare:

  • No loose papers or unbound references are permitted.
  • If a reference is downloaded from the internet, it must be spiral bound, hole-punched and placed in a binder, or stapled as described by PSI.
  • References may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam, but references containing any writing are not allowed.
  • References may be tabbed with permanent tabs only and PSI states that no removable tabs are allowed.

This is exactly why a tabbed Code book is so helpful during preparation. Tabs reduce the time spent flipping so you can focus on what raises your score: correct rule selection, careful reading, and consistent exception/table habits.

Licensing Steps

Virginia’s Master Electrician credential is an individual tradesman license issued through DPOR (Board for Contractors). The process is built around eligibility, application, passing the exam, and license issuance.

  1. Select your trade designation and method of licensure on the Tradesman Exam & License Application (Master Electrical).
  2. Document your qualifying experience pathway using the required experience verification format listed on the application.
  3. Submit your application package and application fee to DPOR as required by the application instructions.
  4. After approval, pay PSI and schedule your exam using PSI’s scheduling process for Virginia tradesman examinations.
  5. Take and pass the Master Electrician exam (90 items, 270 minutes, passing score 63).
  6. Complete any remaining issuance steps required for your Master Electrician license once your exam requirement is satisfied.

State Requirements

Virginia publishes qualification pathways for Master tradesman applicants on the Tradesman Exam & License Application. For Master applicants, the application lists experience-based options including:

  • Nine years of practical experience in the trade, verified by individuals who observed the applicant’s work; or
  • One year of practical experience as a licensed journeyman in the trade.

Virginia’s Tradesman Exam & License Application also includes a reminder that any applicant performing work over $1,000 must contact the Board for Contractors to obtain a Virginia Contractors License. (Tradesman licensing is issued to individuals; contractor licensing is a separate track for businesses.)

Because eligibility is documentation-driven, it’s smart to organize your verification paperwork early so your application package is complete and your exam timeline stays on track.

Test Information and Study Materials

Open-book master exams are performance exams. The goal is to be accurate and efficient at the same time. Most missed questions come from predictable issues—not from never seeing the topic:

  • Searching without a plan and losing minutes
  • Missing an exception that changes the general rule
  • Misreading a table heading, note, or condition
  • Rushing the final choice after finding the correct section

This combo helps you train around those issues with a practical approach that fits real electrician schedules.

1) Build “first stop” instincts

Speed comes from choosing the right destination early. Practice identifying the category before you open the book:

  • Services/feeders/branch circuits: train recognizing whether the question is actually a service requirement, protection question, or conductor selection scenario.
  • Grounding and bonding: slow down and confirm the scenario conditions so you apply the correct rule for the installation described.
  • Raceways and boxes: practice interpreting the wiring method and location details so you go straight to the right rules.
  • Motors and controls: train recognizing the “type” of motor/control question so you don’t waste time searching broadly.
  • Special occupancies/equipment: practice starting in the special rules first when the scenario clearly requires them.

2) Use tabs the right way

Tabs are most valuable when you pair them with discipline. The habit to build is simple:

  • Decide where you’re going before you flip pages.
  • Use tabs to reach the correct area quickly.
  • Find the governing section or table.
  • Read carefully and then check exceptions and notes.

High-impact drill: Work 10–15 practice questions from your study guide. For each question, say (or write down during practice at home) what your first stop will be before you open the NEC. After the set, identify the pattern that slowed you down—wrong first stop, missed exception, table note overlooked, or rushed reading—then drill that weak spot in your next session.

3) Treat tables like a separate subject

Tables can be fast points or easy mistakes. Build a repeatable checklist:

  • Confirm you’re using the correct table for the scenario.
  • Read headings and notes before selecting values.
  • Match the question’s conditions to the table’s assumptions.

This prevents one of the most common master-level errors: choosing a correct-looking number that doesn’t actually apply to the situation described.

4) Build an “exception habit”

Master exams frequently reward the electrician who reads one step deeper. Train the reflex: when you find the rule, immediately look for exceptions and condition-based language (including notes that change how a table is applied).

5) A weekly routine that stays realistic

Consistency beats marathon sessions. A practical weekly rhythm:

  • 2–3 sessions/week: Study guide practice questions + timed NEC navigation drills (short sets, focused on speed and accuracy).
  • 1 session/week: Table-focused practice (questions that force careful note reading).
  • 1 session/week: Mixed review that targets weak areas you identified during timed drills.

This keeps your prep organized and progress-driven—exactly what open-book testing demands.

How does the Master Electrician exam work?

The Master Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
