Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is the technical examination associated with the RBC classification. Candidates preparing for the technical exam should develop a broad understanding of residential building methods, code requirements, construction sequencing, structural systems, plans, specifications, and general building practices.

Residential Building Contractor preparation can include subjects such as layout, excavation, foundations, framing, insulation, ventilation, windows, doors, exterior construction, decks, finishing, masonry, plans and specifications, safety, and jobsite practices. Because the RBC classification covers multiple phases of residential construction, candidates should prepare across the entire building process rather than focusing only on the area in which they have the most field experience.

For candidates seeking Class A or Class B contractor licensing, the business examination requirements are separate from the Residential Building technical examination. Business preparation can involve contractor law, project management, business organization, financial management, contracts, estimating, employment responsibilities, risk management, and Virginia-specific contractor requirements.

The inclusion of both trade and business references makes this rental package useful for candidates preparing for multiple components of the contractor licensing process. The residential books support technical study, while the NASCLA Virginia guide and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code support applicable Class A or Class B business and regulatory preparation.

Open Book Test

Virginia contractor examinations use an open-book test format for applicable technical and business examinations. Candidates should bring the permitted references required for the examination they are taking because reference books are not supplied at the testing center.

Open-book testing requires a combination of subject knowledge and reference-navigation skill. Having the correct books available is important, but candidates also need to know where information is located and which reference is most appropriate for each type of question.

A residential code question may lead directly to the International Residential Code, while a practical construction question involving carpentry, framing, or building methods may be easier to research through Carpentry and Building Construction or the Builders Technical Supplement. Business questions may direct candidates to the applicable NASCLA guide or Virginia regulatory reference.

Some books in this rental package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Highlighting can help draw attention to important material, while permanent tabs can make major chapters or subjects easier to locate. Candidates should still practice using the references repeatedly before examination day so that the tab structure becomes familiar.

Open-book preparation should include timed reference exercises. Select a practice question, identify the subject, decide which book is most appropriate, and locate the supporting information. Repeating this process builds familiarity and helps candidates avoid spending unnecessary time searching through multiple books.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing combines a contractor license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Candidates pursuing Residential Building Contracting must address both the RBC technical qualification and the requirements associated with the contractor class they intend to obtain.

  1. Select the contractor license class. Candidates pursuing this package are typically preparing for Class A or Class B contractor licensing requirements along with the RBC classification.
  2. Select Residential Building Contracting. Residential Building is identified by the abbreviation RBC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable requirements for the RBC classification.
  4. Document qualifying experience. The Qualified Individual must meet the experience requirements associated with the requested contractor class and classification.
  5. Complete the RBC technical examination process. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable examination requirement for Residential Building Contracting.
  6. Complete required contractor pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable pre-license education requirement.
  7. Identify the Designated Employee for Class A or Class B licensing. The Designated Employee is responsible for completing applicable business examination requirements.
  8. Prepare for the Class A or Class B business examination. Use the Virginia business, law, project management, and regulatory references included with this package.
  9. Complete the contractor license application. The business must provide information concerning the license class, classification, Qualified Individual, Responsible Management, Designated Employee when applicable, and other required qualifications.
  10. Submit the completed application through the Virginia contractor licensing process. Licensing is issued after applicable requirements are satisfied and the application is approved.

The RBC technical examination and the Class A or Class B business examination requirements should be treated as separate study tracks. Keeping technical residential construction preparation separate from contractor business study can make a large licensing curriculum easier to organize.

State Requirements

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor classification applies to residential building work involving dwellings and townhouses within the scope established by Virginia contractor regulations. It can include construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, and demolition activities associated with those structures and applicable accessory buildings.

The RBC classification is broad enough to require knowledge across multiple phases of residential construction. A Qualified Individual preparing for this classification should have practical experience relevant to residential building and be prepared to demonstrate technical knowledge through the applicable examination process.

Virginia contractor licensing also separates the technical qualification of the Qualified Individual from the business qualification associated with Class A and Class B licensing. Candidates pursuing either of those license classes should prepare for the additional business examination requirements that apply to the Designated Employee.

Contractor applicants must also satisfy Virginia's applicable pre-license education requirements. Pre-license education is separate from both the RBC trade examination and the Class A or Class B business examination. Candidates should therefore consider the complete licensing process rather than treating any single test as the only requirement.

The Residential Building classification does not itself provide unrestricted authority to perform separately regulated electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting work. Contractor businesses offering those services must have the appropriate authority and qualifications for the work being performed.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong RBC preparation strategy should follow the natural progression of a residential construction project. Begin with layout, site work, excavation, and foundations. Continue through structural framing, exterior construction, insulation, ventilation, finishing, and other phases of residential building.

Foundations and framing deserve careful attention because structural construction is central to residential building work. Candidates should review floor systems, wall framing, roof framing, openings, connections, foundation systems, and applicable residential code provisions.

Exterior construction should include wall assemblies, weather protection, roofing, windows, doors, decks, ramps, and exterior finishes. Candidates should become comfortable moving between practical construction references and the International Residential Code when a precise code requirement is needed.

Plans and specifications should also be incorporated into preparation. Residential contractors regularly work from plans, dimensions, notes, details, schedules, and written project requirements. Candidates should practice interpreting construction documents rather than limiting study to written text.

For Class A or Class B business preparation, establish a separate study schedule for contractor business topics. Use the NASCLA Virginia guide to review subjects such as business management, project management, contracts, financial responsibilities, estimating, employment considerations, and contractor law.

Reference navigation practice should be consistent. When answering a practice question, identify whether it relates to residential code, practical construction, Virginia regulations, or contractor business information. Then choose the appropriate book before beginning the lookup.

When a question is answered incorrectly, locate the supporting information in the appropriate reference and review the surrounding material. This reinforces the concept and teaches candidates where similar information can be found during an open-book examination.

Some book(s) in the package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Candidates should use those features actively during study. Learn what each tab identifies and become familiar with the surrounding content instead of relying only on the highlighted lines.
