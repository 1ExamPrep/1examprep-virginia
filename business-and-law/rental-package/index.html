Exam Details

The Virginia Board for Contractors, within the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, regulates contractor licensing in the Commonwealth. Virginia contractor licenses include both a license class and a classification or specialty. The class establishes the monetary level of contracting work the business may perform, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of work the contractor is authorized to perform.

Commercial Building Contractor, commonly identified as CBC, is a Virginia contractor classification. Candidates pursuing this classification must satisfy the requirements that apply to the business, the Qualified Individual, the selected license class, and the Commercial Building Contractor classification.

The Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination evaluates knowledge across numerous commercial construction subjects. The breadth of the examination is reflected in its reference library. Candidates may need to work with building-code provisions, accessibility standards, OSHA construction safety requirements, concrete practices, reinforcing steel, masonry, carpentry, roofing, gypsum systems, structural steel, steel decking, excavation, and other building-construction topics.

For Class A and Class B contractor applicants, Virginia also has business examination requirements associated with the Designated Employee. The Class B examination consists of the Virginia and General portions. The Class A examination consists of the Virginia, General, and Advanced portions.

The business examination and Commercial Building technical examination serve different functions in the licensing process. Business examination preparation focuses on contractor business, law, project management, and Virginia requirements, while the technical examination focuses on the construction knowledge associated with the Commercial Building Contractor classification.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination is open book. Candidates should prepare not only by reviewing construction concepts but also by learning how to locate information efficiently within the permitted references.

An open-book examination should not be treated as an examination where every answer can simply be searched from scratch. The commercial building reference library is extensive, and effective preparation involves recognizing the subject of a question and knowing which reference is most likely to contain the applicable information.

For example, construction safety questions may require the Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), while accessibility questions may involve ICC A117.1-2017. Questions involving general commercial building requirements can lead candidates to the International Building Code. Concrete, reinforcing steel, masonry, roofing, carpentry, steel deck, gypsum, and excavation subjects each have specialized references within the package.

The books in this package are highlighted and tabbed as part of the special-order preparation. Candidates should become familiar with those navigation aids before test day. Knowing the layout of each book, understanding its table of contents and index, and recognizing commonly tested terminology can make reference lookup more efficient.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing involves several requirements beyond the technical examination. The exact path depends on the license class, classification, business structure, and individuals designated on the contractor application.

  1. Select the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C. Applicants should select the class appropriate for the contracting work the business intends to perform.
  2. Select the Commercial Building Contractor classification. The classification determines the type of contracting work the business is authorized to perform.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on the contractor license. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience and examination requirements.
  4. Complete the Commercial Building technical examination requirement. Candidates pursuing CBC must satisfy the applicable technical examination requirements for the classification.
  5. Identify the Designated Employee for Class A or Class B. Class A and Class B contractor applicants must identify a Designated Employee who satisfies Virginia's applicable requirements.
  6. Complete the applicable Class A/B business examination. Class B uses the Virginia and General portions, while Class A includes Virginia, General, and Advanced portions.
  7. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor license applicants are subject to an eight-hour Board-approved pre-license education requirement.
  8. Complete the Virginia contractor license application. The business must submit the required application information and satisfy the Board's requirements for the requested class and classification.

State Requirements

Virginia licenses contractor businesses through the Board for Contractors. A contractor license identifies both the class of license and the classifications or specialties held by the business. Candidates should therefore distinguish between qualifying for a particular license class and qualifying for the Commercial Building Contractor classification.

For each classification or specialty requested, the business must identify an appropriate Qualified Individual. The Qualified Individual is associated with the technical qualifications for that classification or specialty and must satisfy the applicable Virginia experience and examination requirements.

Class A and Class B applicants must also have a Designated Employee who meets Virginia's requirements. The Designated Employee examination requirement is separate from the technical examination associated with the Commercial Building Contractor classification.

New contractor license applicants must also complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This education requirement is separate from the contractor examinations and addresses basic business information, Virginia regulations, statutes, and requirements relevant to operating a contracting business.

Applicants should treat examination preparation as one part of the complete licensing process. Experience requirements, business information, Qualified Individual requirements, Designated Employee requirements, pre-license education, examination requirements, and the contractor application all play roles in obtaining the appropriate Virginia contractor license.
