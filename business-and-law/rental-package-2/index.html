Exam Details

The Virginia Board for Contractors, within the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, licenses contractor businesses in the Commonwealth. Virginia contractor licenses consist of two parts: the class of license and the classification or specialty. Class A, Class B, and Class C establish different monetary levels of contracting activity, while classifications and specialties establish the types of work the licensed business may perform.

The Commercial Improvement Contracting specialty is associated with technical knowledge involving improvement, repair, alteration, and construction work. The reference library reflects the range of subjects candidates may encounter while preparing, including OSHA construction safety, commercial building codes, residential building codes, carpentry and building construction, concrete materials and practices, masonry, and gypsum construction.

Virginia requires businesses to have a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on the contractor application. The Qualified Individual is the person who meets the applicable experience and qualification requirements for the requested designation. For designations that require an examination, the applicable examination requirement must also be satisfied.

Class A and Class B applicants have an additional business examination requirement involving a Designated Employee. The Class B Designated Employee must successfully complete the Virginia and General portions of the contractor business examination. The Class A Designated Employee must successfully complete the Virginia, General, and Advanced portions.

These business examinations are separate from the specialty examination requirements associated with the Qualified Individual. Candidates preparing for both sides of the licensing process should organize their study accordingly, separating technical construction review from contractor business, law, administrative, and Virginia regulatory preparation.

Open Book Test

Virginia contractor examinations administered through PSI are open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because reference books are not provided at the testing site. This makes familiarity with the reference library an important part of exam preparation.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. Instead, candidates need to combine construction knowledge with the ability to identify which reference addresses a particular question. Searching through an unfamiliar library during the examination can consume valuable time, especially when the references cover several different technical subjects.

For example, a construction safety question may lead to the Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA). A building-code question may require the International Building Code or International Residential Code. Questions concerning concrete, masonry, carpentry, or gypsum construction can require the corresponding technical reference.

Learn the table of contents, index, chapter structure, major terminology, and subject organization of each reference. Repeated reference lookup can help turn the prepared books into practical exam-navigation tools.

Licensing Steps

Preparing for an examination is only one component of becoming a licensed Virginia contractor. Applicants should understand how the business entity, license class, specialty, Qualified Individual, Designated Employee, education requirements, examinations, and contractor application work together.

  1. Determine the appropriate license class. Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C. The class determines the monetary level of contracting work the licensed business may perform.
  2. Select the appropriate classification or specialty. The classification or specialty determines the type of contracting work authorized under the license. Candidates pursuing Commercial Improvement Contracting should select the appropriate specialty on the contractor application.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested. The Qualified Individual must meet Virginia's applicable experience and qualification requirements.
  4. Complete the applicable specialty examination requirement. When an examination is required for the requested designation, the Qualified Individual must satisfy that examination requirement.
  5. Identify a Designated Employee for Class A or Class B. Class A and Class B businesses must declare a Designated Employee who meets Virginia's requirements.
  6. Complete the Class A or Class B business examination. Class B requires the Virginia and General portions. Class A requires the Virginia, General, and Advanced portions.
  7. Complete pre-license education. New Virginia contractor license applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education.
  8. Complete the contractor license application. The business must provide the information and supporting documentation required by the Virginia Board for Contractors for its selected license class and specialty.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses businesses engaged in construction, removal, repair, or improvement of facilities on property owned by others. A Virginia contractor license is therefore a business license rather than simply an individual trade credential.

Every contractor license includes both a class and one or more classifications or specialties. Applicants need to determine which license class matches the level of contracting activity the business intends to perform and which classification or specialty matches the actual work the business plans to offer.

Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on the contractor application. The Qualified Individual must be either a member of Responsible Management or a bona fide full-time employee of the business and must meet the applicable experience and qualification requirements.

Virginia's current Qualified Individual requirements specify minimum experience based on the license class: five years for Class A, three years for Class B, and one year for Class C. The individual must also satisfy the prerequisite applicable to the requested classification or specialty.

Class A and Class B applicants must additionally declare a Designated Employee. The Designated Employee must be a member of Responsible Management or a bona fide full-time employee and must successfully complete the applicable business examinations. Class B requires the General and Virginia portions, while Class A requires the General, Virginia, and Advanced portions.

New contractor applicants must also satisfy Virginia's eight-hour pre-license education requirement. For Class A and Class B applicants, the course must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. The pre-license education course is a separate requirement from the Class A and Class B examinations.
