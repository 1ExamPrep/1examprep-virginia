### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors rather than to the Designated Employee personally. Class A and Class B applicants must satisfy several business, personnel, education, financial, and examination requirements.

1. **Determine the appropriate license class.** Decide whether the business requires a Class A or Class B contractor license based on the contract and annual project-value limits established by Virginia law.
2. **Select the appropriate classification or specialty.** The license class determines project-size limits, while the classification or specialty determines what type of contracting work the business may perform.
3. **Identify the Designated Employee.** Class A and Class B businesses must name a Designated Employee who is at least 18 years old and is either a full-time employee of the business or a member of responsible management.
4. **Complete the required Pre-License Education.** A Designated Employee or member of responsible management for a new contractor business must complete an approved eight-hour business course. This is a separate licensing requirement from the Class A/B examination.
5. **Complete the appropriate business examination.** Class B requires the Virginia and General portions. Class A requires the Virginia, General, and Advanced portions.
6. **Name a Qualified Individual.** The business must have a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested. The Qualified Individual must meet the applicable experience and prerequisite requirements.
7. **Meet the financial requirement.** Class A and Class B firms must provide the required financial verification or satisfy the applicable surety-bond alternative.
8. **Submit the contractor license application.** Complete the DPOR Board for Contractors application and provide the documentation required for the business, Designated Employee, Qualified Individual, and requested classification or specialty.

### State Requirements

Virginia currently defines **Class A contractors** as contractors performing or managing construction, removal, repair, or improvements when a single contract or project is valued at **$150,000 or more**, or when the total value of applicable work performed within a 12-month period is **$1 million or more**.

Virginia currently defines **Class B contractors** as contractors performing or managing construction, removal, repair, or improvements when a single contract or project is valued at **$30,000 or more but less than $150,000**, or when the total value of applicable work performed within a 12-month period is **$250,000 or more but less than $1 million**.

For Class A, the Qualified Individual generally must have a minimum of **five years of experience** in the classification or specialty for which that person is qualifying. For Class B, the Qualified Individual generally must have a minimum of **three years of experience**.

Class A firms must verify net worth or equity of **$45,000** through an accepted financial statement process, or may use the Board's permitted surety-bond alternative. Class B firms must verify net worth or equity of **$15,000 or more**, or may use the permitted surety-bond alternative.

The Designated Employee and Qualified Individual serve different functions. The Designated Employee fulfills the business examination requirement for a Class A or B firm. The Qualified Individual establishes qualification for the particular classification or specialty of work the business wants to perform. One person may satisfy multiple roles when that person meets all applicable requirements.

Virginia also requires a Board-approved eight-hour business course for new contractor license applicants. For Class A and Class B firms, the course must be completed by the Designated Employee or a member of responsible management. The eight-hour Pre-License Education requirement is separate from the Class A/B business examination.
