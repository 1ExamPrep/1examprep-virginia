Open Book Test

The Virginia contractor specialty examination permits candidates to bring approved reference books into the examination. PSI examination rules allow reference books to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before testing. Permanent tabs may be used to organize permitted references, while removable temporary tabs are not allowed. Reference books may not contain unauthorized writing or loose papers.

An open-book examination still requires significant preparation. Having access to the books does not automatically make technical questions easy to answer. Candidates must be able to recognize which reference is most likely to contain the required information and locate the applicable section without spending excessive time searching.

For example, a question involving swimming pool or spa construction requirements may direct the candidate toward the 2021 International Swimming Pool and Spa Code. A question involving general building provisions may require the 2021 International Building Code. Concrete mixture questions can point toward Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures, while specialized shotcrete questions may lead to Guide to Shotcrete.

Reinforcing-bar questions can be approached through Placing Reinforcing Bars, while excavation and sitework concepts can direct candidates toward Pipe and Excavation Contracting. Pool operation and aquatic-system terminology may be addressed through the Pool and Spa Operator Handbook.

During self-study, candidates should practice identifying the strongest keyword in a question before selecting a reference. Once the likely book has been identified, use the index, table of contents, chapter headings, or technical terminology to locate the supporting information. Repeating this process can help transform a collection of large books into a more manageable exam-day resource.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Swimming Pool Construction Contractor technical examination is one component of the overall contractor licensing process. Virginia licenses contractor businesses rather than simply issuing a specialty license based on an individual's exam result.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C. The applicable class depends on the contractor's intended level of contracting activity and the requirements established by the Board.
  2. Select Swimming Pool Construction Contracting. The Virginia specialty abbreviation for Swimming Pool Construction Contracting is POL.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested on the license.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must demonstrate the minimum experience required for the selected contractor license class.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor license applicants must satisfy the Board's pre-license education requirement through the appropriate member of the business.
  6. Complete the specialty examination requirement. Swimming Pool Construction Contracting is among the Virginia specialties that require the Qualified Individual to successfully complete a Board-approved technical examination.
  7. Complete any additional examination requirements that apply. Class A and Class B contractor applicants have additional examination requirements associated with the business's Designated Employee.
  8. Submit the contractor license application. The contractor business must submit the applicable licensing forms and supporting information to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

Passing the technical specialty examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The contractor business must satisfy all licensing requirements associated with its class, specialty, Qualified Individual, Responsible Management, and application before a license can be issued.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses engaged in construction, removal, repair, or improvement work on property owned by others. Virginia contractor licenses consist of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties. The license class determines the permitted level of contracting activity, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of construction work the business is authorized to perform.

Swimming Pool Construction Contracting is identified as POL. The regulatory scope covers construction, repair, improvement, or removal of in-ground swimming pools. Plumbing, electrical, backflow, and HVAC work associated with the project are not included within the POL specialty when those activities require separate trade authority.

Every contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested. Current Virginia contractor application requirements state that the Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management and must have the experience required for the selected license class.

The current minimum experience requirements for a Qualified Individual are one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A. Swimming Pool Construction Contracting is one of the specialties requiring examination pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.

Virginia also requires pre-license education for new contractor license applicants. The Board's contractor information identifies this as a business-focused requirement covering regulations, statutes, and requirements relevant to operating a contracting business. It is separate from technical preparation for the Swimming Pool Construction Contractor examination.

Class A and Class B applicants also have examination requirements associated with the Designated Employee. Candidates should make sure they are preparing for the correct combination of technical specialty and contractor business examinations required for their intended license class.
