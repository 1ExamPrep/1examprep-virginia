Exam Details

The ICC B3 Building Plans Examiner exam is a national certification exam offered by the International Code Council. It is associated with commercial building plan review and evaluates a candidate’s ability to review construction documents for compliance with the International Building Code and related referenced standards.

The ICC B3 exam is listed as an 80-question multiple-choice exam with a 3 1/2-hour time limit. Candidates should expect questions that require code lookup, interpretation, table use, and practical application. The 2021 version of the exam is connected to the 2021 International Building Code, ACI 318-19: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary, and the 2021 ICC Concrete Manual.

Test Information and Study Materials

The best preparation for the ICC B3 Building Plans Examiner exam begins with direct use of the 2021 International Building Code, ACI 318-19: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary, and the 2021 ICC Concrete Manual. Candidates should study from the same references used for exam preparation so the books become familiar before test day.

Start by learning the structure of the International Building Code. Review the table of contents, definitions, chapter titles, key tables, figures, and index. Building plans examiner candidates should understand where to find administration, occupancy classification, types of construction, height and area, fire-resistance-rated construction, fire protection, means of egress, accessibility, interior finishes, structural design, soils and foundations, concrete, masonry, steel, wood, roof assemblies, and special construction provisions.

General administration is a useful starting point. Candidates should understand permits, construction documents, alternative materials and methods, duties of the code official, special inspections, deferred submittals, and general plan review language. These provisions help connect exam questions to the actual role of a building plans examiner.

Occupancy classification and construction type should be studied carefully. Candidates should review occupancy groups, mixed occupancies, accessory occupancies, separated and nonseparated occupancies, types of construction, fire-resistance ratings, allowable height, allowable area, frontage increases, sprinkler increases, and related plan review conditions. These topics are commonly connected to building plan review questions because they influence many other code requirements.

Fire-resistance-rated construction should receive focused review. Candidates should study fire walls, fire barriers, fire partitions, smoke barriers, horizontal assemblies, shaft enclosures, opening protection, penetrations, joints, fireblocking, draftstopping, and rated assembly documentation. Plan review questions may require candidates to determine whether the submitted details provide the required protection.

Means of egress is another major study area. Candidates should review occupant load, number of exits, exit access travel distance, common path of egress travel, dead ends, doors, corridors, stairs, ramps, handrails, guards, exit signs, emergency lighting, exits, and exit discharge. Egress questions often require candidates to use tables and apply requirements to a plan scenario.

Accessibility provisions should be included in regular study. Candidates should review accessible routes, accessible entrances, parking, toilet and bathing rooms, drinking fountains, dwelling and sleeping units where applicable, reach ranges, clearances, and accessible means of egress where applicable. Plan review questions may require candidates to determine whether submitted layouts meet accessibility requirements.

Structural provisions should be reviewed carefully. Candidates should become familiar with general structural design requirements, construction documents, load paths, foundations, concrete, masonry, steel, wood, special inspections, structural observation where applicable, and referenced standards. Building plans examiner questions may not require full engineering design, but candidates should be able to locate and apply code requirements connected to structural plan review.

Concrete preparation should include both ACI 318-19 and the 2021 ICC Concrete Manual. Candidates should review reinforcement, cover, placement, consolidation, curing, formwork, testing, inspection responsibilities, construction joints, embedded items, slab details, columns, beams, walls, and foundations. Concrete questions may require candidates to use the building code, ACI 318-19, or the concrete manual depending on how the question is written.

Soils and foundations should receive focused preparation. Candidates should study foundation design information, bearing, footings, foundation walls, slabs, frost protection where applicable, foundation drainage, concrete and masonry foundation provisions, retaining walls where applicable, and special inspection requirements. Plan review questions may ask whether submitted foundation information is complete or code compliant.

Interior finishes, roof assemblies, public safety, and special construction topics should also be reviewed. Candidates should study finish classification, flame spread and smoke-developed indexes, roof covering classifications, roof drainage, safety glazing, safeguards during construction, special inspection provisions, temporary safeguards, and other safety-related requirements. These areas often appear as lookup questions that reward strong reference familiarity.

Definitions should be reviewed throughout preparation. Many exam questions turn on the exact meaning of a term. When a question uses words related to occupancy, story, fire area, fire separation distance, exit access, exit discharge, rated assembly, special inspection, concrete, reinforcement, approved construction documents, or plan review, definitions may help guide the correct answer.

The included course access helps candidates organize their preparation. Instead of trying to study multiple references without a plan, candidates can work through study topics, review building plan review concepts, practice finding code sections, and build exam readiness through repeated exposure to the material.

Use the rental books actively during study. Practice locating sections by topic, using the indexes, reading surrounding code language, checking table notes, reviewing figures, and following cross-references. The books become more useful when they are part of regular study practice rather than something opened only near exam day.

Timed practice is one of the most important study habits. Since the B3 exam has 80 questions and a 3 1/2-hour time limit, candidates should develop a pacing strategy. Practice answering questions under time pressure, mark longer table-based questions when needed, and return after completing faster questions. This helps build confidence and reduces exam-day stress.
