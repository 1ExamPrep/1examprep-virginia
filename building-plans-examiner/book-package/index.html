Exam Details

The ICC Building Plans Examiner B3 examination contains 80 multiple-choice questions and has a 3.5-hour time limit.

The official examination outline is divided into seven major content areas:

  • General Administration – 5%
  • Building Planning – 21%
  • Footings and Foundations – 8%
  • Floor Construction – 4%
  • Wall Construction and Coverings – 12%
  • Roof/Ceiling Construction – 4%
  • Public Safety and Special Construction – 46%

Public Safety and Special Construction is the largest examination category at 46%. Nearly half of the B3 exam therefore focuses on subjects associated with fire and life safety, means of egress, fire-resistance-rated construction, building separations, special construction, and related plan-review requirements.

Building Planning represents 21% of the examination. Candidates should be comfortable reviewing occupancy classification, construction type, allowable building height and area, mixed occupancies, fire-resistance requirements, building configuration, and other fundamental planning provisions.

Wall Construction and Coverings account for 12%, while Footings and Foundations account for 8%. These areas require candidates to understand structural and construction requirements as they appear on commercial plans.

General Administration, Floor Construction, and Roof/Ceiling Construction make up the remaining portions of the examination. Although these categories carry smaller percentages, they remain part of the official B3 content and should not be omitted from preparation.

Open Book Test

The ICC B3 Building Plans Examiner examination is an open book test. Candidates have three and a half hours to complete 80 multiple-choice questions and must follow current ICC testing rules governing allowable references and materials.

With 210 minutes available for 80 questions, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and thirty-seven seconds per question. Efficient navigation is therefore essential. The books are most useful when candidates already know their general organization and can quickly determine which reference applies to a question.

A question involving occupancy classification, allowable area, construction type, fire-resistance-rated assemblies, means of egress, accessibility coordination, wall assemblies, roof construction, or general building-code administration will usually direct the candidate toward the 2021 International Building Code.

A question involving reinforced concrete design requirements, reinforcement, concrete structural members, anchorage, or other structural concrete provisions may direct the candidate toward ACI 318-19. The 2021 ICC Concrete Manual is useful for understanding concrete quality, field practices, reinforcement placement, inspection-related concepts, and practical application of concrete requirements.

Open-book preparation should include repeated timed searches. Candidates should practice recognizing keywords such as occupancy, construction type, allowable height, allowable area, fire wall, fire barrier, fire partition, shaft enclosure, occupant load, exit access, exit discharge, footing, foundation, concrete cover, reinforcement, floor assembly, wall assembly, and roof construction.

Licensing Steps

The B3 is an ICC certification examination for Building Plans Examiners. It is not a Virginia contractor licensing examination. Candidates seeking employment or authorization to perform building plan review in Virginia should follow the requirements established for the applicable position, locality, code-enforcement agency, or employer.

  2. Identify the required code cycle. This package is designed around the 2021 B3 examination and the 2021 International Building Code.
  3. Obtain the examination references. Use the 2021 IBC, ACI 318-19, and the 2021 ICC Concrete Manual included in this package.

  5. Build IBC navigation skills. Practice locating occupancy, construction type, height and area, fire protection, egress, structural, and special-construction provisions efficiently.
  6. Review structural concrete. Use ACI 318-19 and the ICC Concrete Manual to reinforce concrete plan-review concepts.

  8. Register for the B3 examination. Follow the current ICC examination registration process for the appropriate 2021 code-cycle exam.
  9. Complete applicable Virginia requirements. Meet any additional qualifications established by the employing agency, jurisdiction, or authority for the Building Plans Examiner position.

State Requirements

The ICC B3 examination provides an ICC Building Plans Examiner certification. It does not automatically create a statewide license that independently authorizes a person to perform building plan review throughout Virginia.

Building-code enforcement and employment requirements can involve state and local rules, agency qualifications, job classifications, and employer standards. A Virginia locality or code-enforcement department may use ICC certification as part of its qualification process for plan-review personnel.

Candidates should determine whether the 2021 B3 certification corresponds to the certification or training requirement for the position they are pursuing. The applicable Virginia authority or employer determines any additional qualifications beyond the ICC examination.

The technical knowledge measured by B3 is focused primarily on commercial building plan review. Candidates should therefore expect a broader scope than a residential plans examination, including more complex occupancy, construction-type, allowable-area, fire-protection, egress, structural, and special-construction requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive B3 study plan should follow the official examination weighting. Begin with Public Safety and Special Construction because it represents 46% of the examination. Candidates who devote sufficient preparation time to this category address nearly half of the official content outline.

Means of egress is a major part of public-safety plan review. Candidates should understand occupant loads, required exits, exit access, exit doors, stairways, ramps, corridors, exit passageways, interior exit stairways, travel distance, common path of egress travel, and exit discharge.

Practice identifying the occupancy and occupant load before evaluating egress. Many egress requirements depend on the number of occupants, building use, floor arrangement, or presence of automatic sprinkler protection.

Fire-resistance-rated construction should also receive substantial study time. Review fire walls, fire barriers, fire partitions, smoke barriers, horizontal assemblies, shaft enclosures, penetrations, joints, and opening protectives. Candidates should understand that similar-sounding assemblies can serve different functions and have different requirements.

Fire-protection systems are another important public-safety subject. Plans may include sprinkler systems, fire alarms, standpipes, and other features that affect building design and allowable conditions. Candidates should recognize how these systems interact with other IBC requirements.

Special construction can involve requirements that apply to particular structures, building features, or occupancies. Candidates should learn where specialty topics are located within the IBC and become comfortable moving beyond the most frequently used chapters.

Next, focus on Building Planning, which represents 21% of the exam. Start with occupancy classification. Correctly identifying the occupancy group is fundamental because many later decisions depend on the building's use.

Construction type is equally important. Candidates should understand how Types I through V relate to the fire-resistance characteristics of major building elements and how construction type affects allowable building height and area.

Allowable height and area calculations deserve repeated practice. A plans examiner may need to determine whether a proposed building fits within applicable story, height, and area limitations based on occupancy, construction type, frontage, sprinkler protection, and other conditions.

Mixed-occupancy buildings can require additional analysis. Candidates should understand the difference among occupancy arrangements and how separation requirements or other compliance approaches affect the plan review.

Footings and Foundations represent 8%. Review soil considerations, footing dimensions, foundation systems, concrete requirements, foundation walls, and structural support. Questions may require candidates to coordinate information from the IBC with concrete references.

For structural concrete topics, learn when to move from the IBC into ACI 318-19. ACI 318 contains detailed requirements affecting concrete design and construction that may not be fully reproduced in the IBC.

The 2021 ICC Concrete Manual can help candidates understand the practical meaning of structural concrete provisions. Use it to reinforce subjects involving concrete materials, reinforcement, formwork concepts, placement, consolidation, curing, inspection, and quality.

Wall Construction and Coverings represent 12%. Candidates should review wall systems constructed from concrete, masonry, steel, wood, and other approved materials. Questions may involve structural requirements, fire-resistance, exterior wall provisions, coverings, veneers, and weather protection.

Exterior walls can involve multiple code issues at the same time. A plan-review question might require consideration of structural support, fire separation distance, fire-resistance rating, exterior openings, or materials. Candidates should learn to isolate each code issue within the scenario.

Floor Construction represents 4%. Review floor assemblies, framing, structural support, fire-resistance requirements, openings, and connections to surrounding building elements. Although this category is smaller, floor systems often interact with other structural and fire-safety requirements.

Roof/Ceiling Construction also represents 4%. Study roof assemblies, structural framing, coverings, drainage-related building provisions, fire classifications, and applicable construction requirements.

General Administration represents 5%. Plans examiners should understand construction documents, permit-related information, approvals, code compliance, and administrative responsibilities associated with plan review.

A Building Plans Examiner should be able to determine whether submitted construction documents contain enough information to evaluate compliance. Incomplete or inconsistent plans can prevent a meaningful review, even if individual details appear correct.

The IBC should be studied as an interconnected document rather than as a collection of unrelated chapters. An occupancy decision can affect allowable area. Construction type can affect required fire resistance. Sprinkler protection can influence height, area, and egress. Means of egress depends on occupant load and occupancy. Structural systems must support the building configuration shown on the plans.

Timed practice is important. With 80 questions and 210 minutes, spending too much time on a single detailed lookup can create problems later in the exam. Candidates should answer familiar questions efficiently and mark more difficult questions for additional review when the testing format permits.

Use the references actively during preparation. Practice locating occupancy tables, construction-type information, height and area provisions, fire-resistance chapters, egress requirements, structural chapters, foundation provisions, and concrete requirements until the organization becomes familiar.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The problem may be an incorrect occupancy classification, the wrong construction type, confusion between rated assemblies, a missed exception, an error in an allowable-area calculation, or difficulty navigating ACI 318.

A strong B3 preparation routine combines IBC knowledge, structural concrete familiarity, commercial plan-review reasoning, reference navigation, and timed practice. The objective is to understand how individual code requirements work together within a complete commercial building design.

How does the Building Plans Examiner (B3) exam work?

The Building Plans Examiner (B3) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
