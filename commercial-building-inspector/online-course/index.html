Exam Details

The ICC Commercial Building Inspector B2 exam is a multiple-choice certification exam focused on commercial building inspection. The exam is administered as an open-book exam and is commonly structured with 80 questions and a 3.5-hour time limit. Candidates should be prepared to use the 2021 International Building Code and related listed references to answer inspection-based questions accurately and efficiently.

The exam content includes General Administration, Building Planning, Footings and Foundations, Floor Construction, Wall Construction and Coverings, Roof/Ceiling Construction, and Public Safety and Special Construction. These categories reflect the practical job tasks of a commercial building inspector. Students should be ready to inspect commercial construction for compliance with approved plans, applicable code sections, referenced standards, manufacturer installation instructions when applicable, and required construction details.

General Administration includes scope, permits, inspections, construction documents, approval requirements, code official authority, required notices, certificates, unsafe structures, and general enforcement responsibilities. Students should understand how inspection duties fit into the code administration process and how the International Building Code establishes the authority used for commercial building inspection work.

Building Planning includes occupancy classification, mixed occupancies, building height and area, types of construction, fire-resistance ratings, fire protection systems, interior finishes, accessibility, means of egress, interior environment, safeguards during construction, and related commercial building provisions. This area is important because commercial buildings vary widely by occupancy, size, construction type, risk level, and use. A commercial building inspector must be able to identify which code requirements apply to the specific condition being inspected.

Footings and Foundations includes soils, excavation, footings, foundation walls, retaining walls, slabs, pier foundations, concrete placement, reinforcement, anchor bolts, drainage, dampproofing, waterproofing, and foundation-related structural provisions. Students should be comfortable reviewing foundation construction against code requirements, approved plans, special inspection requirements, and applicable concrete standards.

Floor Construction includes floor framing, structural materials, fire-resistance-rated floor assemblies, openings, penetrations, firestopping, concrete floor systems, steel floor systems, wood floor systems, inspection of structural members, load path considerations, and construction details shown on approved plans. Candidates should understand how to use the code and referenced standards to evaluate floor systems during commercial inspection.

Wall Construction and Coverings includes exterior walls, fire walls, fire barriers, fire partitions, smoke barriers, shaft enclosures, wall coverings, fireblocking, draftstopping, structural wall systems, concrete walls, masonry walls, wood walls, steel framing, glazing, weather protection, and related wall inspection requirements. This area often requires careful use of fire-resistance tables, construction type rules, opening protection requirements, and material-specific provisions.

Roof/Ceiling Construction includes roof assemblies, rooftop structures, roof coverings, roof insulation, fire-resistance-rated ceiling assemblies, structural roof members, roof drainage, attic spaces when applicable, roof access, equipment support, and related inspection items. Students should be ready to identify roof construction requirements and apply the proper code provisions to commercial roof and ceiling conditions.

Public Safety and Special Construction includes means of egress, guards, handrails, ramps, stairways, accessibility, special detailed requirements, high-rise provisions when applicable, atriums, stages, special occupancies, construction safeguards, fire protection features, and other safety-related commercial building topics. These subjects require careful reading because the correct answer may depend on occupancy, occupant load, construction type, building height, fire protection system status, or a specific exception.

Open Book Test

The ICC B2 Commercial Building Inspector exam is an open-book exam. The references for this product are the 2021 International Building Code, ACI 318-19: Building Code Requirements for Structural Concrete and Commentary, and the 2021 ICC Concrete Manual. Students should prepare carefully because open book does not mean the answers will be easy to find without practice. A strong open-book strategy begins with knowing the layout of the 2021 International Building Code. Because ACI 318-19 and the 2021 ICC Concrete Manual are included references for this product, students should also be prepared for concrete-related inspection and structural topics. Commercial building inspection questions may require understanding concrete placement, reinforcement, cover, forms, slabs, foundations, structural concrete requirements, inspection responsibilities, and how code requirements connect to referenced standards.

Commercial building inspection questions often require exact reading. A question may ask for a minimum dimension, a maximum travel distance, a required fire-resistance rating, an occupancy-related limitation, an accessibility requirement, a concrete inspection condition, or a construction detail that changes how the code applies. Students should read each question carefully and avoid answering from general construction experience alone. The correct answer must be based on the approved references used for the exam.

How does the Commercial Building Inspector (B2) exam work?

The Commercial Building Inspector (B2) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
