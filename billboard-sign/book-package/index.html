Exam Details

The Virginia Billboard/Sign Contracting examination is administered through PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors. The technical specialty examination contains 30 questions and allows 75 minutes for completion.

The minimum passing score is 70%, or 21 correct answers.

The official examination content outline is divided into three subject areas:

  • Billboard/Sign Materials – 10 questions
  • Billboard/Sign Erection – 14 questions
  • Safety – 6 questions

Billboard/Sign Erection is the largest examination category with 14 questions. Candidates should devote substantial preparation time to the structural principles, installation methods, supports, foundations, loads, and construction considerations associated with erecting permanent billboard and sign structures.

Billboard/Sign Materials accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should become familiar with the materials and structural components used in sign construction and understand how material characteristics affect design, fabrication, support, and installation.

Safety accounts for 6 questions. Billboard and sign work can expose workers to elevated work, lifting operations, structural hazards, construction equipment, electrical hazards, and other jobsite risks. OSHA construction regulations are therefore an important part of the examination reference set.

The examination is technical in nature and is intended to measure the minimum knowledge expected of the Qualified Individual associated with the BSC specialty. Effective preparation should combine sign-structure knowledge with code navigation and construction-safety review.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Billboard/Sign Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring approved reference books into the examination and must follow current PSI rules governing reference preparation and use.

PSI examination rules allow reference books to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. References must otherwise remain unmarked, and approved permanent tabs may be used. Candidates should prepare their books before test day so they can locate important topics efficiently.

With 30 questions and 75 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. That makes reference navigation an important skill. The ability to identify which book is most likely to contain the answer can save valuable time.

A question involving worker protection, fall hazards, scaffolding, ladders, equipment, or other construction-safety topics should direct attention toward 29 CFR Part 1926. A question involving structural forces, foundations, support systems, or engineering concepts should point toward Engineering Sign Structures. A question involving sign-code requirements should direct the candidate toward the Uniform Sign Code, 1997.

Open-book preparation works best when candidates learn the references before the examination. Simply having the books available does not replace familiarity with their organization. Practice using indexes, chapters, headings, figures, and tables so the materials become efficient tools during the test.

A business seeking this specialty must satisfy the requirements associated with the contractor license class being pursued and designate a Qualified Individual for the specialty.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Billboard/Sign Contracting as the service that provides for the installation, repair, improvement, or dismantling of any billboard or structural sign permanently annexed to real property.

The Virginia specialty definition also states that contractors holding the Highway/Heavy classification and Commercial Building classification can perform this type of work. A contractor holding the BSC specialty may connect or disconnect signs to existing electrical circuits, but the specialty does not include general trade-related plumbing, electrical, or HVAC work.

The BSC specialty requires a technical examination for the Qualified Individual. Passing the Billboard/Sign Contracting exam is part of the contractor licensing process rather than a stand-alone authorization to operate independently of Virginia's licensing requirements.

Each contractor business must meet the requirements associated with its selected license class. The Qualified Individual is responsible for demonstrating the applicable experience and technical qualification for the BSC specialty.

Virginia also requires pre-license education for new contractor licenses. The contractor licensing process can involve requirements applying to the business entity, Responsible Management, Designated Employee, and Qualified Individual depending on the license class being requested.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should follow the examination weighting. Start with Billboard/Sign Erection because this category accounts for 14 of the 30 scored questions. Then devote focused review to Billboard/Sign Materials and OSHA Safety.

For Billboard/Sign Erection, use Engineering Sign Structures as a primary study resource. Billboard structures must resist forces imposed by their own weight, sign components, wind, and other applicable loads. Candidates should develop familiarity with the engineering terminology used to describe these forces.

Wind is especially important in sign construction because billboards and large sign faces can present substantial surface area to moving air. Candidates should understand the basic relationship between wind forces and the structural members that transfer those forces into supports and foundations.

Study the concept of a structural load path. Loads acting on the sign face must be transferred through framing and connections into columns, supports, and ultimately the foundation and soil. Thinking about the complete load path can help candidates understand why individual structural components are designed and installed in particular ways.

Foundations should receive dedicated preparation. A billboard or structural sign depends on its foundation to transfer loads safely into the ground. Candidates should review foundation concepts, support conditions, overturning resistance, and the structural relationship between the sign and supporting soil.

Connections are another important part of sign erection. Structural members must be joined in a way that transfers loads through the structure. Candidates should become familiar with the basic roles of bolts, welds, fasteners, plates, and other connection components discussed in the reference material.

Sign erection also involves construction sequencing. Large structural components may need to be lifted, positioned, aligned, temporarily supported, and permanently secured. Safe erection requires planning and an understanding of how the structure behaves before all permanent components are in place.

For Billboard/Sign Materials, review the structural characteristics and uses of materials commonly associated with sign structures. The Engineering Sign Structures reference can help candidates understand the role of different materials within frames, supports, foundations, and connections.

Material questions may require candidates to recognize how properties such as strength, stiffness, durability, corrosion resistance, or weight affect a sign structure. Focus on understanding why a material is used rather than trying to memorize isolated facts.

The Uniform Sign Code, 1997 should be studied as a code-navigation reference. Become familiar with its organization and the provisions addressing sign construction and installation.

Code questions can require candidates to distinguish among different sign types or identify requirements that apply to a particular structural configuration. Learn how the Uniform Sign Code organizes definitions, structural provisions, installation requirements, and other sign-related rules.

When practicing with the Uniform Sign Code, read the complete provision surrounding an answer. Definitions and exceptions can change how a requirement applies, so candidates should avoid relying on a single isolated sentence without understanding its context.

For Safety, study the construction regulations contained in 29 CFR Part 1926. Billboard installation can involve elevated work, structural steel components, ladders, aerial access, lifting equipment, tools, electrical exposure, and movement of heavy materials.

Fall protection deserves substantial attention. Sign workers may perform work at significant heights while installing, maintaining, repairing, or dismantling structures. Candidates should understand the organization of OSHA requirements affecting elevated construction activities.

Ladders and scaffolds should also be part of safety preparation. Review how access equipment is selected, used, and maintained and become familiar with the OSHA sections addressing these systems.

Personal protective equipment is another fundamental safety subject. Depending on the task, workers may require head, eye, hand, foot, hearing, or fall protection. Learn how OSHA organizes the standards affecting worker protection.

Material handling and lifting operations can be especially relevant to billboard erection. Structural sign components can be large and heavy, requiring equipment and coordinated movement. Candidates should recognize safety concerns associated with suspended loads, equipment operation, and employee exposure.

Electrical hazards can also occur around signs and nearby electrical systems. Virginia's BSC specialty permits a Billboard/Sign contractor to connect or disconnect signs to existing electrical circuits within the defined specialty scope, making awareness of electrical hazards important even though the specialty does not authorize general electrical contracting.

A useful open-book preparation technique is to associate each type of question with a primary reference. Structural design, loads, foundations, and supports should point toward Engineering Sign Structures. Sign-code provisions should point toward the Uniform Sign Code. Construction safety should point toward OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926.

Use permanent tabs strategically within the limits of PSI's reference rules. Tabs work best when they identify major subjects rather than every individual page. A manageable set of clearly labeled sections can help candidates move to the correct area quickly.

Practice with a timer. The 75-minute examination period provides approximately two and a half minutes per question. Some questions may be answered quickly from technical knowledge, leaving additional time for detailed structural or code lookups.

When a practice question is missed, determine the underlying reason. The issue may be weak structural knowledge, unfamiliar terminology, failure to recognize a safety requirement, or difficulty navigating the appropriate reference. Correct the cause of the mistake rather than simply memorizing the answer.

A strong preparation routine combines structural understanding, sign-code knowledge, construction safety, and repeated reference navigation. The objective is to make the three-book package familiar enough that the candidate can quickly identify which reference is likely to contain the required information.
