Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman HVAC examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring qualifying references into the examination subject to current PSI rules governing reference materials.

Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination when prepared in accordance with testing rules. Permanent tabs may be used for reference navigation, while removable tabs are not permitted. Candidates should also follow PSI requirements regarding handwritten notes and other markings in examination references.

The open-book format makes familiarity with the books an important preparation skill. Candidates receive 210 minutes for 85 questions, so spending several minutes searching through every reference for each question can make time management difficult.

A highlighted and tabbed reference package can support a more organized study routine by making major subjects easier to locate. Candidates should still spend time using the books before exam day. Tabs are most useful when the candidate knows what each tab represents and understands which reference is most appropriate for the question.

Fuel-oil questions may require NFPA 31. Broader HVAC technical concepts may be supported by Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning. Virginia licensing and regulatory questions may direct the candidate to the Individual License and Certification Regulations.

Practicing this type of reference selection can make open-book preparation more efficient and help candidates develop a repeatable strategy for locating information.

Licensing Steps

Virginia Journeyman HVAC licensing is an individual tradesman licensing process. Applicants must satisfy the applicable Virginia Board for Contractors requirements before receiving examination approval.

  1. Determine that Journeyman HVAC is the appropriate license. Virginia regulates HVAC as an individual tradesman designation in addition to separate contractor business licensing requirements.
  2. Review the qualification pathways. Virginia recognizes combinations of practical HVAC experience, formal vocational training, related education, apprenticeship completion, and other qualifying routes.
  3. Document practical trade experience. Applicants must provide the required evidence of HVAC experience for the qualification pathway being used.
  4. Document education or training when applicable. Candidates using formal vocational training, technical education, or related college education must provide supporting documentation.
  5. Submit the applicable tradesman application. The Virginia Board for Contractors reviews the applicant’s qualifications.
  6. Receive examination approval. Pre-approval from the Board is required before the candidate may sit for the examination.
  7. Prepare for the Journeyman HVAC exam. Study both technical HVAC topics and Virginia regulatory requirements using the applicable references.
  8. Schedule the examination with PSI. After Board approval, candidates follow PSI procedures for examination registration and scheduling.
  9. Complete the Journeyman HVAC examination. Candidates must meet the applicable Journeyman passing requirement.
  10. Complete the licensing process. Licensure is handled through the Virginia Board for Contractors after the applicable requirements have been met.

State Requirements

Virginia provides multiple pathways for applicants seeking Journeyman HVAC licensure. One pathway allows applicants with four years of practical experience and 240 hours of formal vocational training in the trade to qualify.

Other pathways include five years of practical experience with 160 hours of formal vocational training, six years of practical experience with 80 hours of formal vocational training, or seven years of practical experience with 40 hours of formal vocational training.

Candidates with qualifying postsecondary education may use different combinations. An applicant with an associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year tradesman-related program may qualify with two years of practical experience in the trade.

An applicant with a bachelor’s degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade may qualify with one year of practical experience. Virginia also provides an experience-based pathway for applicants with eight years of practical experience when the required experience documentation is provided.

Approved apprenticeship completion may also affect examination requirements under Virginia regulations. Applicants should use the qualification path that accurately reflects their training, education, apprenticeship, and work experience.

Once licensed, Virginia Journeyman HVAC tradesmen are subject to continuing education requirements for renewal. HVAC tradesmen must complete three hours of Board-approved HVAC continuing education as required for license renewal. The education must relate to the applicable mechanical code and must be completed through a Board-approved provider.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Virginia Journeyman HVAC preparation plan should combine trade knowledge with active use of the references. Since the examination covers more than 20 content categories, breaking the material into smaller subject groups can make studying easier to manage.

Begin with warm-air heating, ventilation, and air conditioning. Together, these subjects represent a significant portion of the exam. Review equipment, airflow, system operation, ventilation requirements, air distribution, heating principles, cooling principles, and applicable mechanical requirements.

Next, focus on duct systems. Review duct materials, construction, supports, installation, insulation, clearances, and air-distribution concepts. Use the International Mechanical Code during study so you become familiar with the chapter structure and the location of duct-related provisions.

Chimneys, flues, vents, and combustion air should be studied together. Candidates should understand how fuel-burning appliances receive combustion air and how combustion products are vented safely. Review clearances, venting methods, termination requirements, and related mechanical provisions.

Boilers and hot-water piping are additional examination areas. Preparation should include hydronic heating fundamentals, boiler components, piping arrangements, controls, and applicable installation requirements.

Controls and low-voltage systems may involve thermostats, relays, transformers, switches, motors, control circuits, and HVAC operating sequences. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning can support broader understanding of how these components interact with heating and cooling equipment.

For fuel-oil piping and storage, spend time working directly with NFPA 31. Learn where provisions covering tanks, piping, equipment, protection, and installation are located. Tabbed reference sections can help candidates return to frequently reviewed subjects more efficiently.

Refrigeration preparation should include refrigerants, system components, refrigeration cycles, refrigerant piping, chilled water, condenser water, and mechanical refrigeration requirements. Understanding the basic refrigeration cycle can provide a foundation for many air-conditioning and refrigeration questions.

Commercial kitchen ventilation is another dedicated test area. Candidates should review hood systems, exhaust ducts, makeup air, clearances, and related mechanical requirements.

Smaller subjects such as insulation, firestopping, and penetration inspection should also remain part of the preparation schedule. Every content area can contribute questions toward the final result.

Use the highlighted and tabbed books while answering practice-oriented questions. First identify the subject. Then choose the reference most likely to contain the information. Use the tabs and index to locate the appropriate section, read the surrounding requirement, and confirm the answer.

This method helps candidates learn the technical material while developing the reference-navigation skills needed for an open-book testing environment.

How does the Journeyman HVAC exam work?

The Journeyman HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
