Exam Details

The Virginia Journeyman HVAC examination is administered by PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates must meet the applicable Board qualification requirements and receive approval before scheduling the examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman HVAC examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring qualifying reference materials into the examination when those materials meet current PSI requirements.

Open-book testing makes reference navigation an important part of preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of each book rather than expecting to search every reference from beginning to end during the exam.

With 210 minutes available for 85 questions, candidates have an average of slightly less than two and a half minutes per item. Efficient reference use can help preserve testing time for questions involving calculations, technical interpretation, or careful review.

During self-study, candidates should practice identifying the subject before reaching for a reference. Mechanical-code questions involving ventilation, ductwork, boilers, combustion air, refrigeration, or exhaust systems may point toward the International Mechanical Code. Questions involving oil-burning equipment or fuel-oil installations may require NFPA 31. Broader HVAC technical concepts can be studied using Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Virginia licensing and regulatory questions should be reviewed through the Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations. Familiarity with which book addresses which topic can make open-book preparation more organized.

Licensing Steps

Virginia Journeyman HVAC licensing is an individual tradesman licensing process administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates must satisfy the applicable eligibility requirements before receiving examination approval.

  1. Select the Journeyman HVAC credential. Confirm that Journeyman Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning is the appropriate Virginia tradesman designation for your licensing goal.
  2. Review the qualifying pathways. Virginia recognizes different combinations of practical HVAC experience, formal vocational training, related education, apprenticeship completion, and other qualifying credentials.
  3. Document practical experience. Applicants must provide evidence supporting the HVAC experience required for the qualification pathway being used.
  4. Document training or education when applicable. Candidates using formal vocational training, technical education, apprenticeship, or related college education must provide the required supporting documentation.
  5. Submit the applicable application. The Virginia Board for Contractors reviews the applicant’s qualifications and supporting information.
  6. Receive examination approval. Board approval is required before a candidate may schedule the Journeyman HVAC examination.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Study HVAC trade knowledge, Virginia regulations, and the applicable references.
  8. Schedule the examination with PSI. Once approved, candidates follow PSI procedures for scheduling and testing.
  9. Complete the examination. Candidates must satisfy the Journeyman passing requirement.
  10. Complete the remaining licensing process. The Virginia Board for Contractors handles license issuance after the applicable licensing requirements have been met.

State Requirements

Virginia provides multiple qualification pathways for applicants seeking Journeyman HVAC licensure. These pathways use different combinations of practical trade experience and formal education or training.

One pathway includes four years of practical experience combined with 240 hours of formal vocational training. Additional pathways recognize five years of practical experience with 160 hours of formal vocational training, six years of practical experience with 80 hours of formal vocational training, or seven years of practical experience with 40 hours of formal vocational training.

Candidates with qualifying postsecondary education may use other pathways. An applicant with a related associate degree or certificate from an appropriate tradesman-related program may qualify with the applicable practical experience. A candidate with qualifying engineering-related education may also have a pathway using a shorter practical-experience requirement.

Virginia regulations also recognize approved apprenticeship completion and other qualifying circumstances. Candidates should apply using the pathway that accurately reflects their education, training, apprenticeship, and practical HVAC experience.

Licensed Virginia Journeyman HVAC tradesmen are also subject to continuing education requirements for renewal. HVAC tradesmen must complete three hours of Board-approved continuing education as required for license renewal. The continuing education must address material related to the applicable mechanical code.

Test Information and Study Materials

A structured preparation plan can make a broad HVAC examination easier to approach. The Live Virtual + Self Study Training format gives candidates two complementary ways to work through the material: scheduled instructor-led review and independent study between training sessions.

Warm-air heating preparation should include equipment, heating principles, system operation, air distribution, controls, combustion, and installation concepts. Candidates should understand both how systems operate and how applicable mechanical requirements affect installation.

Ventilation and air conditioning preparation should include airflow, equipment, cooling principles, mechanical ventilation, air distribution, exhaust, system operation, and applicable code provisions.

Duct systems should be reviewed for materials, supports, installation, construction, insulation, clearances, and air-distribution requirements. Candidates should practice locating duct provisions in the International Mechanical Code instead of relying solely on memorization.

Chimneys, flues, vents, and combustion air are closely related to the operation of fuel-burning equipment. Study venting methods, combustion-air requirements, clearances, terminations, and other relevant mechanical provisions.

Boilers and hot-water piping require knowledge of hydronic systems, equipment, piping arrangements, system components, controls, and applicable installation requirements.

Controls and low voltage can include thermostats, relays, transformers, switches, motors, control circuits, and equipment operating sequences. Understanding how these components interact can help candidates answer questions that test practical HVAC system knowledge.

Fuel-oil systems should be studied using NFPA 31. Candidates should learn where requirements involving piping, tanks, storage, equipment, protection, and installation are located within the standard.

Refrigeration study should include refrigeration cycles, refrigerants, equipment, system components, refrigerant piping, chilled water, condenser water, operating principles, and troubleshooting fundamentals. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides broad technical coverage for these subjects.

Commercial kitchen ventilation is another dedicated exam topic. Candidates should review hoods, exhaust ducts, makeup air, clearances, and applicable mechanical requirements.

Smaller categories such as insulation, firestopping, and penetration inspection should remain part of the study schedule. A comprehensive preparation plan should cover every published content area rather than focusing exclusively on the largest categories.

The self-study portion allows candidates to reinforce what is covered during live sessions. After reviewing a topic in class, candidates can return to the reference books, find the applicable section, work through practice-oriented material, and identify areas that need additional attention.

How does the Journeyman HVAC exam work?

The Journeyman HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
