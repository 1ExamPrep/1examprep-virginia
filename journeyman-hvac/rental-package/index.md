### State Requirements

Virginia provides several ways for an applicant to satisfy the education and experience requirements associated with Journeyman HVAC examination eligibility.

An applicant with a bachelor’s degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade may qualify with one year of practical HVAC experience. An applicant with an associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year tradesman-related program may qualify with two years of practical experience.

Virginia also recognizes formal vocational-training pathways. An applicant with 240 hours of formal vocational training may qualify with four years of practical experience. An applicant with 160 hours of vocational training may qualify with five years of practical experience.

An applicant with 80 hours of formal vocational training may qualify with six years of practical experience, while an applicant with 40 hours of formal vocational training may qualify with seven years of practical experience.

Applicants must provide documentation supporting the particular education, training, and experience combination used to qualify. The Virginia Board for Contractors reviews these materials before issuing examination approval.

Virginia Journeyman and Master HVAC tradesmen are also subject to continuing education requirements for license renewal. HVAC licensees must complete three hours of Board-approved HVAC continuing education during the applicable license term. Continuing education must relate to the current mechanical code and be completed through an approved provider.

Review system components, operating principles, equipment, airflow, ventilation requirements, air distribution, installation requirements, and mechanical-code provisions connected with these subjects.

Duct systems should receive focused study as well. Become familiar with duct materials, installation, supports, insulation, clearances, air-distribution principles, and the sections of the International Mechanical Code that govern duct construction and use.

Chimneys, flues, vents, and combustion air should be studied together because they are closely connected with safe operation of fuel-fired equipment. Review venting methods, termination requirements, combustion-air provisions, clearances, and related mechanical requirements.

## How does the Journeyman HVAC exam work?

The Journeyman HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
