Exam Details

The Virginia Journeyman HVAC examination is administered by PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates must meet the Board’s applicable qualification requirements and receive approval before scheduling the examination.

The current Journeyman/Master HVAC examination portion contains 85 items. A Journeyman candidate must answer 60 questions correctly to meet the Journeyman passing requirement. Candidates receive 210 minutes to complete the examination.

The exam covers both Virginia regulatory topics and technical HVAC knowledge. The current content areas include:

  • Standards of Practice: 3 items.
  • Standards of Conduct: 2 items.
  • Renewal and Reinstatement: 2 items.
  • Definitions: 1 item.
  • Qualification for Licensure: 1 item.

The technical portion covers a wide range of mechanical systems. Candidates should be prepared to move between heating, cooling, ventilation, refrigeration, piping, ductwork, combustion, controls, and fuel-oil topics rather than concentrating on only one part of the HVAC trade.

The Virginia regulatory portion is also important. Questions may address standards of professional practice, conduct, renewal, reinstatement, license qualifications, definitions, and disciplinary provisions. A balanced study plan should include time for both technical and regulatory preparation.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman HVAC examination is an open book test. Candidates may bring qualifying reference materials into the examination in accordance with PSI rules.

Open-book testing rewards familiarity with the code books. Candidates who know where major chapters, tables, definitions, and technical requirements are located can use the references more efficiently than someone opening them for the first time on test day.

Some book(s) in this rental package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. These preparation features can make major sections easier to identify while studying. With 210 minutes available for 85 questions, candidates have an average of slightly under two and a half minutes per question. That makes time management important. Looking through several references for every answer can quickly consume the available testing time.

A productive strategy is to identify the topic first. A question involving ventilation, boilers, duct systems, mechanical refrigeration, or combustion air may direct you toward the International Mechanical Code. Fuel-oil questions may require NFPA 31. Broader HVAC theory, refrigeration principles, service procedures, or system-operation topics may be supported by Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Virginia regulatory questions generally point toward the Individual License and Certification Regulations. Learning these distinctions before test day can help reduce unnecessary searching.

Licensing Steps

Virginia Journeyman HVAC licensure is part of the Commonwealth’s individual tradesman licensing program. Applicants must satisfy the qualification requirements established by the Virginia Board for Contractors before receiving examination approval.

  1. Choose the Journeyman HVAC license. Confirm that the Virginia Journeyman HVAC credential matches the level and trade in which you are seeking individual licensure.
  2. Review the qualification pathways. Virginia recognizes different combinations of education, formal vocational training, and practical HVAC experience.
  3. Document education or training. Candidates using a degree, certificate, or formal vocational-training pathway must provide the documentation required by the Board.
  4. Document practical HVAC experience. The required amount of practical experience depends on the education or training pathway used.
  5. Submit the required application materials. Applicants apply through the Virginia Board for Contractors for examination approval and licensure.
  6. Receive examination authorization. Candidates must receive Board approval before scheduling the Journeyman HVAC examination with PSI.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Study the technical HVAC subjects, Virginia regulations, and applicable reference materials.
  8. Schedule the exam with PSI. After approval, follow the testing provider’s procedures for scheduling the examination.
  9. Complete the Journeyman HVAC examination. Candidates must meet the required Journeyman passing score.
  10. Complete the remaining licensing requirements. Final licensure is handled through the Virginia Board for Contractors after the applicable requirements have been satisfied.

State Requirements

Virginia provides several ways for an applicant to satisfy the education and experience requirements associated with Journeyman HVAC examination eligibility.

An applicant with a bachelor’s degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade may qualify with one year of practical HVAC experience. An applicant with an associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year tradesman-related program may qualify with two years of practical experience.

Virginia also recognizes formal vocational-training pathways. An applicant with 240 hours of formal vocational training may qualify with four years of practical experience. An applicant with 160 hours of vocational training may qualify with five years of practical experience.

An applicant with 80 hours of formal vocational training may qualify with six years of practical experience, while an applicant with 40 hours of formal vocational training may qualify with seven years of practical experience.

Applicants must provide documentation supporting the particular education, training, and experience combination used to qualify. The Virginia Board for Contractors reviews these materials before issuing examination approval.

Virginia Journeyman and Master HVAC tradesmen are also subject to continuing education requirements for license renewal. HVAC licensees must complete three hours of Board-approved HVAC continuing education during the applicable license term. Continuing education must relate to the current mechanical code and be completed through an approved provider.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Virginia Journeyman HVAC study plan should combine technical review, mechanical code navigation, Virginia regulations, and timed practice. The exam covers more than 20 subject categories, so candidates benefit from breaking preparation into manageable groups instead of trying to study everything at once.

Warm-air heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning systems represent a major portion of the exam. Review system components, operating principles, equipment, airflow, ventilation requirements, air distribution, installation requirements, and mechanical-code provisions connected with these subjects.

Duct systems should receive focused study as well. Become familiar with duct materials, installation, supports, insulation, clearances, air-distribution principles, and the sections of the International Mechanical Code that govern duct construction and use.

Chimneys, flues, vents, and combustion air should be studied together because they are closely connected with safe operation of fuel-fired equipment. Review venting methods, termination requirements, combustion-air provisions, clearances, and related mechanical requirements.

Boilers and hot-water piping are additional exam categories. Candidates should understand boiler installation concepts, piping systems, system components, hydronic heating, controls, and relevant mechanical-code requirements.

Controls and low-voltage topics may involve thermostats, switches, relays, transformers, control circuits, motors, and system-control concepts. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning can help candidates build broader technical understanding in these areas.

Fuel-oil piping and storage should be studied using NFPA 31. Learn the organization of the standard and practice locating sections addressing tanks, piping, equipment, installation, protection, and safety requirements.

Refrigeration study should include refrigerants, refrigerant piping, system components, refrigeration theory, chilled and condenser water systems, mechanical refrigeration provisions, and related service concepts. The Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning text provides substantial coverage of these trade areas.

Commercial kitchen venting is another specific content category. Candidates should review hoods, exhaust ducts, clearances, makeup air, ventilation requirements, and related mechanical-code provisions.

The Virginia regulatory portion should remain part of the weekly study schedule. Reviewing these rules in smaller sections can make it easier to remember topics such as professional conduct, license renewal, qualifications, definitions, and standards of practice.

The included 6 months of course access provides time to work through these subjects in a structured way. Candidates can revisit difficult areas, work through practice-oriented review, and use the reference books alongside the course rather than treating the books and online preparation as separate resources.

Timed practice can also improve preparation. The actual exam provides 210 minutes for 85 questions. During practice sessions, monitor how long different types of questions take. Learn when to answer from knowledge, when to verify information in a reference, and when to move past a difficult question and return to it later.

Using highlighted and tabbed references during preparation can make navigation more familiar. When a practice question refers to ventilation, fuel oil, boilers, refrigeration, licensing rules, or another major topic, locate the supporting section in the applicable book. Repeating this process can help build both technical knowledge and reference confidence.

How does the Journeyman HVAC exam work?

The Journeyman HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
