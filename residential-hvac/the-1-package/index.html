Exam Details

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman examination is part of the individual tradesman licensing program administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates must satisfy the applicable qualification requirements and receive approval before scheduling the examination through PSI.

The Residential HVAC examination contains 55 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the examination and must answer at least 39 questions correctly to achieve the required passing score.

The examination includes both Virginia regulatory material and technical residential HVAC subjects. Candidates should prepare for the following areas:

  • Standards of Practice
  • Standards of Conduct
  • Renewal and Reinstatement
  • Definitions
  • Qualification for Licensure
  • Warm Air Heating
  • Ventilation Systems
  • Air Conditioning Systems
  • Duct Systems
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents
  • Hot Water Heaters
  • Hot Water Piping
  • Combustion Air
  • Controls and Low Voltage
  • Fuel Oil Piping and Storage
  • Insulation
  • Refrigerants
  • Refrigerant Piping
  • Chilled and Condenser Water Piping
  • Firestopping and Penetration Inspection

Warm Air Heating represents the largest individual technical category, with Air Conditioning Systems also making up a significant part of the exam. Candidates should nevertheless prepare across the complete outline because ventilation, duct systems, piping, combustion air, controls, refrigerants, code requirements, and regulatory questions collectively account for a substantial portion of the examination.

Virginia currently lists the Residential Tradesman examination fee at $95, paid directly to PSI. That examination fee is separate from The 1 Package pricing.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted references because the books are not provided by the testing center.

Open-book testing makes efficient reference navigation an essential exam skill. A candidate may have strong field experience but still lose valuable time if the books are unfamiliar. With 55 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have a little more than two minutes per question on average.

All included books in The 1 Package are highlighted and permanently tabbed. These preparation features help candidates identify important material and create visible navigation points throughout the references. The tabs are most useful when candidates work with them repeatedly during study and understand what each section contains.

Different examination questions may require different references. Virginia licensing or standards questions may lead to the Individual License and Certification Regulations. Mechanical installation requirements may be found in the International Mechanical Code. Heating, air-conditioning, refrigeration, controls, equipment operation, and troubleshooting subjects may be supported by Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning. Residential load-calculation questions may require Manual J.

The additional NASCLA Virginia guide and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code support broader contractor business, law, project management, and state regulatory preparation.

During practice, candidates should first identify what a question is asking before reaching for a book. Determine whether the subject is regulatory, mechanical code, technical HVAC, load calculations, or contractor business material. Then go directly to the most relevant reference.

State Requirements

Virginia's Residential HVAC Tradesman license became available as an additional residential licensing pathway beginning April 1, 2025. The credential applies to residential HVAC work and does not authorize commercial work.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old and meet an approved combination of practical trade experience and formal vocational training. Current qualification pathways include:

  • 2 years of practical experience plus 160 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 3 years of practical experience plus 120 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 4 years of practical experience plus 80 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 5 years of practical experience plus 40 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 6 or more years of practical experience without using one of the listed vocational-training combinations.

Applicants using a vocational-training pathway must provide documentation of their formal training, while practical experience must be verified according to Virginia's application requirements.

The Virginia Residential Tradesman application is for individuals rather than business entities. Virginia also states that applicants performing contracting work above the applicable contractor licensing threshold must address a Virginia contractor license separately. The individual HVAC credential and contractor business license serve different purposes.

This distinction is especially important for candidates purchasing The 1 Package because the package includes business setup services in addition to individual Residential HVAC exam preparation. Business Formation and EIN Filing can help establish the legal and tax identity of the company, while Contractor Compliance Guidance addresses the separate regulatory considerations involved in operating as a contractor.

Test Information and Study Materials

The 1 Package includes 1 year of course access, giving candidates time to study technical material while also working through licensing and business setup requirements. The course period can be divided into several focused study tracks.

Warm-air heating should receive substantial attention. Review heating equipment, air distribution, controls, ventilation, combustion air, chimneys, flues, vents, and related installation requirements. Use the International Mechanical Code when exact provisions need to be located.

Air-conditioning and refrigeration preparation should include compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, piping, controls, heat transfer, and system operation. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides detailed technical material for these subjects.

Duct systems and ventilation require knowledge of airflow and installation requirements. Candidates should become comfortable identifying applicable code sections while also understanding the practical purpose of each system component.

Manual J should be studied as a calculation method rather than simply a reference to search during the exam. Candidates should understand how factors such as insulation, windows, orientation, infiltration, and outdoor design conditions affect residential heating and cooling loads.

Virginia regulations should be reviewed independently from the technical material. Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, Qualification for Licensure, definitions, renewal, and reinstatement are included in the exam outline and deserve dedicated preparation.

Practice-oriented study should also include reference-navigation drills. Choose a practice question, identify its subject, determine which book is most appropriate, and locate the information using the permanent tabs, index, table of contents, or chapter organization.

When an answer is incorrect, return to the supporting reference and review the surrounding material. This develops deeper understanding while strengthening familiarity with the books.

The Residential HVAC Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.
