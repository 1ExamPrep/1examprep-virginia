### Licensing Steps

Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman licensing is administered through the Board for Contractors. This license is issued to an individual tradesman rather than to a contractor business.

1. **Review the Residential HVAC qualification requirements.** Applicants must satisfy one of Virginia's approved combinations of practical experience and vocational training or another recognized licensing pathway.
2. **Document practical HVAC experience.** Applicants using an experience-based qualification pathway must provide the required experience verification.
3. **Provide vocational-training documentation when applicable.** Candidates using a pathway that combines practical experience with formal vocational training must provide supporting records.
4. **Submit the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application.** Applicants select Residential Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning Tradesman as the requested designation.
5. **Receive Board approval.** Candidates must be approved before scheduling the examination.
6. **Prepare for the examination.** Study the Virginia regulations, 2021 International Mechanical Code, HVAC and refrigeration material, and Manual J residential load calculations.
7. **Schedule and complete the PSI examination.** After Board approval, candidates can proceed through the examination scheduling process.
8. **Complete the licensing process.** The individual must satisfy the applicable examination and licensing requirements before receiving the Residential HVAC Tradesman credential.

The individual Residential HVAC Tradesman license is different from a Virginia contractor business license. Individuals or businesses performing work that falls under Virginia contractor licensing requirements must address the applicable contractor license separately.

### State Requirements

Virginia's residential tradesman licensing pathway became effective in 2025 as an additional route for individuals seeking to work as HVAC mechanics or plumbers in residential structures. Commercial work is not permitted under the Residential HVAC credential.

Applicants must be at least **18 years old** and satisfy an approved qualification pathway. Virginia currently provides several combinations of practical HVAC experience and formal vocational training:

- **2 years of practical experience** plus **160 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **3 years of practical experience** plus **120 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **4 years of practical experience** plus **80 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **5 years of practical experience** plus **40 hours of formal vocational training**.
- **6 or more years of practical experience** in the trade.

Experience-based applicants must provide the required Virginia experience verification. Applicants using vocational training must also provide the applicable certificates of completion or official transcripts.

Virginia also provides a pathway for certain individuals who currently hold a Residential Tradesman license or certification issued by a governing body outside the Commonwealth. Supporting licensure documentation is required for that pathway.

The Residential HVAC license is specifically limited to residential work. It provides a pathway for HVAC mechanics working in dwellings and townhouses and can be used as a step toward obtaining additional HVAC credentials as experience and qualifications increase.

Ventilation and duct systems should be studied together where practical. Candidates should understand airflow, ventilation requirements, duct construction, installation concepts, and code-related requirements. Chimneys, flues, vents, combustion air, hot-water heaters, and hot-water piping represent additional areas that should not be overlooked. These subjects account for a meaningful number of examination questions and require candidates to move comfortably between technical and code-based information.

Controls and low-voltage systems require understanding of basic HVAC control concepts and the relationship between thermostats, equipment controls, and system operation. Candidates should also review insulation, refrigerants, refrigerant piping, chilled and condenser water piping, and firestopping or penetration inspection.

Manual J study should focus on understanding the calculation process rather than simply memorizing isolated values. Review how building characteristics, insulation, windows, orientation, infiltration, climate conditions, and other factors contribute to heating and cooling loads.

## How does the Residential HVAC Tradesman exam work?

The Residential HVAC Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
