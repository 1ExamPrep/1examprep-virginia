Exam Details

The Virginia Residential HVAC examination is administered through PSI as part of Virginia's individual tradesman examination program. Candidates must receive approval through the Virginia Board for Contractors before scheduling the examination.

The Residential HVAC examination contains 55 items. Candidates are given 120 minutes to complete the examination and must achieve a passing score of 39 correct answers.

The current examination content outline includes the following subject areas:

  • Standards of Practice: 3 items
  • Standards of Conduct: 2 items
  • Renewal and Reinstatement: 1 item
  • Definitions: 1 item
  • Qualification for Licensure: 1 item
  • Warm Air Heating: 8 items
  • Ventilation Systems: 4 items
  • Air Conditioning Systems: 6 items
  • Duct Systems: 4 items
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents: 4 items
  • Hot Water Heaters: 3 items
  • Hot Water Piping: 4 items
  • Combustion Air: 2 items
  • Controls/Low Voltage: 3 items
  • Fuel Oil Piping and Storage: 1 item
  • Insulation: 2 items
  • Refrigerants: 2 items
  • Refrigerant Piping: 2 items
  • Chilled and Condenser Water Piping: 1 item

Warm Air Heating is the largest individual technical category, followed by Air Conditioning Systems. Ventilation, duct systems, chimneys and vents, and hot-water piping are also meaningful portions of the exam. Candidates should study the complete content outline rather than concentrating only on refrigeration or air conditioning.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because the books are not provided at the examination site.

The listed regulatory reference for Virginia tradesman examinations is the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations. Residential HVAC technical references include the International Mechanical Code, 2021, Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition, and Manual J - Residential Load Calculation.

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and reference materials containing handwriting are not permitted. Candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination session.

This makes a highlighted and permanently tabbed book package particularly useful for open-book preparation. Candidates receive references that already contain a visual navigation structure, allowing more study time to be spent learning the material and practicing how to use the books.

With 55 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have an average of a little more than two minutes per question. Efficient navigation matters. A candidate who must search through an unfamiliar textbook from beginning to end can quickly lose valuable time.

Practice determining which reference is appropriate before beginning a lookup. A licensing or conduct question may require the Virginia regulations. A mechanical installation question may lead to the International Mechanical Code. Refrigeration theory and equipment questions may be addressed through Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, while residential load-calculation questions may require Manual J.

Licensing Steps

Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman licensing is administered through the Board for Contractors. This license is issued to an individual tradesman rather than to a contractor business.

  1. Review the Residential HVAC qualification requirements. Applicants must satisfy one of Virginia's approved combinations of practical experience and vocational training or another recognized licensing pathway.
  2. Document practical HVAC experience. Applicants using an experience-based qualification pathway must provide the required experience verification.
  3. Provide vocational-training documentation when applicable. Candidates using a pathway that combines practical experience with formal vocational training must provide supporting records.
  4. Submit the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application. Applicants select Residential Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning Tradesman as the requested designation.
  5. Receive Board approval. Candidates must be approved before scheduling the examination.
  6. Prepare for the examination. Study the Virginia regulations, 2021 International Mechanical Code, HVAC and refrigeration material, and Manual J residential load calculations.
  7. Schedule and complete the PSI examination. After Board approval, candidates can proceed through the examination scheduling process.
  8. Complete the licensing process. The individual must satisfy the applicable examination and licensing requirements before receiving the Residential HVAC Tradesman credential.

The individual Residential HVAC Tradesman license is different from a Virginia contractor business license. Individuals or businesses performing work that falls under Virginia contractor licensing requirements must address the applicable contractor license separately.

State Requirements

Virginia's residential tradesman licensing pathway became effective in 2025 as an additional route for individuals seeking to work as HVAC mechanics or plumbers in residential structures. Commercial work is not permitted under the Residential HVAC credential.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old and satisfy an approved qualification pathway. Virginia currently provides several combinations of practical HVAC experience and formal vocational training:

  • 2 years of practical experience plus 160 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 3 years of practical experience plus 120 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 4 years of practical experience plus 80 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 5 years of practical experience plus 40 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 6 or more years of practical experience in the trade.

Experience-based applicants must provide the required Virginia experience verification. Applicants using vocational training must also provide the applicable certificates of completion or official transcripts.

Virginia also provides a pathway for certain individuals who currently hold a Residential Tradesman license or certification issued by a governing body outside the Commonwealth. Supporting licensure documentation is required for that pathway.

The Residential HVAC license is specifically limited to residential work. It provides a pathway for HVAC mechanics working in dwellings and townhouses and can be used as a step toward obtaining additional HVAC credentials as experience and qualifications increase.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Residential HVAC study plan should begin with the examination outline. Warm Air Heating contains eight questions, making it the largest technical category. Candidates should review residential heating equipment, system operation, distribution, combustion, and related mechanical requirements.

Air Conditioning Systems contains six questions and should receive substantial attention as well. Review the refrigeration cycle, system components, heat transfer, equipment operation, controls, and residential cooling principles. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides detailed technical support for these topics.

Ventilation and duct systems should be studied together where practical. Candidates should understand airflow, ventilation requirements, duct construction, installation concepts, and code-related requirements. The International Mechanical Code is especially useful when preparing for questions that require a specific rule or installation standard.

Chimneys, flues, vents, combustion air, hot-water heaters, and hot-water piping represent additional areas that should not be overlooked. These subjects account for a meaningful number of examination questions and require candidates to move comfortably between technical and code-based information.

Controls and low-voltage systems require understanding of basic HVAC control concepts and the relationship between thermostats, equipment controls, and system operation. Candidates should also review insulation, refrigerants, refrigerant piping, chilled and condenser water piping, and firestopping or penetration inspection.

Manual J study should focus on understanding the calculation process rather than simply memorizing isolated values. Review how building characteristics, insulation, windows, orientation, infiltration, climate conditions, and other factors contribute to heating and cooling loads.

Use the highlighted and tabbed books throughout your preparation. Review each tab and identify the subject it represents. Then work through practice questions and locate supporting information using the tabs, table of contents, index, headings, diagrams, and tables.

When a practice answer is incorrect, return to the appropriate reference and study the surrounding section. Understanding why an answer is correct provides stronger preparation than memorizing a single response.

Timed reference practice can also help. With 120 minutes available for 55 items, candidates should learn to decide quickly when a reference lookup is necessary. Questions that can be answered confidently from technical knowledge may not require a search, while detailed regulatory, code, or calculation questions may benefit from reference confirmation.

How does the Residential HVAC Tradesman exam work?

The Residential HVAC Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
