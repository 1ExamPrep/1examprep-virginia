Exam Details

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman examination is part of Virginia's individual tradesman licensing program. Candidates must satisfy the applicable Board for Contractors qualification requirements and receive examination approval before scheduling the test.

The Residential HVAC examination contains 55 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the examination and must answer at least 39 questions correctly to achieve the required passing score.

The examination includes both Virginia regulatory topics and residential HVAC technical subjects. Candidates should prepare for areas that include:

  • Standards of Practice
  • Standards of Conduct
  • Renewal and Reinstatement
  • Definitions

  • Warm Air Heating
  • Ventilation Systems
  • Air Conditioning Systems
  • Duct Systems
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents
  • Hot Water Heaters
  • Hot Water Piping

Warm Air Heating is one of the largest technical areas on the examination, with Air Conditioning Systems also representing an important portion of the test. Candidates should still prepare across the complete outline because smaller categories can collectively account for a significant number of questions.

The examination requires more than general equipment familiarity. Candidates should be able to connect practical HVAC experience with written mechanical requirements, regulatory rules, system concepts, and residential calculation methods.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their permitted references because books are not supplied at the testing center.

An open-book exam rewards candidates who understand both the trade and their references. Searching every book for every answer can consume valuable testing time. A stronger strategy is to identify the subject first and then use the most appropriate reference when a detailed requirement or calculation must be confirmed.

Questions involving Virginia licensing rules may require the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations. Mechanical installation and code questions can lead to the International Mechanical Code. Broader HVAC and refrigeration subjects may be addressed through Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, while residential load-calculation questions may require Manual J.

The additional NASCLA Virginia guide and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code can support broader contractor business, state law, project management, and building-regulatory preparation.

Candidates should practice using tables of contents, indexes, chapter headings, technical tables, diagrams, and calculation sections. Regular use of the references throughout the one-year course period can help turn the books into familiar tools instead of unfamiliar material on test day.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman credential is issued to an individual through the Virginia Board for Contractors. The licensing process includes qualification review, examination approval, exam preparation, testing, and completion of the applicable licensing requirements.

  1. Review the Virginia Residential HVAC qualification pathways. Applicants must meet one of the approved combinations of practical experience and vocational training or another recognized pathway.
  2. Document applicable HVAC experience. Practical experience must be verified as required by the Board.
  3. Provide vocational training documentation when applicable. Candidates using formal education as part of their qualification must submit the required records.
  4. Submit the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application. Applicants request the Residential Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning Tradesman designation.
  5. Receive examination approval. Candidates must be approved before scheduling the exam.
  6. Prepare for the Residential HVAC examination. Use the course, technical references, mechanical code, regulatory material, and Manual J as part of a structured study plan.
  7. Schedule and complete the examination. Candidates proceed through the approved examination process after receiving authorization.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. After satisfying the examination and qualification requirements, the individual can proceed toward issuance of the Residential HVAC Tradesman credential.

Application Service is included with this Ultimate package. This provides additional support during the licensing application process while the candidate works through the applicable qualification, examination, and documentation requirements.

The individual Residential HVAC Tradesman license should not be confused with a Virginia contractor business license. The tradesman credential applies to the individual, while contractor licensing applies separately to businesses performing contracting work that falls within Virginia's contractor licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Virginia provides several experience and vocational-training combinations for applicants seeking Residential HVAC Tradesman examination approval.

  • 2 years of practical experience plus 160 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 3 years of practical experience plus 120 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 4 years of practical experience plus 80 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 5 years of practical experience plus 40 hours of formal vocational training.
  • 6 or more years of practical experience without using one of the listed vocational-training combinations.

Applicants using an experience-based pathway must provide the applicable experience verification. When formal vocational education is used to satisfy a qualification pathway, supporting documentation must also be provided.

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman designation is limited to the residential scope established for the credential. It does not provide authority for commercial HVAC work outside that scope.

Applicants should also understand the distinction between individual tradesman licensing and contractor business licensing. Holding a Residential HVAC Tradesman credential does not automatically satisfy the requirements for a business that must hold a Virginia contractor license.

How does the Residential HVAC Tradesman exam work?

The Residential HVAC Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
