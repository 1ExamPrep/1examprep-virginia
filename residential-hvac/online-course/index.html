Exam Details

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman examination is administered through Virginia's tradesman examination program after the applicant receives approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors. PSI serves as the examination administrator.

The Residential HVAC examination contains 55 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the examination and must answer at least 39 questions correctly to meet the passing requirement.

The examination includes Virginia regulatory subjects and technical residential HVAC material. Areas of preparation include:

  • Standards of Practice
  • Standards of Conduct
  • Renewal and Reinstatement
  • Definitions
  • Qualification for Licensure
  • Warm Air Heating
  • Ventilation Systems
  • Air Conditioning Systems
  • Duct Systems
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents
  • Hot Water Heaters
  • Hot Water Piping
  • Combustion Air
  • Controls and Low Voltage
  • Fuel Oil Piping and Storage
  • Insulation
  • Refrigerants
  • Refrigerant Piping
  • Chilled and Condenser Water Piping
  • Firestopping and Penetration Inspection

Warm-air heating and air-conditioning systems are important areas of technical preparation, but candidates should not overlook the smaller categories. Regulatory questions, ductwork, ventilation, piping, combustion air, controls, and other subjects all contribute to the final exam result.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because the examination site does not provide the books.

The open-book format makes efficient reference navigation an important part of preparation. With 55 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have a little more than two minutes per question on average. Searching through an unfamiliar technical book for every answer can quickly consume valuable examination time.

A stronger approach combines HVAC knowledge with selective reference use. Candidates should learn to recognize whether a question concerns Virginia regulations, a mechanical code requirement, HVAC technical knowledge, or a residential load calculation and then move directly to the most appropriate reference.

The Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations support state-specific regulatory preparation. The International Mechanical Code, 2021 supports mechanical code questions. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition provides broad HVAC and refrigeration technical information. Manual J supports residential heating and cooling load-calculation topics.

Candidates should practice using indexes, tables of contents, chapter headings, technical tables, diagrams, and calculation procedures before examination day. Familiarity with these tools can make book lookups significantly more efficient.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman credential is issued to individuals through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Pre-approval from the Board is required before candidates can schedule the examination.

  1. Review the Residential HVAC eligibility requirements. Virginia provides several qualification pathways based on practical experience and formal vocational training.
  2. Choose the applicable qualification pathway. Determine which combination of HVAC experience and vocational education applies to your background.
  3. Document practical trade experience. Applicants using an experience pathway must provide the required experience verification.
  4. Provide training documentation when applicable. Applicants using vocational education to meet the qualification requirement must submit appropriate certificates or transcripts.
  5. Submit the Residential Tradesman Exam and License Application. Select Residential Heating, Ventilation, and Air Conditioning Tradesman as the requested designation.
  6. Receive Board approval. Examination approval is required before scheduling through PSI.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Review the Virginia regulations, mechanical code, HVAC technical material, and residential load calculations.
  8. Schedule and complete the PSI examination. Once approved, candidates can proceed with examination registration.
  9. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the applicable requirements, the individual can proceed toward issuance of the Residential HVAC Tradesman credential.

The Residential HVAC Tradesman license is an individual license and should not be confused with a Virginia contractor business license. A business performing contracting work that requires contractor licensure must satisfy the applicable contractor business requirements separately.

State Requirements

Virginia's Residential HVAC Tradesman pathway became available as an additional licensing option for individuals working in residential HVAC. The credential is limited to residential structures, and commercial HVAC work is not permitted under the residential license.

Applicants must be at least 18 years old and satisfy one of the state's approved qualification pathways. Current Virginia qualification options include:

  • Two years of practical experience plus 160 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Three years of practical experience plus 120 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Four years of practical experience plus 80 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Five years of practical experience plus 40 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Six or more years of practical experience in the trade.

Applicants qualifying through practical experience must provide the required experience verification. Candidates relying on formal vocational education must provide appropriate documentation of that training.

Virginia also provides a pathway for certain applicants who currently hold a residential tradesman license or certification issued by another governing body. Supporting licensing documentation must be provided when using that qualification pathway.

The Residential HVAC Tradesman credential is separate from journeyman and master HVAC licensing. Residential HVAC experience can also form part of a future pathway toward higher tradesman credentials when the applicable Virginia experience and licensing requirements are met.

Reference Books

  • Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations
    This Virginia regulatory reference covers rules governing individual tradesmen and certifications. Candidates can use it to review qualification requirements, definitions, standards of practice, standards of conduct, renewal, reinstatement, and other state licensing topics that may appear on the examination.
  • International Mechanical Code, 2021
    The 2021 International Mechanical Code provides requirements affecting mechanical systems and equipment. It supports study of ventilation, ductwork, combustion air, equipment installation, chimneys and vents, mechanical clearances, and related code requirements relevant to residential HVAC work.
  • Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition
    This comprehensive HVAC reference supports study of heating, air conditioning, refrigeration cycles, compressors, condensers, evaporators, refrigerants, controls, electrical concepts, air distribution, troubleshooting, and system operation.
  • Manual J - Residential Load Calcs, reprinted 2016, Eighth Full Edition
    Manual J provides the methodology used for residential heating and cooling load calculations. It addresses factors such as building construction, insulation, windows, orientation, infiltration, outdoor conditions, and other variables that influence HVAC equipment sizing loads.

These books address different parts of the examination. Learning which reference is appropriate for each subject is an important part of open-book preparation and can reduce unnecessary searching during the test.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should begin with the fundamentals of residential heating and air conditioning. Candidates should understand equipment operation, heat transfer, airflow, the refrigeration cycle, and how major HVAC components work together as complete systems.

Warm-air heating should receive focused review. Study heating equipment, combustion, air distribution, ventilation, chimneys, flues, vents, controls, and related mechanical requirements. Candidates should understand both practical system operation and the code requirements that affect installation.

Air-conditioning preparation should include refrigeration-cycle concepts, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, refrigerant piping, controls, and heat-transfer relationships. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides broad technical coverage for these areas.

Duct systems and ventilation should also receive regular study. Review airflow concepts, duct construction, installation requirements, ventilation principles, insulation, and related mechanical code provisions. When a question asks for an exact code requirement, practice finding it in the International Mechanical Code rather than relying only on memory.

Residential load calculations require a different type of preparation. Manual J should be studied as a process rather than treated only as a lookup reference. Candidates should become familiar with the factors that influence residential heating and cooling loads and learn how information is organized within the manual.

Virginia regulations should be studied as their own subject area. Candidates should understand licensing qualifications, standards of practice, professional conduct, renewal, and other responsibilities associated with holding an individual Residential HVAC Tradesman license.

Practice questions should be used to identify weak areas. When a question is answered incorrectly, locate the supporting information in the appropriate reference, review the surrounding section, and understand the principle behind the answer.

Timed reference-navigation exercises can also improve exam preparation. Practice identifying a question's subject, choosing the appropriate book, locating the relevant section, and finding the information within a limited period. This creates a more realistic open-book preparation routine.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman Online Exam Prep cover?

The course covers residential heating, ventilation, air conditioning, duct systems, refrigeration, controls, mechanical code requirements, Manual J load calculations, and Virginia licensing regulations.

Is the Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman exam open book?

Yes. The examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references and should become familiar with the books during preparation.

How many questions are on the Residential HVAC exam?

The examination contains 55 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes and need at least 39 correct answers to meet the passing requirement.

What books should I use while preparing?

The listed references are Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations; International Mechanical Code, 2021; Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition; and Manual J - Residential Load Calcs, Eighth Full Edition.

Does the online course replace the reference books?

No. Candidates should study with the references and become familiar with them before examination day. The online course is designed to complement the books through structured review and practice.

Who administers the Virginia Residential HVAC exam?

PSI administers the examination after the applicant receives the required approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors.

What experience is required to apply?

Virginia provides several qualification pathways using combinations of practical experience and formal vocational training, as well as a six-or-more-years practical experience pathway.

Does the Residential HVAC license allow commercial HVAC work?

No. The Virginia Residential HVAC Tradesman credential is limited to residential HVAC work and does not authorize commercial HVAC work.

Is Manual J important for exam preparation?

Yes. Manual J is one of the listed references and supports preparation for residential heating and cooling load-calculation concepts.

Is the Residential HVAC Tradesman license the same as a Virginia contractor license?

No. The Residential HVAC Tradesman license is issued to an individual. Contractor licensing applies separately to businesses performing work that falls under Virginia contractor licensing requirements.

How does the Residential HVAC Tradesman exam work?

The Residential HVAC Tradesman exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
