### Open Book Test

The Virginia Framing Contractor specialty examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted reference materials to the examination center.

PSI allows reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used for indexing. Removable tabs and temporary flags are not permitted. References must otherwise remain unmarked and cannot contain written notes or loose papers.

The open-book format makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should not wait until test day to learn how the books are organized. A large code book can take significant time to search if the candidate does not know where major framing subjects are located.

During preparation, practice using the International Residential Code for residential framing questions involving floors, walls, roofs, openings, stairs, and related building components. Use the International Building Code for broader commercial and structural code concepts associated with framed construction.

Carpentry and Building Construction can be used for practical trade topics such as layout, measuring, framing methods, tools, structural components, rough carpentry sequences, and construction techniques. OSHA should be used regularly to prepare for safety questions involving fall protection, ladders, scaffolds, tools, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other jobsite hazards.

A useful open-book strategy is to identify the subject before opening a reference. If the question is about a framing technique, begin with the carpentry reference. If it asks for a code requirement, select the appropriate building code. If it addresses a worker hazard or protective requirement, start with OSHA.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to businesses and sole proprietors rather than to individual tradespeople and include both a license class and a classification or specialty.

The applicant seeking the Framing Contractor specialty must identify a qualified individual for FRM. Virginia requires qualified individuals for Framing Contracting to be pre-approved and pass the applicable specialty examination.

The qualified individual must have the required relationship with the business. Virginia allows a qualified individual to be a full-time employee working at least 30 hours per week or a member of the company's responsible management.

The qualified individual must also meet the experience requirement for the class of contractor license being requested. Virginia currently requires at least one year of applicable experience for a Class C qualified individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

An Experience Verification Form is required for qualified individuals seeking specialties that require pre-approval for an examination, including Framing Contracting. The applicable experience should relate directly to the framing specialty for which the individual is qualifying.

After the required approval is obtained, the candidate completes the FRM specialty examination through PSI. Virginia requires successful completion of the applicable examination before the contractor license application is finalized.

Applicants for a new Virginia contractor license must also complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. The education requirement is separate from the FRM specialty examination and focuses on contractor business requirements, regulations, and responsibilities.

Class A and Class B applicants also have business examination requirements associated with the designated employee. These business examinations are separate from the Framing Contractor technical examination.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia Framing Contractor study plan should begin with the major framing systems: floors, walls, roofs, ceilings, stairs, and structural openings. Study how each system is laid out, assembled, supported, fastened, and connected to adjacent construction.

Wall framing preparation should include studs, plates, headers, rough openings, bracing, partitions, corners, wall intersections, and load-bearing considerations. Candidates should understand the sequence of laying out and erecting framed walls and how openings affect the framing system.

Floor and ceiling preparation should include joists, beams, girders, subflooring, openings, bearing, bridging or blocking, and related structural components. Understanding how loads transfer through the framing system can help candidates interpret practical exam questions.

Roof preparation should include rafters, trusses, ridge members, hips, valleys, roof openings, sheathing, and common roof-framing terminology. Use Carpentry and Building Construction to reinforce practical methods and the code books to review applicable requirements.

Layout and estimating should also receive dedicated attention. Framing contractors regularly work with measurements, dimensions, material quantities, plans, and project layouts. Candidates should practice reading construction information and translating measurements into practical framing decisions.

Code study should focus on efficient navigation. Learn where structural framing, walls, floors, roofs, stairs, openings, and other building requirements are located in the International Building Code and International Residential Code. Use indexes and chapter organization repeatedly rather than trying to memorize every section number.

OSHA preparation should be integrated throughout the study process. Framing work often involves elevated work, ladders, scaffolds, power tools, lifting materials, exposed edges, and temporary construction conditions. Candidates should understand the safety principles that apply to these activities and practice locating the applicable OSHA requirements.

Once individual subjects become familiar, move into mixed-reference practice. Read a question, decide whether it is primarily a trade-method, code, or safety question, select the appropriate book, and locate the supporting information. This builds both technical knowledge and open-book navigation skills.

## How does the Framing Contractor exam work?

The Framing Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
