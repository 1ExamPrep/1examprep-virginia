### Exam Details

The **Virginia Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting examination** is the technical specialty examination associated with the INS designation. Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors, and the specialty requires successful completion of a Board-approved technical examination for the applicable Qualified Individual.

The examination covers technical knowledge associated with insulation and weather-stripping work, including insulation principles, materials, building construction, installation concepts, safety, and related trade knowledge. Candidates should prepare to distinguish between different insulation products and understand how insulation functions within walls, ceilings, roofs, floors, and other building assemblies.

General construction knowledge is also valuable because insulation work takes place within building systems. Understanding framing, construction materials, measurements, and common building components can help candidates interpret technical questions more effectively.

Construction safety is another important preparation area. Insulation work can involve ladders, elevated work areas, hand and power tools, electrical hazards, protective equipment, material handling, and other jobsite conditions addressed by construction-safety regulations.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting** as the service that provides for the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials classified as insulating media used solely for temperature control or sound control in residential and commercial buildings.

The specialty does not include insulation of mechanical equipment or the ancillary lines and piping associated with mechanical systems. The Residential Building Contractor and Commercial Building Contractor classifications, along with the Home Improvement and Commercial Improvement specialties, also provide for the insulation and weather-stripping function within their applicable scopes.

Virginia contractor licenses consist of two primary components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class determines the monetary level of projects the business is authorized to undertake, while the specialty identifies the type of work the contractor may perform.

The business must provide a Qualified Individual for the specialty. Virginia's current application requirements call for the Qualified Individual to be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management and to meet the experience requirement applicable to the license class.

## How does the Insulation and Weather Stripping Contractor exam work?

The Insulation and Weather Stripping Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
