### Exam Details

The **Virginia Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting examination** is the technical specialty examination associated with the INS designation. Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors, and PSI administers the specialty examination.

- **Specialty:** Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** INS
- **Number of Questions:** 40
- **Time Allowed:** 90 minutes
- **Passing Score:** 70%, or 28 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The current published content outline distributes the 40 questions among the following areas:

- **General Knowledge and Principles:** 10 questions
- **Liens:** 1 question
- **Insulation Types and Properties:** 15 questions
- **Radiant and Reflective Barriers, Vapor Retarders:** 7 questions
- **Safety:** 5 questions
- **Laws and Regulations:** 2 questions

**Insulation Types and Properties** is the largest portion of the examination with 15 questions. General Knowledge and Principles accounts for another 10 questions, meaning these two areas together represent a substantial portion of the exam.

Radiant and reflective barriers and vapor retarders are also important, accounting for 7 questions. Safety represents 5 questions, while liens and laws and regulations complete the published content outline.

With 90 minutes to answer 40 questions, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient preparation should therefore include both technical knowledge and the ability to recognize quickly which reference or subject area applies.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting examination is an **open book test**. Candidates must bring their own allowable references because the books are not supplied at the testing site.

PSI permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed. Removable tabs are not permitted, and references containing writing are not allowed into the examination.

The open-book format makes reference-navigation practice an important part of Virginia INS exam preparation. Candidates should not depend on having enough time to search every reference from beginning to end. Instead, learn which subjects are associated with each book.

For example, questions involving construction-site safety may direct candidates toward **Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)**. Questions involving broader building components and construction principles may be supported by **Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016**. Insulation-specific topics are supported by **Insulation Handbook, 2001**.

Use the references during preparation rather than saving them for exam day. Practice identifying a question's subject, selecting the correct book, finding the applicable section, and carefully interpreting the information.

An open-book exam still requires knowledge. Candidates who understand insulation terminology and basic principles can often determine where to begin looking much faster than candidates who are unfamiliar with the subject.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting** as the service that provides for the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials classified as insulating media used for the sole purpose of temperature control or sound control of residential and commercial buildings.

The specialty does not include insulation of mechanical equipment or the ancillary lines and piping associated with mechanical systems.

This distinction is important when preparing for the examination because the INS specialty focuses on building insulation rather than mechanical-system insulation. Candidates should understand how insulating materials function within residential and commercial building assemblies.

Virginia contractor licenses include both a license class and a classification or specialty. The INS designation establishes the type of contracting work associated with the specialty, while the contractor license class addresses the broader licensing structure applicable to the business.

The contractor business must also designate the appropriate Qualified Individual and complete the licensing requirements associated with the requested class and specialty.

### Test Information and Study Materials

The largest examination category is **Insulation Types and Properties**. Candidates should become familiar with the characteristics of common insulating materials and understand that insulation products can differ in composition, application, thermal behavior, installation requirements, and appropriate use.

Rather than memorizing product names alone, focus on understanding why different insulation systems are selected and how material properties influence performance. Technical questions may require candidates to distinguish between materials or identify a property associated with a particular insulation type.

**General Knowledge and Principles** accounts for 10 questions and should form the foundation of the study plan. Candidates should understand basic insulation terminology, heat-transfer concepts, building-envelope principles, construction conditions, and the purpose of insulation in residential and commercial buildings.

Study insulation as part of the overall building assembly. Walls, ceilings, roofs, floors, and other components can create different installation conditions. Knowledge of general construction can help candidates understand how insulation interacts with framing and other building elements.

**Radiant and Reflective Barriers and Vapor Retarders** account for 7 questions. Review the purpose and characteristics of these materials and understand how they differ from conventional insulation products.

Vapor control is an important part of building-envelope performance. Candidates should understand relevant terminology and the role vapor retarders play within insulated assemblies. Material placement and building conditions can influence how a system performs.

Radiant and reflective materials should also be studied as a distinct category. Candidates should understand that these systems function differently from insulation products that primarily resist conductive heat transfer.

**Safety** represents 5 questions. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 throughout preparation instead of treating construction safety as a final review topic. Insulation work can involve ladders, scaffolding, cutting equipment, electrical hazards, personal protective equipment, material handling, and other jobsite conditions.

The OSHA reference is substantial, so reference navigation is important. Become familiar with its major sections and practice locating information by topic rather than attempting to memorize the entire regulation.

The examination also includes questions on **liens** and **laws and regulations**. These areas represent a smaller number of questions but should still be included in a complete study plan.

When preparing for a timed open-book examination, practice deciding whether a question can be answered from working knowledge or whether a reference lookup is necessary. Looking up every answer can use too much examination time, while guessing at technical details that can be confirmed efficiently may also lead to avoidable mistakes.

Build familiarity with the books by using the same references repeatedly. Review their tables of contents, indexes, terminology, chapter structure, and section headings. Knowing the general location of information can make a major difference when working under a time limit.

When a practice question is missed, determine the reason. It may be an unfamiliar insulation property, confusion about a building principle, difficulty locating an OSHA requirement, or misunderstanding of a vapor-control concept. Returning to the relevant source can turn a missed question into a useful study opportunity.
