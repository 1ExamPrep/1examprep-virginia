Exam Details

The Virginia Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting Examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the INS designation. Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors, and PSI administers the specialty examination.

  • Specialty: Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting
  • Specialty Abbreviation: INS
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Allowed: 90 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 28 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The published examination content outline includes the following subject areas:

  • General Knowledge and Theory: 10 questions
  • Plan Reading, Materials, Estimating, and Take-Offs: 4 questions
  • Spray-On Application: 4 questions
  • Batt/Roll Insulation: 4 questions
  • Blown or Loose-Fill Insulation: 3 questions
  • Rigid-Board and Reflective Insulation: 2 questions
  • Ventilation: 3 questions
  • Vapor Retarders: 4 questions
  • Safety: 6 questions

General Knowledge and Theory represents the largest single examination area. Safety is also significant, while the remaining questions are distributed among different insulation applications, plan reading, estimating, ventilation, and vapor-control subjects.

The examination allows 90 minutes for 40 questions, giving candidates an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient reference navigation can therefore be an important part of exam preparation.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing allowable reference materials into the testing room and complying with current PSI rules governing those references.

Permitted books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted, while removable tabs are not allowed. All rental books included in this package are highlighted and permanently tabbed to support preparation for this open-book testing format.

Highlighted and tabbed books are most useful when candidates practice with them before exam day. Learn what each tab represents and become familiar with the book's table of contents, index, chapters, section headings, tables, and terminology.

A safety question should direct attention toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Insulation-specific subjects such as batt and roll insulation, loose-fill products, reflective systems, vapor retarders, or insulation theory may direct candidates toward the Insulation Handbook. General building methods, plans, materials, and construction concepts may involve Carpentry and Building Construction.

Business and licensing subjects can be studied using the NASCLA Contractor’s Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Virginia 11th Edition, while Virginia-specific building-code material is supported by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2021 Edition, USBC.

Developing these subject-to-reference associations can help reduce unnecessary searching and create a more systematic approach to the open-book exam.

Licensing Steps

Passing the INS examination is one component of Virginia contractor licensing. The license itself is issued to the contracting business, which must satisfy the requirements associated with its selected license class and specialty.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the Insulation and Weather Stripping specialty. The specialty abbreviation is INS.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the requested specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirement associated with the license class being pursued.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy applicable Board-approved pre-license education requirements.
  6. Receive approval for the specialty examination. INS is among the Virginia contractor specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the technical examination.
  7. Prepare for the technical examination. Study insulation theory, materials, installation methods, plans, estimating, ventilation, vapor retarders, and construction safety.
  8. Complete applicable business examination requirements. Additional examination requirements apply according to the contractor license class being pursued.
  9. Complete the contractor application. Submit the required business and Qualified Individual information and other applicable documentation to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The technical exam should therefore be viewed as part of a larger licensing process rather than as the entire licensing requirement.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting as the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials classified as insulating media used solely for temperature control or sound control in residential and commercial buildings.

The INS specialty does not include insulation of mechanical equipment or the ancillary lines and piping associated with mechanical systems.

Virginia contractor licenses consist of two primary components: the contractor license class and the classification or specialty. The license class determines the monetary level of contracting activity permitted, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the contractor may perform.

The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual who satisfies the requirements for the INS specialty. Virginia currently requires at least one year of applicable experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

New contractor license applicants are also subject to Virginia's eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement. This business-focused education is separate from the technical INS examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with General Knowledge and Theory. Understanding basic insulation terminology, heat transfer, insulating materials, thermal resistance, and building-envelope principles creates a foundation for the more specialized portions of the examination.

Next, study the individual insulation systems. Candidates should understand that spray-on insulation, batt and roll insulation, blown or loose-fill insulation, rigid-board insulation, and reflective insulation have different properties and installation methods.

Rather than memorizing isolated terms, learn how each type of insulation is used and what installation considerations affect its performance. Understanding differences between systems can help candidates interpret questions more effectively.

Plan Reading, Materials, Estimating, and Take-Offs requires candidates to work with dimensions, building plans, and quantities. Practice calculating areas and determining material needs from supplied measurements. Carpentry and Building Construction can provide useful context for understanding building components and the locations where insulation is installed.

Ventilation and Vapor Retarders should be studied together with broader building-envelope concepts. Insulation does not operate independently of moisture and air movement. Candidates should understand relevant terminology and how these components relate to insulated assemblies.

Safety accounts for six questions on the published examination outline. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 regularly throughout preparation. Become familiar with the structure of the reference so that safety topics can be located efficiently.

Reference-navigation practice should be part of every study session. Start with a topic, determine the appropriate reference, locate the relevant permanent tab, and then narrow the search using the index, table of contents, or section headings.

The highlighted material can help identify important passages, but candidates should read enough surrounding content to understand the complete requirement or technical principle. Exam questions may depend on details, conditions, exceptions, or specific applications.

The included 6 months of course access gives candidates time to review the technical subjects repeatedly, work through practice-oriented preparation, and build familiarity with the rental references.

When a practice question is missed, return to the relevant book and identify the reason. The problem may be an unfamiliar insulation term, an incorrect estimate, confusion between insulation systems, a safety requirement, or simply difficulty locating the correct section. Understanding the source of the mistake can strengthen future preparation.
