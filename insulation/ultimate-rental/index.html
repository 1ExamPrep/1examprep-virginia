Exam Details

The Virginia Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with insulation and weather stripping contractor licensing preparation in Virginia.

  • Specialty: Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting
  • Specialty Abbreviation: INS
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Allowed: 90 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 28 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers multiple insulation and construction subjects. The technical content includes general knowledge and theory, plan reading, materials, estimating and take-offs, spray-on applications, batt and roll insulation, blown or loose-fill insulation, rigid-board and reflective insulation, ventilation, vapor retarders, and construction safety.

General insulation knowledge provides the foundation for the examination. Candidates should understand basic insulation terminology, materials, thermal principles, installation concepts, and how insulation functions as part of the building envelope.

Preparation should also include differences between various insulation systems. Batt and roll products, loose-fill insulation, rigid-board products, reflective systems, and spray-applied materials have different characteristics and installation considerations.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia contractor licensing process includes requirements that extend beyond successful completion of the specialty examination. Candidates should approach exam preparation and licensing preparation as related but separate parts of the overall process.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia contractor businesses may pursue Class A, Class B, or Class C licensing depending on the type and level of contracting activity involved.
  2. Select Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting. INS identifies the specialty associated with qualifying insulation and weather stripping work.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must identify the individual responsible for meeting the applicable technical qualifications for the requested specialty.
  4. Meet applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the license class and specialty being requested.
  5. Complete applicable pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable contractor pre-license education requirements.
  6. Complete the technical examination requirement. Prepare for and successfully complete the applicable Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting specialty examination.
  7. Complete business examination requirements when applicable. Additional examination requirements depend on the contractor license class being pursued.
  8. Complete the contractor application. Application Service is included with this Ultimate package to support the application process.

Passing the technical examination is one component of Virginia contractor licensing. It does not by itself result in issuance of a contractor license.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Insulation and Weather Stripping Contracting as work involving the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials classified as insulating media used for temperature control or sound control in residential and commercial buildings.

The specialty does not include insulation of mechanical equipment or the ancillary lines and piping associated with mechanical systems.

This distinction helps define the scope of the INS specialty. Candidates should understand that the specialty focuses on building insulation and weather-stripping functions rather than mechanical-system insulation.

Virginia contractor licensing involves the contracting business, a license class, and the classifications or specialties under which the business intends to operate. The Qualified Individual is associated with meeting technical qualifications for the requested specialty.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study strategy should begin with general insulation knowledge and theory. Candidates should understand basic heat-transfer concepts, common insulation terminology, thermal resistance, material characteristics, and how insulation functions within residential and commercial building assemblies.

Next, study the different insulation systems independently. Batt and roll insulation should be reviewed in terms of its materials, installation conditions, fitting, placement, and relationship to framing and other building components.

Blown or loose-fill insulation requires familiarity with material characteristics, coverage, application methods, and installation considerations. Candidates should understand how these products differ from batt, rigid, reflective, and spray-applied insulation systems.

Rigid-board and reflective insulation should be treated as separate categories. Learn the general principles behind these systems and recognize that reflective materials function differently from traditional insulating products.

Spray-on applications introduce their own materials and installation considerations. Preparation should include an understanding of application concepts and the safety considerations associated with insulation work and construction environments.

Ventilation and vapor retarders should also receive focused study. Insulation interacts with moisture and air movement throughout the building envelope. Understanding these relationships can make technical questions easier to interpret.

For plan reading, estimating, and take-offs, practice working with building dimensions and determining material quantities. Candidates should be comfortable reading measurements, identifying relevant building areas, and applying calculations consistently.

Safety should be incorporated into regular study rather than reviewed only at the end. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and become familiar with its organization. Construction work can involve ladders, elevated work areas, tools, electrical hazards, protective equipment, and other jobsite conditions.

The open-book format makes reference navigation an important skill. During a practice question, first decide whether the subject involves insulation, general construction, OSHA safety, contractor business, or Virginia code material. Then choose the most appropriate book before beginning the lookup.

How does the Insulation and Weather Stripping Contractor exam work?
