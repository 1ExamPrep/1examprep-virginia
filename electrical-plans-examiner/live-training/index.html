Open Book Test

The Virginia Electrical Plans Examiner - ICC E3 exam is an open book test. Candidates are expected to use approved references during the exam, but open book testing still requires serious preparation. The code book is valuable only when the candidate knows how to navigate it quickly, read it carefully, and apply the correct rule to the plan condition or question being asked.

Open book testing rewards organization, familiarity, and speed. Candidates should become comfortable using the 2020 NEC table of contents, index, article structure, parts, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Ugly's Electrical References can also help candidates review formulas, conversions, wiring diagrams, electrical symbols, conductor information, and common field reference material used during preparation.

Candidates should practice locating answers under timed conditions. A plan review question may require checking a panel schedule, service size, feeder conductor, overcurrent device, grounding electrode conductor, equipment grounding conductor, transformer installation, motor circuit, or special occupancy detail. A grounding and bonding question may require checking definitions, general requirements, grounding electrode conductor rules, equipment grounding conductor tables, bonding jumper rules, and separately derived system provisions. A calculation question may require reviewing load calculation rules, demand factors, neutral load rules, conductor sizing provisions, and table notes before selecting the best answer.

Candidates should follow current testing rules for approved references, tabs, highlighting, notes, calculators, loose papers, drawings, and materials allowed in the exam room. Open book does not mean unlimited materials. A strong study plan uses the same references that will be used on exam day and builds comfort with locating information under realistic timed conditions.

Test Information and Study Materials

Effective preparation for the Virginia Electrical Plans Examiner - ICC E3 exam should begin with reference navigation. Candidates should learn how the NEC is organized, including chapters, articles, parts, sections, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and informational material. Plans examiner exam questions often depend on knowing where a topic is located and how to read code language precisely.

Electrical plan review requires the ability to connect drawings, schedules, calculations, and code requirements. Candidates should practice reviewing service sizes, feeder schedules, panel schedules, branch circuit layouts, equipment ratings, grounding and bonding details, transformer information, motor loads, conductor types, raceway sizes, overcurrent devices, and installation notes. The goal is to determine whether the information provided on the plans aligns with the applicable NEC requirements.

Services, feeders, and branch circuits should receive focused study. Candidates should review service conductor requirements, service equipment, service disconnects, feeder sizing, branch circuit ratings, conductor ampacity, adjustment and correction factors, neutral conductors, equipment ratings, continuous loads, noncontinuous loads, demand factors, and overcurrent protection. These topics often appear in both calculation-based and plan review-style questions.

Load calculations are an important part of electrical plans examiner preparation. Candidates should practice dwelling, commercial, and equipment-based load calculation concepts as applicable to the exam outline. Plan review questions may require checking calculated loads against conductor sizes, feeder ratings, service equipment ratings, panel schedules, demand factors, neutral load rules, or equipment ratings.

Grounding and bonding are major electrical plans examiner study areas. Candidates should review grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductors, equipment grounding conductors, bonding jumpers, service bonding, bonding of separately derived systems, grounding of equipment, effective ground-fault current path concepts, and conductor sizing rules. Plans examiner questions may require evaluating whether grounding and bonding details shown on a riser diagram or equipment schedule are consistent with NEC requirements.

Wiring methods and materials should be studied carefully. Candidates should review raceways, cables, boxes, fittings, supports, securing requirements, protection from physical damage, wet location requirements, underground installations, conductor insulation, box fill, raceway fill, bends, pull boxes, junction boxes, and conductor identification. Plan review questions may require candidates to evaluate whether the wiring method shown is suitable for the location or equipment described.

Motors and motor circuits are important for electrical plans examiner preparation. Candidates should practice using motor tables and applying rules for motor branch-circuit conductors, overload protection, short-circuit and ground-fault protection, controllers, disconnecting means, multiple motors, and motor feeders. A plan review-style question may require comparing a motor schedule, conductor size, overcurrent device, and disconnect rating against NEC requirements.

Transformers, panelboards, switchboards, and equipment topics should also be included in the study plan. Candidates should review transformer overcurrent protection, conductor sizing, grounding of separately derived systems, working space, equipment clearances, panelboard requirements, switchboards, disconnects, appliances, luminaires, heating equipment, air-conditioning equipment, and other equipment for general use. These topics may require both direct code lookup and interpretation of plan details.

Special occupancies and special equipment can make a major difference on the exam. Candidates should review requirements for hazardous locations, health care facilities, assembly occupancies, agricultural buildings, marinas, recreational vehicles, swimming pools, electric vehicle charging equipment, generators, optional standby systems, emergency systems, fire pumps, signs, and other specialized installations as applicable to the exam outline. These topics may contain rules that modify or add to general NEC requirements.

Electrical symbols, notes, and schedules should be included in the study routine. Plans examiner candidates should practice identifying how information is shown on electrical drawings, panel schedules, riser diagrams, equipment schedules, legends, notes, and specifications. A question may require using several pieces of plan information before applying the correct NEC rule.

Calculations should be practiced regularly. Candidates should work through service load calculations, feeder calculations, conductor ampacity, voltage drop concepts, box fill, raceway fill, motor calculations, transformer calculations, overcurrent protection, grounding conductor sizing, and demand factor applications. Ugly's Electrical References can support formula review and quick technical lookup, but candidates should also practice locating the controlling NEC requirement.

Definitions are important throughout the NEC. Candidates should practice checking defined terms because a question may turn on the code meaning of a specific word or phrase. Terms related to accessible, readily accessible, continuous load, feeder, branch circuit, service, grounded conductor, grounding conductor, bonding jumper, raceway, equipment, premises wiring, separately derived system, and special equipment can affect how an answer should be selected.

Tables and notes should be reviewed carefully. Electrical plans examiner questions often require candidates to apply conductor ampacity tables, equipment grounding conductor tables, raceway fill tables, box fill rules, motor tables, correction factors, adjustment factors, demand factors, and special installation conditions. The correct answer may depend on a note, exception, condition of use, or the exact wording of the plan review question.

Timed practice is important for this exam. Candidates should practice answering questions while using the 2020 NEC and Ugly's Electrical References in a realistic way. Some questions may be answered quickly from a familiar article, while others may require checking a drawing detail, reviewing a definition, comparing multiple code sections, or completing a calculation. How does the Electrical Plans Examiner (E3) exam work?

The Electrical Plans Examiner (E3) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
