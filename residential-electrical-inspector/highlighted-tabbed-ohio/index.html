Exam Details

The ICC Residential Electrical Inspector - E1 exam is a certification exam for candidates who inspect residential electrical installations for code compliance. The exam focuses on electrical systems in one- and two-family dwellings and related accessory structures within the scope of residential electrical provisions. Candidates should be prepared to evaluate installed conditions and determine whether the work meets the applicable code requirements.

The exam commonly includes 60 multiple-choice questions with a 2-hour time limit. It is an open-book exam. Candidates preparing for the 2021 exam version should study from the International Residential Code, 2021. Because the exam is timed, candidates need both residential electrical knowledge and strong code navigation skill.

Major study areas include general electrical requirements, services, branch circuits, feeders, wiring methods, power and lighting distribution, devices, lighting fixtures, appliances, swimming pools, grounding and bonding, overcurrent protection, and residential electrical safety requirements. Candidates should also be ready for questions involving definitions, working clearances, conductor connections, splices, conductor identification, outlet requirements, equipment installation, and inspection conditions.

General electrical questions may involve scope, definitions, listed and labeled equipment, approved materials, working space, access, electrical equipment condition, conductor terminations, identification, guarding, correction issues, and general installation safety. Candidates should become familiar with residential electrical terminology because many exam questions depend on precise code language.

Service questions may involve service size and rating, service conductors, service disconnecting means, panelboard location, working space, service grounding, grounding electrode systems, grounding electrode conductor sizing, bonding, and service equipment installation. Residential services are a major study area because they are central to electrical safety and appear frequently in inspection work.

Branch circuit and feeder questions may involve feeder conductor sizing, branch circuit ratings, overcurrent protection, conductor ampacity, required circuits, receptacle outlets, lighting outlets, appliance circuits, bathroom circuits, kitchen circuits, laundry circuits, garage circuits, outdoor outlets, and required GFCI protection. Candidates should pay close attention to location, equipment type, circuit rating, and dwelling area because small details can change the correct answer.

Wiring method and distribution questions may involve cable installation, raceways, boxes, splices, conductor support, protection from physical damage, box fill, outlet boxes, junction boxes, panelboards, disconnects, and installations in damp or wet locations. Inspectors must understand both what wiring methods are permitted and how those wiring methods must be installed.

Devices, lighting fixtures, and appliance questions may involve receptacles, switches, luminaires, ceiling fans, appliances, equipment disconnects, required outlets, fixture support, appliance branch circuits, and installation clearances. These questions often appear as practical inspection situations, so candidates should study the requirements and practice applying them to real residential conditions.

Swimming pool and similar installation questions may involve bonding, grounding, receptacle locations, equipment clearances, pool pump motors, lighting, disconnects, and safety requirements for pools, spas, hot tubs, and similar equipment. These topics require careful study because water and electrical systems create special safety concerns.

How does the Residential Electrical Inspector (E1) exam work?

The Residential Electrical Inspector (E1) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
