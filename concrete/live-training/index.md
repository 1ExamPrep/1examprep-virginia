### Exam Details

The Virginia Concrete Contracting examination is part of the contractor specialty examination program administered through PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Candidates must receive eligibility approval before taking the specialty examination.

- **Specialty:** Concrete Contracting
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** CEM
- **Regulatory Authority:** Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors
- **Testing Provider:** PSI
- **Exam Delivery:** Computer-administered examination

Virginia requires candidates to satisfy the applicable education and work-experience requirements established by the Board before applying for the contractor specialty examination. After approval, candidates can register and schedule the examination through PSI.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Concrete Contracting examination allows approved reference books to be used during testing. Candidates should prepare for the open-book format by learning how to navigate their references efficiently before exam day.

Open-book testing still requires strong preparation. Technical books such as building codes, OSHA regulations, concrete standards, formwork manuals, and reinforcing-steel references can contain hundreds of pages. A candidate who begins searching unfamiliar books only after reading an exam question may spend too much time locating information.

A better strategy is to become familiar with each book during study. Learn the table of contents, index structure, chapter organization, common terminology, major tables, illustrations, and frequently used sections. Practice identifying the subject of a question and deciding which reference is most likely to contain the answer before opening a book.

For example, a question about construction safety may point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. A question about structural concrete requirements may involve ACI 318-14. Concrete materials, proportioning, curing, strength, and durability may lead to Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Reinforcing-bar placement can lead to Placing Reinforcing Bars, while formwork subjects can be addressed through SP-4 Formwork for Concrete.

Repeated reference practice during both live virtual training and self-study can help candidates develop faster navigation habits and a clearer approach to open-book questions.

### Licensing Steps

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses and requires applicants to satisfy the requirements associated with the appropriate license class and classification or specialty. The Concrete Contracting technical examination is one component of that process.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
2. **Select the applicable classification or specialty.** Concrete Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation CEM.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate an appropriate Qualified Individual for each requested classification or specialty.
4. **Meet applicable experience requirements.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the contractor license class being pursued.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor license applicants.
6. **Apply for specialty examination eligibility.** The applicable education and work-experience requirements must be completed before examination approval.
7. **Schedule the examination through PSI.** After approval, the candidate can register and schedule the Concrete Contracting specialty exam.
8. **Complete the licensing application process.** The contractor business must satisfy the remaining Board requirements and submit the applicable contractor license application.

Passing the specialty examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The business must also complete the remaining requirements associated with its selected license class, organizational structure, Qualified Individual, and application.

For reinforcing steel, become familiar with bar placement, supports, ties, splices, tolerances, and field terminology. Use Placing Reinforcing Bars during practice so the organization of the book becomes familiar before exam day.

Formwork preparation should include form materials, construction loads, lateral pressures, shores, reshores, bracing, wall forms, slab forms, and safe construction practices. SP-4 Formwork for Concrete provides focused information for this area.

Excavation study should include practical site work along with safety considerations. Pipe and Excavation Contracting supports technical preparation, while 29 CFR Part 1926 provides OSHA safety requirements applicable to construction work.

During self-study, use practice questions as navigation exercises. Read the question, identify the primary subject, select the reference most likely to contain the answer, locate the relevant section, and confirm the information. This process can help candidates become more comfortable moving between books instead of searching them randomly.

## How does the Concrete Contractor exam work?

The Concrete Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
