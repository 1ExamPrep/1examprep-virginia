Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing involves more than passing a single technical examination. The Board for Contractors licenses contractor businesses and requires applicants to meet the requirements associated with their selected license class and classification or specialty.

  1. Choose the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C. The license class establishes the level of contracting activity the business may perform under Virginia requirements.
  2. Select the Concrete Contracting specialty when applicable. Concrete Contracting is identified by the specialty code CEM.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. Each classification or specialty on a Virginia contractor license requires an appropriate Qualified Individual. The individual must meet the Board's requirements for the selected designation.
  4. Document the required experience. The Qualified Individual must meet the experience requirement associated with the contractor license class being pursued.
  5. Obtain examination pre-approval when required. Concrete Contracting is among the specialties for which the Qualified Individual must be pre-approved and pass a Board-approved technical examination.
  6. Complete required pre-license education. Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor license applicants. This requirement is separate from trade examination preparation.
  7. Complete required examinations. Candidates must satisfy the technical examination and any other examination requirements that apply to the license class and role within the contracting business.
  8. Submit the contractor license application. The contractor business must complete the applicable Virginia Board for Contractors application and provide the required supporting information.

The technical Concrete Contractor examination is therefore one component of a broader licensing process. Candidates should organize their exam preparation alongside their experience verification, pre-license education, business documentation, and other licensing requirements.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses businesses engaged in construction, removal, repair, or improvement work on property owned by others. Virginia contractor licenses have two main components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class establishes the permitted level of contracting activity, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of work the business is authorized to perform.

For the Concrete Contracting specialty, the Qualified Individual is required to meet the Board's applicable experience qualifications and successfully complete the approved technical examination. Current Virginia contractor application instructions identify minimum Qualified Individual experience requirements based on license class: one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

The Board also requires eight hours of pre-license education for applicants seeking a new Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license. For Class A and Class B applicants, the pre-license education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the education.

Pre-license education covers contractor business and regulatory requirements and is separate from the Concrete Contractor technical examination. Candidates should account for both requirements when planning their licensing process.

How does the Concrete Contractor exam work?

The Concrete Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.

  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
