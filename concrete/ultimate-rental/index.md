### Exam Details

The Virginia Concrete Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the CEM designation. The Virginia Board for Contractors requires candidates seeking this specialty to meet the applicable qualification requirements and receive approval for the examination. PSI administers the specialty examination.

- **Exam Name:** Concrete Contracting Examination
- **Specialty:** Concrete Contracting (CEM)
- **Number of Questions:** 50
- **Time Allowed:** 120 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 35 correct answers
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

The examination covers the major technical areas involved in concrete construction, including estimating, layout and excavation, formwork, reinforcing steel, general concrete knowledge, and construction safety. Candidates should prepare across the full scope of concrete contracting rather than concentrating only on mixing and placing concrete.

General concrete concepts represent a significant part of the examination. Candidates should understand concrete ingredients and properties, mixture considerations, placement, finishing, curing, testing, repair, and practical construction procedures. Reinforcement and formwork are separate areas that also require dedicated preparation.

Layout and excavation are important because concrete construction begins before concrete reaches the project site. Site preparation, excavation, dimensions, grades, and related construction practices can directly affect foundation and concrete work. Safety requirements are also tested and should be incorporated into the overall study plan.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Concrete Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials to the testing center because the books are not supplied during the examination.

The current Virginia Concrete Contracting examination information permits reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and references containing written notes are not permitted. Candidates may not write in reference materials during the examination.

All rental books included in this Ultimate package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. The prepared format can support faster reference navigation, but candidates should still spend substantial time using the books before test day. Effective open-book preparation means understanding the material and knowing where information is located.

With 50 questions and 120 minutes, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Searching randomly through several books can quickly consume valuable testing time. Practice recognizing which reference is likely to contain the answer before beginning a detailed search.

For example, a reinforcing steel question may direct you toward *Placing Reinforcing Bars* or ACI 318, while formwork questions may require the SP-4 reference. ### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor businesses are licensed according to a license class and one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia contractor licensing includes Class A, Class B, and Class C licenses.
2. **Select the Concrete Contracting specialty.** Concrete Contracting is identified by the abbreviation CEM.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the CEM specialty.
4. **Document qualifying experience.** The Qualified Individual must meet the applicable experience requirements and submit the required experience verification for a specialty requiring examination approval.
5. **Complete applicable pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the required eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
6. **Obtain approval for the CEM examination.** Concrete Contracting is among the Virginia specialties requiring examination pre-approval.
7. **Schedule and take the examination through PSI.**
8. **Complete any additional examination requirements associated with the requested license class.**
9. **Prepare and submit the contractor license application and supporting documentation.Application Service is included** with this Ultimate package to support customers through the contractor application portion of the licensing process. Passing the CEM technical examination is one component of licensing and does not by itself result in issuance of a Virginia contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines Concrete Contracting as work connected with processing, proportioning, batching, mixing, conveying, and placing concrete composed of materials commonly used by the concrete industry.

The CEM specialty also includes finishing, coloring, curing, repairing, testing, sawing, grinding, grouting, placing film barriers, sealing, and waterproofing. Construction and assembly of forms, molds, slipforms, pans, centering, and the use of reinforcing steel are also included within the defined scope.

Virginia regulations additionally recognize that the Commercial Building Contractor, Residential Building Contractor, and Highway/Heavy Contractor classifications provide authority for concrete work within the scope of those classifications.

Concrete Contracting is among the classifications and specialties for which a Qualified Individual must receive examination pre-approval. Applicable experience verification is part of the qualification process.

The contractor business must also satisfy requirements associated with its selected license class, business organization, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, examinations, education, application, and other licensing requirements that apply to the specific application.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Concrete exam preparation should combine technical understanding with regular reference navigation. Begin by separating your study into major subject groups: concrete materials and practices, reinforcing steel, formwork, layout and excavation, estimating, and safety.

For general concrete study, review the properties and behavior of concrete, materials used in mixtures, production and placement practices, curing, finishing, testing, durability, repair, and related construction procedures. Use the concrete mixture reference alongside ACI material so you become familiar with both practical concrete technology and structural requirements.

Reinforcing steel should be treated as its own study area. Learn the terminology associated with bars, placement, supports, spacing, development, and reinforced concrete construction. Practice deciding whether a particular question belongs in *Placing Reinforcing Bars* or ACI 318.

Formwork preparation should include the purpose of forms, form materials, supporting systems, construction methods, and practical considerations associated with forming concrete. Become familiar with the organization of SP-4 so you can locate formwork information efficiently.

Layout and excavation preparation should include site work, excavation practices, grades, dimensions, and the construction operations that occur before concrete placement. Understanding these subjects helps candidates connect concrete construction with the conditions required to properly prepare a project.

Safety preparation should include OSHA construction requirements. Rather than attempting to memorize the entire federal standard, learn its organization and practice finding provisions associated with particular construction activities and hazards.

Use timed practice throughout the preparation period. When a practice question requires a reference lookup, identify the correct book first. When an answer is missed, review both the technical concept and the location where the information can be found.

## How does the Concrete Contractor exam work?

The Concrete Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
