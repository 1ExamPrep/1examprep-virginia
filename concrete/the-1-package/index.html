Exam Details

The Virginia Concrete Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the CEM designation. Candidates pursuing this specialty must complete the applicable Virginia Board for Contractors qualification process before taking the specialty examination administered through PSI.

  • Exam Name: Concrete Contracting Examination
  • Specialty: Concrete Contracting (CEM)
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 35 correct answers
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The examination evaluates knowledge across the primary technical areas involved in concrete contracting. The published content outline includes estimating, layout and excavation, formwork, reinforcing steel, general concrete, and construction safety.

  • Estimating: 5 questions
  • Layout and Excavation: 10 questions
  • Formwork: 6 questions
  • Reinforcing Steel: 7 questions
  • General Concrete: 16 questions
  • Safety: 6 questions

General Concrete is the largest examination area, while Layout and Excavation is the second largest. Candidates should still prepare across all six categories because formwork, reinforcement, safety, and estimating represent substantial portions of the technical examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Concrete Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing permitted reference materials because the testing center does not provide the books.

Virginia's examination information permits approved references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and written notes in the references are prohibited.

All rental references in The 1 Package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Candidates should use the books repeatedly during the study period so the location of major topics becomes familiar. Highlighting and tabs are most effective when combined with an understanding of the underlying technical material.

The 120-minute testing period provides an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds for each of the 50 questions. Efficient reference navigation can therefore be valuable. Candidates should practice identifying the subject first and then choosing the appropriate book before beginning a detailed search.

For example, reinforcing-steel questions may require Placing Reinforcing Bars or ACI 318. Formwork topics may point toward SP-4. Concrete materials and mixture questions can be researched in Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures, while excavation questions may require Pipe and Excavation Contracting. Construction safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor business must satisfy the requirements applicable to its license class and requested classification or specialty.

  1. Establish the business entity. The 1 Package includes Business Formation assistance for an LLC or Corporation.
  2. Obtain an EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included with the package.
  3. Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Virginia licenses contractors as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
  4. Select the Concrete Contracting specialty. Virginia identifies Concrete Contracting with the abbreviation CEM.
  5. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable requirements for the specialty.
  6. Document qualifying experience. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements applicable to the requested license class and specialty.
  7. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
  8. Complete the required examination process. The Qualified Individual must receive the applicable approval and successfully complete the CEM examination.
  9. Prepare the contractor application. Application Service is included with The 1 Package.
  10. Address applicable contractor compliance requirements. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included with the package.

Successful completion of the CEM examination is one part of the licensing process. A Virginia contractor license is issued only after the applicant satisfies the applicable licensing requirements.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Concrete Contracting as work connected with processing, proportioning, batching, mixing, conveying, and placing concrete composed of materials common to the concrete industry.

The specialty also includes finishing, coloring, curing, repairing, testing, sawing, grinding, grouting, placement of film barriers, sealing, and waterproofing. Construction and assembly of forms, molds, slipforms, pans, centering, and the use of reinforcing steel are also included within the CEM scope.

Virginia regulations also recognize that the Commercial Building Contractor, Residential Building Contractor, and Highway/Heavy Contractor classifications provide authority for concrete work within their respective scopes.

Candidates pursuing the CEM specialty must satisfy the requirements that apply to the contractor license class, business structure, Qualified Individual, experience, examinations, pre-license education, and application. Test Information and Study Materials

A productive CEM preparation strategy should follow the examination content outline. Start with General Concrete because it represents the largest portion of the exam. Study concrete ingredients, mixture characteristics, placement, consolidation, finishing, curing, testing, durability, repair, and practical construction procedures.

Layout and Excavation should receive significant attention as well. Concrete work depends on proper site preparation, dimensions, grades, excavation, and earthwork before placement begins. Practice connecting excavation concepts with the construction sequence of foundations and other concrete work.

Reinforcing Steel should be reviewed using both Placing Reinforcing Bars and the applicable structural concrete reference. Become familiar with reinforcing terminology, placement, supports, splices, and detailing concepts while learning which reference best addresses a particular question.

Formwork deserves its own study sessions. Review form materials, forming systems, loads, bracing, shores, construction practices, and other procedures used to contain and support freshly placed concrete.

Safety preparation should include direct use of 29 CFR Part 1926. Learn how the federal construction safety reference is organized and practice locating information related to excavation, concrete work, equipment, fall protection, and worker safety.

Estimating should include practical calculation work. Candidates should be comfortable evaluating dimensions, quantities, concrete volume, materials, and other information that may be required to estimate concrete construction work.
