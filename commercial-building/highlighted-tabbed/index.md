### Exam Details

The CBC technical examination contains **80 scored questions**. Candidates must achieve a minimum passing score of **70%, or 56 correct answers**. The total examination time is **180 minutes**.

The examination content is divided into the following subject areas:

- **Site Work – 8 questions**
- **Concrete including Reinforcement – 9 questions**

- **Metals – 9 questions**

Associated trades and finishes include areas such as drywall, metal stud framing, insulation and moisture control, doors, windows, and glazing. ### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the **Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors**. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities, and each license includes a class together with the classification or specialty that determines the type of work the contractor is authorized to perform.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the Commercial Building Contractor classification.** Virginia identifies Commercial Building Contractor with the abbreviation CBC.

4. **Meet applicable experience requirements.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements applicable to the license class and classification.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
6. **Complete the required examination process.** Successful completion of required examinations occurs before applying for contractor licensure.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Commercial Building Contractors** as contractors whose contracts include construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition on real property owned, controlled, or leased by another person involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures.

The Commercial Building Contractor classification uses the abbreviation **CBC**. The classification includes a broad range of construction functions performed on qualifying commercial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings and structures, including multi-family housing where applicable under Virginia's contractor regulations.

The CBC classification does **not** provide authorization for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. Those activities require the appropriate classifications or specialties.

The CBC classification also does not authorize construction, repair, or improvement of dwellings and townhouses as those structures are defined by the applicable Virginia building code requirements.

Because the CBC classification encompasses significant commercial construction responsibilities, the technical examination covers multiple trades and construction disciplines. Candidates should be prepared to demonstrate knowledge of structural components, building materials, site operations, safety, building codes, accessibility, estimating, plans, and construction methods.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Preparation for the Virginia CBC technical exam should reflect the broad range of subjects on the examination. A useful strategy is to divide study time according to the exam categories and work directly with the reference most closely associated with each subject.

For **Site Work**, review excavation, grading, earthwork, trenching, and related site operations. **Pipe and Excavation Contracting** can help reinforce terminology, construction methods, and practical considerations associated with these activities.

For **Concrete including Reinforcement**, candidates should review concrete materials, preparation, placement, finishing, curing, reinforcement, bar placement, and construction quality. Using **The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction** with **Placing Reinforcing Bars** provides focused coverage of these related subjects.

For **Masonry**, study brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, masonry tools, wall construction, and related procedures using **Modern Masonry**.

The **Metals** section requires familiarity with steel construction, including steel joists, joist girders, and steel deck. Candidates should learn how to navigate both **Technical Digest No. 9** and the **SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck**.

**Carpentry** preparation should include layout, framing, floors, walls, roofs, structural components, materials, tools, and common construction practices. **Carpentry and Building Construction** provides a broad practical resource for this category.

For **Thermal and Moisture Protection**, candidates should understand roofing and other building-envelope considerations. Review roof systems, materials, installation procedures, moisture protection, and related estimating concepts.

**Associated Trades and Finishes** include drywall, metal stud framing, insulation and moisture control, doors, windows, and glazing. The **Gypsum Construction Handbook** is particularly important for gypsum wall and ceiling construction.

For **Safety**, candidates should become familiar with the organization of **29 CFR Part 1926**. Rather than attempting to memorize an entire regulatory reference, practice locating sections dealing with common construction hazards and requirements.

**Estimating and Plan Reading** should include regular practice with measurements, quantities, dimensions, construction drawings, and the interpretation of building information. Candidates should read each problem carefully and keep units consistent when calculations are required.

**General Building Code Requirements** require effective navigation of the **International Building Code 2021** and the accessibility reference. Study life safety, means of egress, accessibility, fire protection, occupancy, and other general code provisions included in the exam outline.

## How does the Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam work?

The Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
