### Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination is an **open book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their permitted references and following the applicable examination rules for reference materials.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should spend time learning the structure of each book, including tables of contents, indexes, chapter headings, code sections, technical tables, diagrams, and terminology.

A question involving construction safety may direct the candidate toward **29 CFR Part 1926**. Questions dealing with occupancy, construction types, means of egress, fire protection, or other building code provisions may require the **International Building Code 2021**. Accessibility questions may point toward **ICC A117.1-2017**.

Concrete questions may require **The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction**, while questions involving reinforcing steel may require **Placing Reinforcing Bars**. Masonry questions can direct candidates toward **Modern Masonry**, while structural steel questions may involve the steel joist technical digest or the **SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck**.

The goal during exam preparation should be to identify a question's subject, connect that subject with the correct reference, and locate the relevant information efficiently. Repeated practice can help candidates avoid wasting time searching through the wrong book.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Commercial Building Contractors** as contractors whose work includes qualifying construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures.

The classification abbreviation is **CBC**. The Commercial Building Contractor classification encompasses a wide range of construction activities, which is reflected in the breadth of subjects candidates must study for the technical examination.

The CBC classification does **not** provide authorization for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. Contractors performing those services must hold the appropriate classifications or specialty authority required for that work.

The contractor business must also satisfy the requirements associated with its chosen license class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, applicable examinations, pre-license education, and the Virginia contractor license application.

The technical examination is only one component of the licensing process. Candidates preparing for the CBC examination should understand that exam preparation and licensing requirements are related but separate parts of becoming properly licensed.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor study plan should combine instructor-led review with regular independent practice. The CBC classification covers enough construction disciplines that candidates benefit from dividing study time into focused subject areas.

For **building code** preparation, work directly with the **International Building Code 2021**. Become familiar with its chapter structure, definitions, tables, section numbers, and index. Practice identifying whether a question concerns occupancy, construction type, fire protection, means of egress, or another code topic before searching.

For **accessibility**, use **ICC A117.1-2017** to review accessible routes, clearances, doors, entrances, circulation spaces, and other accessibility criteria. Accessibility questions can require precise navigation, so repeated practice is valuable.

For **site work and excavation**, review excavation, trenching, grading, earthwork, and related construction operations using **Pipe and Excavation Contracting**. Candidates should also understand how site-work subjects can overlap with OSHA construction safety requirements.

For **concrete and reinforcement**, study concrete materials, placement, finishing, curing, reinforcing bars, supports, and quality practices. Use **The Contractor’s Guide to Quality Concrete Construction** together with **Placing Reinforcing Bars** to connect concrete procedures with reinforcement installation.

For **masonry**, review brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, materials, tools, and construction techniques using **Modern Masonry**. Practical familiarity with masonry terminology helps candidates recognize the nature of technical questions more quickly.

For **metals**, candidates should become familiar with steel joists, joist girders, steel deck, erection, fastening, handling, and installation. The steel joist technical digest and SDI steel deck manual provide targeted information for these topics.

**Carpentry and Building Construction** supports study involving framing, floor systems, walls, roof construction, structural components, layout, materials, and practical building procedures. These subjects can also reinforce estimating and plan-reading preparation.

For **gypsum construction**, use the **Gypsum Construction Handbook** to review wall systems, ceiling systems, framing, gypsum products, installation procedures, and finishing.

For **roofing**, use **Roofing Construction and Estimating** to review roof materials, roof construction, measurement, quantity calculations, installation concepts, and estimating.

For **safety**, become comfortable navigating **29 CFR Part 1926**. Rather than attempting to memorize the entire OSHA reference, candidates should learn how the regulations are organized and practice finding requirements related to common construction hazards.

Self-study should include timed reference exercises. Start with a practice question, identify its subject, choose the appropriate reference, locate the information, and evaluate whether the question was answered both correctly and efficiently.

Live virtual training can help candidates establish priorities and understand difficult commercial construction concepts. Independent study can then reinforce those concepts through reading, reference navigation, review, and practice-oriented preparation.

Candidates should pay extra attention to weaker areas. Someone with extensive carpentry experience may need more work in accessibility, reinforcing steel, masonry, OSHA, or code navigation. A candidate with concrete experience may need more time with roofing, structural steel, gypsum systems, or excavation.

A balanced study routine can help candidates strengthen both technical knowledge and exam-reference familiarity rather than depending entirely on prior field experience.

## How does the Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam work?

The Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
