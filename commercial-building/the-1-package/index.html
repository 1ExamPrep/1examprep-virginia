Exam Details

The Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination is associated with the Commercial Building Contractor classification, abbreviated CBC, and is administered through PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors.

  • Exam: Commercial Building Technical Contractor
  • Classification: Commercial Building Contractor (CBC)
  • Number of Questions: 80
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 56 correct answers
  • Time Allowed: 180 minutes
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The published examination outline divides the test among major commercial construction subjects:

  • Site Work: 8 questions
  • Concrete Including Reinforcement: 9 questions
  • Masonry: 9 questions
  • Metals: 9 questions
  • Carpentry: 9 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 8 questions
  • Associated Trades and Finishes: 7 questions
  • Safety: 7 questions
  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 8 questions
  • General Building Code Requirements: 6 questions

This distribution illustrates why CBC preparation requires a broad reference collection. Concrete, masonry, metals, and carpentry each receive significant attention, while site work, roofing and moisture protection, estimating, safety, associated trades, and code requirements remain substantial parts of the examination.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors rather than to individual employees. Contractor licenses consist of a license class and a classification or specialty. The license class determines applicable project-value limits, while the classification or specialty establishes the type of construction work the business is authorized to perform.

  1. Establish the business entity. The 1 Package includes Business Formation assistance for an LLC or Corporation.
  2. Obtain the business EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included with the package.
  3. Select the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  4. Select the Commercial Building classification. CBC identifies the Commercial Building Contractor classification.
  5. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate a person who meets the applicable requirements for the Commercial Building classification.
  6. Document qualifying experience. The applicable experience documentation must be completed as part of the qualification process.
  7. Complete required pre-license education. Virginia requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants.
  8. Complete the applicable examinations. CBC is among the classifications requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.
  9. Prepare and submit the contractor license application. Application Service is included with The 1 Package.
  10. Address ongoing contractor requirements. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included to help customers understand requirements relevant to establishing and operating the business.

Passing the Commercial Building technical examination is one part of the licensing process. The business must satisfy all applicable licensing requirements before a Virginia contractor license is issued.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Commercial Building Contractors as contractors whose work includes construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition on real property owned, controlled, or leased by another person involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures.

The CBC classification does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. It also does not authorize construction, repair, or improvement of dwellings and townhouses as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

Virginia contractor licenses are issued in Class A, Class B, and Class C. Current Virginia licensing information identifies the license class as the portion of the contractor license that determines the applicable monetary limitations on projects, while the classification or specialty determines what type of work the contractor may perform.

The Commercial Building classification requires an appropriately qualified individual. Virginia identifies CBC among classifications requiring examination pre-approval and an Experience Verification Form for the Qualified Individual.

New contractor applicants must also complete eight hours of approved pre-license education. The business may have additional requirements depending on the license class and organizational structure, including financial documentation for applicable license classes.

Test Information and Study Materials

CBC preparation should be organized around the examination content areas. For concrete, candidates should work with both the concrete construction and reinforcing steel references. For masonry, become familiar with terminology, materials, mortar, brick, block, stone, and standard construction practices.

Metals preparation requires candidates to distinguish between steel joist and steel deck subjects. Using the specialized references repeatedly helps develop that recognition. Carpentry preparation should include framing, materials, layout, and general building methods.

Thermal and moisture protection preparation should include roofing materials, systems, installation practices, and related estimating concepts. Associated-trades study should include drywall, gypsum systems, metal stud framing, insulation, moisture-control concepts, doors, windows, and glazing.

Safety preparation should include familiarity with 29 CFR Part 1926. Instead of trying to memorize the entire OSHA construction standard, candidates can learn how major topics are organized and practice finding requirements efficiently.

Estimating and plan-reading preparation should focus on understanding construction documents, quantities, dimensions, and the information typically required to interpret commercial projects. Building-code preparation should include navigation of the International Building Code and accessibility requirements in ICC A117.1.

How does the Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam work?
