Exam Details

The Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination is administered through PSI as part of the Virginia Board for Contractors examination program. The technical examination is associated with the Commercial Building Contractor classification, abbreviated CBC.

  • Examination: Commercial Building Technical Contractor
  • Classification: Commercial Building Contractor (CBC)
  • Scored Questions: 80
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 56 correct answers
  • Total Time Allowed: 180 minutes
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The published examination outline divides the scored questions among ten major construction areas:

  • Site Work: 8 questions
  • Concrete Including Reinforcement: 9 questions
  • Masonry: 9 questions
  • Metals: 9 questions
  • Carpentry: 9 questions
  • Thermal and Moisture Protection: 8 questions
  • Associated Trades and Finishes: 7 questions
  • Safety: 7 questions
  • Estimating and Plan Reading: 8 questions
  • General Building Code Requirements: 6 questions

Associated Trades and Finishes includes subjects such as drywall, metal stud framing, insulation and moisture control, doors, windows, and glazing. General Building Code Requirements includes life safety and egress, accessibility, fire protection requirements, and general code provisions.

Because the exam distributes questions across many different construction disciplines, candidates benefit from a balanced preparation plan. Concrete, masonry, metals, and carpentry each account for nine questions, while site work, thermal and moisture protection, and estimating and plan reading each account for eight questions. Safety, finishes, accessibility, and building-code topics are also significant parts of the examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because the testing center does not provide the books.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and reference materials containing written notes are not permitted. Candidates may not write in their references during the examination session.

All books in this rental package are highlighted and tabbed to support more organized studying and reference navigation. Candidates should still practice using every reference before examination day. The goal is to recognize the subject of a question and determine which book is most likely to contain the needed information before beginning a detailed search.

With a broad technical exam covering everything from concrete reinforcement and masonry to steel decking, roofing, accessibility, site work, and safety, book familiarity can be especially valuable. Practice using indexes, tables of contents, section headings, code chapters, tables, and permanent tabs so navigation becomes part of the study process.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. The Commercial Building Contractor classification is one of Virginia's primary contractor classifications.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Identify the Commercial Building classification. Candidates pursuing commercial building authority request the CBC classification as applicable to their contractor license.
  3. Designate a Qualified Individual. The business must identify a Qualified Individual who meets Virginia's requirements for the CBC classification.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirement associated with the requested license class.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor license applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
  6. Complete the required examination process. The Qualified Individual must complete the Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination when required for the classification.
  7. Complete any additional examinations applicable to the selected license class.
  8. Submit the contractor license application and required documentation.
  9. Meet the remaining business, financial, and licensing requirements applicable to the selected class.

Passing the CBC technical examination is one component of the Virginia contractor licensing process. Examination success does not by itself issue a contractor license.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Commercial Building Contractors as contractors whose work includes construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition on real property owned, controlled, or leased by another person involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures.

The CBC classification does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. Contractors performing those regulated functions must have the appropriate licensing or specialty authority. The CBC classification also does not authorize construction, repair, or improvement of dwellings and townhouses as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

The broad scope of the CBC classification explains the range of topics found on the technical examination. Commercial building contractors may encounter site preparation, structural concrete, reinforcement, masonry, structural steel, framing, roofing, moisture protection, interior finishes, accessibility, safety, estimating, plans, and building-code requirements.

Virginia contractor applicants must also satisfy requirements applicable to their selected license class, including Qualified Individual requirements, pre-license education, examination requirements, and application documentation.

Test Information and Study Materials

The CBC technical exam requires candidates to move between several construction disciplines. A useful preparation method is to organize study by the published content areas rather than reading every reference from beginning to end without a plan.

For site work, practice locating information involving excavation, earthwork, site preparation, and associated construction operations. Concrete preparation should include reinforcing steel, placement practices, concrete quality, and construction procedures. Masonry study should cover brick, block, stone, mortar, and common masonry construction principles.

For metals, become familiar with steel joists, joist girders, steel deck systems, structural handling practices, and erection concepts. Carpentry study should include framing and general construction methods. Thermal and moisture protection preparation should include roofing and moisture-control concepts.

The exam also evaluates associated trades and finishes, including drywall, metal stud framing, insulation, moisture control, doors, windows, and glazing. Safety questions make familiarity with OSHA construction standards important, while estimating and plan-reading questions require candidates to interpret project information and construction quantities.

Building-code preparation should include general code provisions, life safety, egress, accessibility, and fire-protection requirements. Because code questions depend on specific provisions, candidates should practice navigating the International Building Code and accessibility reference efficiently.

Highlighted and tabbed books can support this process by making major areas easier to find. Use the books repeatedly while studying so the tabs become familiar. A tab is most useful when you already understand why you are going to that section and what type of information you expect to find.
