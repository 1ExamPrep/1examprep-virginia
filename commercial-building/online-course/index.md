### Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination is an **open book test**. Candidates may bring approved reference materials into the examination center subject to PSI's reference-material rules.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs and temporary flags are not allowed. Reference materials containing written notes are not permitted, and candidates may not write in their references during the examination.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. The examination covers a large number of technical subjects, and searching through unfamiliar references can consume valuable testing time. Candidates should practice connecting each subject area with the appropriate reference before exam day.

A question involving occupancy, means of egress, fire protection, or other building-code requirements may lead to the **International Building Code 2021**. Accessibility questions may require familiarity with **Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities**, while construction safety questions should direct candidates toward **29 CFR Part 1926**.

Concrete questions may involve **The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction**, while reinforcing-steel questions may direct attention toward **Placing Reinforcing Bars**. Questions involving masonry can point toward **Modern Masonry**, and metals questions may require the steel joist technical digest or the **SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck**.

Practice identifying the subject first, selecting the appropriate reference second, and locating the information third. This method can help make open-book exam preparation more efficient.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the **Virginia Board for Contractors** within the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor licenses include a license class and one or more classifications or specialties that determine the work the business is authorized to perform.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the Commercial Building Contractor classification.** Virginia identifies this classification with the abbreviation CBC.
3. **Designate the Qualified Individual.** The business must identify a Qualified Individual for the classification requested.
4. **Meet applicable experience requirements.** The Qualified Individual must document the experience required for the applicable contractor license class.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable Board-approved pre-license education requirement.
6. **Complete applicable business examination requirements.** Class A and Class B applicants must satisfy the business examination requirements associated with the Designated Employee.
7. **Receive approval for the CBC technical examination.** CBC is a classification requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.
8. **Prepare for the technical exam.** Study the examination content areas and practice using the approved technical references.
9. **Schedule and take the examination through PSI.** Follow the current PSI registration process after receiving eligibility.
10. **Complete the licensing application process.** Passing the examination is one component of obtaining the Virginia contractor license and CBC classification.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Commercial Building Contractors** as contractors whose work includes construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition involving real property owned, controlled, or leased by another person for commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures.

The Commercial Building Contractor classification is abbreviated **CBC**. The classification includes a broad range of construction functions associated with qualifying commercial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings and structures, including applicable multi-family housing.

The CBC classification does **not** provide authorization for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. Contractors performing those activities must hold the classifications or specialty authority required for that work.

The classification also does not authorize construction, repair, or improvement of dwellings and townhouses as defined under the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

Virginia requires applicants seeking the CBC classification to identify a Qualified Individual and satisfy applicable experience and examination requirements. The Commercial Building classification is among the Virginia classifications requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.

Passing the technical CBC examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The contractor business must also satisfy the requirements applicable to its license class, business structure, Qualified Individual, Designated Employee when applicable, pre-license education, application, and other Board requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan for the Virginia CBC examination should follow the official content outline and devote time to every major category. Because several subjects carry similar question counts, candidates should avoid concentrating exclusively on one trade.

For **Site Work**, study excavation, grading, trenching, earthwork, site preparation, and equipment-related concepts. **Pipe and Excavation Contracting** provides a focused reference for these topics.

For **Concrete including Reinforcement**, review concrete materials, preparation, placing, finishing, curing, reinforcement, and bar-placement practices. Use **The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction** with **Placing Reinforcing Bars** to connect concrete work with reinforcement requirements.

The **Masonry** section requires familiarity with brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, tools, and common construction methods. **Modern Masonry** supports review of these trade subjects.

For **Metals**, candidates should become comfortable with steel joists, joist girders, steel decking, handling, erection, fastening, and related construction practices. Both the steel joist technical digest and the SDI steel deck manual are important references for this subject area.

**Carpentry** preparation should include framing, structural components, floor systems, walls, roofs, materials, layout, and construction terminology. Understanding practical construction processes can also make plan-reading and estimating questions easier to interpret.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** includes roofing and building-envelope considerations. Candidates should review roofing systems, materials, installation methods, moisture protection, and related construction concepts.

For **Associated Trades and Finishes**, review drywall, metal stud framing, insulation and moisture control, doors, windows, and glazing. The **Gypsum Construction Handbook** is particularly useful for gypsum wall and ceiling systems.

**Safety** preparation should include repeated navigation of **29 CFR Part 1926**. Candidates should know how to locate regulations involving common construction hazards rather than attempting to memorize the entire reference.

**Estimating and Plan Reading** requires practical construction knowledge combined with careful interpretation of dimensions, drawings, quantities, and project information. Practice reading questions carefully and keeping units consistent when calculations are required.

For **General Building Code Requirements**, become familiar with the organization of the **International Building Code 2021** and the accessibility reference. Review life safety, means of egress, accessibility, fire protection, and general code provisions.

Timed reference practice is especially useful. Read a practice question, identify its subject, choose the likely reference, locate the appropriate section, and confirm the answer. Repeating this sequence helps build both technical knowledge and open-book navigation skills.

## How does the Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam work?

The Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
