### Exam Details

The **Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination** is administered through **PSI** for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. The examination is associated with the Commercial Building Contractor classification, abbreviated **CBC**.

The examination contains **80 scored questions**. Candidates must achieve a minimum passing score of **70%, or 56 correct answers**. The examination provides **180 minutes** for completion. The examination program may also include experimental questions that are not scored.

The official content outline divides the scored examination into the following areas:

Associated trades and finishes include subjects involving drywall, metal stud framing, insulation and moisture control, doors, windows, and glazing. General building code requirements include life safety and egress, accessibility, fire protection requirements, and general code provisions.

The exam covers a wide range of commercial construction knowledge. Candidates should avoid relying on a single reference or subject area and instead build familiarity with the complete collection of technical books.

### State Requirements

The Virginia **Commercial Building Contractor (CBC)** classification includes construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, and demolition involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings or structures.

The CBC classification does **not** provide authorization for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. Contractors performing those services must have the appropriate licensing or specialty authority required by Virginia.

The CBC classification also does not provide for construction, repair, or improvement of dwellings and townhouses as those structures are defined under the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

The Commercial Building classification incorporates a broad range of construction functions, which is reflected in the technical examination. A CBC candidate must be prepared to demonstrate knowledge across structural systems, building materials, site work, finishes, roofing, safety, estimating, plans, accessibility, and building code requirements.

Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for the Commercial Building classification, and the Qualified Individual must successfully complete the approved technical examination in addition to satisfying applicable experience requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive CBC exam study plan should follow the official subject weighting. Concrete, masonry, metals, and carpentry each account for **9 questions**, making these four categories important areas for focused preparation.

For **concrete and reinforcement**, candidates should understand concrete construction procedures, reinforcement placement, forming, placement, finishing, curing, and common field practices. Use the concrete guide together with **Placing Reinforcing Bars** to connect concrete construction concepts with reinforcement requirements.

For **masonry**, review brick, block, stone, mortar, masonry layout, walls, openings, materials, tools, and construction procedures using **Modern Masonry**.

The **Metals** portion requires familiarity with steel construction. The steel joist technical digest and steel deck manual provide focused resources for subjects involving structural steel joists, joist girders, deck installation, handling, erection, and construction practices.

**Carpentry** preparation should include structural framing, construction terminology, layout, materials, tools, floor systems, walls, roofs, openings, and general building practices. **Carpentry and Building Construction** is particularly useful for strengthening practical construction knowledge in this category.

**Thermal and Moisture Protection** accounts for 8 questions. Candidates should understand roofing and building-envelope concepts, moisture protection, insulation, weather protection, and related construction methods.

**Site Work** also contains 8 questions. Use **Pipe and Excavation Contracting** to review excavation, grading, trenching, site preparation, and earthwork principles.

**Estimating and Plan Reading** contains 8 questions. Candidates should practice interpreting drawings, dimensions, construction information, and material quantities. Plan-reading questions often require both construction knowledge and careful interpretation of the information presented.

**Safety** accounts for 7 questions. Candidates should become familiar with the structure of OSHA's construction regulations and practice locating requirements in **29 CFR Part 1926**. Safety preparation may involve fall protection, excavation, scaffolding, ladders, personal protective equipment, tools, electrical hazards, material handling, and other construction operations.

**General Building Code Requirements** include life safety, egress, accessibility, fire protection, and general code provisions. Candidates should learn how the **International Building Code 2021** is organized and practice locating information using chapter titles, tables, section numbers, and the index.

Accessibility deserves dedicated preparation as well. Become familiar with terminology and requirements in **Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities**, particularly the organization of provisions governing accessible routes, clearances, doors, circulation, and building elements.

Open-book practice should be timed. Candidates can select a construction question, identify its subject, choose the appropriate reference, and locate the relevant information. Repeating this process helps develop faster book selection and reference navigation.

When reviewing missed practice questions, determine whether the problem came from technical knowledge, code interpretation, calculation, plan reading, or choosing the wrong reference. This method turns practice questions into a guide for future study.

## How does the Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam work?

The Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
