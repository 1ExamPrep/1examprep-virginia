### Open Book Test

The Virginia Commercial Building Technical Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing permitted references to the examination because the testing center does not provide the books.

An open-book examination should not be approached as a simple exercise in searching through references. Commercial building involves too many technical subjects for candidates to rely on unfamiliar books during a timed test. Preparation should include repeated use of the references so candidates know where different types of construction information are located.

Reference-navigation practice can begin by learning each book's purpose. OSHA regulations are associated with construction safety. The International Building Code addresses commercial building-code requirements. ICC A117.1 addresses accessibility. Specialized references cover masonry, reinforcing steel, concrete, gypsum systems, steel decking, steel joists, roofing, excavation, and carpentry.

When practicing, read the question and identify the trade or code subject before selecting a book. This prevents unnecessary searching through multiple references. Then use the table of contents, index, chapter structure, headings, and other navigation features to locate the relevant information efficiently.

Open-book preparation works best when reference navigation reinforces actual construction knowledge. A candidate who understands the terminology in a question is better able to select the correct book and locate the applicable section.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor businesses are licensed according to a license class and one or more classifications or specialty designations.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Commercial Building Contractor classification applies to qualifying construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, and demolition work involving commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental, and accessory-use buildings and structures.

Because commercial building construction involves numerous specialized disciplines, the CBC technical examination covers a broad collection of trade subjects. Candidates should be prepared to work with concepts involving building structure, concrete, masonry, carpentry, structural steel, roofing systems, accessibility, finishes, excavation, safety, estimating, and building-code requirements.

The CBC classification does not by itself provide unrestricted authority to perform separately regulated electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting work. Appropriate licensing or specialty authority is required when regulated work falls outside the authority provided by the Commercial Building classification.

Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the requirements applicable to the selected license class and classification. ### Test Information and Study Materials

Commercial Building Technical Contractor preparation should be organized by trade category. Instead of reading all fourteen references in sequence, candidates can build study sessions around major construction areas and pair each area with the appropriate books.

For site work and excavation, review site preparation, excavation terminology, earthwork methods, and safety considerations. Use Pipe and Excavation Contracting together with applicable OSHA material to connect field practices with construction safety.

For concrete preparation, work with both concrete construction and reinforcing steel references. Understand the relationship between concrete placement, reinforcement, structural performance, and field quality practices. Candidates should become comfortable distinguishing questions involving concrete itself from questions specifically involving reinforcing bars.

Masonry preparation should include brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, and commonly used construction procedures. The masonry reference should become the first source candidates consider when a question uses masonry-specific terminology.

Metals can involve steel joists, joist girders, and steel decking. Candidates should understand which specialized reference applies to each system. This is an area where simply remembering that information is contained in a "steel book" is not enough. Efficient preparation means knowing whether a question belongs in the steel joist reference or the steel deck manual.

Carpentry study should cover fundamental construction methods, framing, materials, and related building practices. Gypsum systems and interior construction should be reviewed separately using the Gypsum Construction Handbook.

Roofing and moisture protection require familiarity with roof assemblies, materials, construction procedures, and estimating concepts. Accessibility preparation should involve direct use of ICC A117.1 so candidates understand how dimensional and technical accessibility provisions are organized.

Building-code questions should be practiced with the International Building Code and Virginia code reference. Code preparation should focus on locating information efficiently rather than attempting to memorize an entire code book. Become familiar with chapter organization and frequently encountered subjects involving occupancy, egress, fire and life safety, and general commercial building requirements.

Construction safety is another distinct area of preparation. OSHA's construction standards are extensive, so candidates should develop familiarity with their organization and learn how to identify the section associated with a particular workplace hazard or operation.

## How does the Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam work?

The Commercial Building Contractor (CBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
