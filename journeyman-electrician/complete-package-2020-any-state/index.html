Exam Details

This package is aligned to a Journeyman Electrician exam that uses the 2020 National Electrical Code (NEC) and is taken as an open book test. Journeyman exams commonly evaluate a mix of:

  • Code navigation and application (finding the right article/table, applying rules correctly, identifying exceptions).
  • Electrical theory and calculations (practical math used in sizing, capacity planning, and load-related decisions).
  • Installation knowledge rooted in code compliance and jobsite standards.
  • Safety and professionalism as reflected through code requirements and correct interpretation.

Because the NEC is central to the test, the strongest strategy is to become extremely comfortable with how it’s organized—chapters, articles, sections, tables, annexes—and how quickly you can move from a question to the right location in the book. Your study plan should build two skills at the same time: (1) learning core concepts and (2) learning how to retrieve the correct code support fast.

Open Book Test

This is an Open Book Test, which means your results are strongly influenced by how efficiently you can use your NEC. Open-book testing rewards preparation, not guessing. Candidates who do best typically have:

  • Fast navigation habits (knowing where topics live and how to jump to them).
  • Practice using tables and exceptions (many code questions depend on the details).
  • Comfort with code language (understanding what the text is actually requiring).
  • A repeatable method for approaching questions: locate, confirm, apply, and eliminate wrong options.

Licensing Steps

Journeyman licensing processes vary by jurisdiction, but most follow a similar path. This section outlines the typical progression so you can map your preparation to the stage you’re in.

  1. Confirm eligibility based on experience and training history (often apprenticeship hours and/or supervised work history).
  2. Submit an application to the licensing authority or exam administrator for approval to test.
  3. Schedule the exam once approved and choose a testing date that allows time for structured preparation.
  4. Prepare with code-based study and calculations practice, focusing on both knowledge and speed.
  5. Take the open-book exam using your NEC, navigating efficiently and managing time carefully.
  6. Complete any final steps required for license issuance after passing (paperwork, fees, or documentation).

This complete package is built to support the step that makes the biggest difference: your preparation between approval-to-test and exam day. The better your study structure, the more confidently you can approach code questions and calculations without getting stuck.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
