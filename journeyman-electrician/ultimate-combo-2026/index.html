Open Book Test

The Virginia tradesman examination rules allow candidates to bring reference books. Study guides and sample examinations are not allowed in the exam room. Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam, but they must otherwise be unmarked and not written in. This makes preparation with the correct reference material and clean code navigation habits especially important.

Open-book testing does not mean the exam is easy. It means candidates must know how to locate information quickly, understand what the question is asking, and apply the correct rule under time pressure. A candidate who has not practiced with the code book may spend too much time searching and not enough time answering. State Requirements

Virginia regulates individual tradesmen through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Electrical tradesmen must hold the appropriate credential before performing regulated electrical work in the Commonwealth. The state’s tradesman licensing structure includes electrical licensing, and candidates must satisfy the Board’s education, experience, application, examination, and licensing requirements.

Virginia’s tradesman application process requires candidates to use the correct application for individuals seeking electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas fitting licensure. The application process is separate from business contractor licensing. A journeyman electrician candidate should focus on the individual tradesman license requirements, not a contractor business license, unless they are also pursuing a separate contractor license for a business entity.

Electrical candidates should also be aware that local ordinances or other requirements may apply depending on where they plan to work. State licensure is a major requirement, but local rules may affect work practices, permitting, inspection procedures, or business operations. How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
