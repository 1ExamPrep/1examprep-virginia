### Open Book Test

You’re preparing for an **open book** testing environment, where code navigation speed is a real advantage. Open book doesn’t mean easy—it means you’re expected to use the NEC efficiently while the clock keeps moving.

The strongest open-book strategy is a repeatable process:

- **Turn the question into a keyword:** “ampacity,” “box fill,” “conduit fill,” “bonding jumper,” “grounding electrode,” “GFCI,” “AFCI,” “motor circuit,” “service sizing,” etc.
- **Choose the fastest route:** use the index when the question wording is unfamiliar; use tabs when you recognize the article neighborhood.
- **Confirm the complete requirement:** read the full section and scan for exceptions, notes, and table footnotes.
- **Match the conditions to the scenario:** location, voltage, occupancy, equipment type, and wiring method can change the correct answer.
- **Keep your pace steady:** confirm, answer, move on—avoid long searches that disrupt your timing.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
