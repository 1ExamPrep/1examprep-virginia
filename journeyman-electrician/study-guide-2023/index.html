Exam Details

Virginia tradesman licensing is regulated by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR) through the Board for Contractors. The tradesman examinations are administered by PSI. PSI’s Virginia Individual License and Certification Candidate Information Bulletin lists the Journeyman Electrician portion as:

  • 90 items
  • 270 minutes to complete
  • Passing score: 63
  • Exam fee: $100 (fee is valid for one year from the date of payment; non-refundable and non-transferable)

The bulletin also provides a topic breakdown that includes both professional standards and technical electrical knowledge. Journeyman-level candidates should be ready for questions that cover ethics and professional responsibility (standards of practice, standards of conduct, renewal and reinstatement, definitions, qualification for licensure, revocation of licensure) as well as broad electrical competencies (services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, motors and controls, utilization equipment, lighting, and more).

Because the exam includes both rule-based and application-based questions, your study plan should train two skills at the same time: (1) recognizing what the question is really asking, and (2) confirming the correct rule quickly in your references.

Open Book Test

Virginia tradesman examinations are open book. PSI’s bulletin states that all examinations are open book and that you must bring your own references because they are not provided at the test site.

Open-book rules that matter on exam day:

  • No study guides are permitted.
  • No loose papers or unbound references are permitted.
  • If a reference is downloaded from the internet, it must be spiral bound or hole-punched and placed in a binder, or stapled in the left-hand corner.
  • References may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam.
  • Reference materials containing any writing are not allowed into the examination.
  • You may not write in reference materials during the exam session.
  • References may be tabbed with permanent tabs only; no removable tabs are allowed.

Open book doesn’t remove the pressure—it shifts it. The test rewards the electrician who can find and apply the correct requirement efficiently. That’s why timed practice is so effective: it forces you to develop a reliable method for locating answers, confirming exceptions, and moving forward without losing momentum.

Licensing Steps

Virginia’s Board for Contractors oversees the licensing process for individual tradesmen. The general pathway to a Virginia Journeyman Electrician license includes meeting eligibility requirements, applying for examination approval, completing the PSI exam, and maintaining your license through renewal.

  1. Confirm you meet journeyman eligibility. Virginia regulations list experience standards used for examination approval (see State Requirements below).
  2. Prepare documentation. DPOR requires applicants to submit documentation of identification, vocational training, and experience.
  3. Submit your application to the Board. The Board approves applications for testing, and PSI notes a notification is sent after your application is approved.
  4. Register and schedule through PSI. Once approved, you pay and schedule your exam through PSI’s registration process.
  5. Take and pass the Journeyman Electrician exam. Use an open-book strategy with strong reference navigation and time management.
  6. Renew on time. Electrician licenses in Virginia expire on a three-year cycle and have continuing education requirements tied to renewal.

This study guide is designed to support the “exam performance” part of that journey—helping you practice the same type of thinking you’ll need on test day, not just review concepts in the abstract.

State Requirements

Virginia’s DPOR Tradesman Exam & License Application lists multiple journeyman qualification pathways. For journeyman applicants, the application instructs candidates to select one option that matches their experience and training history. The listed routes include (among others):

  • Four years of practical experience in the trade and 240 hours of formal vocational training in the trade.
  • Five years of practical experience and 160 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Six years of practical experience and 80 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Seven or more years of practical experience and 40 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Associate’s degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year program in a tradesman-related field from an accredited community college or technical school, plus two years of practical experience in the trade.
  • Bachelor’s degree or certificate of completion in an engineering curriculum related to the trade, plus one year of practical experience in the trade.
  • Eight years of practical experience in the trade (verified by individuals who have observed the applicant’s work in the trade).
  • Individuals currently certified or licensed as a journeyman or master outside Virginia (with the required Letter of Good Standing and proof of licensure).

Because Virginia offers multiple routes, your best prep plan is one that focuses on what remains consistent across all applicants: NEC understanding, strong navigation habits, careful reading, and steady pacing. That’s what this combo is built to support.

Test Information and Study Materials

With 270 minutes to complete the Journeyman Electrician portion, many candidates assume time won’t be an issue. But long exams create a different challenge: staying consistent deep into the testing session. Your preparation should build both technical readiness and test stamina.

Use the 12 practice exams to train the skills the exam actually demands:

  • Question recognition: learn to spot whether you’re being tested on definitions, general electrical knowledge, installation requirements, grounding and bonding rules, conductor sizing concepts, raceways/boxes, motors/controls, or professional standards.
  • Reference navigation: practice locating the correct chapter, section, or topic efficiently. The goal is to confirm answers without turning every question into an extended search.
  • Answer discipline: reduce careless mistakes by reading the question carefully, noting qualifiers (like “most appropriate,” “required,” “minimum,” or “best”), and confirming what is actually being asked.
  • Pacing and momentum: learn when to confirm in the reference and when to move on, so you don’t sacrifice easy points for one time-consuming question.

A practical way to study with this guide:

  • Start with one timed practice exam. Use it as a baseline to identify what topics cost you the most points.
  • Review missed questions with purpose. Don’t stop at the correct answer—identify why your choice was wrong and what rule or concept you needed to apply.
  • Create a “repeat list.” Any topic you miss twice becomes a priority until it turns into a strength.
  • Save the 2 full final exams for the end. Treat them like dress rehearsals: timed, distraction-free, and as close to exam conditions as possible.

Open-book exams reward a calm, methodical approach. When you’ve practiced enough, you stop “hoping you’ll find it” and start knowing exactly how you’ll handle each question type.

FAQ Section

Is the Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam open book?

Yes. PSI’s Virginia tradesman Candidate Information Bulletin states that all examinations are open book, and candidates must bring their own references because they are not provided at the test site.

How many questions are on the Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam?

PSI lists the Journeyman Electrician portion as 90 items.

How much time do I get to complete the exam?

PSI lists 270 minutes to complete the Journeyman Electrician portion.

What score do I need to pass?

PSI lists a passing score of 63 for the Journeyman Electrician portion.

Who regulates Journeyman Electrician licensing in Virginia?

Virginia tradesman licensing is regulated by DPOR through the Board for Contractors, and PSI administers the examinations.

What experience is required to be approved to take the journeyman exam?

Virginia regulations state that an applicant for examination approval to be licensed as a journeyman in a trade must meet one of the listed experience standards, including holding a valid journeyman license in the trade for at least one year or having nine years of practical experience in the trade.

What reference books are listed for the Virginia electrician exam?

PSI lists the Virginia Board for Contractors Individual License and Certification Regulations as the regulatory reference for all tradesman exams and lists electrician references including NFPA 70 (National Electrical Code) and the American Electricians’ Handbook (16th edition). Code questions are based on the code edition listed in the bulletin.

Can I highlight or tab my reference books?

PSI states references may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam, and references may be tabbed with permanent tabs only (no removable tabs). PSI also states reference materials containing any writing are not allowed into the examination.

How often do Virginia electrician licenses renew, and is continuing education required?

Virginia regulations state electrician licenses expire three years from the last day of the month in which they were issued. For renewal or reinstatement, the regulations require three hours of continuing education for the electrical designation from a Board-approved provider.

How should I use the two full final exams?

Use them late in your prep as readiness checks. Take each final under timed conditions, then review results to identify the last weak areas to tighten up before you sit for your PSI exam.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
