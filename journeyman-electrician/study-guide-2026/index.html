Exam Details

Virginia’s Journeyman Electrician exam is administered through PSI as part of the Commonwealth of Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR) tradesman program. The PSI Candidate Information Bulletin outlines the Journeyman Electrician portion as:

  • Total Questions: 70 items
  • Time Allowed: 210 minutes
  • Passing Score: 49
  • Exam Fee: $100

The exam outline includes both trade knowledge and Virginia regulatory topics. That means strong preparation is balanced: you practice NEC-driven electrical questions and you get comfortable with the licensing and conduct topics that can show up as “easy points” if you’ve trained for them.

Virginia’s PSI outline for the Journeyman Electrician portion includes categories such as services/feeders/branch circuits, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, motors and controls, utilization equipment, lighting, low voltage requirements, communication systems, and fire detection/alarm systems—along with standards of practice and conduct and other licensing-related topics. A practice-exam plan is one of the best ways to prepare for that kind of mixed blueprint.

Open Book Test

Yes—Virginia’s PSI bulletin states that all examinations are open book. Open book is a real advantage only if you train for it correctly. Open book does not mean open time. It means the exam rewards candidates who can quickly locate the right rule and apply it accurately without getting lost in their references.

Virginia’s open-book rules are strict enough that your prep should match them:

  • You must bring your own references (references are not provided at the test site).
  • No study guides are permitted in the exam room.
  • No loose papers or unbound references are allowed.
  • References may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam, but reference materials containing any writing are not allowed into the examination.
  • Permanent tabs only; no removable tabs.

Because the exam is open book, the winning strategy is not to memorize everything. The winning strategy is to become fast and accurate at the actions that earn points:

  • Read the question twice before opening the book. Identify the task: definition, requirement, exception, table value, or calculation setup.
  • Go to the “code neighborhood” first. Start at the likely chapter/article/part, then narrow down. Random flipping wastes time.
  • Confirm exceptions and notes. Many wrong answers come from missing one exception or one table note.
  • Protect your pace. If a question turns into a time sink, move on and return later after securing easier points.

This study guide supports open-book success by giving you repeated practice opportunities to navigate, confirm, decide, and move on—until that process feels automatic.

Licensing Steps

Virginia’s Journeyman Electrician licensing path runs through DPOR’s Board for Contractors tradesman program. While each applicant’s documentation can look different, the general flow is consistent:

  1. Meet Virginia’s examination-approval requirements for Journeyman Electrician (experience and education pathway).
  2. Submit your application for examination approval through the Board for Contractors process.
  3. Receive approval and schedule your PSI exam through the Virginia tradesman testing system.
  4. Prepare your test-day references to comply with open-book rules (no writing, permanent tabs only, no study guides).
  5. Take the exam and pass by meeting or exceeding the required passing score.
  6. Complete remaining licensing requirements and follow DPOR instructions for license issuance and renewal.

This product is designed to strengthen the step you can control most: exam performance. When you’ve trained with realistic, timed practice, the testing room feels less stressful and your decision-making stays steadier.

State Requirements

Virginia’s Board for Contractors regulations list multiple experience-and-education combinations that can qualify an applicant for examination approval as a Journeyman Electrician. The regulation includes a clear table of options, including:

  • Bachelor’s degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade + 1 year of practical experience in the trade.
  • Associate degree or certificate from at least a two-year program in a tradesman-related field + 2 years of practical experience.
  • 240 hours of formal vocational training in the trade + 4 years of practical experience.
  • 160 hours of formal vocational training in the trade + 5 years of practical experience.
  • 80 hours of formal vocational training in the trade + 6 years of practical experience.
  • 40 hours of formal vocational training in the trade + 7 years of practical experience.
  • No formal vocational training + 8 years of practical experience.

These options help many electricians qualify through different pathways, but they also highlight why early planning matters. If you’re close to eligibility, a practice-based study plan helps you build readiness steadily rather than cramming at the end of your approval window.

Reference Books

Virginia’s PSI bulletin lists the references used to develop Journeyman Electrician exam questions and notes that candidates must bring their own references. Key electrician references listed include:

  • Virginia Board for Contractors – Individual License and Certification Regulations
    Regulatory reference for all tradesman examinations, supporting licensing, standards of conduct, renewal topics, and other Virginia rules that can appear on the exam.
  • National Electrical Code (NEC), NFPA 70
    The core reference for code rules, definitions, installation requirements, and tables. Train fast navigation, accurate exception reading, and correct table usage under timed conditions.
  • American Electricians’ Handbook, 16th Edition
    A trade reference supporting electrical fundamentals, practical knowledge areas, and broader theory concepts commonly tested at the journeyman level.

Because the exam is open book but time-limited, the goal is to make your references work for you—through familiarity, clean navigation, and practiced lookup habits.

Test Information and Study Materials

Virginia’s outline makes it clear that journeyman success is about breadth and consistency. Even if your day-to-day work is heavily residential, commercial, industrial, or service-focused, the exam expects competency across multiple NEC areas plus basic electrical fundamentals and Virginia licensing/regulatory topics.

Here’s a practical way to use your 12 practice exams plus 2 full final exams as a complete prep system:

  • Step 1: Take a timed diagnostic exam. Use Practice Exam 1 to find your baseline. Work straight through without pausing to “study” mid-exam. This shows where you lose points—slow lookups, rushed reading, weak topics, or pacing issues.
  • Step 2: Build a simple miss log. For every missed question, record the reason in one short line: misread, wrong code section, missed exception, table note overlooked, calculation setup error, or Virginia-regulation topic gap.
  • Step 3: Review by proving the answer. Don’t just memorize the correct choice. Locate the supporting NEC section (or regulation reference) and confirm why it applies. This trains the open-book skill that matters most: accurate confirmation under time pressure.
  • Step 4: Train “two-pass pacing.” First pass: answer questions you can solve efficiently and keep moving. Second pass: return to tougher questions with the time you protected. This prevents one hard question from stealing time from ten easier ones.
  • Step 5: Use the 2 full final exams as simulations. Save them for late in your prep. Take them timed, in a quiet setting, with minimal interruptions. Review carefully afterward—your biggest improvements often happen after a full simulation because it reveals pacing and endurance issues you can still fix.

Practice exams also help you eliminate the “small leaks” that cost big points: choosing an answer without reading an exception, grabbing a table value without checking notes, confusing similar NEC rules, or rushing through a question that hinges on one key phrase. The more you practice, the less those patterns show up.

FAQ Section

Is the Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam open book?

Yes. Virginia’s PSI Candidate Information Bulletin states all examinations are open book. Candidates must bring their own approved references, and strict rules apply (no study guides, permanent tabs only, and no writing in reference materials).

How many questions are on the Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam?

The PSI bulletin lists the Journeyman Electrician portion as 70 items.

How long do I have to complete the exam?

The PSI bulletin lists 210 minutes to complete the Journeyman Electrician portion.

What score do I need to pass?

The PSI bulletin lists a passing score of 49 for the Journeyman Electrician portion.

What does Virginia require for exam approval as a journeyman electrician?

Virginia’s Board for Contractors regulations list multiple qualifying options based on combinations of education (such as vocational hours or degrees) and practical experience, including pathways like 240 hours of training with four years of experience, up to eight years of experience with no training.

Can I highlight and tab my code book?

Virginia’s PSI bulletin allows references to be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam and allows permanent tabs only. References containing any writing are not permitted into the exam.

What’s the best way to study if I work full-time?

Use short timed sessions during the week for practice and review (20–45 minutes), then reserve longer blocks for full practice exams and your two final simulations. This builds speed and confidence without burnout.

How do I get faster at open-book NEC questions?

Speed comes from repetition with intention. When you miss a code-based question, locate the exact NEC section or table that supports the correct answer and practice finding that same location again later. Over time, you recognize where information lives and waste less time searching.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
