Exam Details

Virginia electrician licensure is regulated through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, Board for Contractors, Tradesmen Program. The Board licenses tradesmen, including electricians, and PSI administers the individual license and certification examinations for approved candidates. Candidates must submit the required application materials and receive approval before scheduling the exam.

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is commonly listed as 70 questions with 210 minutes allowed. The passing score is 70%, which equals 49 correct answers. The exam is open book and should be prepared for as a timed trade examination covering both electrical knowledge and Virginia tradesman regulatory material.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is an open-book examination. Open book does not mean easy. Candidates still need to understand electrical concepts, recognize how questions are written, locate information quickly, apply code rules correctly, and manage time across 70 questions in 210 minutes.

Open-book electrical exams reward reference familiarity. Students should practice using the National Electrical Code table of contents, index, article structure, definitions, tables, exceptions, notes, and common code sections before exam day. Students should also review Virginia tradesman regulations because the exam includes state-specific regulatory content that is separate from NEC article lookup.

Licensing Steps

Virginia journeyman electrician licensing begins with meeting the Board for Contractors Tradesmen Program entry requirements. Candidates must qualify through a combination of practical trade experience, formal vocational training, approved education, or other pathways accepted by the Board before they can become licensed.

A common Virginia journeyman electrician pathway includes four years of practical experience in the trade and 240 hours of formal vocational training. Other experience and education combinations may also qualify, including additional years of practical experience with reduced formal training hours or qualifying education from an accredited community college or technical school with practical experience. Applicants should use the current Virginia tradesman application instructions to determine the exact path that applies to their background.

A typical Virginia journeyman path includes gaining practical electrical experience, completing the required formal vocational training or approved education, documenting experience with the required forms, submitting the tradesman exam and license application, receiving approval from the Board, scheduling the PSI examination, passing the Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam, and completing the remaining license issuance steps.

Experience documentation is important. Candidates should keep records of employers, dates of work, practical experience, training completion, certificates, transcripts, supervisor information, application forms, exam approval notices, exam results, and licensing documents. Accurate records help support the application and reduce delays during the review process.

Virginia master electrician licensure is a separate step. A master electrician credential generally requires additional qualification beyond the journeyman level, such as experience as a licensed journeyman or other verified experience accepted by the Board, along with passing the master examination. Contractor licensing is also separate and relates to business authority, license class, and specialty classification.

State Requirements

Virginia has statewide tradesman licensing through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation and the Board for Contractors. The Board licenses individual tradesmen and also licenses contractor businesses. This product is focused on the individual Virginia Journeyman Electrician license and the electrical knowledge needed for journeyman-level exam preparation.

For Virginia journeyman electrician licensure, candidates must meet one of the Board’s accepted combinations of practical experience and formal vocational training or education. A widely used route is four years of practical experience with 240 hours of formal vocational training. Students should understand the difference between individual tradesman licensure and contractor licensing. A journeyman electrician license supports individual trade qualification. A Virginia contractor license involves a business license class and a classification or specialty that determines the type and value of contracting work the business may perform.
