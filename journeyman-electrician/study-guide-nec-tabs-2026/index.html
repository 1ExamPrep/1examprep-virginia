The Virginia Journeyman Electrician examination is administered by PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The examination is computer-based and is used to evaluate whether a candidate has the electrical knowledge needed for journeyman-level work. Licensing Steps

  1. Review the Virginia journeyman electrician qualification paths. Virginia allows candidates to qualify through combinations of practical experience and formal vocational training, certain education-based routes, or verified experience-only routes.
  2. Complete the required experience and training. Common Virginia journeyman paths include four years of practical experience with 240 hours of formal vocational training, five years with 160 hours, six years with 80 hours, seven years with 40 hours, or ten years of verified practical experience.
  3. Gather documentation. Candidates should collect experience verification forms, certificates of completion, official transcripts when applicable, and identification required for the application.
  4. Submit the Tradesman Exam and License Application. Applicants must apply through the Virginia licensing process and follow DPOR instructions for examination and license approval.
  5. Prepare for the journeyman electrician exam. Study electrical theory, code application, calculations, services, feeders, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, equipment, and safety.
  6. Schedule and take the PSI examination. Candidates approved for testing schedule through PSI and follow the candidate information bulletin for testing-center rules.
  7. Pass the journeyman electrician exam. The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is listed as 70 questions, 210 minutes, and a 70% passing score.
  8. Complete final licensing requirements. After passing, candidates must satisfy any remaining DPOR requirements before the license is issued.
  9. Maintain the license. Licensed tradesmen must follow Virginia renewal and continuing education requirements that apply to their license classification.

State Requirements

Virginia electrician licensing is regulated by the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. The Board regulates individual tradesmen, including electricians, and establishes entry requirements based on education, examination, and experience.

Virginia journeyman electrician candidates may qualify through several pathways. A common path includes four years of practical experience and 240 hours of formal vocational training. Other experience-and-training combinations include five years of practical experience with 160 hours of formal vocational training, six years with 80 hours, or seven years with 40 hours. Virginia also recognizes certain education-based routes, including an associate degree or certificate from a two-year tradesman-related program from an accredited community college or technical school plus practical experience, and a bachelor’s degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade plus practical experience.

Virginia also provides an experience-only route in which ten years of practical experience may be verified by individuals who observed the applicant’s work. Candidates should keep accurate records of employers, supervisors, dates, job duties, training hours, certificates, and transcripts. Proper documentation is important because licensing approval depends on the applicant meeting the required pathway.

Virginia’s journeyman license allows the individual to perform electrical work within the scope of the license and applicable law. Broader contracting activity may require a contractor license or business credential in addition to individual tradesman licensure. Students planning to operate a business or contract directly with customers should review the licensing requirements that apply to contractors and business entities.

Because licensing rules affect the legal ability to perform electrical work, candidates should treat exam preparation and licensing compliance as separate responsibilities. This product helps with study and NEC navigation, while the candidate remains responsible for following Virginia’s application, documentation, testing, and renewal requirements.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
