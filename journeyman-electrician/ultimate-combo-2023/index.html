Exam Details

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is administered through PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, Board for Contractors. Candidates must satisfy the education and work experience requirements set by the Board before applying for the examination. Once the application is approved, the candidate may schedule the exam through PSI.

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician portion contains 70 items. The passing score is 49 correct answers, and candidates are allowed 210 minutes to complete this portion. The exam is designed to confirm that the applicant has the minimum level of knowledge needed to work as a licensed Virginia journeyman electrician.

Exam content includes Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, Renewal and Reinstatement, Definitions, Qualification for Licensure, Revocation of Licensure, General Electrical Knowledge, Requirements for Electrical Installations, Electrical-Electronic Fundamentals, Services, Feeders, Branch Circuits, Service and Feeder Requirements, Grounding and Bonding, Conductors and Cables, Raceways and Boxes, Special Occupancies, Special Equipment, Electrical Signs and Outline Lighting, Motors and Controls, Utilization Equipment, Lighting, General Low Voltage Requirements, Communication Systems, and Fire Detection and Alarm Systems.

Because the exam includes both trade knowledge and regulatory topics, candidates should not limit study time to code lookup alone. A strong preparation plan includes reviewing Virginia rules, understanding standards of practice, practicing electrical calculations, reading electrical questions carefully, and becoming more familiar with how multiple-choice exam questions are written.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is an open book test. Candidates must bring their own references because references are not provided at the test site. Open book testing can be helpful, but it still requires serious preparation. Candidates must know how to locate information quickly, understand what the question is asking, apply the correct rule, and manage time across the full exam.

Virginia exam reference rules do not allow study guides in the examination room. Loose papers and unbound references are not allowed. Downloaded references must be properly bound, hole-punched and placed in a binder, or stapled in the left-hand corner. References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam, but reference materials containing writing are not allowed. Candidates may not write in reference materials during the exam.

References may be tabbed with permanent tabs only. Removable tabs are not allowed. This makes reference preparation important before test day. Candidates should organize approved materials early, avoid handwritten notes, and practice using references in the same clean format they expect to use for the exam.

The National Electrical Code 2023 Paperback with Tabs supports open book preparation by helping candidates practice finding major code sections during study. Tabs can make the book easier to navigate, while repeated use helps candidates build a working memory of the code layout. The strongest open book candidates usually know where to begin looking before they open the book.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia journeyman electrician licensing path begins with meeting the Board for Contractors’ education and work experience requirements. Candidates must complete the required background before applying for the examination. The Board reviews the application, and candidates are notified after approval.

A typical path includes completing qualifying electrical work experience, completing required pre-license education or vocational training as applicable, gathering documentation, submitting the Tradesman Exam and License application, receiving Board approval to test, registering and scheduling the exam through PSI, taking the Virginia Journeyman Electrician examination, passing the required exam portion, and completing the licensing process through DPOR.

Application organization matters. Candidates should keep experience verification records, education documents, training records, identification, and application materials together before beginning the process. Missing or incomplete documentation can slow down approval and make the licensing process more stressful.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen who work in electrical, plumbing, HVAC, gas fitting, water well construction, elevator mechanics, backflow prevention, and related regulated trades. Virginia uses the term certification for individual tradesmen, but state certification is mandatory for these trades unless an exemption applies.

Applicants for a Virginia tradesman license must satisfy the Board’s education and work experience requirements before applying for examination. Candidates should use the most current application forms and submit the required information for the correct trade and license level.

Virginia requires pre-license education for tradesmen applicants. For journeyman and master tradesman licenses, continuing education is also required before renewal. For the electrical trade, the continuing education course must be 3 hours in length and must be approved by the Board for Contractors.

Tradesman licenses may also be affected by local ordinances, laws, or requirements imposed by other state agencies, courts, or localities. State licensure does not remove the need to comply with local rules that apply where work is performed.

Virginia also provides application options for reciprocity, exam agreements, universal license recognition, military experience, and other credential-related pathways when applicable. Candidates using one of those pathways should organize all supporting documentation before applying.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
