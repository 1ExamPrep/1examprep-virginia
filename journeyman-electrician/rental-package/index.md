### State Requirements

The **Virginia Board for Contractors** regulates individual tradesmen, including Journeyman and Master Electricians. Virginia provides several combinations of formal education or vocational training and practical experience that can qualify an applicant for Journeyman Electrician licensure.

An applicant with a **bachelor's degree in an engineering curriculum related to the electrical trade** may qualify with one year of practical experience in the trade.

An applicant with an **associate degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year tradesman-related program** may qualify with two years of practical experience.

Virginia also recognizes the following combinations of formal vocational training and practical experience for Journeyman Electrician applicants:

- **240 hours of formal vocational training** plus four years of practical experience.
- **160 hours of formal vocational training** plus five years of practical experience.
- **80 hours of formal vocational training** plus six years of practical experience.
- **40 hours of formal vocational training** plus seven years of practical experience.

Virginia regulations also include additional pathways involving approved apprenticeship and other recognized training or experience. Applicants must provide documentation that supports the specific qualification method being used.

Virginia requires **pre-approval from the Board** before a candidate can sit for a Virginia-administered tradesman examination. Applicants should submit complete experience and education documentation so the Board can determine eligibility.

After licensure, Virginia Journeyman Electricians must complete Board-approved continuing education as a condition of renewal. Electricians are required to complete an approved **three-hour electrical continuing-education course** during each applicable renewal period.

Continuing-education courses must be approved by the Virginia Board for Contractors. The course must be completed before the license expiration date for it to satisfy the renewal requirement.

Virginia's Journeyman Electrician license is an individual tradesman credential. It is separate from a Virginia contractor business license. A company or individual contracting for work that falls under Virginia's contractor licensing laws must separately satisfy the applicable contractor licensing requirements.

## How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
