Exam Details

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is administered by PSI for the Commonwealth of Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. The Board for Contractors approves applications for testing, and candidates must apply through the required Virginia tradesman exam and license process before scheduling the examination.

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician portion includes 70 items. The passing score is 49 correct answers, and candidates are allowed 210 minutes to complete this portion. The exam outline includes both Virginia regulatory topics and electrical trade knowledge. Regulatory topics include standards of practice, standards of conduct, renewal and reinstatement, definitions, qualification for licensure, and revocation of licensure.

Electrical trade topics include general electrical knowledge, requirements for electrical installations, electrical-electronic fundamentals, services, feeders and branch circuits, service and feeder requirements, grounding and bonding, conductors and cables, raceways and boxes, special occupancies, special equipment, electrical signs and outline lighting, motors and controls, utilization equipment, lighting, general low-voltage requirements, communication systems, and fire detection and alarm systems.

The exam is designed to measure minimum competency in the principles, practices, statutes, and regulations that apply to Virginia tradesmen. Candidates should study the full outline instead of focusing only on one section. While some areas have more questions than others, smaller categories can still affect the final score and should be included in a complete preparation plan.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam is open book. Candidates must bring their own approved references because references are not provided at the test site. No study guides are permitted in the examination room, and no loose papers or unbound references are permitted. Downloaded references must be properly bound according to PSI’s rules.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. Reference materials containing writing are not allowed into the examination. Candidates may not write in reference materials during the examination session. References may be tabbed with permanent tabs only, and removable tabs are not allowed.

Open book preparation should focus on speed, accuracy, and familiarity. Candidates should practice finding definitions, table values, article sections, branch-circuit rules, service and feeder rules, grounding and bonding provisions, conductor requirements, raceway rules, and motor-related requirements before exam day. The more familiar the NEC becomes during study, the easier it is to use the book efficiently during an open book exam.

Licensing Steps

Virginia journeyman electrician licensing is handled through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Candidates seeking the Journeyman Electrical designation must apply through the Tradesman Exam and License Application process and meet one of the qualifying education, training, or experience pathways before taking the exam.

Common journeyman qualification pathways include four years of practical experience in the trade and 240 hours of formal vocational training; five years of practical experience and 160 hours of formal vocational training; six years of practical experience and 80 hours of formal vocational training; seven or more years of practical experience and 40 hours of formal vocational training; an associate degree or certificate from at least a two-year tradesman-related program from an accredited community college or technical school plus two years of practical experience; or a bachelor’s degree or certificate of completion from an accredited college or university in an engineering curriculum related to the trade plus one year of practical experience.

Virginia also provides a pathway based on eight years of practical experience verified by individuals who have observed the applicant’s work in the trade, and a pathway for individuals currently certified or licensed as a journeyman or master by a governing body located outside the Commonwealth of Virginia. Applicants must provide the required documentation for the pathway they select.

The general licensing path includes selecting the Journeyman Electrical designation, submitting the required application and attachments, receiving approval from the Board, scheduling the PSI exam, taking the Virginia Journeyman Electrician exam, and completing any remaining licensing steps required by the Board. Candidates should keep experience verification forms, training records, transcripts, license certifications, and identification documents organized throughout the process.

A practical study timeline should begin before exam approval. Candidates can review the exam content outline, set a study schedule, use the Virginia Journeyman Electrician Study Guide for trade and regulatory review, work through the Electrician Calculations Study Guide for math practice, and use the National Electrical Code 2026 Paperback to build code navigation speed.

State Requirements

Virginia regulates individual tradesmen through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. The Journeyman Electrical designation applies to individuals seeking Virginia tradesman licensure in the electrical trade. Applicants must meet the Board’s requirements for experience, training, education, examination, reciprocity, apprenticeship, or card exchange, depending on the method of licensure selected.

For the examination pathway, journeyman applicants must select one qualifying method and provide the required attachments. These may include an Individual Experience Verification Form, certification of completion, official transcripts, apprenticeship documentation, certification or letter of good standing from another jurisdiction, or other required application materials based on the applicant’s situation.

Virginia requires applicants to be at least 18 years old and to provide identifying information as part of the application process. Applicants must also answer disciplinary and criminal history questions and certify that the information provided is accurate. Application fees are not refundable, and candidates should read the application instructions carefully before submitting materials.

Any applicant performing work over $1,000 must contact the Board for Contractors to obtain a Virginia contractor license. The individual journeyman license and the business contractor license are separate licensing matters. A journeyman electrician preparing for the individual tradesman exam should understand the difference between personal trade licensure and business contractor licensing.

Because Virginia’s journeyman electrician exam includes both regulatory and trade subject areas, candidates should study more than the NEC alone. Standards of practice, conduct, renewal, reinstatement, qualifications, and revocation appear on the outline along with electrical installation and code topics. A complete study plan should include both Virginia licensing rules and electrical trade content.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
