Exam Details

Virginia tradesman licensing is administered through the Commonwealth of Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR), and DPOR contracts with PSI to conduct its examination program. PSI’s Candidate Information Bulletin for Virginia tradesman exams explains that candidates must take the specific examination for the license subcategory they are applying for (including Journeyman Electrician).

For the Journeyman Electrician portion, PSI’s current bulletin lists:

  • 70 items
  • Passing score: 49
  • Time allowed: 210 minutes

PSI’s bulletin also lists the Journeyman Electrician examination fee as $100. The exam is designed to test a minimum level of knowledge regarding principles, practices, statutes, and regulations tied to the trade, so your preparation is strongest when you study in a way that blends Code comprehension with performance-based practice.

Open Book Test

PSI’s Virginia Candidate Information Bulletin states: all examinations are open book. It also specifies important rules about reference materials that directly affect how you should prepare:

  • You must bring your own references (references are not provided at the test site).
  • No loose papers or unbound references are permitted.
  • References may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam.
  • Reference materials containing any writing are not allowed into the examination.
  • Permanent tabs only are allowed; no removable tabs.

That’s exactly why the National Electrical Code 2023 paperback with tabs is included in this combo. In open-book testing, the NEC isn’t just a reference—it’s the tool you use to earn points. Tabs support the skill that matters most: fast, accurate navigation.

When you study with a tabbed NEC, you can train a repeatable “lookup routine” that holds up under time pressure:

  • Step 1: Identify the topic category (services, branch circuits, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, special occupancies, etc.).
  • Step 2: Use tabs to jump to the most likely chapter or Article area quickly.
  • Step 3: Read the full rule and scan for exceptions before you commit.
  • Step 4: Confirm tables and notes when sizing, ratings, or conductor rules are involved.

The goal isn’t to flip pages faster. The goal is to stop searching and start locating. That’s how you protect your time and improve your accuracy at the same time.

Licensing Steps

Virginia journeyman licensing is handled through DPOR’s tradesmen program, and applications are submitted with the required documentation for the method of licensure you’re using. A practical path most candidates follow looks like this:

  1. Choose the correct trade designation and method of licensure
    DPOR’s Tradesman Exam & License Application requires you to select the trade designation (Journeyman Electrical) and one method of licensure.
  2. Complete the required experience and training pathway
    Virginia allows multiple qualification routes based on practical experience paired with formal vocational training hours (or approved education/degree options).
  3. Gather attachments and verification forms
    The application lists required attachments depending on your route—such as experience verification and training completion documents or transcripts.
  4. Submit your application and fee to DPOR
    Application fees are not refundable, and DPOR requires your payment method to be submitted with the application package.
  5. After approval, schedule your exam with PSI
    PSI’s bulletin explains DPOR has contracted with PSI to conduct the tradesman examination program.
  6. Prepare for open-book performance
    Build speed with your code-book routine while practicing questions until your pacing feels consistent.

This combo is focused on the step that makes the biggest difference for most candidates: preparation that improves performance under a clock. You’ll be practicing the same way you’ll be tested—finding the right rule, verifying it, and answering decisively.

State Requirements

Virginia’s DPOR Tradesman Exam & License Application lists multiple journeyman qualification pathways. For journeyman applicants, the application instructs candidates to select one option that matches their experience and training history. The listed routes include (among others):

  • Four years of practical experience in the trade and 240 hours of formal vocational training in the trade.
  • Five years of practical experience and 160 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Six years of practical experience and 80 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Seven or more years of practical experience and 40 hours of formal vocational training.
  • Associate’s degree or certificate of completion from at least a two-year program in a tradesman-related field from an accredited community college or technical school, plus two years of practical experience in the trade.
  • Bachelor’s degree or certificate of completion in an engineering curriculum related to the trade, plus one year of practical experience in the trade.
  • Eight years of practical experience in the trade (verified by individuals who have observed the applicant’s work in the trade).
  • Individuals currently certified or licensed as a journeyman or master outside Virginia (with the required Letter of Good Standing and proof of licensure).

Because Virginia offers multiple routes, your best prep plan is one that focuses on what remains consistent across all applicants: NEC understanding, strong navigation habits, careful reading, and steady pacing. That’s what this combo is built to support.

How to use the Virginia Journeyman Electrician Study Guide

Your study guide is your practice engine. Use it to build coverage across the NEC and trade knowledge areas while developing better decision-making under time pressure.

  • Work in timed sets: even 10–20 question sets build pacing and reduce hesitation.
  • Track misses by topic: write down the category (services, grounding, wiring methods, motors, special occupancies, tables, etc.).
  • Prove answers in the Code: for every missed question, locate the supporting NEC section and read the rule and exceptions.
  • Repeat weak categories: improvement comes from revisiting the same topics until your accuracy becomes consistent.

This turns your study guide into a roadmap. Every missed question tells you exactly what to work on next.

How to use the Tabbed NEC (2023) to build speed

Code speed is built through repetition. Tabs help you practice with a consistent routine so you spend less time flipping pages and more time applying the rule correctly.

  • Navigation drill (10–15 minutes): pick one topic (like wiring methods), then locate 5–10 related sections quickly using tabs and headings.
  • Exception drill: for any section you find, immediately scan and read exceptions—many exam questions are built around what changes the general rule.
  • Table drill: practice locating the same high-traffic tables repeatedly so you stop losing time when a question depends on notes and footnotes.

As your navigation improves, you’ll notice a shift: the Code starts to feel smaller and more familiar—because you know where to go.

A realistic weekly routine for working electricians

  • 2–4 study sessions per week (45–90 minutes): study-guide practice sets + code verification.
  • 2 short navigation drills (10–15 minutes): tabbed NEC lookups by topic.
  • 1 review block (20–30 minutes): redo a small set from your weakest topic category and confirm improvement.

Consistency beats marathon sessions. Regular code lookups are what make open-book exams feel manageable.

FAQ

What’s included in the Virginia Study Guide & NEC Combo?

Is the Virginia journeyman exam open book?

Yes. PSI’s Virginia Candidate Information Bulletin states that all examinations are open book.

How many questions are on the Journeyman Electrician portion?

PSI’s bulletin lists the Journeyman Electrician portion as 70 items with 210 minutes to complete that portion.

What score do I need to pass?

PSI’s bulletin lists the passing score for the Journeyman Electrician portion as 49.

What are the rules for tabs and markings in reference books?

PSI states references may be highlighted, underlined, and/or indexed prior to the exam, but reference materials containing any writing are not allowed. PSI also states references may be tabbed with permanent tabs only and that no removable tabs are allowed.

What are some Virginia qualification routes to apply for the journeyman exam and license?

DPOR’s Tradesman Exam & License Application lists multiple pathways, including combinations of practical experience and vocational training hours (such as four years of experience with 240 hours of training), as well as degree-based options and an experience-verification route.

Why do tabs matter if the exam is open book?

Open book rewards navigation skill. Tabs help you practice faster lookups, reduce time wasted searching, and build a repeatable routine for finding the correct section, exceptions, and tables under timed conditions.

How does the Journeyman Electrician exam work?

The Journeyman Electrician exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
