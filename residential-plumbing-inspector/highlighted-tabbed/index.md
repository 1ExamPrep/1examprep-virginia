### Test Information and Study Materials

The 2021 P1 Residential Plumbing Inspector exam contains **60 questions** and provides **2 hours** for completion. The time limit means candidates should develop a study strategy that includes timed reference lookup rather than relying only on untimed reading.

ICC identifies several primary subject areas for P1 preparation. These include **General Requirements, Fixtures, Water Heaters, Water Supply and Distribution, Sanitary Drainage, and Vents**. Each of these subjects deserves focused attention during preparation.

General requirements form the foundation for interpreting residential plumbing provisions. Candidates should understand how the code establishes scope, terminology, installation principles, and relationships between different sections. Definitions are especially important because a question may depend on the precise code meaning of a familiar plumbing term.

Fixture-related study should include learning where the code addresses plumbing fixtures and how requirements concerning installation, clearances, connections, and related provisions are organized. Instead of memorizing random sections, candidates should understand the structure of the relevant chapters so they can locate specific requirements when needed.

Water heater preparation should include familiarity with the portions of the code dealing with installation and associated safety requirements. Questions can require candidates to recognize which provisions apply to a residential water heater installation and then locate supporting requirements in the code.

Water supply and distribution is another major study area. Candidates should practice navigating requirements related to residential water distribution systems and become comfortable reading any applicable tables and dimensional provisions. Code tables require careful attention because the correct answer can depend on headings, conditions, notes, and specific variables stated in the question.

Sanitary drainage is an important area for plumbing inspectors because drainage installations must satisfy numerous design and installation requirements. Candidates preparing for the P1 exam should become comfortable locating drainage provisions and distinguishing them from requirements associated with venting or water distribution.

Venting should be treated as a separate study category. Candidates should understand where venting provisions are located and practice identifying which venting rule applies to a particular scenario. Questions may require careful reading of both the problem and the applicable code section.

A useful preparation routine is to begin each practice question by identifying its subject before touching the book. Decide whether the question is primarily about fixtures, water heaters, supply and distribution, drainage, venting, or a general requirement. Then move to the relevant chapter or section using the tabs, table of contents, or index.

Once you find the likely section, read enough surrounding material to understand the full requirement. Avoid selecting an answer based only on a highlighted sentence without considering exceptions, limitations, definitions, tables, or referenced provisions. Code questions are often written to test whether the candidate notices exactly which condition applies.

Timed practice is particularly valuable because the P1 exam allows approximately two minutes per question on average. During preparation, practice locating answers within a similar time window. If a question is taking too long, learn to recognize when it may be more efficient to mark it for later review and continue through the exam.

## How does the Residential Plumbing Inspector (P1) exam work?

The Residential Plumbing Inspector (P1) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

- Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
- The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
- Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
- Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
