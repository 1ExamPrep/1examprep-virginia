### Test Information and Study Materials

The best way to prepare for the ICC P1 Residential Plumbing Inspector exam is to study directly from the International Residential Code while practicing code lookup under timed conditions. Candidates should begin by learning the layout of the book. Review the table of contents, plumbing chapter titles, definitions, major tables, exceptions, and section headings. Knowing where information is located helps reduce stress and saves time during the exam.

Fixtures are an important study area. Candidates should understand requirements related to fixture installation, fixture fittings, minimum required fixtures, clearances, approved materials, connections, and inspection considerations. Fixture questions may require candidates to identify proper installation requirements or determine whether a described condition meets the code.

Water heaters are another major topic. Candidates should review provisions related to installation, location, access, safety devices, temperature and pressure relief, discharge piping, protection, and related inspection requirements. Water heater questions often require careful reading because small details in the question can affect the correct answer.

Water supply and distribution should be studied carefully. Candidates should become familiar with pipe materials, sizing concepts, pressure requirements, protection from contamination, valves, supports, joints, and installation requirements. Inspection questions may involve determining whether a water distribution system is properly installed, protected, tested, or supported.

Sanitary drainage is a core P1 topic. Candidates should review drainage pipe sizing, slope, cleanouts, fixture connections, fittings, materials, joints, and installation rules. Drainage questions may require table use or careful application of minimum slope and sizing requirements. Students should practice reading tables and checking notes because drainage provisions often depend on exact conditions.

Venting is one of the most important areas for residential plumbing inspector preparation. Candidates should review vent types, vent sizing, vent connections, vent termination, wet venting, common venting concepts, and minimum installation requirements. Venting questions can be challenging because candidates must understand both the purpose of the vent and the code rules that govern its installation.

Traps and cleanouts should also be reviewed in detail. Candidates should understand trap requirements, trap seals, prohibited traps, cleanout access, cleanout locations, and inspection expectations. These topics frequently appear in residential plumbing inspection because they affect system function, maintenance access, and health protection.

Materials, joints, connections, grades, pipe support, and testing are also important. Candidates should know how to locate approved materials, determine proper joining methods, check support spacing, review required tests, and identify installation conditions that may damage building components or compromise system performance.

A strong study plan should include topic review, code reading, lookup drills, and timed practice. Candidates should avoid relying only on field experience. Field experience is valuable, but the exam is based on the code. The correct answer is the one supported by the International Residential Code, even when common jobsite practices vary.

## How does the Residential Plumbing Inspector (P1) exam work?

The Residential Plumbing Inspector (P1) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

- Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
- The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
- Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
- Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
