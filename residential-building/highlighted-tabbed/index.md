### Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is a Qualified Individual technical examination associated with the RBC classification. Virginia requires the Qualified Individual for this classification to satisfy the applicable experience requirements and successfully complete the Board-approved technical examination.

The RBC examination contains **80 questions**. Candidates have **160 minutes** to complete the examination and must achieve a minimum passing score of **70%, or 56 correct answers**.

The examination covers a wide range of residential construction subjects:

- **Layout:** 7 questions
- **Excavation:** 6 questions

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors within the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor licenses are issued to businesses and consist of a license class combined with one or more classifications or specialties.

1. **Select the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The class determines the applicable level of contracting activity.
2. **Select Residential Building Contracting.** The classification abbreviation is RBC.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
4. **Meet the required experience qualification.** The Qualified Individual must document the experience required for the license class and classification.
5. **Complete the RBC examination approval process.** Residential Building is among the classifications requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.
6. **Prepare for and pass the RBC examination.** Candidates should study residential construction principles, code requirements, and reference navigation.
7. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor businesses must satisfy the Board-approved pre-license education requirement.
8. **Complete applicable business examinations.** Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must provide the required information for its class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, RBC classification, and other applicable requirements.

The RBC technical exam is one component of the overall licensing process. Passing the examination does not by itself result in contractor licensure. The contractor business must satisfy all requirements associated with its selected license class and classification before a license can be issued.

### State Requirements

Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty placed on a contractor license. The Qualified Individual must be either a full-time employee of the business meeting the applicable employment requirement or a member of Responsible Management.

Current Virginia contractor application requirements state that a Qualified Individual must have at least **two years of applicable experience for a Class C license**, **three years for Class B**, and **five years for Class A**. An Experience Verification Form is required when the requested designation requires examination pre-approval.

Residential Building is specifically identified as one of the classifications requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and pass the appropriate examination.

Virginia also requires new contractor businesses to complete **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education**. For Class A and Class B applicants, the pre-license education must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of Responsible Management completes the requirement.

The pre-license course is a general contractor business course covering regulations, statutes, and requirements relevant to operating a contractor business. It is separate from the RBC technical examination.

The Residential Building classification covers construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, and demolition involving dwellings and townhouses as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, including accessory buildings and structures on those properties.

The RBC classification does not itself provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. It also does not authorize work on commercial, industrial, institutional, or governmental structures outside the residential buildings covered by the classification.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A strong RBC preparation strategy should follow the progression of a residential building project. Begin with layout and excavation, then move into foundations and framing. This approach helps candidates understand how individual exam subjects connect as parts of a complete construction process.

Framing should receive substantial attention. Candidates should review floor systems, walls, ceilings, roofs, openings, structural connections, common construction methods, and applicable residential code provisions. Use Carpentry and Building Construction for practical explanations and the International Residential Code for specific code requirements.

Exterior construction is the largest individual content area on the exam. Review exterior wall systems, weather protection, openings, windows, doors, exterior finishes, decks, walks, ramps, and other residential exterior components. Understanding how construction techniques relate to code requirements can improve preparation for questions presented from either perspective.

Prints, Plans, and Specifications is another important category. Practice reading residential construction drawings, identifying symbols and dimensions, interpreting notes, and understanding how plans communicate the relationship between building components.

Safety and Employee Protection also deserves focused preparation. Residential building projects involve hazards associated with excavation, elevated work, tools, ladders, scaffolding, material handling, framing, and other construction activities. Candidates should be comfortable with general construction safety principles.

Timed practice is also helpful. With approximately two minutes available per question, candidates should learn when a reference lookup is useful and when they already know enough to answer without spending extra time searching.

When a practice question is missed, find the supporting information in the appropriate book and review the complete section. This method reinforces both the technical concept and the location of the information, helping develop stronger open-book examination habits.

## How does the Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam work?

The Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
