About this Residential Building Contractor (RBC) package

Prepare for the Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination with a comprehensive collection of residential construction, building code, business, law, project management, and technical references. The Virginia Residential Building Contractor Book Package brings together key resources for candidates preparing for the Virginia Residential Building Contractor (RBC) classification and the broader contractor licensing process.

Virginia defines a Residential Building Contractor as a contractor whose work includes the construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition of dwellings and townhouses, as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, along with accessory buildings and structures located on those properties. The RBC classification is designed for contractors whose work is focused on residential construction rather than commercial, industrial, institutional, or governmental buildings.

The scope of the Virginia RBC classification is broad. Residential construction can involve site layout, excavation, foundations, structural framing, insulation, ventilation, windows, doors, exterior construction, decks, ramps, interior and exterior finishing, masonry, plans and specifications, safety, and other building activities. Successful exam preparation therefore requires familiarity with both construction principles and code requirements.

This package combines the International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021 with construction references such as Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016 and the Builders Technical Supplement, 2011. It also includes the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2018 and NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 14th Edition for broader contractor business, law, project-management, and regulatory study.

Because Virginia contractor examinations are open book, candidates benefit from becoming familiar with their references well before test day. Owning the correct books is an important first step, but effective preparation also involves understanding how the references are organized, learning where major subjects are located, and practicing how to move quickly from an exam question to the appropriate chapter, table, index entry, or code section.

Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is a Qualified Individual examination associated with the RBC classification. The Virginia Board for Contractors requires candidates seeking this classification through examination to receive the applicable approval and successfully complete the Board-approved technical examination.

The current RBC examination contains 80 scored questions. Candidates are allowed 160 minutes to complete the examination and must achieve a minimum score of 70%, or 56 correct answers.

The examination covers a broad range of residential building topics, including:

  • Layout: residential construction layout and related building concepts.
  • Excavation: site preparation and excavation principles.
  • Foundations: residential foundation systems and construction requirements.
  • Framing: structural framing methods and residential construction practices.
  • Insulation: insulation concepts and applicable construction requirements.
  • Ventilation: residential ventilation requirements and principles.
  • Windows, Doors, and Skylights: installation and code-related considerations.
  • Exterior Construction: exterior materials, assemblies, and residential building practices.
  • Decks, Walks, and Ramps: construction methods and applicable residential requirements.
  • Finishing: residential finishing materials and construction practices.
  • Masonry: masonry construction concepts applicable to residential buildings.
  • Swimming Pools and Hot Tubs: applicable residential construction knowledge.
  • Prints, Plans, and Specifications: interpreting construction documents and project information.
  • Safety and Employee Protection: safe construction practices and employee protection.
  • Construction Debris, Cleanup, and Disposal: jobsite cleanup and disposal practices.

The examination may include questions based on the listed references as well as trade knowledge and general industry practices. Candidates with extensive residential construction experience can benefit from that practical background, but the written exam can also require precise code knowledge and familiarity with terminology that may not be used every day in the field.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the testing site.

Virginia's contractor examination program currently bases applicable ICC code questions on the 2021 edition. The International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021 is therefore an important reference for current RBC code preparation.

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs, temporary flags, and similar removable indexing products are not allowed. References containing handwriting are not permitted, and candidates may not write in their books during the examination session.

Loose papers and unbound references are also prohibited. Candidates using permitted downloaded reference material must prepare it in an acceptable bound format before arriving for the examination.

With 80 scored questions and 160 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per scored question. This makes reference-navigation skills important. A candidate who understands the construction material and knows where information is located can use the books selectively rather than searching through multiple references for every question.

During preparation, practice identifying the subject first. A code question may lead to the 2021 International Residential Code, while a construction-method question may be better addressed through Carpentry and Building Construction or the Builders Technical Supplement. Developing that judgment can make open-book testing significantly more manageable.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors within the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor licenses consist of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The class establishes the level of contracting activity permitted and carries its own qualification requirements.
  2. Select Residential Building Contractor. The applicable classification abbreviation is RBC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must have a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested on the license.
  4. Meet the required experience level. The Qualified Individual must document the minimum amount of relevant experience associated with the contractor class being pursued.
  5. Complete the RBC examination approval process. Residential Building is one of the classifications requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the appropriate technical examination.
  6. Prepare for and pass the RBC examination. Candidates should study residential construction principles and become familiar with the applicable technical and code references.
  7. Complete Virginia contractor pre-license education. New contractor applicants must complete the required eight-hour Board-approved pre-license education course.
  8. Complete applicable business examinations. Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee business examination requirements.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. The business must provide the required information regarding Responsible Management, the Qualified Individual, license class, RBC classification, and other applicable qualifications.

The RBC technical examination is only one component of obtaining a Virginia contractor license. Candidates should prepare for the technical examination while also addressing the business and administrative requirements associated with the contractor class they intend to obtain.

State Requirements

Virginia requires each contractor business to identify a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty on its license. Residential Building is specifically listed among the classifications requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and successfully complete an examination.

The Qualified Individual must be either a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management. Virginia currently requires at least one year of applicable experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

An Experience Verification Form is required for a Qualified Individual seeking pre-approval for a designation that requires an examination. Candidates should make sure their documented experience corresponds to the residential building work required for the RBC classification.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. This course addresses general contractor business and regulatory requirements and is separate from preparation for the RBC technical examination.

The scope of the RBC classification includes construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, and demolition involving dwellings and townhouses as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, including accessory buildings and structures on the property.

The RBC classification does not by itself authorize electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. It also does not authorize the contractor to construct, remove, repair, or improve commercial, industrial, institutional, or governmental structures outside the dwellings and townhouses covered by the classification.

The Residential Building classification does encompass a broad range of residential construction functions, which is why the examination covers many different building subjects. Candidates should be prepared to work across the full range of residential construction knowledge rather than limiting study to one specialty or phase of construction.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong RBC study plan should follow the major residential construction phases. Begin with layout, excavation, and foundations so the preparation process follows the natural progression of a residential building project. Review site layout concepts, excavation practices, foundation construction, and the corresponding residential code requirements.

Framing deserves significant attention because it is a major part of residential building work. Study floor, wall, ceiling, and roof framing along with structural connections, openings, spans, and common residential construction methods. Use the IRC for specific code requirements and Carpentry and Building Construction for broader construction understanding.

Exterior construction should include review of weather protection, exterior finishes, openings, windows, doors, skylights, decks, walks, ramps, and other residential assemblies. Understanding both construction technique and code compliance can help candidates handle questions that approach the same topic from different directions.

Plans and specifications should also be part of regular preparation. Residential contractors frequently work from drawings and written specifications, and the RBC examination includes questions involving construction-document interpretation. Practice identifying dimensions, details, symbols, notes, and relationships between different parts of a residential project.

Safety and employee protection should not be overlooked. Residential construction exposes workers to hazards involving excavation, ladders, scaffolds, tools, heights, structural work, and material handling. Candidates should be comfortable with basic construction safety concepts relevant to contractor operations.

Open-book practice should be performed throughout the study process. Choose a question, identify the subject, select the most appropriate book, and locate the supporting information. Repeat the process using the index, table of contents, code chapters, technical tables, and other navigation tools.

When a practice question is answered incorrectly, locate the source material and review the surrounding section. Understanding why the correct answer applies creates stronger preparation than memorizing individual answers without context.

Frequently Asked Questions

What books are included in the Virginia Residential Building Contractor Book Package?

The package includes NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 14th Edition; Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2018; International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021; Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016; and Builders Technical Supplement, 2011.

Is the Virginia Residential Building Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The Virginia RBC examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not provided at the testing site.

How many questions are on the Virginia RBC examination?

The current examination contains 80 scored questions. Candidates receive 160 minutes and need a minimum score of 70%, or 56 correct answers.

What topics are covered on the RBC exam?

The exam covers residential building subjects including layout, excavation, foundations, framing, insulation, ventilation, windows and doors, exterior construction, decks and ramps, finishing, masonry, swimming pools and hot tubs, plans and specifications, safety, and construction cleanup.

Can I highlight and tab my RBC exam books?

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed. Removable tabs and handwritten notes are not permitted.

What does the Virginia RBC classification cover?

The classification covers construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition of dwellings and townhouses, as defined by the USBC, including applicable accessory buildings and structures on those properties.

Does the RBC classification include electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas fitting work?

No. The Residential Building classification does not by itself authorize electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services.

Can an RBC contractor work on commercial buildings?

The RBC classification does not authorize construction, repair, removal, or improvement of commercial, industrial, institutional, or governmental structures outside the dwellings and townhouses covered by the classification.

How much experience does a Virginia RBC Qualified Individual need?

The minimum experience depends on the contractor class: one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A in the applicable classification or specialty.

Does passing the RBC exam automatically issue a Virginia contractor license?

No. Passing the RBC examination is one component of the licensing process. The contractor business must also satisfy the requirements for its selected license class, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, applicable business examinations, and completed application.

How does the Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam work?

The Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
