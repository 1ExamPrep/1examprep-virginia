Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is the technical examination associated with the RBC classification. Virginia requires the Qualified Individual for RBC to receive the applicable pre-approval and successfully complete the Board-approved examination before the classification can be added to a contractor license through the examination pathway.

Virginia uses PSI to administer Qualified Individual contractor examinations. Candidates must complete the eligibility and approval process before scheduling the applicable technical examination. The current Virginia Qualified Individual specialty examination fee is $85, paid directly to PSI and separate from the package pricing shown above.

Residential Building Contractor preparation covers a broad range of construction knowledge. Candidates should be ready to study residential building methods, layout, excavation, foundations, structural framing, insulation, ventilation, exterior construction, windows and doors, decks and ramps, finishing, masonry, construction plans, specifications, safety, and other residential building topics.

The examination can include information found in listed references as well as trade knowledge and accepted construction practices. Practical residential building experience is therefore valuable, but candidates should also prepare to work with written code language, technical tables, construction terminology, plans, and reference-based questions.

For candidates pursuing Class A or Class B contractor licensing, separate contractor business examination requirements apply to the Designated Employee. Class B requires the applicable General and Virginia portions, while Class A requires the applicable General, Virginia, and Advanced portions. These business examinations are distinct from the RBC technical examination completed by the Qualified Individual.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination uses an open-book test format with permitted reference books. Candidates are responsible for bringing the approved references they intend to use because books are not supplied at the examination center.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should understand which book is most appropriate for different types of questions and learn how to use tables of contents, indexes, chapters, code sections, technical tables, and permanent tabs efficiently.

Virginia's PSI examination rules allow reference books to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Reference books must otherwise remain unmarked. Study guides and sample examinations are not permitted as examination references.

Some book(s) included in this Ultimate package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. The prepared references can make important sections easier to identify, but candidates should still spend time learning what the tabs represent and how the books are organized.

A residential code question may direct the candidate to the International Residential Code, while a practical framing or carpentry question may be better addressed through Carpentry and Building Construction or the Builders Technical Supplement. Contractor business questions may require a NASCLA guide or Virginia regulatory reference.

Practicing this decision-making process before test day can improve reference efficiency. The objective is not to search every book for every answer. Candidates should develop enough technical knowledge to recognize the subject and then use the appropriate reference when detailed confirmation is needed.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. A business pursuing Residential Building Contracting must satisfy the requirements associated with its chosen contractor class and the RBC classification.

  1. Determine the contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Residential Building Contracting. The applicable Virginia classification abbreviation is RBC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the RBC classification.
  4. Document the required experience. The Qualified Individual must satisfy Virginia's applicable experience requirement for the contractor class being pursued.
  5. Complete the RBC examination approval process. Residential Building is a classification requiring pre-approval and successful completion of the applicable technical examination.
  6. Prepare for the RBC examination. Study residential construction methods, code requirements, technical references, plans, safety, and open-book navigation.
  7. Complete contractor pre-license education. Virginia requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses.
  8. Complete Class A or Class B business examinations when applicable. The Designated Employee must successfully complete the examination portions required for the selected license class.
  9. Complete the contractor license application. The firm must provide information regarding Responsible Management, the Qualified Individual, Designated Employee when applicable, license class, and RBC classification.
  10. Submit the required licensing materials. The Board reviews the contractor application and supporting information before issuing the license.

State Requirements

Virginia requires every contractor business to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on its license. Residential Building is one of the classifications requiring pre-approval and successful completion of an examination.

The Qualified Individual must be either a full-time employee of the contractor business or a member of Responsible Management. Current Virginia requirements establish a minimum of one year of applicable experience for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

An Experience Verification Form is required for Qualified Individuals seeking designations that require pre-approval for an examination. Candidates preparing for RBC should therefore have qualifying residential building experience documented as part of the licensing process.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the course must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of Responsible Management completes the requirement.

The pre-license education course is a general contractor business course and is separate from the RBC technical examination and separate from the Class A or Class B Designated Employee examinations.

The Residential Building classification applies to residential building work within Virginia's defined RBC scope. It does not independently provide authority for separately regulated electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services. A contractor business performing those services must have the appropriate licensing authority for the work.
