Exam Details

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is a Qualified Individual technical examination associated with the RBC classification. Residential Building is one of the classifications for which the Qualified Individual must receive the applicable approval and successfully complete the required Board-approved technical examination.

The RBC examination contains 80 questions. Candidates receive 160 minutes to complete the examination and must achieve a minimum passing score of 70%, or 56 correct answers.

The examination covers a broad range of residential construction subjects, including:

  • Layout: residential construction layout and project setup.
  • Excavation: excavation and site-preparation concepts.
  • Foundations: residential foundation systems and related construction requirements.
  • Framing: floor, wall, ceiling, and roof framing concepts.
  • Insulation: insulation methods and residential building requirements.
  • Ventilation: applicable ventilation principles and construction requirements.
  • Windows, Doors, and Skylights: installation and residential code considerations.
  • Exterior Construction: exterior materials, systems, and assemblies.
  • Decks, Walks, and Ramps: construction methods and residential requirements.
  • Finishing: interior and exterior finishing concepts.
  • Masonry: residential masonry construction principles.
  • Swimming Pools and Hot Tubs: applicable residential construction knowledge.
  • Prints, Plans, and Specifications: understanding construction drawings and written project information.
  • Safety and Employee Protection: construction safety concepts.
  • Construction Debris, Cleanup, and Disposal: jobsite cleanup and disposal practices.

The examination may include information found directly in the references as well as general trade knowledge and accepted construction practices. Candidates should therefore combine reference-book study with their practical understanding of residential construction.

Virginia uses PSI as its examination vendor for Qualified Individual contractor specialty and classification exams. Candidates must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the RBC technical examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the examination site.

Open-book testing makes efficient reference navigation important. With 80 questions and 160 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per question. A candidate who attempts to search through multiple unfamiliar books for every answer can quickly use valuable testing time.

A more effective approach is to develop enough residential construction knowledge to recognize the subject being tested and then use the books strategically when a code requirement, table, technical detail, or confirmation is needed.

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed under applicable testing rules, while removable tabs are not. Candidates should also follow examination requirements regarding handwritten notes, loose papers, and binding.

The online exam prep course supports open-book preparation by encouraging candidates to work with the references throughout the study process. When reviewing foundations, framing, exterior construction, or another topic, candidates can identify which reference is most useful and practice locating the supporting information.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. A contractor license consists of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Residential Building Contracting. The classification abbreviation is RBC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The amount of experience required depends on the contractor license class.
  5. Obtain approval for the RBC examination. Residential Building is one of the classifications requiring technical examination pre-approval.
  6. Prepare for and pass the RBC technical examination. Candidates should review residential construction knowledge, code requirements, technical references, and open-book navigation.
  7. Complete the required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education.
  8. Complete applicable business examinations. Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. The business must provide the required information for its class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, RBC classification, and other applicable requirements.

The RBC examination is one component of Virginia contractor licensing. Passing the technical exam does not by itself create a contractor license. The business must satisfy all requirements associated with its selected contractor class and classification before the Board issues the license.

State Requirements

Virginia requires every contractor firm to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty shown on the license. The Qualified Individual must have the experience and additional qualification required for the requested designation.

For a Class C contractor, the Qualified Individual must have at least one year of applicable experience. A Class B Qualified Individual must have at least three years of applicable experience, while a Class A Qualified Individual must have at least five years of applicable experience.

The Qualified Individual must also be at least 18 years old and must be a full-time employee of the firm or a member of Responsible Management as required by Virginia contractor regulations.

Residential Building is among the classifications requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and successfully complete a technical examination. Candidates should document relevant residential building experience as required through the application process.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The pre-license education is a general business course covering statutes, regulations, and requirements applicable to contractor businesses. It is separate from preparation for the RBC technical examination.

The Residential Building classification covers work on dwellings and townhouses as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, including applicable accessory buildings and structures. It does not authorize electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services and does not extend to commercial, industrial, institutional, or governmental structures outside the residential scope defined for RBC.

Reference Books

  • NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 14th Edition
    This contractor-focused reference supports study of business organization, project management, estimating, contracts, financial management, risk management, employment considerations, and other contractor business subjects. It can also help candidates preparing for broader contractor licensing requirements beyond the RBC technical examination.
  • Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2018
    This Virginia-specific code reference provides state building-code and regulatory information useful for understanding construction requirements within the Commonwealth and the relationship between Virginia rules and model building codes.
  • International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021
    This residential code reference covers requirements affecting one- and two-family dwellings and townhouses. Candidates should become familiar with provisions involving building planning, foundations, floors, walls, roofs, means of egress, exterior construction, structural systems, and other residential building requirements.
  • Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016
    This practical construction reference supports review of residential building methods, carpentry, foundations, floor systems, framing, roofs, exterior construction, interior work, materials, tools, and construction-document concepts.
  • Builders Technical Supplement, 2011
    This technical supplement provides additional material useful for residential building contractor examination preparation. It works alongside the IRC and construction textbook to strengthen review across specialized building and technical subjects.

The books serve different purposes, and candidates should learn which reference is most useful for particular types of questions. A question asking for an exact residential code requirement may require the IRC, while a construction-method question may be easier to address through Carpentry and Building Construction or the Builders Technical Supplement.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive RBC study strategy can follow the normal progression of residential construction. Begin with layout and excavation, then move through foundations, framing, exterior construction, and finishing. Studying in this order helps candidates understand how different phases of a project relate to one another.

Foundations and framing deserve careful review. Candidates should understand foundation types, floor systems, walls, openings, roof structures, structural connections, and residential construction techniques. Use the International Residential Code when precise requirements are needed and the carpentry reference for broader construction explanations.

Exterior construction should include exterior wall systems, weather protection, windows, doors, roofs, finishes, decks, walks, and ramps. Many residential construction questions require candidates to understand both how an assembly is built and what code provisions govern it.

Prints, plans, and specifications should also be incorporated into regular study. Practice reading dimensions, symbols, notes, details, and relationships between different parts of a set of residential construction documents.

Safety and employee protection remains important for residential contractors. Review safe work practices involving excavation, elevated work, ladders, scaffolds, tools, framing, material handling, and other hazards encountered during building projects.

Reference navigation should be practiced throughout the course. When answering a question, first identify whether it is primarily asking about code, construction technique, business information, or another technical subject. Then select the reference most likely to contain the answer.

Practice using the index rather than scanning the book page by page. Learn common terminology and alternate terms that may appear in an index. Become familiar with chapter organization so that you can narrow a search before opening the reference.

Timed practice is also valuable. The RBC examination provides approximately two minutes per question on average. During practice, avoid spending too long on a single reference lookup. Learn to answer what you know, use the books efficiently when needed, and return to more difficult questions when appropriate.

When an answer is incorrect, locate the underlying information in the appropriate reference and review the surrounding section. This helps candidates understand the principle instead of memorizing an isolated answer that may be presented differently on the actual examination.

The online exam prep course provides a structured framework for this process. Candidates can review construction topics, practice questions, identify weaknesses, and return to the references for reinforcement. This repeated cycle helps make study more purposeful and helps candidates develop greater familiarity with both residential construction concepts and the books.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does the Virginia Residential Building Contractor Online Exam Prep Course cover?

The course focuses on residential building subjects including layout, excavation, foundations, framing, insulation, ventilation, exterior construction, windows and doors, finishing, masonry, plans and specifications, safety, and reference navigation.

Is the Virginia Residential Building Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The RBC examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references and should become familiar with them before examination day.

How many questions are on the Virginia RBC examination?

The examination contains 80 questions. Candidates receive 160 minutes and must achieve a minimum score of 70%, or 56 correct answers.

What books should I use while preparing for the RBC exam?

The provided references include NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, 14th Edition; Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2018; International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021; Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016; and Builders Technical Supplement, 2011.

Does the online course replace the reference books?

No. The course is designed to work alongside the reference books. Candidates should use the books regularly during preparation so they become familiar with their organization and content.

Can I highlight and tab my exam books?

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed under the applicable examination rules, while removable tabs are not permitted.

What does the Virginia RBC classification cover?

The classification covers construction, remodeling, repair, improvement, removal, or demolition involving dwellings and townhouses as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, including applicable accessory buildings and structures.

Does the RBC classification include electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting work?

No. The Residential Building classification does not itself provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services.

How much experience does the Qualified Individual need?

The minimum applicable experience is one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Does Virginia require contractor pre-license education?

Yes. New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This requirement is separate from the RBC technical examination.

Does passing the RBC examination automatically issue a contractor license?

No. Passing the RBC technical examination is one part of the licensing process. The contractor business must also satisfy applicable requirements for its license class, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, business examinations when required, and completed application.

How does the Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam work?

The Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
