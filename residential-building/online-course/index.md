### Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Building Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the examination site.

Open-book testing makes efficient reference navigation important. With 80 questions and 160 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes per question. A candidate who attempts to search through multiple unfamiliar books for every answer can quickly use valuable testing time.

A more effective approach is to develop enough residential construction knowledge to recognize the subject being tested and then use the books strategically when a code requirement, table, technical detail, or confirmation is needed.

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed under applicable testing rules, while removable tabs are not. Candidates should also follow examination requirements regarding handwritten notes, loose papers, and binding.

The online exam prep course supports open-book preparation by encouraging candidates to work with the references throughout the study process. When reviewing foundations, framing, exterior construction, or another topic, candidates can identify which reference is most useful and practice locating the supporting information.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. A contractor license consists of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select Residential Building Contracting.** The classification abbreviation is RBC.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The amount of experience required depends on the contractor license class.
5. **Obtain approval for the RBC examination.** Residential Building is one of the classifications requiring technical examination pre-approval.
6. **Prepare for and pass the RBC technical examination.** Candidates should review residential construction knowledge, code requirements, technical references, and open-book navigation.
7. **Complete the required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education.
8. **Complete applicable business examinations.** Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must provide the required information for its class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, RBC classification, and other applicable requirements.

The RBC examination is one component of Virginia contractor licensing. Passing the technical exam does not by itself create a contractor license. The business must satisfy all requirements associated with its selected contractor class and classification before the Board issues the license.

### State Requirements

Virginia requires every contractor firm to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty shown on the license. The Qualified Individual must have the experience and additional qualification required for the requested designation.

For a **Class C** contractor, the Qualified Individual must have at least **one year of applicable experience**. A **Class B** Qualified Individual must have at least **three years of applicable experience**, while a **Class A** Qualified Individual must have at least **five years of applicable experience**.

The Qualified Individual must also be at least 18 years old and must be a full-time employee of the firm or a member of Responsible Management as required by Virginia contractor regulations.

Residential Building is among the classifications requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and successfully complete a technical examination. Candidates should document relevant residential building experience as required through the application process.

Virginia also requires **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education** for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The pre-license education is a general business course covering statutes, regulations, and requirements applicable to contractor businesses. It is separate from preparation for the RBC technical examination.

The Residential Building classification covers work on dwellings and townhouses as defined by the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, including applicable accessory buildings and structures. It does not authorize electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting services and does not extend to commercial, industrial, institutional, or governmental structures outside the residential scope defined for RBC.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive RBC study strategy can follow the normal progression of residential construction. Begin with layout and excavation, then move through foundations, framing, exterior construction, and finishing. Studying in this order helps candidates understand how different phases of a project relate to one another.

**Foundations and framing** deserve careful review. Candidates should understand foundation types, floor systems, walls, openings, roof structures, structural connections, and residential construction techniques. Use the International Residential Code when precise requirements are needed and the carpentry reference for broader construction explanations.

**Exterior construction** should include exterior wall systems, weather protection, windows, doors, roofs, finishes, decks, walks, and ramps. Many residential construction questions require candidates to understand both how an assembly is built and what code provisions govern it.

**Prints, plans, and specifications** should also be incorporated into regular study. Practice reading dimensions, symbols, notes, details, and relationships between different parts of a set of residential construction documents.

**Safety and employee protection** remains important for residential contractors. Review safe work practices involving excavation, elevated work, ladders, scaffolds, tools, framing, material handling, and other hazards encountered during building projects.

Reference navigation should be practiced throughout the course. When answering a question, first identify whether it is primarily asking about code, construction technique, business information, or another technical subject. Then select the reference most likely to contain the answer.

Practice using the index rather than scanning the book page by page. Learn common terminology and alternate terms that may appear in an index. Become familiar with chapter organization so that you can narrow a search before opening the reference.

Timed practice is also valuable. The RBC examination provides approximately two minutes per question on average. During practice, avoid spending too long on a single reference lookup. Learn to answer what you know, use the books efficiently when needed, and return to more difficult questions when appropriate.

When an answer is incorrect, locate the underlying information in the appropriate reference and review the surrounding section. This helps candidates understand the principle instead of memorizing an isolated answer that may be presented differently on the actual examination.

The online exam prep course provides a structured framework for this process. Candidates can review construction topics, practice questions, identify weaknesses, and return to the references for reinforcement. ## How does the Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam work?

The Residential Building Contractor (RBC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.
