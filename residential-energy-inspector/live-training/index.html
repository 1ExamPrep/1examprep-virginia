Exam Details

The exam is designed to evaluate whether a candidate can apply energy code requirements, review residential plans, interpret field inspection conditions, understand compliance documentation, and use the approved reference efficiently.

Candidates should prepare for a timed, reference-based exam that requires efficient use of the International Energy Conservation Code, 2021. Residential energy inspector and plans examiner exams commonly require candidates to apply energy code requirements to one- and two-family dwelling conditions, residential buildings, building thermal envelope assemblies, insulation placement, fenestration, air leakage control, duct systems, mechanical equipment, service water heating, lighting, documentation, and plan review conditions.

Exam questions may include direct code lookup questions, inspection-style questions, and plan review-style questions. Construction experience is helpful, but exam answers must be supported by the code language and the requirements stated in the reference. Open Book Test

The Virginia Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner ICC 79 exam is an open book test. Candidates are expected to use approved references during the exam, but open book testing still requires serious preparation. The code book is valuable only when the candidate knows how to navigate it quickly, read it carefully, and apply the correct rule to the inspection condition, plan review item, or question being asked.

Open book testing rewards organization, familiarity, and speed. Candidates should become comfortable using the 2021 IECC table of contents, chapter layout, definitions, residential energy provisions, tables, notes, exceptions, and cross-references. Candidates should practice moving from a question topic to the correct section and then reading the full requirement before selecting an answer.

For example, a building envelope question may require reviewing climate zone requirements, insulation tables, fenestration provisions, air leakage requirements, and installation details. A duct question may require checking duct insulation, duct location, sealing requirements, testing or documentation provisions, and exceptions. A plan review question may require comparing notes, schedules, details, compliance documentation, and code requirements before selecting the best answer.

Candidates should follow current testing rules for approved references, tabs, highlighting, notes, calculators, drawings, loose papers, and materials allowed in the exam room. Test Information and Study Materials

Effective preparation for the Virginia Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner ICC 79 exam should begin with reference navigation. Candidates should learn how the 2021 IECC is organized, including its administrative provisions, residential energy chapters, definitions, tables, notes, exceptions, and compliance requirements. Energy code questions often depend on knowing where a topic is located and how to read the code language precisely.

Residential energy plan review requires the ability to connect drawings, details, schedules, compliance documents, and code requirements. Candidates should practice reviewing insulation notes, wall sections, roof and ceiling details, foundation details, window and door schedules, mechanical schedules, duct layouts, lighting notes, energy certificates, and compliance documentation. The goal is to determine whether the information shown appears to align with the applicable IECC requirements.

Building thermal envelope requirements should receive focused study. Candidates should review insulation requirements for ceilings, walls, floors, foundations, slabs, crawl spaces, basements, and other envelope components. Questions may require candidates to identify the correct requirement based on the building component, climate zone, construction condition, or exception. Candidates should also understand how installation quality and continuity affect energy code compliance.

Fenestration is a major residential energy code topic. Candidates should review requirements for windows, skylights, glazed doors, U-factors, solar heat gain coefficient provisions where applicable, area-weighted concepts when applicable, and exceptions. Plan review questions may ask candidates to compare a window schedule or specification against the code requirement, while inspection questions may focus on whether the installed fenestration matches the required documentation.

Air leakage and air sealing requirements should be studied carefully. Candidates should review provisions related to the building thermal envelope, leakage control, air sealing locations, testing or verification concepts when applicable, and documentation. Inspection-style questions may describe gaps, penetrations, attic access openings, recessed lighting conditions, rim joists, shafts, utility penetrations, or other locations where air sealing may be required.

Duct systems are important for residential energy inspection and plans examiner preparation. Candidates should review duct insulation requirements, duct sealing provisions, duct location considerations, testing or verification requirements when applicable, and related exceptions. Questions may require candidates to evaluate whether ducts are located in conditioned or unconditioned space, whether insulation is required, or whether sealing and documentation requirements apply.

Mechanical system provisions should also be included in the study plan. Candidates should review mechanical equipment efficiency documentation, controls, programmable thermostat concepts where applicable, ventilation-related provisions, equipment sizing documentation when applicable, and system-specific requirements. Plan review questions may require checking mechanical schedules, equipment notes, controls, or compliance documentation against IECC requirements.

Service water heating requirements should be reviewed as part of residential energy preparation. Candidates should study hot water pipe insulation requirements, controls, equipment information, circulation system provisions when applicable, and related exceptions. Inspection and plan review questions may describe piping arrangements, equipment locations, control methods, or documentation details that must be compared to the code.

Lighting provisions can appear in residential energy questions. Candidates should review residential lighting requirements, fixture or lamp efficiency provisions where applicable, controls, and documentation. These questions may be direct lookup questions or may appear as plan review conditions based on lighting notes or fixture schedules.

Compliance paths are especially important for energy code preparation. Candidates should understand that residential energy compliance may be shown through different code-supported paths. Questions may require recognizing whether a condition is being evaluated under a prescriptive path, performance path, simulated performance approach, energy rating approach, or other code-supported method. Candidates should focus on how the code organizes these options and what documentation is needed to support compliance.

Definitions should be reviewed regularly. Terms related to conditioned space, building thermal envelope, fenestration, U-factor, R-value, air barrier, continuous insulation, duct, mechanical system, service water heating, residential building, and energy compliance can affect how a question should be answered. A question may turn on the exact code meaning of a word or phrase, so candidates should practice checking definitions before choosing an answer.

Tables and notes should be reviewed carefully. Energy code questions often require candidates to apply insulation tables, fenestration requirements, duct insulation requirements, climate-related provisions, and exception language. The correct answer may depend on a table note, component type, location, assembly condition, documentation method, or the exact wording of the question.

Inspection preparation should include field-condition review. Candidates should practice evaluating whether insulation appears continuous, whether air sealing is addressed, whether ducts are insulated or sealed as required, whether fenestration information matches documentation, whether energy features are visible for inspection, and whether required certificates or compliance documents are provided. An inspector must be able to connect code language to what is shown or installed.

Plans examiner preparation should include drawing and documentation review. Candidates should practice reviewing wall sections, roof sections, foundation details, window schedules, door schedules, mechanical plans, duct layouts, energy compliance reports, notes, and specifications. A plans examiner must be able to identify missing information, inconsistent details, and conditions that may require clarification before approval.

Timed practice is important for this exam. Candidates should practice answering questions while using the 2021 IECC in a realistic way. Some questions may be answered quickly from a familiar table, while others may require checking definitions, reviewing exceptions, comparing several sections, or interpreting a plan review scenario. The ability to move efficiently through the code can make a significant difference during open book testing.

A strong study plan combines live instruction, self-study review, code reading, inspection scenario practice, plan review practice, and exam-style questions. Students should use live virtual training to organize difficult topics and use self-study time to reinforce code navigation, compliance paths, building envelope requirements, mechanical provisions, and weak areas. Consistent preparation helps candidates build the reference skills and residential energy code knowledge needed for a more confident exam experience.

How does the Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam work?

The Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
