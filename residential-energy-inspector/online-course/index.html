Exam Details

The ICC Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam, also known as ICC exam 79, is a 60-question multiple-choice exam with a 2-hour time limit. The exam is based on the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code and measures a candidate’s ability to apply residential energy code requirements during plan review and inspection activities.

The exam is designed around the job tasks of a residential energy inspector and plans examiner. Candidates may be asked to identify whether construction documents include required energy information, determine whether building envelope assemblies comply with code requirements, evaluate insulation and fenestration requirements, review air leakage provisions, identify duct and mechanical system requirements, and apply provisions related to alterations, additions, and changes of use or occupancy.

Major exam content areas include General Plans and Inspection, Alterations, Additions, and Change of Use or Occupancy, Building Envelope, and Mechanical Systems. These areas reflect the practical work performed by residential energy code personnel. A candidate should be ready to use the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code to answer questions involving both proposed designs and installed work.

General Plans and Inspection includes the review of construction documents, required energy details, inspection responsibilities, code administration concepts, and documentation needed to demonstrate compliance. Students should understand that residential energy plan review is not just a paperwork step. It is the process of confirming that submitted plans include enough information to show how the project will meet energy code requirements.

Alterations, Additions, and Change of Use or Occupancy includes energy requirements that apply when existing residential buildings are modified. These questions may require careful reading because the code may treat new construction, additions, alterations, repairs, and changes in occupancy differently. Students should study how the code applies when work is limited to part of a building and how compliance is evaluated for the affected portion of the project.

Building Envelope is a major part of the ICC 79 exam. This area includes insulation, fenestration, doors, skylights, ceilings, walls, floors, foundations, slabs, crawl spaces, basements, air leakage, vapor retarders when applicable, thermal envelope requirements, and related inspection points. Students should be comfortable using tables and understanding the difference between prescriptive values, performance-based compliance, and component-specific requirements.

Mechanical Systems includes residential energy provisions connected to heating, cooling, ducts, piping, controls, ventilation, system efficiency, equipment sizing concepts, and related installation requirements. Students should be prepared to identify energy-related mechanical requirements and connect them to plan review or inspection scenarios. Mechanical system questions may involve duct insulation, duct sealing, duct testing, piping insulation, thermostats, system controls, or other energy conservation measures.

Open Book Test

The ICC 79 Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam is an open-book exam. The approved reference for this product is the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code. Students should still prepare carefully because open book does not mean the answers will be easy to find without practice. The 2-hour time limit requires candidates to locate information quickly, read code language accurately, and apply the correct section to the question being asked.

A strong open-book strategy begins with knowing the layout of the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code. Students should become familiar with the residential provisions, definitions, compliance path structure, tables, climate zone information, building envelope requirements, mechanical system provisions, and rules for additions and alterations. The more familiar a student is with the reference, the less time they lose searching during the exam.

Energy code questions often require exact reading. A question may ask for a minimum insulation value, a maximum fenestration value, a required test, a documentation requirement, or a condition that changes how the code applies. Students should read each question carefully and avoid answering from general construction experience alone. The correct answer must be based on the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Virginia Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner (ICC - 79) Online Exam Prep course is designed to help students study the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code in a focused and practical way. The strongest preparation comes from combining code reading, guided review, and repeated question practice.

Students should begin by learning the layout of the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code. The energy code is organized differently from many trade codes because it includes compliance paths, climate-based requirements, tables, performance options, and prescriptive provisions. Students should know where the residential provisions are located and how to move between definitions, general requirements, building envelope rules, and mechanical system requirements.

Definitions are an important part of ICC 79 preparation. Residential energy questions may depend on the meaning of terms related to the building thermal envelope, conditioned space, fenestration, skylights, opaque doors, continuous insulation, air barrier, vapor retarder, mechanical systems, ducts, additions, alterations, and other code-specific language. Students should become comfortable checking definitions before applying a requirement.

Climate zone information is also important. Energy requirements often depend on the project’s climate zone, and students should understand how climate-based tables affect insulation values, fenestration requirements, and other thermal envelope provisions. When a question includes a location or climate condition, students should pay close attention to the code table or requirement that applies to that condition.

Building envelope topics should receive the most consistent study time. Students should review ceiling insulation, wall insulation, floor insulation, basement walls, crawl space walls, slab edge insulation, mass walls, fenestration, skylights, doors, air leakage, recessed lighting, access openings, and related inspection concerns. Many energy code questions require students to compare a proposed assembly to a required value or identify which condition must be met for compliance.

Mechanical system topics should also be studied carefully. Students should review duct insulation, duct sealing, duct testing, mechanical system controls, piping insulation, equipment sizing concepts, ventilation-related energy provisions, and system documentation. These questions may appear in both inspection and plan review formats. A plan review question may ask whether the required detail is shown on the plans, while an inspection question may ask whether installed work satisfies the code.

Alterations and additions require careful reading because the code may treat existing buildings differently from new construction. Students should study how the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code applies to additions, alterations, repairs, and changes of use or occupancy. Questions in this area may describe limited work and ask what portion of the project must comply or whether a specific exception applies.

Practice questions are especially valuable for ICC 79 preparation. A good study method is to identify the subject of the question, determine the likely chapter or section, locate the applicable table or requirement, and then compare the code language to the answer choices. Students should review missed questions carefully instead of simply moving to the next question. The review process is where speed and accuracy improve.

Because the exam is open book, students should develop a clean reference navigation strategy before exam day. The goal is not to memorize the entire 2021 International Energy Conservation Code. The goal is to know where to find information, understand how the code is structured, and apply the exact requirement to the scenario presented. A steady study routine helps students build the familiarity needed to work efficiently during the 2-hour exam.

How does the Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam work?

The Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
