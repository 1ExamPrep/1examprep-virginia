### Exam Details

The exam is designed around the job tasks of a residential energy inspector and plans examiner. Candidates may be asked to identify whether construction documents include required energy information, determine whether building envelope assemblies comply with code requirements, evaluate insulation and fenestration requirements, review air leakage provisions, identify duct and mechanical system requirements, and apply provisions related to alterations, additions, and changes of use or occupancy.

Major exam content areas include General Plans and Inspection, Alterations, Additions, and Change of Use or Occupancy, Building Envelope, and Mechanical Systems. These areas reflect the practical work performed by residential energy code personnel. A candidate should be ready to use the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code to answer questions involving both proposed designs and installed work.

Students should understand that residential energy plan review is not just a paperwork step. Alterations, Additions, and Change of Use or Occupancy includes energy requirements that apply when existing residential buildings are modified. These questions may require careful reading because the code may treat new construction, additions, alterations, repairs, and changes in occupancy differently. Students should study how the code applies when work is limited to part of a building and how compliance is evaluated for the affected portion of the project.

## How does the Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam work?

The Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

- Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
- The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
- Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
- Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
