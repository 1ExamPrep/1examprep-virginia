How does the Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam work?

The Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.

Is the Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner exam open book?

ICC certification exams are open book for most categories; the ICC bulletin for your exam lists the references allowed. Highlighted and tabbed books save the most time.

Who issues the Residential Energy Inspector / Plans Examiner license or certification in Virginia?

It comes from the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD). Buying a package here does not register you or make you eligible; the board approves you first, and the exam vendor schedules the test.

How long do I have to get certified after I am hired?

Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply for DHCD certification within 12 months of hire; technical assistants such as inspectors and plans examiners have 18 months.

What keeps the certification current?

Certified code officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years, plus any mandatory training such as code change classes.
