Exam Details

The Virginia Home Improvement Contracting examination is the specialty examination associated with the HIC designation. The Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors regulates contractor licensing, and PSI administers the specialty examination.

  • Examination: Virginia Home Improvement Contracting
  • Specialty Abbreviation: HIC
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 35 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The published HIC examination content outline contains eleven subject areas:

  • Siding: 5 questions
  • Finish Carpentry: 5 questions
  • Roofing: 5 questions
  • Insulation: 5 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

The broad content outline reflects the nature of home improvement work. Candidates may encounter questions involving structural components, exterior finishes, interior finishes, concrete and masonry work, thermal insulation, residential code requirements, accessibility, construction safety, and common building materials.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Home Improvement Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not provided at the testing site.

PSI permits examination references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs and flags are not allowed. Loose papers and unbound references are also prohibited under the published examination rules.

Candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination session. Study guides are not permitted as examination-room references. Candidates should prepare and organize allowable references before arriving for the exam.

The examination allows 120 minutes for 50 questions, which equals an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. That makes efficient book navigation an important exam skill. Candidates who know which reference addresses a particular construction subject can avoid spending valuable time searching through unrelated material.

A useful preparation method is to practice identifying the subject of a question before searching. A concrete question should point toward the concrete reference, while questions about drywall, masonry, siding, insulation, accessibility, safety, or residential code provisions may require different books. Repeated navigation practice helps candidates build a mental map of their reference library.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors. Each license includes a license class and one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Choose the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the Home Improvement Contracting specialty. HIC is the specialty designation for qualifying home improvement work within its defined scope.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must document the experience required for the contractor license class being pursued.
  5. Complete pre-license education. New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must satisfy Virginia's eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
  6. Receive approval for the specialty examination. Home Improvement Contracting is among the specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.
  7. Pass the HIC specialty examination. Prepare for the construction topics included in the published examination content outline and complete the PSI examination.
  8. Complete additional requirements associated with the selected license class. Class A and Class B applicants have business examination requirements associated with the Designated Employee.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. Complete the application process and provide the documentation required by the Virginia Board for Contractors.

Passing the HIC specialty examination is one part of the licensing process. Successful completion of the exam does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license.

State Requirements

Under current Virginia contractor regulations, Home Improvement Contracting means services providing repairs or improvements to dwellings and townhouses as defined in the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, or structures annexed to those dwellings or townhouses.

The Residential Building Contractor classification also provides for the home improvement function. The HIC specialty does not authorize electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas fitting functions.

The HIC scope does not include new construction functions beyond the existing building structure except for decks, patios, driveways, and utility outbuildings that do not require a permit under the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

Virginia's contractor regulations also provide that HIC encompasses certain specialty functions within its authorized residential improvement scope, including work associated with masonry, painting and wall covering, roofing, flooring, finish carpentry, insulation and weather stripping, and other improvement activities addressed by the regulations.

The contractor business must meet the requirements associated with the chosen Class A, Class B, or Class C license in addition to qualifying for the HIC specialty.

    Provides information concerning painting and decorating materials, surface preparation, application methods, tools, coatings, and practical painting and wall-covering procedures.
  • Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone, Clois E. Kicklighter, 10th Edition
    Covers masonry materials and construction methods involving brick, concrete block, stone, mortar, layout, tools, and common masonry practices relevant to residential improvement work.
  • Standard Practice for Installation of Rigid Poly(Vinyl Chloride) (PVC) Siding and Soffit - ASTM D 4756
    Provides standardized practices for installing rigid PVC siding and soffit. It supports preparation involving siding installation details, attachment, layout, and related procedures.

    Provides information about insulation materials, thermal performance, installation considerations, building applications, and principles associated with insulating residential structures.
  • Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition
    Covers gypsum products and construction techniques, including wall and ceiling systems, installation methods, fastening, finishing, and other drywall-related construction practices.
  • Finish Carpenter's Manual, Jim Tolpin, 1993
    Focuses on finish carpentry techniques, trim work, fitting, layout, interior woodworking, tools, and practical procedures associated with completed residential interiors.
  • ICC A117.1-2017 Standard for Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities
    Provides technical criteria related to accessibility and usability of building elements and facilities, including dimensions, clearances, routes, doors, and other accessibility-related construction provisions.

Test Information and Study Materials

The HIC examination requires candidates to prepare across numerous construction trades. A practical study plan should divide preparation according to the published content outline rather than trying to read every reference from beginning to end.

For Concrete, review materials, placement, finishing, curing, preparation, and common residential concrete practices. For Masonry, study brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, dimensions, and typical construction techniques.

For Painting and Wall Covering, focus on surface preparation, coating selection, application methods, tools, and common finishing procedures. For Drywall, review gypsum products, fastening, wall and ceiling systems, joints, finishing, and installation practices.

Rough Carpentry and Framing is one of the larger exam categories. Candidates should understand framing terminology, structural members, layout, floor and wall systems, roof framing, fastening, and basic residential building techniques. Carpentry and Building Construction provides broad coverage for these areas.

For Siding, become familiar with exterior wall preparation, installation practices, attachment methods, expansion considerations, and PVC siding and soffit requirements. The ASTM siding standard included in this package provides focused material for that subject.

Finish Carpentry preparation should include trim, doors, interior woodwork, fitting, layout, measurement, and finishing procedures. The Finish Carpenter's Manual and general carpentry reference can be used together when reviewing these topics.

For Roofing, review residential code requirements and general construction principles applicable to roof systems. Insulation preparation should include insulation materials, placement, thermal concepts, and installation requirements.

The Safety category should be studied with 29 CFR Part 1926. Candidates should become comfortable navigating OSHA requirements rather than attempting to memorize an entire federal safety reference.

For Windows and Doors, review rough openings, installation, dimensions, framing considerations, accessibility requirements when applicable, and residential code provisions.

During study sessions, practice reference lookups under time limits. Begin by identifying the topic, choose the most likely book, use its index or table of contents, and locate the relevant section. This routine can help improve navigation speed while reinforcing trade knowledge.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Virginia Home Improvement Contractor HIC specialty?

Home Improvement Contracting covers qualifying repairs and improvements to dwellings and townhouses, and structures annexed to them, within the scope established by Virginia contractor regulations.

Is the Virginia HIC exam open book?

Yes. The Virginia Home Improvement Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own allowable references and follow PSI's examination rules.

How many questions are on the Virginia HIC exam?

The examination contains 50 questions.

How much time is allowed for the HIC exam?

Candidates have 120 minutes to complete the examination.

What score is required to pass?

The minimum passing score is 70%, or 35 correct answers out of 50 questions.

What subjects are tested on the Virginia Home Improvement Contractor exam?

The published content outline includes Concrete, Masonry, Painting and Wall Covering, Drywall, Rough Carpentry and Framing, Siding, Finish Carpentry, Roofing, Insulation, Safety, and Windows and Doors.

Does the exam include OSHA safety questions?

Yes. Safety is a published examination category, and Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA) is included in this complete book package.

Does the HIC exam cover framing and carpentry?

Yes. Rough Carpentry and Framing and Finish Carpentry are both separate content areas on the examination.

Can permanent tabs and highlighting be used in exam references?

PSI's published rules allow references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the exam, and permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed.

Does the HIC specialty include electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas fitting work?

No. Virginia's definition of the HIC specialty specifically excludes electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and gas fitting functions.

Does passing the HIC exam automatically give me a Virginia contractor license?

No. Passing the technical specialty exam is one component of the Virginia contractor licensing process. The business must also satisfy the applicable requirements for its license class, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, application, and other required documentation.

Why use a complete book package for an open-book examination?

A complete reference collection lets candidates study the construction subjects represented on the exam while practicing how to locate information in the books. Repeated use can build greater familiarity with the references before test day.

How does the Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam work?

The Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
