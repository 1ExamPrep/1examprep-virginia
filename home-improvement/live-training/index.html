Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA)

The Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 covers federal occupational safety and health requirements applicable to construction work. Safety is an important part of home improvement and residential construction, and candidates should become familiar with how construction safety requirements are organized within the OSHA reference.

Study areas may include personal protective equipment, tools, electrical hazards, ladders, scaffolds, material handling, housekeeping, fall protection, and other jobsite safety subjects. Developing familiarity with the OSHA reference can make it easier to locate regulations while working through construction-safety questions.

International Residential Code, 2021

The International Residential Code for One- and Two-Family Dwellings, 2021 provides residential construction requirements applicable to a wide range of building components and systems.

Candidates should become comfortable using the table of contents, index, chapter structure, definitions, tables, and figures within the IRC. Rather than trying to memorize an entire code book, effective preparation involves understanding where different residential construction subjects are located and practicing how to find applicable requirements.

Carpentry and Building Construction

Carpentry and Building Construction, 2016 provides practical information related to construction materials, tools, methods, building components, and carpentry practices.

Carpentry is a significant part of many home improvement projects, so candidates should become familiar with construction terminology, measurements, framing concepts, building materials, tools, and common installation procedures.

During preparation, focus not only on understanding the construction concepts but also on learning how this reference is organized. Knowing whether a topic is located in a general construction chapter or a specialized section can improve reference-navigation efficiency.

Concrete Construction

The Contractor's Guide to Quality Concrete Construction - 4th Edition provides information related to concrete materials and construction practices.

Candidates can use this reference to review concepts involving concrete work, placement, construction procedures, materials, and quality practices. Home improvement projects may involve concrete in foundations, slabs, walkways, structural components, and other applications, making it important to distinguish concrete-related questions from masonry or general building questions.

Painting and Decorating

The Painting & Decorating Craftsman's Manual and Textbook, Eighth Edition, 1995 provides a dedicated reference for painting and decorating topics.

Preparation may involve surface conditions, preparation techniques, painting materials, coatings, tools, application methods, and other practical considerations associated with painting and decorating work.

Surface preparation is particularly important in finish work. Candidates should understand that different substrates and coatings may require different preparation methods and application procedures.

Modern Masonry

Modern Masonry - Brick, Block, Stone, Clois E. Kicklighter, 10th Edition provides information associated with masonry construction and materials.

The reference covers areas involving brick, concrete block, stone, mortar, masonry tools, and construction methods. Candidates should become familiar with masonry terminology and understand how masonry construction differs from concrete placement and general carpentry work.

Practicing with the reference can help candidates identify where information about masonry materials, installation methods, and construction details is located.

PVC Siding and Soffit

The Standard Practice for Installation of Rigid Poly(Vinyl Chloride) (PVC) Siding and Soffit - ASTM D 4756 provides a specialized technical standard addressing PVC siding and soffit installation practices.

Because standards such as ASTM D 4756 are more specialized than general construction textbooks, candidates should spend time understanding their organization and terminology. Knowing where installation requirements and procedures are located can make the reference easier to use during preparation.

Insulation

The Insulation Handbook, 2001 provides information related to insulation materials, applications, installation considerations, and building insulation practices.

Candidates should become familiar with different insulation concepts and understand the role insulation plays within residential construction. This reference provides a dedicated source for questions involving insulation rather than requiring candidates to rely solely on broader residential construction books.

Gypsum Construction

The Gypsum Construction Handbook, 7th Edition provides technical information related to gypsum construction systems, materials, wall and ceiling applications, assemblies, and installation practices.

Gypsum products are widely used in residential and commercial construction. Candidates should review terminology, installation methods, system components, and common applications while learning how to locate technical information within this handbook.

Finish Carpentry

The Finish Carpenter's Manual, Jim Tolpin, 1993 focuses on finish-carpentry practices and techniques.

While general carpentry references cover broader construction subjects, finish carpentry involves more specialized work. Candidates can use this reference to study finish work, trim, measurements, installation methods, tools, and other detailed carpentry subjects.

Understanding the difference between general building carpentry and finish carpentry can also help candidates determine which reference should be used for a particular question.

Accessibility Requirements

The ICC A117.1-2017 Standard for Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities provides technical requirements associated with accessibility and usability.

Accessibility standards often contain specific measurements, dimensions, clearances, configurations, and technical requirements. Candidates should practice locating this information directly within the standard rather than attempting to memorize every numerical requirement.

Reference Navigation

Working efficiently with technical reference books is an important part of contractor exam preparation. The Virginia Home Improvement Contractor materials cover many different construction areas, and no single book contains every answer.

Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question before beginning a reference search. For example:

  • A construction safety question should direct you toward OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926.
  • A residential building-code question may require the 2021 International Residential Code.
  • A brick, block, or stone question may require Modern Masonry.
  • A gypsum wall or ceiling question may require the Gypsum Construction Handbook.
  • An accessibility question may require ICC A117.1-2017.
  • A painting question may require the Painting & Decorating Craftsman's Manual and Textbook.

Learning these associations can reduce unnecessary searching and make study sessions more productive.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin your preparation by separating the exam material into major construction categories. Working with one subject at a time can make a large group of reference books easier to manage.

For each category, identify the primary reference and become familiar with its table of contents, index, major chapters, definitions, tables, and illustrations. Then practice locating specific construction terms or concepts within the book.

When studying code books and standards, focus on learning their organization as well as the technical information they contain. Standards and codes frequently present information differently from construction textbooks, so candidates should practice working with each format.

For practical trade subjects such as carpentry, masonry, painting, insulation, concrete, and gypsum work, review terminology, tools, materials, installation procedures, and common construction methods.

Repeated reference practice can help candidates develop a more consistent process: identify the subject, select the applicable book, use the index or table of contents, locate the relevant section, and carefully read the information before selecting an answer.

How does the Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam work?

The Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
