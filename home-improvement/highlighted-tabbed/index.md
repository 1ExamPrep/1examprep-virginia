### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to businesses and consist of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia uses Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license classes.
2. **Select the appropriate specialty.** Candidates seeking to perform work within the Home Improvement Contracting scope pursue the HIC specialty.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contracting business must identify an individual who satisfies the applicable qualification requirements for the requested specialty.
4. **Complete applicable experience requirements.** The Qualified Individual must meet the requirements associated with the contractor license class and specialty being requested.
5. **Complete Virginia pre-license education.** New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants are subject to Virginia's required eight hours of approved pre-license education.
6. **Complete the specialty examination requirement.** Follow the Board for Contractors and PSI process applicable to the Home Improvement Contracting specialty examination.
7. **Complete additional requirements for the selected license class.** Virginia's contractor licensing process includes requirements that vary according to the license class being pursued.
8. **Submit the contractor license application.** Complete the application and provide the information and supporting documentation required by the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The technical specialty exam is one part of the overall licensing process. Candidates should prepare for the HIC exam while also completing the separate requirements applicable to the contractor business and selected license class.

### State Requirements

Virginia contractor licenses contain two basic components: the **license class** and the **classification or specialty**. The class establishes the level of contracting activity allowed under the license, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of contracting work the business may perform.

Home Improvement Contracting is a specialty within the Virginia contractor licensing system. It is associated with qualifying repair and improvement work involving dwellings, townhouses, and related structures within the scope established by Virginia contractor regulations.

Virginia's licensing structure also distinguishes Home Improvement Contracting from independently regulated electrical, plumbing, HVAC, and gas-fitting functions. Candidates should understand the scope of the specialty they are pursuing as part of their overall licensing preparation.

New applicants seeking a Virginia Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license must complete eight hours of approved pre-license education. This business-focused licensing requirement is separate from technical preparation for the Home Improvement Contractor specialty examination.

### Test Information and Study Materials

The breadth of the Home Improvement Contractor examination makes a category-based study strategy especially useful. Rather than attempting to memorize every page of every reference, candidates can divide preparation into major construction subjects and learn which book supports each category.

For **concrete**, review preparation, concrete materials, placement, finishing, curing, and quality construction practices. For **masonry**, focus on brick, concrete block, stone, mortar, layout, and common installation methods.

When studying **painting and decorating**, review surface preparation, coating systems, application techniques, tools, and finishing procedures. For **drywall and gypsum construction**, become familiar with gypsum products, fastening, wall and ceiling assemblies, joints, finishing, and common installation procedures.

**Rough carpentry and framing** should include residential construction terminology, framing members, layout, floor systems, wall framing, roof framing, fastening, and related building practices. Carpentry and Building Construction can also support preparation involving windows, doors, and general construction methods.

For **siding**, study installation practices involving rigid PVC siding and soffit, including preparation, fastening, positioning, and installation requirements. Use the ASTM D 4756 standard as a focused reference for this area.

**Finish carpentry** preparation can include interior trim, fitting, measurement, layout, doors, woodworking, and finishing techniques. Use the Finish Carpenter's Manual together with the broader carpentry reference to reinforce these practical subjects.

For **insulation**, review the different types of insulating materials, installation considerations, thermal principles, and building applications. For **safety**, practice navigating OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 and locating requirements associated with common construction hazards.

The International Residential Code should be incorporated into study sessions involving code-regulated residential construction. ICC A117.1 should be used when accessibility-related technical criteria are relevant.

As you study, practice locating information rather than simply reading highlighted passages. ## How does the Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam work?

The Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
