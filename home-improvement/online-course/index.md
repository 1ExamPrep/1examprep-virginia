### Exam Details

The **Virginia Home Improvement Contracting Examination** is the technical specialty examination associated with the HIC designation. Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors, and PSI administers the specialty examination.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Home Improvement Contracting examination is an **open book test**. Candidates must bring their own allowable references because the testing center does not provide the books used for the examination.

PSI permits allowable reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs and flags are not permitted. Study guides are not permitted as exam-room references, and candidates must follow the applicable PSI rules governing reference materials.

The open-book format makes reference-navigation practice an important part of preparation. Candidates have 120 minutes to answer 50 questions, which provides an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Searching an unfamiliar book without knowing where to begin can consume valuable testing time.

A more effective method is to identify the subject first. A workplace-safety question may direct you toward OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. A residential code question may require the International Residential Code. A masonry question may point toward Modern Masonry, while drywall information may be located in the Gypsum Construction Handbook.

Practice this process while studying. Determine the subject, choose the most relevant reference, use the index or table of contents, locate the applicable section, and carefully read the surrounding material. Technical questions may depend on conditions, exceptions, measurements, or specific installation requirements, so finding the correct page is only part of the process.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors and consist of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the Home Improvement Contracting specialty.** HIC identifies the applicable specialty for qualifying home improvement work.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the applicable specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirements.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the requested license class and specialty.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants must complete eight hours of approved pre-license education.
6. **Complete the HIC technical examination requirement.** Follow the Board for Contractors and PSI procedures applicable to the specialty examination.
7. **Complete additional requirements associated with the license class.** Virginia has separate requirements that may apply depending on whether the business is pursuing Class A, Class B, or Class C licensing.
8. **Submit the contractor license application.** Complete the Virginia Board for Contractors application process and provide the required supporting documentation.

Passing the HIC technical examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Exam completion alone does not issue a Virginia contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Home Improvement Contracting** as the service providing repairs or improvements to dwellings and townhouses as defined in the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, or structures annexed to those dwellings or townhouses.

The Residential Building Contractor classification also provides for the home improvement function. The HIC specialty does not provide for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting functions.

The HIC specialty also does not include new-construction functions beyond the existing building structure except for decks, patios, driveways, and utility outbuildings that do not require a permit under the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

Virginia contractor licenses contain both a license class and a classification or specialty. Candidates pursuing HIC should therefore understand that qualifying for the specialty is only one component of the complete contractor licensing process.

New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants are also required to complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This business-focused licensing requirement is separate from technical preparation for the HIC specialty examination.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive HIC study plan should divide preparation into the examination's major construction categories. This approach helps candidates connect each subject with the appropriate technical reference.

For **concrete**, review materials, preparation, placement, finishing, curing, and quality-control practices. For **masonry**, study brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, tools, and typical residential masonry procedures.

For **painting and wall covering**, review surface preparation, coatings, tools, application techniques, and finishing procedures. For **drywall**, become familiar with gypsum products, fasteners, wall and ceiling systems, joints, finishing methods, and installation practices.

**Rough carpentry and framing** requires familiarity with framing terminology, structural members, floor and wall systems, roof construction, layout, fastening, and common residential building practices. The general carpentry reference is also useful when studying windows, doors, and other building components.

For **siding**, review installation practices, fastening, layout, exterior preparation, and the technical requirements associated with rigid PVC siding and soffit. For **finish carpentry**, focus on trim, fitting, measurement, interior woodworking, doors, and finishing techniques.

For **roofing**, review residential code provisions and construction practices associated with residential roof systems. For **insulation**, study insulation materials, thermal principles, placement, and installation considerations.

The **safety** portion of the exam should include regular practice with OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Instead of attempting to memorize the entire reference, become comfortable using its organization and locating requirements involving common construction hazards and safe work practices.

For **windows and doors**, study openings, framing considerations, installation, dimensions, residential code requirements, and applicable accessibility criteria.

During practice, use the technical references instead of relying only on memory. Start by identifying which trade or code subject the question addresses, then select the most likely book and locate the supporting material. Repeated reference-navigation practice can help make the open-book format more manageable.

## How does the Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam work?

The Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
