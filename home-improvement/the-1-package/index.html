Exam Details

The Virginia Home Improvement Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the HIC designation. Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors, and PSI serves as the examination vendor.

  • Specialty: Home Improvement Contracting
  • Specialty Abbreviation: HIC
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 35 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination covers a broad range of residential construction subjects. Major content areas include concrete, masonry, painting and wall covering, drywall, rough carpentry and framing, siding, finish carpentry, roofing, insulation, safety, and windows and doors.

The variety of exam subjects makes organized preparation especially important. Candidates should develop a working understanding of the construction topics while learning which reference is most likely to contain the technical information associated with each subject.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Home Improvement Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing allowable references and following the testing requirements governing those materials.

Open-book testing does not eliminate the need to study. Candidates still need to identify the subject being tested, select the appropriate reference, locate the relevant information, and interpret that information within the available examination time.

The exam contains 50 questions and allows 120 minutes for completion. That gives candidates an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Efficient book navigation can therefore be an important part of preparation.

Some books in The 1 Package are highlighted and tabbed for exam preparation. Candidates should use those navigation aids together with the table of contents, index, chapter organization, and technical headings found throughout the references.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing involves several components beyond the technical examination. The 1 Package combines preparation and support services to help customers organize the process.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia contractor licensing uses Class A, Class B, and Class C license classes.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Home Improvement Contracting as services providing repairs or improvements to dwellings and townhouses as defined in the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, or structures annexed to those dwellings or townhouses.

The Residential Building Contractor classification also provides for this function. The HIC specialty does not provide for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting functions.

The specialty also does not include new construction functions beyond the existing building structure except for decks, patios, driveways, and utility outbuildings that do not require a permit under the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

Virginia contractor licensing applies to the contracting business, and the license includes a class together with classifications or specialty designations. Technical examination completion is therefore one part of a larger licensing process involving the business, Qualified Individual, applicable education and experience requirements, and application documentation.

How does the Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam work?

The Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
