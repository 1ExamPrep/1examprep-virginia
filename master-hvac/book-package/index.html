Exam Details

The Virginia Master HVAC credential is regulated through the Virginia Board for Contractors as an individual tradesman license. The Board regulates individuals working in heating, ventilation, and air conditioning as well as other skilled trades.

Candidates must receive pre-approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before sitting for a tradesman examination administered through PSI. The current examination fee for a Master Tradesman examination is $125 and is paid directly to PSI after the candidate has received approval.

Master HVAC preparation should cover the knowledge necessary to work with heating, ventilation, air conditioning, refrigeration, mechanical systems, controls, equipment, and applicable code requirements. Candidates should also understand the Virginia regulations governing individual tradesmen and the responsibilities associated with Master-level licensure.

Technical study should include system operation as well as code compliance. A Master HVAC candidate may need to interpret requirements involving mechanical equipment, duct systems, ventilation, exhaust, combustion air, installation clearances, refrigeration, heating equipment, controls, and other HVAC subjects.

Virginia currently bases applicable ICC-code examinations on the 2021 code editions. Candidates working with the mechanical code should therefore become familiar with the structure and terminology of the International Mechanical Code, 2021.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Master HVAC examination is open book. Candidates should prepare by working directly with the permitted technical references rather than relying only on general HVAC knowledge.

Open-book testing still requires significant preparation. Technical codes and trade references can contain hundreds of pages of information. Searching unfamiliar books during a timed examination can consume valuable time. Candidates should learn where major subjects are located before testing.

A question involving Virginia licensing, qualification, renewal, or professional requirements may direct the candidate toward the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations. Mechanical-code questions may require the International Mechanical Code, 2021. Oil-burning equipment questions may call for NFPA 31, while broader refrigeration, air conditioning, heating, electrical, and troubleshooting topics may be addressed in Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Reference navigation should become part of regular exam preparation. Practice using the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter headings, code tables, and cross-references. Candidates should also become comfortable recognizing the terminology used in HVAC codes and textbooks because the correct term can lead directly to the information needed.

Candidates who highlight, underline, index, or tab books for testing should prepare the references in accordance with the current PSI rules governing examination materials.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Master HVAC credential is an individual tradesman license. Candidates must meet the applicable qualification requirements and receive approval from the Board before taking the examination.

  1. Review the Master HVAC qualification requirements. Determine which current experience and licensing pathway applies to your background.
  2. Document the required experience. Applicants must provide evidence showing they meet the qualification standard for Master-level HVAC licensure.
  3. Submit the applicable application. The Virginia Board for Contractors reviews the candidate’s qualifications and application materials.
  4. Receive Board approval to test. Pre-approval is required before a candidate can sit for the Master HVAC examination.
  5. Schedule the examination through PSI. After approval, candidates may complete the applicable registration and exam-scheduling process.
  6. Study the technical and regulatory references. Candidates should work directly with the Virginia regulations, International Mechanical Code, NFPA 31, and Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.
  7. Complete the Master HVAC examination. Candidates must satisfy the applicable examination requirement for Master-level licensure.
  8. Complete remaining Board requirements. Any remaining licensing requirements must be completed before the individual credential is issued.

Current Virginia regulations provide multiple pathways for applicants seeking Master-level licensure in a trade. An applicant may qualify after holding a valid residential HVAC tradesman license for three years, after holding a valid Journeyman license in the trade for at least one year, or by documenting nine years of practical experience in the trade.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual HVAC tradesmen as well as contractor businesses. The individual Master HVAC license and the contractor business license are separate credentials.

A Virginia Master HVAC license demonstrates that the individual has satisfied the applicable qualification and examination requirements for Master-level HVAC licensure. A contracting business, however, must separately obtain the appropriate Virginia contractor license and classification or specialty before performing regulated contracting work under the business.

For a Virginia contractor business seeking the HVAC classification, the Qualified Individual must hold the applicable valid HVAC tradesman credential required by the Board. The business must also satisfy the requirements associated with its contractor license class and any other applicable licensing obligations.

Virginia contractor licenses are issued separately from individual tradesman licenses. Businesses may hold Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor licenses depending on the level of contracting activity authorized under state requirements.

Virginia also requires continuing education for HVAC tradesmen as part of the applicable license-renewal process. Continuing education used for renewal must come from a Board-approved provider and must satisfy the requirements established for the trade.

Exam preparation and continuing education are different. This book package is intended for candidates preparing for the Master HVAC examination and does not replace any Board-approved continuing education required for an existing license renewal.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia Master HVAC study plan should combine trade review with direct use of the reference materials. Candidates can divide preparation into specific areas such as mechanical code, refrigeration, air conditioning, heating, electrical controls, ventilation, oil-burning equipment, troubleshooting, and Virginia regulations.

Mechanical-code study should involve regular use of the International Mechanical Code, 2021. Candidates should become familiar with its chapter organization and learn where requirements involving equipment installation, duct systems, exhaust, ventilation, combustion air, chimneys, vents, and other mechanical subjects are located.

Ventilation and exhaust requirements deserve focused attention. HVAC professionals work with systems that affect indoor air quality, moisture, contaminants, heat, and building pressure relationships. Candidates should understand the purpose of these systems and know how to find applicable code provisions.

Duct-system preparation should include materials, construction, installation, supports, clearances, insulation, penetrations, and other requirements affecting air distribution. Candidates should practice locating both general requirements and technical details in the mechanical code.

Refrigeration and air-conditioning preparation should make extensive use of Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning, 22nd Edition. Review the refrigeration cycle, system components, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, servicing concepts, system operation, and troubleshooting principles.

Electrical knowledge is also important in HVAC work. Candidates should be comfortable with motors, relays, contactors, thermostats, controls, electrical troubleshooting, and the relationship between electrical components and HVAC system operation.

Heating preparation should include equipment types, combustion principles, controls, air distribution, system operation, and safety. Oil-burning equipment should be reviewed using NFPA 31 so candidates become familiar with the specialized installation requirements found in that standard.

Candidates should also spend time on troubleshooting. Master-level HVAC work often requires understanding symptoms, identifying possible system causes, and determining the appropriate diagnostic approach. Studying system operation makes troubleshooting questions easier to analyze because candidates understand what each component is intended to do.

Virginia regulatory review should be included throughout the study process. Do not wait until the end to open the Individual License and Certification Regulations. Become familiar with the language and organization of the regulations so state-specific questions can be addressed efficiently.

Practice questions can double as reference-navigation exercises. Read the question, determine whether it concerns regulations, mechanical code, oil-burning equipment, refrigeration, air conditioning, or another HVAC subject, select the appropriate book, and locate the supporting information.

This approach helps candidates build both technical knowledge and reference familiarity. Open-book testing works best when the books are used as tools to confirm information rather than as substitutes for understanding the trade.

How does the Master HVAC exam work?

The Master HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
