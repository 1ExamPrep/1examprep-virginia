Exam Details

The Virginia Master HVAC credential is an individual tradesman license regulated through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Virginia identifies the trade as Heating, Ventilation and Air Conditioning, commonly abbreviated HVA within contractor licensing materials.

Candidates must receive pre-approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before sitting for the applicable Master Tradesman examination. PSI administers Virginia tradesman examinations after the candidate has received approval from the Board.

The current Virginia Master Tradesman examination fee is $125 and is paid directly to PSI. Candidates should complete the applicable qualification and application requirements before attempting to schedule the examination.

Master HVAC preparation should include a broad range of technical knowledge. Important areas include heating and cooling equipment, refrigeration principles, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, mechanical equipment, electrical controls, troubleshooting, oil-burning equipment, and Virginia regulations.

Master-level preparation should focus on understanding complete HVAC systems rather than memorizing isolated facts. Candidates should understand how components interact, how code requirements affect installations, and how to identify the appropriate technical reference when additional information is needed.

The Virginia Board for Contractors separately regulates contractor businesses and individual tradesmen. A Master HVAC tradesman credential is an individual license. Virginia contractor regulations identify a Master HVAC tradesman license as the applicable Qualified Individual credential for an HVAC contractor business, but the business itself must separately satisfy contractor licensing requirements.

Open Book Test

The Virginia tradesman examinations are open book. Candidates should prepare by working directly with the permitted reference materials and becoming familiar with how each book is organized before exam day.

An open-book examination still requires substantial preparation. HVAC references contain extensive technical information, and candidates can lose valuable testing time if they must search unfamiliar books from beginning to end. A stronger approach is to understand the subject first and use the reference to confirm technical details.

A question involving Virginia licensing requirements may direct the candidate toward the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations. Mechanical-system requirements may require the International Mechanical Code, 2021. Oil-fired equipment questions may involve NFPA 31, while broader refrigeration, heating, air conditioning, electrical, service, and troubleshooting subjects may be found in Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning.

Candidates should practice using tables of contents, indexes, definitions, chapter headings, tables, and cross-references. Learning the terminology used in the references can make searching faster because a correct technical term often leads directly to a useful index entry or code section.

Reference preparation must follow current testing rules. Candidates who highlight, underline, index, or tab books should make sure their reference preparation complies with the current requirements that apply at the examination site.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Master HVAC license is issued to an individual tradesman. Candidates must satisfy the applicable qualification, application, and examination requirements before the credential can be issued.

  1. Review the Virginia Master HVAC qualification requirements. Determine which current experience, education, or existing-license pathway applies to your background.
  2. Prepare the required documentation. Applicants must provide the qualification and experience information requested by the Virginia Board for Contractors.
  3. Submit the Tradesman Exam and License Application. The Board reviews individual tradesman applications before granting examination eligibility.
  4. Receive Board approval. Pre-approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors is required before a candidate may sit for the examination.
  5. Schedule the examination with PSI. Once approval has been received, candidates can proceed with PSI examination registration and scheduling.
  6. Prepare with the applicable references. Study the Virginia regulations, International Mechanical Code, NFPA 31, and Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning while developing efficient reference-navigation habits.
  7. Complete the Master HVAC examination. Candidates must satisfy the applicable examination requirement for Master-level licensure.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Any additional Board requirements must be satisfied before the individual Master HVAC license is issued.

Candidates intending to operate a contracting business should also review the separate Virginia contractor business requirements. An individual Master HVAC license and a contractor business license serve different purposes and are not interchangeable.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual tradesmen working in HVAC, electrical, plumbing, gas fitting, and other regulated trades. The Master HVAC credential is issued to the individual who satisfies the applicable licensing requirements.

Virginia contractor businesses are separately licensed. Contractor licenses consist of a license class and a classification or specialty. Class A, Class B, and Class C determine the level of contracting activity permitted, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the business may perform.

For an HVAC contractor business, Virginia contractor regulations identify a Master HVAC tradesman license issued by the Board for Contractors as the required Qualified Individual credential for the HVAC classification. The business must still meet the other applicable contractor licensing requirements.

Virginia also requires continuing education for licensed HVAC tradesmen as a condition of renewal. The current requirement for HVAC is three hours of Board-approved continuing education during the applicable renewal cycle. Continuing education used for license renewal must come from an approved provider.

Exam preparation and continuing education serve different purposes. This Virginia Master HVAC Exam Prep training is intended for candidates preparing for the licensing examination. It does not replace Board-approved continuing education required for renewal of an existing HVAC license.

Virginia also introduced a Residential HVAC Mechanic licensing pathway beginning in 2025. That credential is separate from the full Master HVAC license. Candidates pursuing unrestricted Master HVAC licensure should follow the requirements applying specifically to the Master HVAC credential.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia Master HVAC study plan should combine live instruction, independent review, practice-oriented preparation, and direct use of the reference materials. Candidates can divide their study time among mechanical code, refrigeration, air conditioning, heating, ventilation, ducts, electrical systems, controls, troubleshooting, oil-burning equipment, and Virginia regulations.

Mechanical-code preparation should include regular work with the International Mechanical Code, 2021. Learn how the code organizes requirements involving equipment, ventilation, exhaust, duct systems, combustion air, chimneys, vents, and mechanical installations. Understanding the chapter structure can reduce unnecessary searching during an open-book examination.

Ventilation and exhaust systems deserve focused review. Candidates should understand why these systems are required, how they affect indoor environments, and where applicable installation requirements can be found in the mechanical code.

Ductwork preparation should include materials, construction, supports, insulation, clearances, penetrations, and air-distribution concepts. Candidates should connect practical duct installation knowledge with the technical provisions contained in the mechanical code.

Refrigeration preparation should include the refrigeration cycle, compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, refrigerants, pressure-temperature relationships, system operation, service procedures, and diagnostic concepts. Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides extensive material for these areas.

Air-conditioning preparation should include airflow, cooling equipment, temperature control, humidity, system components, servicing, and troubleshooting. Candidates should understand how individual components interact rather than studying each component in isolation.

Heating preparation should include system types, combustion concepts, equipment operation, controls, distribution systems, venting, and safety. Candidates working with oil-fired systems should become familiar with NFPA 31 and understand how that standard is organized.

Electrical knowledge is another important part of HVAC preparation. Review motors, relays, contactors, thermostats, control circuits, electrical components, and troubleshooting methods. HVAC systems rely heavily on electrical controls, so candidates should understand how electrical operation relates to mechanical performance.

Troubleshooting should be practiced as a logical process. Begin with the reported symptom, understand normal system operation, identify likely causes, and determine which components or operating conditions should be checked. This approach is more useful than simply memorizing lists of symptoms and answers.

Virginia regulatory material should remain part of the study schedule throughout preparation. Candidates should work with the Individual License and Certification Regulations and become comfortable locating licensing requirements, definitions, qualifications, and professional responsibilities.

Live virtual classes provide a structured way to review these subjects with scheduled instruction. Self-study provides the additional time needed to revisit difficult topics, practice navigation, and reinforce material covered during live sessions.

Practice questions can also become reference-navigation exercises. Read the question, identify whether it involves regulations, mechanical code, oil-burning equipment, refrigeration, air conditioning, heating, electrical systems, or troubleshooting, and then select the appropriate reference before searching for the answer.

How does the Master HVAC exam work?

The Master HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
