### Open Book Test

The **Virginia Master HVAC examination is an open-book test**. Candidates may bring authorized reference materials into the examination center in accordance with PSI's rules.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used where permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates are not permitted to write in their reference materials during the examination session.

An open-book examination still rewards preparation. With 85 items and 210 minutes, candidates need to manage their testing time carefully. ### Licensing Steps

### State Requirements

Virginia establishes specific experience standards for applicants seeking licensure as a master in a trade. A Master HVAC applicant must furnish evidence that one of the accepted experience standards has been met.

Current Virginia regulations provide three pathways for a master-level applicant:

- Hold a valid **Residential HVAC Tradesman** license in the trade for three years;
- Hold a valid **Journeyman** license in the trade for a minimum of one year; or
- Document **nine years of practical experience** in the trade for which the master license is sought.

These requirements reflect the advanced level of experience expected from a Virginia Master HVAC tradesman. The examination is designed to test knowledge beyond basic equipment identification. Candidates should be prepared to work with heating and cooling principles, system installation requirements, ventilation, ductwork, combustion, refrigeration, controls, boilers, piping, commercial kitchen ventilation, and professional regulations.

Virginia Master HVAC tradesman licenses expire three years from the last day of the month in which the license was issued. As a condition of renewal, HVAC tradesmen must complete **three hours of Board-approved continuing education** related to applicable building code changes during the renewal period.

The continuing education requirement applies to both journeyman and master HVAC tradesmen. An individual holding both journeyman and master designations in the same trade does not have to duplicate the HVAC continuing education course solely because both designations are held.

### Test Information and Study Materials

The Virginia Master HVAC exam covers both broad HVAC theory and practical installation knowledge. Candidates should be comfortable identifying system components, understanding equipment operation, interpreting system requirements, and applying trade principles to common installation and service situations.

**Warm air heating** is an important exam area. Study furnace operation, heating components, airflow principles, equipment installation, controls, venting, and related safety considerations. Understand how the parts of a forced-air heating system interact rather than memorizing isolated definitions.

**Ventilation and air conditioning systems** are also major areas of the examination. Candidates should review ventilation concepts, exhaust requirements, air movement, cooling principles, system components, and common HVAC installation practices.

**Duct systems** require familiarity with airflow distribution, duct construction concepts, installation practices, insulation, and related system considerations. Flash cards can be useful for reinforcing terms and components while reference-book practice can strengthen your ability to locate detailed requirements.

**Chimneys, flues, vents, and combustion air** require careful study because improper installation can create serious operating and safety problems. Candidates should understand the purpose of these systems and the relationship between appliance operation, combustion, venting, and air supply.

**Boilers and hot water piping** are included on the Master HVAC examination. Review hydronic heating concepts, boiler system components, water circulation, piping arrangements, safety controls, and other principles associated with hot-water heating systems.

**Controls and low-voltage systems** are another part of the exam outline. HVAC professionals should understand basic control sequences, thermostatic control, low-voltage components, and how controls affect heating and cooling system operation.

**Refrigerants and refrigerant piping** require understanding of refrigeration principles and system operation. Study how refrigerant moves through a system, the function of major refrigeration components, and concepts associated with refrigerant piping and system performance.

The examination also includes **fuel oil piping and storage, insulation, commercial kitchen venting, chilled and condenser water piping, and firestopping and penetration inspection**. These subjects reinforce why Master-level exam preparation should cover the full outline rather than concentrating only on residential air conditioning.

## How does the Master HVAC exam work?

The Master HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
