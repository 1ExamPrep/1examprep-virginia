Exam Details

The Virginia Journeyman/Master HVAC examination is administered by PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Candidates must receive approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before sitting for the examination.

The current Virginia Journeyman/Master HVAC examination contains 85 items. Master HVAC candidates must answer at least 64 items correctly to achieve the required Master passing score. Candidates are allowed 210 minutes to complete the examination.

The examination outline includes the following subject areas:

  • Standards of Practice
  • Standards of Conduct
  • Renewal and Reinstatement
  • Definitions
  • Qualification for Licensure
  • Revocation of Licensure
  • Warm Air Heating
  • Ventilation Systems
  • Air Conditioning Systems
  • Duct Systems
  • Chimneys, Flues, and Vents
  • Boilers
  • Hot Water Piping
  • Combustion Air
  • Controls and Low Voltage
  • Fuel Oil Piping and Storage
  • Insulation
  • Commercial Kitchen Venting
  • Refrigerants
  • Refrigerant Piping
  • Chilled and Condenser Water Piping
  • Firestopping and Penetration Inspection

The number of questions assigned to individual topics varies. Warm air heating, ventilation systems, and air conditioning systems each represent significant portions of the examination, while other areas test the candidate's working knowledge across the complete HVAC trade.

Because the exam combines technical HVAC subjects with Virginia licensing and professional-practice material, a balanced study plan should include both trade knowledge and regulatory review. The 250 flash cards can help candidates rotate through those subjects instead of concentrating on only one area of the examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Master HVAC examination is an open-book test. Candidates may bring authorized reference materials into the examination center in accordance with PSI's rules.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used where permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates are not permitted to write in their reference materials during the examination session.

An open-book examination still rewards preparation. With 85 items and 210 minutes, candidates need to manage their testing time carefully. Searching an entire book for every question can slow down progress. A better preparation strategy is to develop enough familiarity with HVAC systems and terminology to recognize the subject first, then use the appropriate reference to confirm the answer when necessary.

Flash cards are well suited to this style of preparation because they encourage quick recall. Reviewing terms, systems, components, operating principles, code-related concepts, and regulatory subjects can help you identify the nature of a question more quickly. Pairing flash-card practice with reference navigation can also help you learn which book or section is most useful for a particular subject.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Master HVAC license is an individual tradesman license regulated by the Virginia Board for Contractors. The licensing process requires candidates to satisfy the applicable experience requirements and obtain Board approval before taking the examination.

  1. Meet the Master HVAC experience requirement. Virginia regulations provide multiple experience pathways for applicants seeking a master license in a trade.
  2. Complete the Tradesman Exam and License Application. HVAC applicants use the individual tradesman licensing process administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation.
  3. Submit the application for Board review. Pre-approval from the Board is required before a candidate can sit for the examination.
  4. Schedule the approved examination with PSI. Once approved, the candidate may follow PSI's scheduling procedures for the Virginia Master HVAC examination.
  5. Prepare for the Master HVAC exam. Study the technical content outline, Virginia regulatory material, and applicable authorized references. Use the 250 study flash cards to reinforce trade knowledge and improve recall.
  6. Take and pass the examination. The Master HVAC portion consists of 85 items, with 64 correct answers required for the Master passing score.
  7. Complete the licensing process. After satisfying the examination and other applicable requirements, the Virginia Board for Contractors processes the individual license according to its licensing procedures.

The Master HVAC tradesman credential is an individual professional license. It should not be confused with a Virginia contractor business license. A contractor firm performing HVAC contracting may have separate licensing requirements through the Virginia Board for Contractors.

State Requirements

Virginia establishes specific experience standards for applicants seeking licensure as a master in a trade. A Master HVAC applicant must furnish evidence that one of the accepted experience standards has been met.

Current Virginia regulations provide three pathways for a master-level applicant:

  • Hold a valid Residential HVAC Tradesman license in the trade for three years;
  • Hold a valid Journeyman license in the trade for a minimum of one year; or
  • Document nine years of practical experience in the trade for which the master license is sought.

These requirements reflect the advanced level of experience expected from a Virginia Master HVAC tradesman. The examination is designed to test knowledge beyond basic equipment identification. Candidates should be prepared to work with heating and cooling principles, system installation requirements, ventilation, ductwork, combustion, refrigeration, controls, boilers, piping, commercial kitchen ventilation, and professional regulations.

Virginia Master HVAC tradesman licenses expire three years from the last day of the month in which the license was issued. As a condition of renewal, HVAC tradesmen must complete three hours of Board-approved continuing education related to applicable building code changes during the renewal period.

The continuing education requirement applies to both journeyman and master HVAC tradesmen. An individual holding both journeyman and master designations in the same trade does not have to duplicate the HVAC continuing education course solely because both designations are held.

Test Information and Study Materials

The Virginia Master HVAC exam covers both broad HVAC theory and practical installation knowledge. Candidates should be comfortable identifying system components, understanding equipment operation, interpreting system requirements, and applying trade principles to common installation and service situations.

Warm air heating is an important exam area. Study furnace operation, heating components, airflow principles, equipment installation, controls, venting, and related safety considerations. Understand how the parts of a forced-air heating system interact rather than memorizing isolated definitions.

Ventilation and air conditioning systems are also major areas of the examination. Candidates should review ventilation concepts, exhaust requirements, air movement, cooling principles, system components, and common HVAC installation practices.

Duct systems require familiarity with airflow distribution, duct construction concepts, installation practices, insulation, and related system considerations. Flash cards can be useful for reinforcing terms and components while reference-book practice can strengthen your ability to locate detailed requirements.

Chimneys, flues, vents, and combustion air require careful study because improper installation can create serious operating and safety problems. Candidates should understand the purpose of these systems and the relationship between appliance operation, combustion, venting, and air supply.

Boilers and hot water piping are included on the Master HVAC examination. Review hydronic heating concepts, boiler system components, water circulation, piping arrangements, safety controls, and other principles associated with hot-water heating systems.

Controls and low-voltage systems are another part of the exam outline. HVAC professionals should understand basic control sequences, thermostatic control, low-voltage components, and how controls affect heating and cooling system operation.

Refrigerants and refrigerant piping require understanding of refrigeration principles and system operation. Study how refrigerant moves through a system, the function of major refrigeration components, and concepts associated with refrigerant piping and system performance.

The examination also includes fuel oil piping and storage, insulation, commercial kitchen venting, chilled and condenser water piping, and firestopping and penetration inspection. These subjects reinforce why Master-level exam preparation should cover the full outline rather than concentrating only on residential air conditioning.

A useful study method is to divide the 250 cards into manageable groups. Review one group until you can answer most cards confidently, then move to another group while continuing to revisit missed material. This creates spaced repetition and keeps difficult subjects in your study rotation.

When a flash card identifies a weak subject, open the applicable reference and study that topic in context. Locate the chapter or section where related information appears. Over time, this connects recall with reference navigation, which is especially valuable for a timed open-book examination.

You can also practice with a timer. Choose a subject, locate a related rule or technical concept in your reference material, and note how quickly you can find it. The objective is not to memorize an entire technical book. The objective is to understand enough about the subject to know where to look and how to confirm an answer efficiently.

FAQ Section

How many flash cards are included?

This study product includes 250 Virginia Master HVAC exam prep flash cards.

Is the Virginia Master HVAC exam open book?

Yes. The Virginia Master HVAC examination is open book. Candidates must follow PSI rules regarding authorized references, permanent tabs, notes, and materials allowed in the examination center.

How many questions are on the Virginia Master HVAC exam?

The Journeyman/Master HVAC examination contains 85 items. A Master HVAC candidate must answer at least 64 items correctly to achieve the required Master passing score.

How much time is allowed for the Virginia Master HVAC exam?

Candidates receive 210 minutes to complete the Virginia Journeyman/Master HVAC examination.

Who administers the Virginia Master HVAC exam?

PSI administers the examination for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation and the Virginia Board for Contractors.

Do I need approval before scheduling the exam?

Yes. Virginia requires Board pre-approval before a tradesman candidate may sit for the examination.

What subjects should I study for the Master HVAC exam?

The exam covers Virginia licensing regulations and technical subjects including warm air heating, ventilation, air conditioning, ducts, boilers, hot water piping, combustion air, controls, oil systems, commercial kitchen venting, refrigerants, refrigerant piping, chilled and condenser water piping, and firestopping.

Can I use removable tabs in my exam books?

No. PSI permits permanent tabs but does not allow removable tabs in examination references. Reference materials must comply with the examination provider's rules.

Do these flash cards replace the reference books?

No. The flash cards are supplemental exam-preparation materials. Candidates should also study the applicable authorized references and practice locating information within them.

What experience is required to become a Virginia Master HVAC tradesman?

Virginia provides multiple qualification pathways, including three years holding a valid Residential HVAC Tradesman license, at least one year holding a valid journeyman license in the trade, or nine years of practical experience in the trade.

Does a Virginia Master HVAC license require continuing education?

Yes. HVAC tradesmen must complete three hours of Board-approved continuing education related to applicable building code changes as a condition of renewal.

How should I use the 250 study flash cards?

Study the cards in smaller groups, mark subjects that require additional attention, and repeatedly review weaker areas. Pair the flash cards with reference-book navigation so you practice both knowledge recall and finding information efficiently during an open-book exam.

How does the Master HVAC exam work?

The Master HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
