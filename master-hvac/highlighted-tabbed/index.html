Licensing Steps

The Virginia Master HVAC credential is an individual tradesman license. Candidates must satisfy the applicable Board requirements before the license can be issued.

  1. Review the Master HVAC qualification requirements. Determine which education, experience, or existing-license pathway applies to your background.
  2. Document the required qualifications. Applicants must provide the information requested by the Virginia Board for Contractors to establish eligibility.
  3. Submit the applicable application. The Board reviews the application and supporting information before examination approval is granted.
  4. Receive approval to test. Candidates must obtain the required approval before scheduling the Master HVAC examination.
  5. Schedule the examination. After approval, candidates can proceed with the applicable examination registration process.
  6. Prepare with the technical references. Study the Virginia regulations, International Mechanical Code, NFPA 31, and Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning while practicing reference navigation.
  7. Complete the required examination. Candidates must satisfy the applicable Master-level examination requirement.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Any outstanding Board requirements must be completed before the individual license is issued.

Candidates who intend to use the Master HVAC credential in connection with a contracting business should remember that the individual tradesman license and the Virginia contractor business license are separate credentials. The business must independently satisfy the contractor licensing requirements that apply to its class and classification or specialty.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual HVAC tradesmen as well as contractor businesses. A Master HVAC license is issued to an individual and reflects satisfaction of the applicable qualification and examination requirements for that trade.

A contractor business license is issued separately. Virginia contractor businesses may hold Class A, Class B, or Class C licenses and must also have the appropriate classification or specialty for the work the business performs.

For contractor businesses performing HVAC work, the appropriately qualified individual must meet the current Virginia requirements associated with the HVAC classification or specialty. Holding the Master HVAC credential can be an important part of that qualification structure, but it does not replace the business licensing process.

Virginia also imposes renewal and continuing-education requirements on individual tradesmen. Candidates preparing for initial licensure should distinguish between exam preparation and continuing education used for license renewal. This highlighted and tabbed book package is intended for examination preparation and does not replace Board-approved continuing education.

Virginia regulatory requirements can address qualifications, professional responsibilities, renewal, reinstatement, and other licensing matters. This makes the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations an important part of preparation rather than simply a background reference.

How does the Master HVAC exam work?

The Master HVAC exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
