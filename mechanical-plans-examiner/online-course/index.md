### Exam Details

The **ICC Mechanical Plans Examiner examination** is identified as **Exam ID M3**. The 2021 exam contains **50 multiple-choice questions** and has a **2-hour time limit**.

The examination evaluates the knowledge used by a Mechanical Plans Examiner when reviewing mechanical construction documents for compliance with applicable codes and standards. Plans examiners review submitted designs, identify deficiencies, communicate applicable requirements, and evaluate corrections or revisions before plans move forward in the permitting process.

The official M3 content areas include:

- **General Administration:** 5%
- **Appliances and Equipment:** 15%
- **Exhaust and Ventilation Systems:** 25%
- **Duct Systems:** 8%
- **Combustion Air:** 21%
- **Chimneys and Vents:** 17%
- **Fuel Supply Systems:** 9%

**Exhaust and Ventilation Systems** is the largest examination category at 25%. Candidates should prepare for questions involving environmental exhaust, ventilation, commercial kitchen systems, hazardous exhaust, product-conveying systems, and related mechanical-code requirements.

**Combustion Air** represents 21% of the examination. Candidates should become comfortable determining applicable combustion-air methods, sources, openings, ducts, sizing, and related requirements for fuel-burning appliances.

**Chimneys and Vents** represents 17% of the exam. Preparation should include vent types, sizing, clearances, connectors, terminations, chimneys, installation requirements, and other fuel-gas venting provisions.

**Appliances and Equipment** accounts for 15%. This area can include mechanical equipment, water heaters, boilers, refrigeration systems, furnaces, heaters, and other equipment subject to mechanical-code plan review.

Duct Systems, Fuel Supply Systems, and General Administration make up the remaining examination content. Candidates should prepare across all categories because the M3 exam is intended to evaluate broad plan-review knowledge.

### Open Book Test

The ICC Mechanical Plans Examiner M3 examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates should prepare by becoming familiar with both the International Mechanical Code and International Fuel Gas Code rather than assuming that having the books available will make each question easy to answer.

With 50 questions and a 2-hour limit, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Some questions can be answered quickly through code familiarity, while others may require locating a table, interpreting an exception, comparing several requirements, or reviewing a detailed plan-review condition.

Efficient reference selection is therefore important. A question involving ventilation, exhaust, ducts, equipment, commercial kitchen systems, refrigeration, or other mechanical installations will often begin in the **International Mechanical Code, 2021**. Questions involving combustion air, fuel-burning appliances, fuel supply, chimneys, vents, vent connectors, regulators, or fuel gas piping may require the **International Fuel Gas Code, 2021**.

Online preparation should include regular reference-navigation drills. Candidates can begin with a sample question, identify the subject, select the most appropriate code, locate the chapter, and then narrow the search to the exact section, table, definition, or exception.

Open-book preparation is most effective when technical understanding and navigation skills develop together. Candidates should avoid relying only on memorization because the exam emphasizes application of code requirements to mechanical plan-review situations.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Effective M3 preparation should follow the examination outline rather than treating the entire Mechanical Code and Fuel Gas Code as equally weighted material. This helps candidates devote more study time to the subjects that represent the greatest percentage of the examination.

**General Administration** represents 5% of the examination. Candidates should understand project administration, plan-review responsibilities, communication of code requirements, and how deficiencies in submitted documents are identified and documented.

**Appliances and Equipment** represents 15%. Preparation can include water heaters, boilers, refrigeration equipment, furnaces, heaters, mechanical equipment rooms, installation requirements, and related equipment provisions.

**Exhaust and Ventilation Systems** represents 25% and should receive substantial preparation time. Candidates should review mechanical and natural ventilation, exhaust systems, clothes dryer exhaust, commercial kitchen hoods, grease ducts, makeup air, hazardous exhaust systems, emergency exhaust, and product-conveying systems.

**Duct Systems** represents 8%. Candidates should study duct and plenum materials, construction, insulation, support, installation, fire and smoke dampers, and provisions related to maintaining fire-resistance and smoke-control requirements.

**Combustion Air** represents 21% and is one of the most important fuel-gas study areas. Candidates should practice determining the required size, location, source, and number of combustion-air openings or ducts for various appliance configurations.

**Chimneys and Vents** represents 17%. Preparation should include chimney types, vent systems, vent connectors, sizing, clearances, support, terminations, listings, materials, and installation requirements.

**Fuel Supply Systems** represents 9%. Candidates should review gas piping materials, sizing, supports, shutoff valves, regulators, and related fuel supply provisions.

Plan-review study should involve more than answering isolated factual questions. Candidates should practice reading a scenario, identifying the proposed mechanical condition, determining which code topic applies, locating the applicable requirement, and deciding whether the submitted design complies.

When a practice question is answered incorrectly, candidates should locate the supporting code provision and review the surrounding material. The correct answer may depend on an exception, definition, table note, occupancy condition, equipment type, system location, or another detail that is easy to overlook.

Timed practice is also valuable. The 2-hour exam requires candidates to manage both direct-answer questions and more detailed code lookups. Candidates should develop a habit of answering familiar questions efficiently and reserving additional time for questions involving calculations, tables, or multiple code provisions.

Repeated code navigation can build familiarity with the references. Over time, candidates should begin to recognize where major subjects are located without relying completely on the index. This can make open-book exam work more efficient.

## How does the Mechanical Plans Examiner (M3) exam work?

The Mechanical Plans Examiner (M3) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

- Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
- The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
- Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
- Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
