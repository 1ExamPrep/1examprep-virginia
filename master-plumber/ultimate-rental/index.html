Exam Details

The Virginia Master Plumber credential is an individual tradesman license regulated through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Virginia's Tradesmen Program regulates plumbing along with electrical, HVAC, gas fitting, and other individual trades.

Pre-approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors is required before a candidate may sit for the examination. Once approval has been issued, candidates can proceed with examination scheduling through PSI, which administers Virginia tradesman examinations.

The current Master Tradesman examination fee is $125 and is paid directly to PSI. Candidates should complete the applicable Board application and qualification requirements before attempting to schedule the examination.

Master Plumber preparation should cover both Virginia regulatory material and technical plumbing topics. Candidates should understand the requirements that apply to individual licensure while also developing strong knowledge of plumbing systems and the 2021 International Plumbing Code.

Technical preparation should include drainage, waste and vent systems, water distribution, plumbing fixtures, backflow protection, storm drainage, traps, interceptors, piping materials, and general plumbing requirements. Candidates should also become comfortable interpreting technical tables and locating information within the plumbing code.

Master-level preparation benefits from understanding how complete plumbing systems interact. Water distribution supplies fixtures, drainage removes waste, venting protects drainage-system operation and trap seals, and backflow protection helps safeguard potable-water systems. Understanding those relationships can make technical code provisions easier to interpret.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Master Plumber credential is issued to an individual rather than a contracting business. Candidates must complete the applicable qualification, application, and examination process before the individual license can be issued.

  1. Review the Master Plumber qualification requirements. Determine which current Virginia qualification pathway applies to your experience and licensing background.
  2. Prepare the required experience documentation. Applicants must provide the information required by the Virginia Board for Contractors to establish eligibility.
  3. Submit the applicable Tradesman application. The Board reviews the application before approving the candidate to test.
  4. Receive Board pre-approval. Virginia requires Board approval before a candidate may sit for the Master Plumber examination.
  5. Schedule the examination through PSI. Once approved, candidates can proceed with the current registration and scheduling process.
  6. Prepare with the applicable references. Use the Virginia regulations and International Plumbing Code throughout study while practicing technical questions and code navigation.
  7. Complete the required examination. Candidates must satisfy the applicable Master Plumber examination requirement.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Any additional requirements must be satisfied before the individual credential is issued.

The included Application Service adds support to this process by helping candidates organize the application portion of the licensing pathway. The service is included with the Ultimate package along with the rental books and one year of course access.

Candidates who plan to operate a plumbing contracting business should remember that individual Master Plumber licensure and contractor business licensing are separate. The contractor business must independently satisfy the applicable Virginia contractor licensing requirements.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual plumbers through the Tradesmen Program. The Master Plumber license is an individual credential associated with regulated plumbing work.

Virginia requires pre-approval before tradesman candidates can sit for the applicable examination. Applicants should complete the current application requirements and submit the documentation needed to establish their qualifications.

Virginia contractor businesses are regulated separately from individual tradesmen. A contracting business may need a Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license together with the appropriate classification or specialty for the work being performed.

This distinction matters for candidates who intend to own or qualify a plumbing contracting business. A Master Plumber license can serve as an important individual credential associated with plumbing contracting, but the business itself must complete the contractor licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Virginia Master Plumber study strategy should combine technical review, practice-oriented learning, and repeated use of the reference books. Candidates can organize preparation around drainage, waste and vents, water supply, backflow prevention, storm drainage, fixtures, traps, interceptors, code calculations, and Virginia regulations.

Drainage, waste and vent systems should receive substantial attention. Candidates should understand drainage pipe sizing, slopes, fittings, cleanouts, building drains, building sewers, vent configurations, vent sizing, and trap-seal protection. The goal is to understand both how these systems function and where the applicable code requirements are located.

Water-supply preparation should include water-distribution piping, materials, sizing concepts, pressure considerations, valves, connections, and system protection. Candidates should practice locating and interpreting the applicable tables within the International Plumbing Code.

Backflow prevention should also be included in regular study. Candidates should understand cross-connection hazards and the importance of protecting potable-water systems from contamination. Reference practice should include finding the applicable backflow provisions and recognizing the terminology used for different protection methods.

Plumbing-fixture study should include installation requirements, fixture connections, components, clearances, and related technical provisions. Candidates should become familiar with where common fixture requirements are located within the code.

Traps and interceptors are another important preparation area. Study trap seals, prohibited arrangements, interceptors, separators, and the role these components play within a properly functioning plumbing system.

Storm drainage preparation should include roof drainage, conductors, storm drains, sizing methods, and related code requirements. Candidates should practice working with applicable tables so they can identify what information is required before selecting a value.

Virginia regulatory material should be studied throughout the preparation period. The Individual License and Certification Regulations should become a working reference rather than a book reviewed only immediately before the examination.

How does the Master Plumber exam work?

The Master Plumber exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
