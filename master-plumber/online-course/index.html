Exam Details

The Virginia Master Plumber credential is an individual tradesman license regulated through the Virginia Board for Contractors. The Board regulates individual tradesmen engaged in plumbing and several other skilled trades throughout Virginia.

Candidates must receive pre-approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before sitting for the applicable tradesman examination. After approval, examinations are administered through PSI. The current Master Tradesman examination fee is $125 and is paid directly to PSI.

Master-level plumbing preparation should cover a broad range of technical knowledge. Candidates should understand plumbing-system layout, piping materials, fixtures, water distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, traps, interceptors, backflow prevention, storm drainage, and the application of code requirements to plumbing installations.

A Master Plumber should also understand how plumbing systems interact. Water-distribution systems must provide proper service to fixtures, sanitary drainage must carry waste effectively, venting protects trap seals, and backflow prevention helps protect potable-water systems. Exam preparation should therefore focus on complete plumbing systems rather than isolated facts.

The International Plumbing Code, 2021 is a primary technical reference for preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the code and know how to locate chapters, definitions, tables, technical requirements, and cross-references.

The Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations should also be studied throughout preparation. Master-level candidates need to understand applicable Virginia licensing terminology, qualification requirements, professional responsibilities, and other regulatory subjects in addition to technical plumbing material.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Master Plumber examination is open book. Candidates should prepare with the permitted references and become familiar with their organization before exam day.

An open-book examination still requires significant preparation. The International Plumbing Code contains extensive technical information, and searching unfamiliar sections during a timed examination can consume valuable time. Candidates should develop a reliable method for identifying the subject of a question and navigating directly toward the applicable information.

Questions involving Virginia licensing, qualification, professional conduct, renewal, or regulatory terminology may direct candidates toward the Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations. Technical questions involving plumbing fixtures, water distribution, drainage, venting, traps, materials, backflow protection, or storm drainage may require the International Plumbing Code, 2021.

Candidates should practice using the table of contents, index, definitions, chapter headings, code tables, and cross-references. Understanding plumbing terminology is especially valuable because an accurate technical term can often lead directly to the appropriate index entry or code provision.

A useful study method is to treat each practice question as a reference-navigation exercise. Read the question, identify the plumbing subject, select the appropriate reference, locate the relevant provision or table, and verify the answer. Repeating this process helps build familiarity with the books.

Candidates who prepare their books with highlighting, underlining, indexing, or tabs should make sure the materials comply with current examination rules before arriving at the testing center.

Licensing Steps

The Virginia Master Plumber credential is issued to an individual tradesman. Candidates must satisfy the applicable qualification, application, and examination requirements before the license can be issued.

  1. Review the Master Plumber qualification requirements. Determine which Virginia experience or existing-license pathway applies to your background.
  2. Document the required practical experience. Applicants must provide the experience information required by the Virginia Board for Contractors.
  3. Submit the applicable Tradesman application. The Board reviews the candidate’s qualification information before granting examination eligibility.
  4. Receive Board approval to test. Virginia requires pre-approval before candidates may sit for the Master Plumber examination.
  5. Schedule the examination through PSI. Once approved, candidates can complete the applicable testing registration and scheduling process.
  6. Prepare with the applicable references. Study the Virginia regulations and the 2021 International Plumbing Code while practicing technical questions and code navigation.
  7. Complete the Master Plumber examination. Candidates must satisfy the examination requirement for Master-level licensure.
  8. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Any outstanding Board requirements must be completed before the individual credential is issued.

Virginia provides multiple qualification pathways for Master tradesman applicants. These include pathways based on experience as a licensed Journeyman, applicable experience as a Virginia licensed residential plumber tradesman, or qualifying practical experience in the plumbing trade.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual plumbers through the Tradesmen Program. The Master Plumber credential is issued to an individual rather than directly to a contracting business.

This distinction is important for candidates planning to operate a plumbing company. A Virginia contractor business license and an individual Master Plumber license are separate credentials. A contractor business must independently satisfy the requirements for the appropriate contractor license class and classification or specialty.

Virginia contractor businesses may operate under Class A, Class B, or Class C licenses depending on the level of contracting activity authorized by state requirements. The classification or specialty identifies the type of contracting work the business is permitted to perform.

A Master Plumber credential can serve as an important individual qualification for a plumbing contractor business, but holding the individual license does not automatically create or issue a contractor business license.

Virginia also requires continuing education for licensed plumbers as part of the applicable license-renewal process. Continuing education used for renewal must meet Board requirements and is separate from preparation for the initial Master Plumber examination.

The online exam prep course is designed for examination preparation. It should not be treated as a substitute for Board-approved continuing education required for renewal unless a course is separately approved for that purpose.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia Master Plumber study strategy should combine plumbing knowledge with repeated code-navigation practice. Candidates can divide preparation into major areas such as water supply, fixtures, drainage, venting, traps, backflow protection, storm drainage, materials, calculations, and Virginia regulations.

Water-supply and distribution preparation should include piping materials, sizing concepts, valves, pressure considerations, system protection, and installation requirements. Candidates should know where applicable sizing information appears within the International Plumbing Code and practice using relevant tables.

Plumbing fixtures should also receive focused review. Candidates should study fixture installation, connections, clearances, fixture fittings, materials, and other code provisions affecting common plumbing fixtures.

Sanitary drainage is another important part of Master Plumber preparation. Candidates should review drainage-system sizing, slopes, cleanouts, fittings, building drains, building sewers, connections, and piping requirements. Technical tables should be practiced rather than simply read.

Venting should receive dedicated study because it plays an essential role in protecting trap seals and maintaining proper drainage-system operation. Candidates should understand vent terminology, connections, configurations, and sizing while learning how to locate applicable provisions efficiently.

Traps, interceptors, and separators should also be incorporated into the study schedule. Review trap requirements, prohibited configurations, trap-seal protection, interceptors, and related installation rules.

Backflow prevention is an important part of plumbing-system safety. Candidates should understand the purpose of protecting potable-water supplies from contamination and become familiar with the provisions governing cross-connection control and backflow protection.

Storm drainage preparation should include roof drainage, conductors, storm drains, system sizing, and related plumbing-code requirements. Candidates should become comfortable identifying when a storm-drainage table or technical provision applies.

Piping materials should be reviewed throughout the preparation process. Different applications may require specific materials, joining methods, fittings, or installation conditions. Candidates should understand how the code organizes material requirements and limitations.

Calculations and table use are also important elements of plumbing preparation. Rather than memorizing isolated values, candidates should practice identifying the correct table, understanding what information is required, and interpreting the result correctly.

Virginia regulatory material should remain part of the study plan throughout preparation. Candidates should become familiar with the terminology used by the Board and understand the requirements associated with individual Master Plumber licensure.

Online study can help candidates revisit difficult areas repeatedly. If vent sizing, drainage tables, water distribution, or regulatory topics require additional work, candidates can spend more time reviewing those sections before moving forward.

Practice questions are most useful when candidates verify answers within the references. This builds both subject knowledge and open-book navigation skills at the same time. Over time, candidates can become more familiar with where information is located and how different plumbing subjects are organized within the code.

How does the Master Plumber exam work?

The Master Plumber exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
