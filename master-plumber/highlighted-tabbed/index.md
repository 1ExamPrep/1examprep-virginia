### Exam Details

The Virginia Master Plumber credential is an individual tradesman license regulated through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates must meet the applicable qualification requirements and receive Board approval before taking the Master Plumber examination.

The current Master Plumber trade examination contains 100 questions. Candidates must achieve a score of at least 70 percent, which requires 70 correct answers. The examination allows 195 minutes.

The examination covers a broad range of plumbing knowledge. Major areas include general plumbing knowledge, materials and specifications, traps and interceptors, backflow prevention, plumbing fixtures, water supply systems, sanitary drainage, venting, storm drainage, and other code-based plumbing subjects.

Master-level candidates should understand more than individual installation procedures. They should be able to interpret code requirements, recognize appropriate materials, understand system design principles, use technical tables, and apply plumbing requirements to practical situations.

The Master designation reflects a higher level of proficiency within the trade. Virginia defines a Master as an individual with the ability, proficiency, and qualifications necessary to plan and lay out installation details and supervise installation, repair, and maintenance work while applying the applicable requirements of the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code.

Because plumbing work involves interconnected systems, candidates should prepare across the entire trade rather than concentrating on only one subject. Water distribution affects fixtures, drainage depends on appropriate sizing and slope, venting protects trap seals, and backflow prevention protects potable-water systems. Understanding how these systems work together supports both exam preparation and professional plumbing practice.

### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates individual plumbers through the Tradesmen Program. The Master Plumber license is issued to an individual and is separate from the contractor license issued to a plumbing business.

Virginia contractor businesses are licensed independently and may hold Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor licenses. The license class determines the level of contracting activity permitted, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the contractor may perform.

For a plumbing contractor business, the appropriately qualified individual must hold the required Virginia plumbing tradesman credential. A Master Plumber license therefore has an important role in the qualification structure of a plumbing contractor business, but it does not automatically create a contractor license for the company.

Candidates who plan to operate a plumbing contracting business should understand this distinction. Individual Master Plumber licensure establishes the tradesman credential, while the company must separately satisfy contractor licensing requirements.

Virginia also requires continuing education for licensed plumbers as part of the applicable renewal process. Continuing education used for license renewal must satisfy Board requirements and is separate from initial Master Plumber exam preparation.

The *Virginia Board for Contractors, Individual License and Certification Regulations* is therefore an important part of preparation. Candidates should become familiar with terminology, qualification provisions, professional responsibilities, renewal requirements, and other regulatory material associated with holding an individual tradesman license.

## How does the Master Plumber exam work?

The Master Plumber exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
