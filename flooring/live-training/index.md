### Exam Details

The **Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination** is the technical specialty examination associated with the FLR designation. Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors, and the specialty examination is administered through PSI.

- **Specialty:** Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting
- **Specialty Code:** FLR
- **Number of Questions:** 50
- **Time Allowed:** 120 minutes
- **Passing Score:** 70%, or 35 correct answers
- **Testing Provider:** PSI

The published exam content outline is organized into six principal subject areas:

- **Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone:** 16 questions
- **Resilient Flooring:** 6 questions
- **Wood Flooring:** 10 questions
- **Carpeting:** 8 questions
- **Estimating Materials:** 5 questions
- **Safety:** 5 questions

Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone is the largest single subject area on the examination, followed by Wood Flooring and Carpeting. Candidates should still prepare across every published topic because the exam also measures knowledge of resilient products, estimating, and jobsite safety.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination is an **open book test**. Open-book testing makes familiarity with the examination references especially important. Candidates should know which book is most likely to contain the information needed for a particular question and should practice locating relevant sections efficiently.

The exam allows 120 minutes for 50 questions. That gives candidates an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Spending several minutes searching through the wrong reference can make it difficult to complete the examination efficiently.

PSI reference rules permit approved books to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used, while removable tabs are not permitted. Candidates preparing for the open-book exam should organize their references according to current testing requirements and practice using those references throughout their study process.

Live virtual instruction and self-study preparation can be particularly useful for an open-book examination because candidates can develop both technical knowledge and book-navigation habits. The objective is not simply to own the reference books. Candidates should understand what each reference covers and how to locate information when a question requires a code, standard, installation method, safety regulation, or trade procedure.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors. A contractor license includes a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations. The FLR specialty identifies the type of flooring work the business is qualified to perform under Virginia contractor regulations.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting specialty.** Candidates pursuing flooring work within the defined Virginia specialty scope use the FLR designation.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** Contractor businesses must identify a Qualified Individual for each applicable classification or specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must have the required experience associated with the license class being pursued.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** Virginia requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor license applicants.
6. **Complete the required specialty examination process.** The FLR Qualified Individual must satisfy the examination requirements applicable to the specialty.
7. **Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable.** Class A and Class B contractor applicants have separate examination requirements involving the Designated Employee.
8. **Submit the contractor license application.** Complete the Virginia Board for Contractors application process and provide the information and supporting documentation required for the requested license class and specialty.

Passing the FLR specialty examination is an important part of the process, but passing the exam alone does not issue a Virginia contractor license. The business must satisfy the complete licensing requirements for the selected class and specialty.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting as the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials commonly used in the flooring industry.

The specialty includes work involving wood and wood-composite flooring, carpet securing products, vinyl, linoleum, ceramic, marble, stone, and other types of tile. It also includes installation or replacement of subflooring, leveling products, and other materials necessary to facilitate flooring or floor-covering installation.

Structural floor joists and other structural components of the flooring system are outside the specific scope of the FLR specialty.

The Qualified Individual for a Virginia contractor business must meet experience requirements connected to the requested license class. Virginia currently requires a minimum of one year of applicable experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for applicants seeking a new Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license. This requirement is separate from the technical specialty examination and is intended to address general business, regulatory, and contractor licensing subjects.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A strong FLR study plan should begin with the examination content outline. With 16 questions devoted to Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone, tile preparation deserves significant attention. Candidates should review substrates, surface preparation, setting materials, layout, cutting, grout, movement accommodation, installation methods, and recognized tile-industry practices.

The ANSI standards and ceramic, glass, and stone tile installation handbook provide technical guidance for this portion of the exam. Setting Tile reinforces practical trade knowledge and can help candidates connect technical requirements with actual installation processes.

Wood Flooring accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should study hardwood installation, substrate conditions, moisture considerations, layout, fastening, sanding, and finishing. Hardwood Floors: Laying, Sanding, and Finishing is especially useful for developing familiarity with the work sequence and technical considerations associated with wood floors.

Carpeting accounts for eight examination questions. Preparation should include measurement, layout, cushion, tack strips, seam planning, stretching, trimming, fitting, and floor preparation. Candidates should also be comfortable distinguishing carpet installation methods from those used for resilient, wood, and tile flooring.

Resilient Flooring accounts for six questions. Review substrate preparation, layout, adhesives, seams, cutting, and installation techniques. Because resilient flooring can reflect imperfections in the surface below it, floor preparation is an important part of this subject.

Estimating Materials accounts for five questions. Candidates should practice calculating square footage and determining flooring quantities from dimensions supplied in a problem. Careful reading and consistent units are important when answering estimating questions.

Safety accounts for five questions. Use OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 to become familiar with construction safety requirements relevant to flooring contractors. Flooring work can involve cutting tools, saws, grinders, adhesives, electrical equipment, material handling, and active construction environments, making safety preparation an important part of the exam.

For an open-book test, include reference navigation in every stage of study. When reviewing a practice question, first identify the subject area. Determine whether the question concerns tile standards, wood flooring, carpet, resilient flooring, estimating, or safety. Then identify the reference most likely to contain the supporting information.

Live training can help candidates see how technical subjects are organized, while self-study gives candidates more opportunities to repeat the lookup process. Repetition can make it easier to remember where important material is located and how each book is structured.
