### Exam Details

The **Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination** is the technical specialty examination associated with the FLR designation. The examination is administered through **PSI** for the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The examination contains **50 questions**, allows **120 minutes** for completion, and requires a minimum passing score of **70%, or 35 correct answers**.

The official content outline is divided into six major categories:

- **Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone – 16 questions**
- **Resilient Flooring – 6 questions**
- **Wood Flooring – 10 questions**
- **Carpeting – 8 questions**
- **Estimating Materials – 5 questions**
- **Safety – 5 questionsCeramic Floor Tile and Stone** is the largest examination area. Candidates should devote significant preparation time to tile substrates, surface preparation, setting materials, installation methods, layout, grout, movement accommodation, cutting, and recognized industry practices.

**Wood Flooring** is another major category. Preparation should include moisture considerations, subfloor conditions, flooring layout, installation, fastening, sanding, finishing, and practical hardwood-floor techniques.

**Carpeting** requires familiarity with measuring, layout, seams, cushion, tack strips, stretching, fitting, floor preparation, and installation procedures. **Resilient Flooring** requires knowledge of substrates, adhesives, layout, cutting, seams, and installation techniques.

**Estimating Materials** tests the candidate's ability to work with dimensions and flooring quantities. **Safety** requires familiarity with construction safety requirements relevant to flooring work, tools, materials, and jobsite conditions.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination is an **open book test**. Candidates are responsible for bringing allowable references and following current PSI rules governing reference materials.

Approved references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted under PSI reference rules, while removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates should prepare their books in accordance with current testing requirements.

The examination provides 120 minutes for 50 questions, giving candidates an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. That makes reference navigation an important part of preparation.

A candidate who knows a technical subject but spends several minutes searching the wrong reference can lose valuable testing time. The course therefore emphasizes identifying the type of question first and then selecting the most appropriate reference.

Hardwood questions can direct candidates toward Hardwood Floors: Laying, Sanding, and Finishing. General flooring concepts may be supported by Stanley Complete Flooring or Builder’s Guide to Floors. Safety questions should direct attention toward OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should begin with **Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone** because it represents the largest portion of the FLR examination. Combine standards-based study with practical installation review so you understand both technical requirements and the trade methods behind them.

Start with **substrate preparation**. Flooring performance depends heavily on the condition of the surface beneath the finished material. Candidates should understand leveling, cleanliness, stability, moisture-related concerns, and compatibility with the selected flooring system.

For tile work, review mortars, adhesives, grout, layout, cutting, setting, movement accommodation, and recognized installation methods. Use the ANSI reference to become comfortable with technical standards and the ceramic tile handbook to reinforce installation assemblies and methods.

Use **Setting Tile** to strengthen practical understanding of tile installation. Knowing how a process is performed can make it easier to interpret technical questions about materials, tools, preparation, or installation sequence.

For **Wood Flooring**, concentrate on moisture conditions, substrate preparation, layout, flooring direction, fastening, sanding, and finishing. Hardwood flooring requires careful preparation because wood responds to environmental conditions and moisture changes.

Review sanding as a complete process rather than an isolated task. Candidates should understand surface preparation, equipment, abrasive progression, and how sanding affects the final finish.

For **Carpeting**, study room measurement, layout, carpet direction, cushion, tack strips, seams, stretching, fitting, and trimming. Carpet questions may combine installation knowledge with estimating concepts.

For **Resilient Flooring**, review substrate preparation, adhesives, cutting, layout, seams, and installation. Because resilient materials can reveal surface imperfections, floor preparation deserves particular attention.

**Estimating Materials** should include repeated calculation practice. Candidates should be comfortable calculating square footage, flooring quantities, tile requirements, and other material needs using the dimensions provided in a problem.

Read estimating questions carefully and keep units consistent. A correct formula can still lead to the wrong answer if measurements are converted incorrectly or an installation condition is overlooked.

For **Safety**, study OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Flooring contractors may work with saws, grinders, cutting tools, adhesives, portable electrical tools, ladders, and construction materials in active work environments.

Review personal protective equipment, hand and power tools, electrical safety, material handling, housekeeping, and other OSHA concepts relevant to flooring work.

Reference navigation should be incorporated into every stage of preparation. When working through a practice question, first determine whether it concerns tile, hardwood, carpet, resilient flooring, estimating, or safety. Then identify the reference most likely to contain the supporting information.

Practice under timed conditions to build realistic exam habits. With 120 minutes for 50 questions, candidates should develop enough trade familiarity to answer straightforward questions efficiently and reserve reference lookups for technical details that require confirmation.

When an answer is missed, determine why. The problem may be an unfamiliar flooring term, incorrect installation method, calculation error, misunderstanding of a material, or difficulty finding the applicable information in a reference. Correcting the underlying weakness is more effective than memorizing a single answer.

The online course provides a study structure for bringing these subjects together. Candidates can work through the trade topics, practice exam-oriented questions, reinforce reference navigation, and build familiarity with the technical language used throughout flooring contracting.

## How does the Floor and Floor Covering Contractor exam work?

The Floor and Floor Covering Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.
