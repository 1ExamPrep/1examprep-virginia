Exam Details

The Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination is associated with the FLR specialty administered under the Virginia Board for Contractors. The Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation oversees contractor licensing, while PSI administers contractor specialty examinations.

Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty designation on the contractor license. Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting is among the specialties for which applicants must receive approval and complete the applicable technical examination requirement.

The exam process is intended to confirm knowledge related to the trade and the specialty. Flooring candidates should be prepared to work with subjects involving wood flooring, resilient flooring, ceramic and stone tile, carpeting, surface preparation, estimating, construction safety, installation methods, and trade practices.

The technical preparation process should also include familiarity with the references associated with flooring and contractor work. Different questions may require different books, so studying effectively involves learning how each reference is organized and what subjects it covers.

The listed books in The 1 Package span several areas of contractor knowledge. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports safety preparation. ANSI standards and the tile installation handbook support ceramic and stone tile study. Hardwood flooring and general flooring references support installation and finishing topics. The NASCLA Virginia contractor guide and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code support broader contractor, business, law, project management, and building code study.

Open Book Test

The Virginia contractor specialty examination is an open book test. PSI permits candidates to bring qualifying reference books into the examination, subject to its current examination rules.

Reference books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs may be used, while removable tabs are not permitted. The references must otherwise remain unmarked, and handwritten notes in the books are not permitted under PSI examination rules.

Because the examination is open book, effective preparation involves learning how to locate information quickly. Open book does not eliminate the need to study. Candidates who attempt to research every question from scratch can spend too much time searching through multiple references.

A better approach is to develop familiarity with the purpose and organization of each book. When a question relates to OSHA safety, the candidate should know which reference to reach for. When the question involves tile installation standards, the ANSI or tile handbook references may be more relevant. Hardwood flooring questions may point toward the dedicated hardwood flooring book or a general flooring reference.

Using the highlighted and tabbed books during preparation helps make that process more familiar. Repeated navigation can help candidates recognize where major subjects are located and reduce unnecessary searching during the examination.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing applies to the contracting business rather than simply issuing a contractor license to an individual. The business selects the appropriate license class and classification or specialty and identifies the individuals required to satisfy the applicable licensing requirements.

  1. Determine the contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The class determines the monetary level of work the licensed business may perform.
  2. Select the Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting specialty. FLR is the Virginia specialty designation for flooring and floor covering contracting work.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses businesses engaged in construction, removal, repair, or improvement work on property owned by others. A Virginia contractor license consists of a license class and a classification or specialty. The class determines the monetary level of contracting work the business may undertake, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the contractor is authorized to perform.

Virginia defines Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting, abbreviated FLR, as the service that provides for installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials common to the flooring industry. The scope includes wood and wood-composite flooring, tack strips and other products used to secure carpet, vinyl, linoleum, ceramic, marble, stone, and other tile.

The FLR specialty also includes installation or replacement of subflooring, leveling products, and other materials necessary to facilitate flooring or floor covering installation. The specialty does not include installation, repair, or removal of floor joists or other structural components of the flooring system.

Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty. The Qualified Individual must either be a full-time employee of the business working at least 30 hours per week or be listed as a member of Responsible Management.

Minimum experience requirements depend on the contractor license class. A Qualified Individual associated with a Class C license must have at least one year of experience in the appropriate classification or specialty. Class B requires at least three years, and Class A requires at least five years.

New contractor applicants must also complete eight hours of pre-license education. For Class A and Class B applicants, the course must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, it must be completed by a member of Responsible Management.

Pre-license education is a separate requirement from the technical FLR examination. Class A and Class B businesses may also have examination requirements associated with the Designated Employee in addition to the Qualified Individual specialty requirement.
