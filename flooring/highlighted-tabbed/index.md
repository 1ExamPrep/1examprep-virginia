### Exam Details

The **Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination** is a technical specialty examination for Qualified Individuals seeking the FLR designation. Virginia identifies FLR as one of the specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of an approved technical examination.

The examination contains **50 scored questions**, provides **120 minutes** for completion, and requires a minimum passing score of **70%, or 35 correct answers**.

The major examination areas include:

- **Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone**
- **Resilient Flooring**
- **Wood Flooring**
- **Carpeting**
- **Estimating Materials**
- **Safety**

Ceramic tile and stone make up a substantial part of the technical knowledge required for the FLR examination. Candidates should study recognized installation methods, substrates, mortars, adhesives, grout, movement accommodation, layout, cutting, setting procedures, and material requirements.

Wood-flooring preparation should include layout, substrate conditions, moisture considerations, fastening, installation techniques, sanding, and finishing. Carpet and resilient-flooring preparation should include surface preparation, measuring, layout, seams, adhesives, cushion, tack strips, and installation procedures.

Estimating is another important part of preparation. Candidates should be comfortable calculating square footage, determining material quantities, interpreting dimensions, and applying the information presented in trade scenarios.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting** as installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials commonly used in the flooring industry.

The FLR specialty includes **wood and wood-composite flooring, tack strips and other products used to secure carpet, vinyl and linoleum, ceramic, marble, stone, and other types of tile**.

Virginia's definition also includes installation or replacement of subflooring, leveling products, and other materials necessary to facilitate installation of flooring or floor coverings.

The specialty does not include installation, repair, or removal of floor joists or other structural components of the flooring system. Structural work beyond the FLR scope requires the appropriate contractor authorization.

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses the business entity. The contractor license class establishes the monetary level of work the business may undertake, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the contractor is authorized to perform.

Virginia identifies FLR as one of the specialties for which Qualified Individuals must successfully complete an approved technical examination in addition to meeting applicable experience requirements.

Passing the FLR examination is only one part of obtaining the contractor license. Applicants must complete the full Board licensing process before performing work that requires a Virginia contractor license.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Begin preparation with **ceramic tile and stone**. This subject requires both practical installation knowledge and familiarity with industry standards. Use the ANSI reference with the ceramic tile handbook and Setting Tile so technical requirements can be connected with real-world installation methods.

Study substrate conditions before focusing only on the finished material. Flooring systems depend on a suitable supporting surface, and candidates should understand preparation, flatness, stability, moisture-related concerns, leveling, and compatibility with the selected installation system.

For ceramic tile, review layout, setting materials, mortars, adhesives, grout, joints, movement accommodation, cutting, and installation methods. Learn the organization of the ANSI reference so individual provisions can be located efficiently.

Because this package contains the **August 2024 ANSI edition**, candidates should remember that the examination's listed reference basis is the 2017 edition. Use the newer book as the included reference while keeping the exam edition distinction in mind during preparation.

The **Handbook for Ceramic Tile** supports study of recognized tile installation methods. Learn how the handbook organizes assemblies according to substrate, location, and installation conditions so you can quickly narrow a question to the relevant method.

**Setting Tile** reinforces practical skills such as surface preparation, layout, cutting, setting, and grouting. Practical trade knowledge can help candidates interpret technical exam questions more efficiently.

For **wood flooring**, study moisture considerations, substrate conditions, layout, installation direction, fastening, sanding, and finishing. Wood can change dimension as environmental conditions change, so moisture awareness is an important part of proper installation.

Use **Hardwood Floors: Laying, Sanding, and Finishing** to review the complete sequence of hardwood work. Understand how preparation, installation, sanding, and finishing affect the performance and appearance of the completed floor.

For **resilient flooring**, focus on surface preparation, adhesives, layout, cutting, seams, and installation techniques. The finished appearance of resilient flooring can be strongly affected by substrate irregularities and poor preparation.

For **carpeting**, review measuring, layout, cushion, tack strips, seams, stretching, trimming, and installation methods. Understanding material dimensions and room layout also supports estimating questions.

**Estimating Materials** requires comfort with common flooring calculations. Practice calculating floor area, tile quantities, material requirements, and other measurements using consistent units.

Read each estimating question carefully. Dimensions, waste requirements, layout conditions, or material sizes can affect the final quantity. Organize calculations before selecting an answer.

Use **Stanley Complete Flooring** for broad practical review across flooring categories. **Builder’s Guide to Floors** can reinforce knowledge of floors, substrates, construction systems, and general installation considerations.

For **Safety**, study OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. Flooring contractors may work with saws, grinders, portable electrical tools, adhesives, cutting equipment, ladders, and construction materials while operating around other trades.

Review personal protective equipment, hand and power tools, electrical safety, housekeeping, and material-handling requirements. Candidates should understand both safe work practices and how to locate applicable OSHA provisions quickly.

Highlighted sections and tabs can reduce the amount of time spent searching, but they do not replace understanding. During practice, first identify whether the question concerns tile, hardwood, resilient flooring, carpet, estimating, or safety. Then select the most appropriate reference.

A productive open-book method is to answer familiar questions directly and use the references for technical details, standards, calculations, or provisions that require confirmation. Avoid searching multiple books when the subject clearly belongs to one reference.

Practice under timed conditions. With 120 minutes for 50 scored questions, candidates average approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Time spent learning book navigation before test day can make the examination more manageable.

When reviewing missed questions, identify the underlying reason. A mistake may come from selecting the wrong tile method, misunderstanding a substrate requirement, making a measurement error, confusing flooring materials, or failing to locate the applicable OSHA provision.

A strong preparation routine combines technical reading, trade knowledge, calculation practice, OSHA review, and repeated use of the highlighted and tabbed references.

## How does the Floor and Floor Covering Contractor exam work?

The Floor and Floor Covering Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
