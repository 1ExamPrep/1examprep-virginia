### State Requirements

Virginia defines Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting as work involving the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials commonly used in the flooring industry.

The FLR specialty includes materials such as wood and wood-composite flooring, carpet securing products, vinyl, linoleum, ceramic, marble, stone, and other types of tile.

The specialty also includes installation or replacement of subflooring, leveling products, and other materials necessary to support flooring or floor-covering installation. Structural floor components are treated separately from the specific flooring specialty scope.

Virginia contractor licensing requirements involve both the business and the individuals responsible for meeting qualification requirements. The Qualified Individual is associated with the technical qualifications for the requested classification or specialty.

Candidates preparing for the FLR exam should study the technical trade material while also understanding that exam completion is only one part of the overall licensing process.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Begin your FLR preparation with the largest exam category: **Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone**. Review substrates, surface preparation, tile layout, cutting, mortars, adhesives, grout, movement accommodation, and recognized installation procedures.

Use the ANSI reference and the ceramic, glass, and stone tile installation handbook to develop familiarity with technical standards and recognized installation methods. Setting Tile provides additional practical information that can help reinforce how tile work is performed in the field.

For **Wood Flooring**, review moisture conditions, substrate preparation, flooring layout, fastening methods, sanding, and finishing. Hardwood Floors: Laying, Sanding, and Finishing provides focused preparation for these topics.

For **Carpeting**, study measurement, room layout, cushion, tack strips, seams, stretching, fitting, trimming, and floor preparation. Candidates should understand how carpet installation methods differ from tile, wood, and resilient flooring systems.

For **Resilient Flooring**, review adhesives, floor preparation, substrates, layout, seams, cutting, and installation procedures. Surface preparation is especially important because conditions beneath the finished material can affect the final installation.

**Estimating Materials** requires candidates to work with dimensions and flooring quantities. Practice calculating square footage and other material requirements using the measurements provided in sample problems. Careful reading and consistent units are important.

The **Safety** portion of the exam requires familiarity with construction safety topics. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation involving tools, personal protective equipment, electrical safety, material handling, housekeeping, and other jobsite conditions.

Reference navigation should be part of every study session. Instead of reading every book from beginning to end, practice working from a question to the applicable reference. Determine the subject first, select the correct book, and then use the tabs, index, and section headings to locate the relevant information.

This process is particularly useful for a timed open-book examination. Candidates who understand where subjects are located can spend less time deciding where to search and more time reading the actual requirement or installation guidance.

## How does the Floor and Floor Covering Contractor exam work?

The Floor and Floor Covering Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
