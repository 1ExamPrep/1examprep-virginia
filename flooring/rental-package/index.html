Exam Details

The Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination is the technical specialty exam associated with the FLR designation. The examination measures knowledge related to flooring installation, material preparation, estimating, and construction safety.

  • Specialty: Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting
  • Specialty Abbreviation: FLR
  • Number of Questions: 50
  • Time Allowed: 120 minutes
  • Passing Score: 70%, or 35 correct answers
  • Testing Provider: PSI

The examination content is organized into six major areas:

  • Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone: 16 questions
  • Resilient Flooring: 6 questions
  • Wood Flooring: 10 questions
  • Carpeting: 8 questions
  • Estimating Materials: 5 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone represents the largest portion of the examination. Candidates should devote meaningful study time to tile installation standards, substrates, surface preparation, setting materials, layout, grout, movement considerations, and installation procedures.

Wood Flooring and Carpeting are also significant portions of the exam. Resilient flooring, estimating, and safety complete the published content areas and should be included in a complete preparation plan.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting examination is an open book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing allowable reference materials and following the current testing rules that apply to those materials.

Approved books may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted, while removable tabs are not allowed. Preparing the books before the exam can help candidates build familiarity with the layout of each reference and improve their ability to move quickly between important sections.

The examination provides 120 minutes for 50 questions. That gives candidates an average of approximately two minutes and twenty-four seconds per question. Reference-navigation skills can therefore play an important role in time management.

A strong preparation strategy begins with identifying the type of question before opening a book. For example, a question about tile installation standards will likely require a different reference than a question involving hardwood flooring, OSHA safety, carpet installation, or contractor business requirements.

Using the rental books throughout your preparation can help you become familiar with which subjects belong in each reference. The goal is to develop a repeatable search process rather than approaching every question as a completely new lookup.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to businesses and sole proprietors and include a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Determine the appropriate license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the FLR specialty. Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation FLR.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the applicable specialty.
  4. Meet the experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience requirements associated with the license class being pursued.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable pre-license education requirements.
  6. Complete the required technical examination process. Candidates pursuing the FLR specialty must satisfy the applicable examination requirements.
  7. Complete any additional business examination requirements. Additional requirements may apply depending on the contractor license class.
  8. Submit the contractor license application. Complete the required Virginia Board for Contractors application process and provide the required supporting information.

Passing the technical examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. Candidates must also satisfy the other requirements applicable to their selected license class and specialty.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Flooring and Floor Covering Contracting as work involving the installation, repair, improvement, or removal of materials commonly used in the flooring industry.

The FLR specialty includes materials such as wood and wood-composite flooring, carpet securing products, vinyl, linoleum, ceramic, marble, stone, and other types of tile.

The specialty also includes installation or replacement of subflooring, leveling products, and other materials necessary to support flooring or floor-covering installation. Structural floor components are treated separately from the specific flooring specialty scope.

Virginia contractor licensing requirements involve both the business and the individuals responsible for meeting qualification requirements. The Qualified Individual is associated with the technical qualifications for the requested classification or specialty.

Candidates preparing for the FLR exam should study the technical trade material while also understanding that exam completion is only one part of the overall licensing process.

Test Information and Study Materials

Begin your FLR preparation with the largest exam category: Ceramic Floor Tile and Stone. Review substrates, surface preparation, tile layout, cutting, mortars, adhesives, grout, movement accommodation, and recognized installation procedures.

Use the ANSI reference and the ceramic, glass, and stone tile installation handbook to develop familiarity with technical standards and recognized installation methods. Setting Tile provides additional practical information that can help reinforce how tile work is performed in the field.

For Wood Flooring, review moisture conditions, substrate preparation, flooring layout, fastening methods, sanding, and finishing. Hardwood Floors: Laying, Sanding, and Finishing provides focused preparation for these topics.

For Carpeting, study measurement, room layout, cushion, tack strips, seams, stretching, fitting, trimming, and floor preparation. Candidates should understand how carpet installation methods differ from tile, wood, and resilient flooring systems.

For Resilient Flooring, review adhesives, floor preparation, substrates, layout, seams, cutting, and installation procedures. Surface preparation is especially important because conditions beneath the finished material can affect the final installation.

Estimating Materials requires candidates to work with dimensions and flooring quantities. Practice calculating square footage and other material requirements using the measurements provided in sample problems. Careful reading and consistent units are important.

The Safety portion of the exam requires familiarity with construction safety topics. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation involving tools, personal protective equipment, electrical safety, material handling, housekeeping, and other jobsite conditions.

Reference navigation should be part of every study session. Instead of reading every book from beginning to end, practice working from a question to the applicable reference. Determine the subject first, select the correct book, and then use the tabs, index, and section headings to locate the relevant information.

This process is particularly useful for a timed open-book examination. Candidates who understand where subjects are located can spend less time deciding where to search and more time reading the actual requirement or installation guidance.
