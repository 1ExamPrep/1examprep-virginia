Where should you start if your Virginia exam is coming up?

Three things trip up most Virginia candidates, and none of them is a lack of trade knowledge. The first is the clock. The contractor and tradesman exams are open book, and the people who run out of time are the ones who look up answers they should already know roughly where to find. Tab your references, read the table of contents until you can place any topic in its chapter, and drill the index with a timer.

The second is the business side. Most Class A and B firms sit the business and law portion as well as the trade exam, and contract law, lien rules and the Virginia regulations catch people who only studied their trade. The third is the edition. Your PSI candidate bulletin names the reference books your test is written to, and the books you bring must match it. Questions we get asked most

How long should I study for a Virginia contractor exam?

Most candidates do well with six to eight weeks at about 90 minutes a day, split between finding answers fast in the references, the business and law material, and full-length timed practice in the last two weeks.

Is the exam open book, and which books do I bring?

The PSI contractor and tradesman exams are open book. Bring the editions named in your candidate bulletin; highlighting and permanent tabs are allowed, removable flags and loose notes are not.

What is the difference between Class A, B and C?

The class sets the size of job your firm may take: Class C up to under $10,000 a job, Class B up to under $120,000 a job and $750,000 a year, Class A above that. Class A and B also need the business exams.

Are the practice questions the real exam questions?

No. Licensing exam questions are confidential and are not published. Practice questions are original material written to the same subjects and format.

Does buying a package register me for the exam?

No. The Board for Contractors approves you first, and PSI schedules the exam. Code officials go through DHCD and ICC instead.
