Exam Details

The ICC Commercial Energy Plans Examiner exam is identified by exam code 78. It is part of the International Code Council’s certification exam program and is used to measure knowledge related to commercial energy plan review. The 2021 exam version uses the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code as the key reference.

The ICC 78 exam is built around the job tasks of a commercial energy plans examiner. Students should expect to study commercial energy provisions that apply to submitted construction documents, including code administration, definitions, building thermal envelope requirements, fenestration, air leakage, insulation, mechanical systems, HVAC controls, service water heating, electrical power, lighting systems, lighting controls, additional efficiency requirements, existing building provisions, and referenced standards.

The 2021 International Energy Conservation Code includes both commercial and residential energy provisions. For the ICC 78 Commercial Energy Plans Examiner exam, students should focus on the commercial provisions used to evaluate commercial building plans and supporting documents. The commercial energy provisions include requirements that affect building design, equipment selection, system controls, lighting design, envelope construction, documentation, and compliance paths.

Commercial energy plan review questions often require careful reading and strong code navigation. A question may describe a submitted plan detail, equipment schedule, lighting layout, envelope assembly, glazing condition, mechanical system, control sequence, or compliance documentation issue. The correct answer may depend on locating the proper section, checking definitions, reviewing a table, reading notes or exceptions, and applying the requirement to the plan condition described.

Students preparing for the ICC 78 exam should focus on applying code provisions to construction documents. This includes understanding what information should appear on plans, how energy code requirements connect to building systems, and how to evaluate whether a submitted design demonstrates compliance with the commercial provisions of the International Energy Conservation Code.

Open Book Test

The ICC 78 Commercial Energy Plans Examiner exam is an open book exam. Because it is open book, students should prepare by learning how to use the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code quickly and accurately. The exam is not only a test of energy code knowledge; it is also a test of code navigation, reading comprehension, and the ability to apply code requirements to plan review situations.

Open book testing does not make the exam easy. Students who are unfamiliar with the code book may spend too much time searching for answers. Strong preparation includes repeated practice with the book, review of chapter organization, familiarity with definitions, use of the index, and practice applying tables, notes, and exceptions.

For an open book commercial energy plans examiner exam, students should practice identifying the subject of the question before searching for the answer. A question about insulation may require review of building thermal envelope provisions. A question about windows or skylights may require review of fenestration requirements. A question about HVAC equipment or controls may require mechanical system provisions. A question about lighting may require lighting power or lighting control provisions. Recognizing the topic quickly helps students move to the correct part of the code book.

Students should use the rental book actively during preparation. Helpful habits include reviewing chapter headings, marking major code areas, studying definitions, learning where important tables are located, and answering timed practice questions. The goal is to make the book easier to use during the exam and reduce the time spent searching for each answer.

Test Information and Study Materials

The ICC 78 Commercial Energy Plans Examiner exam preparation process should be centered on the 2021 International Energy Conservation Code. This book is the main reference for the exam and the primary tool students must learn to navigate. Since the exam is open book, the ability to find answers efficiently is just as important as understanding commercial energy plan review concepts.

Students should begin by reviewing the structure of the International Energy Conservation Code. The 2021 edition includes commercial provisions and residential provisions. For the Commercial Energy Plans Examiner exam, students should place special focus on the commercial provisions used during plan review. Important areas include scope and administration, definitions, general requirements, commercial energy efficiency, existing buildings, referenced standards, and commercial compliance paths.

Definitions are especially important in energy code preparation. Terms such as building thermal envelope, continuous insulation, conditioned space, fenestration, skylight, opaque door, R-value, U-factor, SHGC, economizer, daylight zone, automatic control, commissioning, and additional efficiency package can affect how a requirement applies. A student who skips definitions may misunderstand the plan condition or select the wrong code section.

Building thermal envelope requirements should receive focused review. Students should study provisions related to roofs, walls, floors, slab-on-grade floors, below-grade walls, doors, windows, skylights, continuous insulation, air barriers, air leakage, vestibules, and insulation installation. Plan review questions may ask whether a submitted envelope assembly, insulation value, or fenestration detail satisfies the applicable requirement.

Fenestration and glazing provisions are important commercial energy topics. Students should understand how windows, skylights, glazed doors, U-factors, solar heat gain coefficients, area limits, daylight zones, and related plan information are addressed in the code. These questions often require careful use of tables, notes, and exceptions.

Mechanical system requirements are another major study area. Students should review HVAC equipment efficiency, system controls, economizers, duct and piping insulation, ventilation-related energy provisions, commissioning-related requirements, equipment schedules, and required documentation. Commercial energy plans examiners may need to review whether the submitted mechanical design shows compliance with applicable energy code provisions.

Service water heating should also be included in the study schedule. Students should review water heating equipment, piping insulation, controls, heat traps, circulation systems, and related commercial energy requirements. Plan review questions in this area may involve identifying whether the submitted system includes required controls or documentation.

Electrical power and lighting systems are central to commercial energy plan review. Students should study lighting power allowances, lighting controls, automatic shutoff, occupancy sensors, daylight-responsive controls, exterior lighting, controlled receptacles where applicable, and related documentation. Commercial lighting questions can involve both code concepts and table-based application, so students should practice using the relevant sections repeatedly.

Existing building provisions should also be reviewed. Commercial energy plans examiners may encounter additions, alterations, repairs, or changes of occupancy. These situations can involve different requirements from new construction. Students should understand where existing building requirements are located and how they apply to commercial energy plan review questions.

Timed practice is important because energy plan review questions can require several steps. A student may need to identify the system, choose the correct chapter, read the definition, check a table, review an exception, and then apply the requirement to a drawing or design condition. Practicing under timed conditions helps students build the habit of moving efficiently while still reading carefully.

Students should also review missed questions in detail. A missed answer may come from using the wrong chapter, overlooking a definition, missing a table note, confusing residential and commercial provisions, or applying a general construction assumption instead of the code language. Careful review helps students correct those habits before exam day.

How does the Commercial Energy Plans Examiner exam work?

The Commercial Energy Plans Examiner exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
