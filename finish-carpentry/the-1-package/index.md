### Exam Details

The Virginia Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the **FIN** designation under the Virginia Board for Contractors. The examination is administered through PSI.

- **Exam Name:** Finish Carpentry Contracting Examination
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** FIN
- **Number of Questions:** 30
- **Time Allowed:** 60 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 21 correct answers
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

The published examination outline divides the 30 questions among eight technical areas:

- **Fabrication:** 6 questions
- **Installation:** 6 questions
- **Materials:** 5 questions
- **Finishing:** 3 questions
- **Hand Tools:** 3 questions
- **Safety:** 3 questions
- **Machines:** 2 questions
- **Plans and Estimating:** 2 questions

Fabrication and Installation are the two largest exam categories, with six questions each. Materials follows with five questions. Together, these subjects account for more than half of the technical examination and reflect the practical skills involved in producing and installing finished woodwork.

Finishing, Hand Tools, and Safety each contribute three questions. Machines and Plans and Estimating each contribute two questions. Candidates should prepare across every category because each question contributes directly toward the minimum passing score.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials to the examination because books are not supplied at the testing center.

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates should prepare and use their permitted references in accordance with the current PSI examination rules.

The FIN exam allows 60 minutes for 30 questions, which provides an average of approximately two minutes per question. Candidates benefit from knowing which reference applies to each type of question before beginning a detailed search.

Questions involving fabrication, trim work, fitting, installation, or finish-carpentry methods may direct candidates toward *Finish Carpenter's Manual*. Cabinet construction, furniture components, joinery, and assembly questions may be supported by *The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction*. Safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

A strong open-book strategy is to identify the subject first, select the most relevant reference, and then use the table of contents, index, chapter headings, and technical terminology to locate supporting information efficiently.

The objective is not to search for every answer. Candidates should build enough trade knowledge to recognize straightforward questions and use their references strategically when detailed technical information, unfamiliar terminology, or a specific procedure needs to be located.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Finish Carpentry Contracting** as the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork.

The FIN specialty does not include installation of ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone. Those materials are outside the Finish Carpentry Contracting scope established in Virginia's contractor regulations.

The Virginia Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications, along with the Home Improvement and Commercial Improvement specialties, also provide authority for finish-carpentry functions within their respective scopes.

Finish carpentry should be distinguished from structural framing. The FIN specialty focuses on finished building components and detailed millwork rather than structural framing systems. Candidates should therefore concentrate their technical preparation on cabinets, trim, casing, flooring, countertops, fabrication, fitting, installation, tools, materials, and related finishing work.

The contracting business must satisfy all requirements associated with its selected license class and FIN specialty. The technical exam is an important step, but Virginia contractor licensing also involves business, education, qualification, experience, and application requirements.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Fabrication accounts for six exam questions and should receive substantial attention. Candidates should understand measuring, layout, cutting, shaping, joining, fitting, and assembly. Finish-carpentry components frequently require close tolerances, making accuracy an important part of both preparation and professional work.

Installation also contains six questions. Candidates should review procedures involving cabinets, door and sash casing, baseboards, countertops, wooden flooring, trim, and other millwork. Existing walls, floors, openings, corners, and dimensions can all influence installation methods.

Materials accounts for five questions. Study common wood products, manufactured products, cabinet components, fasteners, adhesives, trim materials, and the characteristics that affect cutting, fastening, joinery, installation, and finishing.

Finishing contributes three questions. Candidates should understand surface preparation and how workmanship during fabrication and installation affects the final appearance of completed woodwork. Proper preparation, sanding, fitting, and finishing are closely connected in high-quality finish carpentry.

Hand Tools also accounts for three questions. Finish carpentry depends on accurate use of measuring, marking, cutting, fitting, fastening, trimming, and shaping tools. Candidates should understand which tools are appropriate for specific tasks and how to use them safely.

Machines accounts for two questions. Cabinet and millwork fabrication may involve woodworking machines used for cutting, drilling, shaping, or sanding. Candidates should understand the general purpose of common equipment and the importance of safe operation.

Safety accounts for three questions. Finish carpentry can involve saws, routers, nailers, drills, sanders, ladders, portable tools, and other equipment. Candidates should practice navigating 29 CFR Part 1926 and connecting safety requirements to practical construction situations.

Plans and Estimating accounts for two questions. Candidates should be comfortable interpreting dimensions, calculating lengths and quantities, understanding project information, and determining material requirements for finish-carpentry installations.

## How does the Finish Carpentry Contractor exam work?

The Finish Carpentry Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
