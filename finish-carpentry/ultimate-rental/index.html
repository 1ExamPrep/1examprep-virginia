Exam Details

The Virginia Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the FIN designation under the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates pursuing this specialty must satisfy the applicable Qualified Individual and examination requirements before the specialty can be added to a contractor license.

The examination focuses on the practical knowledge used in finish-carpentry work. Preparation should address subjects such as fabrication, installation, materials, finishing, hand tools, machinery, plans, estimating, and construction safety.

Fabrication and installation are especially important because finish carpentry depends on accurate measurements, proper layout, controlled cutting, detailed fitting, and quality assembly. Candidates should understand how finished components are produced and how they are installed in completed or partially completed building spaces.

Materials knowledge is also important. Different woods, manufactured products, fasteners, adhesives, and cabinet components can behave differently during cutting, fitting, fastening, assembly, and finishing. Candidates should study the characteristics and common applications of materials used in finish-carpentry work.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials to the examination because the testing center does not supply the books.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who understand where information is located. Knowing that a subject is somewhere in a reference is different from being able to locate it efficiently under examination conditions. Candidates should become familiar with each book's table of contents, index, chapter structure, illustrations, terminology, and major subject divisions.

A question involving trim fabrication, layout, fitting, installation, or finish-carpentry techniques may lead to Finish Carpenter's Manual. Cabinet or furniture construction questions may be supported by The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction. Workplace safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

A practical open-book strategy is to identify the trade subject before selecting a reference. Determine whether the question involves fabrication, installation, materials, finishing, hand tools, machines, estimating, or safety. Then use the appropriate book rather than searching through several references.

The objective is to develop enough trade knowledge to answer straightforward questions confidently while using the references strategically for technical details, unfamiliar terminology, procedures, or measurements that require additional support.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Virginia contractor licenses combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the Finish Carpentry Contracting specialty. Virginia identifies this specialty with the abbreviation FIN.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable requirements for the FIN specialty.
  4. Document qualifying experience. Qualified Individuals for FIN must provide the applicable experience verification.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable approved pre-license education requirements.
  6. Complete the required FIN technical examination.
  7. Complete any additional examination requirements associated with the selected contractor license class.
  8. Prepare the contractor license application and supporting documentation.
  9. Submit the required licensing materials through the Virginia Board for Contractors process.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Finish Carpentry Contracting as the service that provides for the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork.

Finish Carpentry Contracting does not include installation of ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone. Those materials fall outside the specific FIN specialty scope.

Virginia also recognizes the Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications and the Home Improvement and Commercial Improvement specialties as providing authority for finish-carpentry work within their applicable scopes.

Qualified Individuals seeking the FIN designation are among those required to satisfy the applicable experience-verification and technical examination requirements. The contractor business must also satisfy the licensing requirements associated with its selected class, business structure, education, examinations, and application.

Finish carpentry should be distinguished from structural framing. The FIN specialty focuses on finished components, cabinets, trim, flooring, countertops, casing, and millwork rather than the structural framing of a building.

Test Information and Study Materials

Fabrication should receive substantial attention during preparation. Candidates should understand layout, measurements, cutting, shaping, joining, fitting, assembly, and the steps used to produce finished components. Accuracy is particularly important because minor errors can affect both function and appearance.

Installation is another major finish-carpentry subject. Candidates should study the methods used to install cabinets, casing, baseboards, countertops, wooden flooring, trim, and other millwork. Existing walls, floors, openings, and building conditions can affect how finish components are fitted and secured.

Materials knowledge helps candidates understand how different wood products, manufactured products, fasteners, adhesives, and cabinet components perform. Material selection can influence cutting, fastening, joinery, finishing, durability, and overall appearance.

Finishing study should include surface preparation and the steps required to produce a professional finished result. Candidates should recognize how proper fitting, sanding, preparation, and workmanship affect the appearance of finished woodwork.

Hand tools are central to finish-carpentry work. Candidates should understand common tools used for measuring, marking, cutting, fitting, fastening, trimming, and adjustment. Tool selection often depends on the material, required level of precision, and specific task being performed.

Woodworking machinery may also be used for cutting, drilling, shaping, sanding, and fabrication. Candidates should understand the general function of commonly used machines and recognize safe operating practices.

Construction safety should remain part of the study routine throughout preparation. Finish carpenters may use saws, routers, drills, sanders, nailers, ladders, portable power tools, and stationary equipment. The OSHA construction regulations provide an important reference for applicable worker-protection requirements.

Plans and estimating connect technical carpentry knowledge with project requirements. Candidates should practice interpreting dimensions, calculating lengths and quantities, understanding project information, and translating measurements into material and installation needs.

The included 1 year of course access gives candidates time to combine structured online study with direct use of the reference books. A candidate can review a subject in the course and then locate related information in the appropriate technical reference.

Practice-oriented preparation should also include reviewing mistakes. If a question is answered incorrectly, determine whether the issue involved terminology, material selection, tool knowledge, installation technique, measurement, safety, or inefficient reference navigation. Then return to the supporting material and reinforce the concept.

Reference-navigation practice can help candidates develop a repeatable process. First identify the subject, then select the appropriate book, locate the relevant chapter or section, and evaluate the information in relation to the question. Repeating this process during study can make open-book testing more manageable.
