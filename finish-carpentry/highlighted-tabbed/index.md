### Exam Details

The Virginia Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. Virginia identifies the specialty with the abbreviation **FIN**, and the examination is administered through PSI.

- **Exam Name:** Finish Carpentry Contracting Examination
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** FIN
- **Number of Questions:** 30
- **Time Allowed:** 60 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 21 correct answers
- **Exam Format:** Multiple choice
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues contractor licenses as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
2. **Select the Finish Carpentry Contracting specialty.** Virginia identifies this specialty with the abbreviation FIN.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who meets the applicable requirements for the FIN specialty.
4. **Meet the applicable qualification requirements.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy Virginia's applicable experience and examination requirements.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
6. **Receive the applicable examination approval.** Virginia specialty contractor examinations require the appropriate qualification process before testing.
7. **Schedule and complete the FIN technical examination through PSI.**
8. **Complete any additional examination requirements associated with the selected contractor license class.**
9. **Prepare and submit the contractor license application and required documentation.**

Passing the Finish Carpentry Contracting technical examination is one component of Virginia contractor licensing. Successful completion of the FIN exam does not by itself issue a contractor license. The business must satisfy all requirements applicable to its license class and specialty.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Finish Carpentry Contracting** as the service that provides for the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork.

The FIN specialty specifically does not include installation of ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone. Those materials fall outside the finish carpentry specialty as defined by Virginia contractor regulations.

Virginia also recognizes the Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications and the Home Improvement and Commercial Improvement specialties as providing authority for finish carpentry work within their respective scopes.

Finish carpentry should also be distinguished from structural framing. The FIN specialty focuses on finished components and millwork rather than the construction of structural framing systems. Candidates preparing for the examination should therefore concentrate on finish installation, cabinets, trim, flooring, countertops, millwork, tools, materials, and related safety practices.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Fabrication is an important area of finish-carpentry preparation. Candidates should understand how millwork, cabinets, trim components, and other finished wood elements are produced and assembled. Study should include measuring, layout, cutting, fitting, joining, and maintaining accuracy throughout the fabrication process.

Installation should receive substantial attention because finish carpenters must properly fit completed components into existing building conditions. Candidates should review cabinet installation, casing, baseboards, countertops, wooden flooring, trim, and other millwork applications included within Virginia's FIN scope.

Materials knowledge is also important. Different wood products and manufactured materials behave differently during cutting, fastening, fitting, and finishing. Candidates should understand common material characteristics and how material selection can affect fabrication and installation.

Finishing questions may involve the preparation and completion of wood surfaces and components. Candidates should understand the relationship between surface preparation, workmanship, appearance, and the finished result. Accurate fitting is especially important because finish carpentry is often highly visible in completed construction.

Hand Tools should be treated as a dedicated study area. Finish carpentry depends on accurate use of measuring, marking, cutting, shaping, fastening, and fitting tools. Candidates should understand tool selection, safe use, and the tasks for which different tools are suited.

Machines are another examination subject. Woodworking machinery can support cutting, shaping, drilling, sanding, and other fabrication operations. Candidates should understand the general purpose and safe operation of equipment commonly associated with finish-carpentry work.

Safety should be incorporated throughout preparation rather than studied only as a final subject. Finish carpenters may use saws, routers, drills, nailers, sanders, ladders, portable power tools, and stationary woodworking equipment. The OSHA construction reference can help candidates review applicable worker-protection requirements.

Plans and Estimating require candidates to connect drawings, measurements, and project information with the work being performed. Finish carpentry frequently depends on precise dimensions because trim, cabinets, counters, flooring, and millwork must fit existing spaces accurately.

Candidates should practice interpreting dimensions and determining material quantities rather than relying only on memorized terminology. Measurement errors can affect both exam calculations and real-world finish work, so careful plan reading is a valuable preparation skill.

A useful study routine can combine trade review with reference navigation. Study one subject, such as cabinet construction or trim installation, and then work through related practice questions. When additional information is needed, identify the most appropriate reference and locate the supporting section.

When a practice question is missed, determine why. The issue may involve terminology, tool selection, installation procedure, material knowledge, safety, a measurement, or inefficient reference navigation. Reviewing the reason for the mistake can make study more targeted.

Timed practice can also help candidates prepare for the 60-minute examination. With an average of about two minutes per question, candidates should become comfortable answering familiar trade questions efficiently while preserving additional time for questions that require reference searches or calculations.

The pre-highlighted and permanently tabbed format supports this preparation by making important areas easier to identify. Candidates should become familiar with the references themselves so the tabs function as efficient navigation aids rather than substitutes for technical knowledge.

## How does the Finish Carpentry Contractor exam work?

The Finish Carpentry Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.

- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
