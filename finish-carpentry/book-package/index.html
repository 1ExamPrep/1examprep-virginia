Exam Details

The Virginia Millwork and Finish Carpentry examination is administered through PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors. The examination contains 30 questions, has a 90-minute time limit, and requires a minimum passing score of 70%, or 21 correct answers.

The official examination content outline is divided into eight subject areas:

  • Fabrication – 6 questions
  • Installation – 5 questions
  • Materials – 5 questions
  • Finishing – 4 questions
  • Hand Tools – 3 questions
  • Safety – 3 questions
  • Machines – 2 questions
  • Plans and Estimating – 2 questions

Fabrication is the largest individual category with 6 questions. Candidates should understand the methods used to cut, shape, assemble, and prepare millwork, cabinets, trim, and related finish carpentry components.

Installation and Materials each account for 5 questions. Installation questions can involve fitting, fastening, alignment, layout, and placement of finish carpentry components, while material questions can require candidates to recognize wood products, sheet goods, hardware, fasteners, adhesives, and other materials used in finish carpentry and cabinet work.

Finishing represents 4 questions. Candidates should be familiar with surface preparation, sanding, finishing materials, application techniques, and the steps used to produce a completed wood surface.

Hand Tools and Safety each represent 3 questions. Candidates should know the purpose and proper use of common finish carpentry tools while understanding the OSHA safety requirements that apply to construction work.

Machines and Plans and Estimating each account for 2 questions. Although smaller sections, both are important to a complete preparation plan because finish carpenters routinely use woodworking machinery and interpret dimensions, details, quantities, and specifications from construction documents.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Millwork and Finish Carpentry examination is an open book test. Candidates are permitted to use the approved references during the examination and must follow current PSI rules regarding allowable reference materials.

The 90-minute testing period provides an average of approximately three minutes per question. This gives candidates time to perform reference lookups when needed, but efficient navigation remains important. Spending several minutes searching through the wrong book can reduce the time available for more difficult questions.

The official reference list includes Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Finish Carpenter's Manual by Jim Tolpin, and The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction.

A fabrication, trim, installation, or general finish carpentry question may direct the candidate toward the Finish Carpenter's Manual. Questions involving cabinetmaking, joinery, furniture construction, casework, and related woodworking concepts may be supported by The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction. OSHA questions should direct attention to 29 CFR Part 1926.

PSI allows approved reference material to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed. Candidates should follow current testing rules when preparing books for the examination and should avoid unauthorized notes or loose materials.

Open-book preparation works best when candidates actively use their references during study. Learn the table of contents, index, terminology, diagrams, and major chapters so that a question can be matched to the appropriate reference quickly.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses consist of a license class and a classification or specialty that determines the type of work the business may perform. Finish Carpentry Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation FIN.

  1. Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Identify whether the business is pursuing a Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license based on the scope and monetary level of work it intends to perform.
  2. Select the Finish Carpentry specialty. Request FIN when the business intends to perform work within Virginia's defined Finish Carpentry Contracting scope.
  3. Identify a Qualified Individual. Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on the contractor license.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must have qualifying experience in the specialty. Virginia requires at least one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.
  5. Complete the applicable contractor application requirements. Submit the information and documentation required by the Virginia Board for Contractors.
  6. Meet examination requirements. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable technical examination requirement for the FIN specialty.
  7. Prepare for the Millwork and Finish Carpentry exam. Study Fabrication, Installation, Materials, Finishing, Hand Tools, Safety, Machines, and Plans and Estimating.
  8. Practice reference navigation. Become familiar with the Finish Carpenter's Manual, furniture and cabinet reference, and OSHA regulations before test day.
  9. Schedule testing through PSI. Follow the current registration and scheduling process for the Virginia specialty examination.
  10. Complete remaining licensing requirements. Satisfy the Board requirements associated with the contractor license class and specialty being requested.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Finish Carpentry Contracting as work involving the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork.

The FIN specialty does not include installation of ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone. Those materials fall outside the defined Finish Carpentry Contracting scope.

Virginia also recognizes that the Residential Building and Commercial Building classifications and the Home Improvement Contracting and Commercial Improvement Contracting specialties provide for finish carpentry functions within their respective scopes.

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses the business entity. The license class determines the monetary level of work the business may perform, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work authorized.

Every licensed contractor must identify a Qualified Individual for each specialty on the license. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience and prerequisite requirements associated with the requested designation.

Passing the Millwork and Finish Carpentry examination is an important technical qualification, but examination success alone does not automatically issue a Virginia contractor license. The business must complete all applicable Board licensing requirements.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should follow the official examination weighting. Begin with Fabrication, then devote substantial attention to Installation and Materials. Together, these three areas account for 16 of the 30 questions.

For Fabrication, study how finish carpentry components are measured, cut, shaped, joined, and assembled. Accuracy matters because small errors can affect fit, alignment, appearance, and installation quality.

Practice working with dimensions and layout. Finish carpentry frequently involves transferring measurements from an opening or plan to a piece of trim, cabinet component, countertop, casing, or other millwork.

Joinery should receive focused review. Cabinet and furniture construction can involve multiple methods for connecting wood components, and candidates should understand why different joints are used in different situations.

The Finish Carpenter's Manual is especially useful for practical installation and fabrication topics. Become familiar with sections covering interior trim, casings, moldings, cabinets, doors, and other finish carpentry work.

The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction is useful for developing a deeper understanding of cabinet assemblies, case construction, doors, drawers, joinery, and woodworking processes.

For Installation, review methods for fitting finish components to actual field conditions. Walls, floors, ceilings, doors, and openings may not always be perfectly square, level, or plumb, so finish carpentry often requires careful fitting.

Door and window casing should be studied as complete assemblies. Candidates should understand layout, joints, fastening, reveals, fitting, and the relationship between the trim and the surrounding opening.

Baseboards and other moldings require similar attention. Practice recognizing the correct installation sequence and how inside corners, outside corners, joints, and changes in wall conditions affect the work.

Cabinet installation can involve layout, leveling, fastening, alignment, fitting to walls, countertop preparation, and coordination between adjacent units. Candidates should understand how cabinets are installed as a complete system rather than as isolated boxes.

Countertop work may involve measurement, fitting, joining, support, openings, edge treatment, and installation. Focus on the general principles described in the included references.

For Materials, review common solid-wood products, plywood, engineered wood products, sheet goods, veneers, hardware, fasteners, adhesives, and other materials associated with finish carpentry and cabinet construction.

Wood movement is an important finish carpentry concept. Changes in moisture can affect the dimensions of wood products, which can influence joints, fitting, installation, and finishing.

Material selection also affects appearance and performance. Different wood species and manufactured products have different properties that influence machining, fastening, finishing, and suitability for specific applications.

For Finishing, study surface preparation as carefully as the final coating. A high-quality finish depends on proper sanding, cleaning, preparation, and application.

Review the basic types of stains, sealers, finishes, and coatings discussed in the trade references. Candidates should understand the purpose of each stage rather than simply memorizing product names.

Sanding should be approached as a progressive process. Understanding abrasive selection, grain direction, surface defects, and preparation helps explain why certain finishing procedures produce better results.

Hand Tools represent three questions. Review measuring tools, layout tools, cutting tools, chisels, planes, hammers, nail sets, hand saws, and other common finish carpentry equipment.

Candidates should know both the purpose of a tool and the proper way to use it. Questions may focus on selecting the appropriate tool for a specific operation rather than simply identifying its name.

Machines account for two questions. Review common woodworking machinery and power equipment used in millwork and cabinet construction. Understand the function of each machine and the operations it is designed to perform.

Machine safety should be studied alongside machine operation. Cutting and shaping equipment can create serious hazards, so guarding, workpiece control, appropriate setup, and safe operating procedures are important.

Plans and Estimating account for two questions. Practice reading dimensions, elevations, sections, schedules, cabinet layouts, and other construction information relevant to finish work.

Estimating may involve determining quantities of trim, cabinets, flooring, molding, sheet goods, or other materials. Candidates should use consistent units and verify dimensions before completing calculations.

For Safety, study 29 CFR Part 1926. Finish carpenters may work with ladders, scaffolds, portable power tools, woodworking equipment, adhesives, coatings, heavy cabinets, and other construction materials.

Personal protective equipment should be included in preparation. Eye protection, hearing protection, respiratory considerations, and other protective measures may apply depending on the work being performed.

Ladder and scaffold safety are relevant because finish carpentry can involve work above floor level when installing crown molding, upper cabinets, tall trim, or other components.

Housekeeping also matters in finish work. Offcuts, cords, tools, fasteners, sawdust, and packaging can create trip or other hazards if work areas are not maintained properly.

A useful open-book strategy is to associate each examination subject with the most likely reference. Practical finish carpentry and installation questions should often direct attention toward the Finish Carpenter's Manual. Cabinet and furniture construction questions can point toward the illustrated cabinet reference. Safety questions should direct attention toward OSHA.

Practice under timed conditions. With 90 minutes for 30 questions, candidates average approximately three minutes per question. Straightforward trade questions should be answered efficiently so more time remains for detailed reference searches.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. The problem may result from unfamiliar terminology, choosing the wrong woodworking method, misunderstanding a material, using the wrong tool, or failing to locate the applicable OSHA requirement.

A strong Virginia Finish Carpentry exam preparation routine combines practical trade knowledge, fabrication and installation skills, material familiarity, finishing techniques, tool knowledge, plan reading, estimating, OSHA safety, and repeated reference navigation.
