### Exam Details

The Virginia Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the **FIN** designation under the Virginia Board for Contractors. The examination is administered through PSI.

- **Exam Name:** Finish Carpentry Contracting Examination
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** FIN
- **Number of Questions:** 30
- **Time Allowed:** 60 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 21 correct answers
- **Exam Format:** Multiple choice
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

The published examination outline divides the 30 questions among eight subject areas:

- **Fabrication:** 6 questions
- **Installation:** 6 questions
- **Materials:** 5 questions
- **Finishing:** 3 questions
- **Hand Tools:** 3 questions
- **Safety:** 3 questions
- **Machines:** 2 questions
- **Plans and Estimating:** 2 questions

Fabrication and Installation are the two largest categories, with six questions each. Materials follows with five questions. Together, these three areas account for more than half of the examination, making practical finish-carpentry knowledge a major part of preparation.

Finishing, Hand Tools, and Safety each contribute three questions. Machines and Plans and Estimating each account for two questions. Candidates should prepare across all eight areas because even the smaller sections can affect the final passing score on a 30-question examination.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted references to the examination because the testing center does not provide the books.

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates should prepare their references in accordance with the applicable testing rules before arriving at the examination center.

The exam allows 60 minutes for 30 questions, providing an average of approximately two minutes per question. That makes efficient reference navigation especially important. Candidates who spend several minutes searching through the wrong book may create unnecessary pressure during the remainder of the examination.

Questions involving fabrication, trim techniques, installation, layout, or finish-carpentry procedures may point toward *Finish Carpenter's Manual*. Questions involving cabinet construction, furniture components, joinery, fabrication methods, and cabinet assemblies may be supported by *The Complete Illustrated Guide to Furniture and Cabinet Construction*. Construction-safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

A productive open-book strategy is to identify the subject before reaching for a reference. Determine whether the question involves fabrication, installation, materials, finishing, tools, machines, estimating, or safety. Then select the reference most likely to contain the supporting information.

The goal is not to look up every answer. Candidates should build enough familiarity with the trade to answer straightforward questions efficiently and reserve reference searches for technical details, unfamiliar terminology, calculations, or procedures that need confirmation.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the Finish Carpentry Contracting specialty.** Virginia identifies this specialty using the abbreviation FIN.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable requirements for the FIN specialty.
4. **Document qualifying experience.** Qualified Individuals for FIN must provide the applicable experience verification as part of the licensing process.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must complete the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
6. **Receive the required examination approval.** FIN is among the Virginia specialty designations requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.
7. **Schedule and complete the Finish Carpentry Contracting examination through PSI.**
8. **Complete any additional examination requirements associated with the selected contractor license class.**
9. **Prepare and submit the Virginia contractor license application and required supporting documentation.**

Passing the Finish Carpentry Contracting examination is one component of the Virginia contractor licensing process. Successful completion of the FIN examination does not by itself result in issuance of a contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Finish Carpentry Contracting** as work involving the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork.

The specialty does not include installation of ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone. Those materials are outside the specific FIN scope established by Virginia contractor regulations.

Virginia also recognizes the Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications and the Home Improvement and Commercial Improvement specialties as providing authority for finish-carpentry functions within their respective scopes.

The FIN specialty should be distinguished from structural framing. Finish carpentry focuses on the visible and detailed components installed after much of the structural building work has been completed. Typical subjects include cabinets, trim, casing, baseboards, flooring, countertops, millwork, fitting, finishing, tools, and related installation procedures.

The contractor business must satisfy the requirements associated with its selected license class, Qualified Individual, specialty, education, experience, examinations, and application before the Virginia contractor license is issued.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Fabrication represents six questions and should receive significant attention during preparation. Candidates should understand measurement, layout, cutting, shaping, fitting, joining, assembly, and the sequence used to create finished wood components. Accuracy matters because small errors can affect the fit and appearance of completed finish-carpentry work.

Installation also accounts for six questions. Study the procedures involved in fitting and installing cabinets, casing, baseboards, countertops, wooden flooring, trim, and other millwork. Candidates should understand how existing building conditions, walls, floors, openings, and dimensions affect finish installation.

Materials accounts for five questions. Candidates should be familiar with common wood products, manufactured materials, cabinet materials, millwork components, fasteners, adhesives, and other materials encountered in finish carpentry. Understanding how materials behave during cutting, fastening, joining, and finishing can help with practical questions.

Finishing contributes three questions. Study surface preparation and the processes used to complete finished woodwork. Candidates should understand how workmanship before finishing affects the final appearance and how surface condition, fitting, sanding, and preparation relate to the finished product.

Hand Tools also accounts for three questions. Finish carpentry involves measuring, marking, cutting, fitting, fastening, shaping, trimming, and adjusting components. Candidates should understand the general purpose and proper use of common hand tools used for these tasks.

Machines accounts for two questions. Finish carpentry and cabinet work can involve woodworking machinery used for cutting, shaping, drilling, sanding, and fabrication. Candidates should understand what common machines are designed to do and how they fit into the fabrication process.

Safety contributes three questions and should be integrated throughout preparation. Finish carpenters may work with saws, routers, drills, sanders, nailers, ladders, portable power tools, and woodworking machinery. Candidates should become familiar with the OSHA reference and learn where requirements involving tools, protective equipment, ladders, and related hazards can be found.

Plans and Estimating accounts for two questions. Although it is a smaller portion of the exam, measurement and quantity calculations are central to finish carpentry. Candidates should practice interpreting dimensions, calculating lengths and areas where appropriate, and understanding how plans or project information translate into material and installation requirements.

A strong study routine should combine trade review with reference navigation. Study one subject at a time, such as fabrication or cabinet installation, and then work through practice-oriented questions. When additional information is needed, locate the supporting material in the appropriate reference instead of simply memorizing an answer.

When a practice question is missed, determine what caused the mistake. Was the issue terminology, tool selection, material knowledge, installation procedure, measurement, or an inefficient reference search? Reviewing the reason for an error makes preparation more targeted and can prevent the same type of mistake from recurring.

Timed practice can be valuable because candidates have only 60 minutes for 30 questions. Work toward a steady pace while recognizing that some questions will be faster than others. Familiar trade questions may be answered quickly, allowing more time for calculations or reference lookups.

Reference-navigation practice should also include knowing when not to search. If a straightforward finish-carpentry principle is already well understood, spending several minutes looking for confirmation may not be the best use of examination time. Candidates should develop confidence in well-practiced knowledge while using the books strategically for questions that benefit from detailed support.
