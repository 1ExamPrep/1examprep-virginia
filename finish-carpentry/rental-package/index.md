### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the Finish Carpentry Contracting specialty.** Virginia identifies this specialty using the abbreviation FIN.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable requirements for the specialty.
4. **Document qualifying experience.** Qualified Individuals for FIN must provide the applicable experience verification.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
6. **Receive examination approval.** FIN is one of the Virginia specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of a technical examination.
7. **Schedule and complete the FIN technical examination through PSI.**
8. **Complete any additional examination requirements associated with the selected contractor license class.**
9. **Prepare and submit the Virginia contractor license application and supporting documentation.**

Passing the Finish Carpentry Contracting technical examination is one component of Virginia contractor licensing. Successful completion of the FIN examination does not by itself result in issuance of a contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Finish Carpentry Contracting** as work involving the installation, repair, and finishing of cabinets, sash casing, door casing, wooden flooring, baseboards, countertops, and other millwork.

The FIN specialty does not include installation of ceramic tile, marble, or artificial or cultured stone. Those materials fall outside the specific scope of Finish Carpentry Contracting.

Virginia also recognizes the Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications and the Home Improvement and Commercial Improvement specialties as providing authority for finish-carpentry work within their applicable scopes.

Qualified Individuals seeking the FIN designation must complete the applicable experience-verification and examination requirements. The contractor business must also satisfy the requirements associated with its selected license class, business structure, education, examinations, and application.

Finish carpentry should be distinguished from structural framing. The specialty focuses on finished components and millwork, including cabinets, casing, flooring, baseboards, countertops, and related detail work. Candidates should concentrate their technical preparation on the fabrication and installation of these finished elements rather than structural building framing.

### Test Information and Study Materials

Fabrication represents six questions and should receive significant attention during preparation. Candidates should understand measuring, layout, cutting, shaping, fitting, joinery, assembly, and the sequence used to produce finished wood components. Accuracy is especially important because small measurement or layout errors can affect the fit and appearance of completed work.

Installation also contains six questions. Study the methods used to fit and install cabinets, sash and door casing, baseboards, countertops, wooden flooring, trim, and other millwork. Candidates should understand how existing walls, floors, openings, and dimensions influence finish installation.

Materials accounts for five questions. Candidates should become familiar with common wood products, manufactured materials, cabinet components, fasteners, adhesives, and other materials used in finish carpentry. Understanding how different materials respond to cutting, fastening, joining, and finishing can help with practical exam questions.

Finishing contributes three questions. Study surface preparation and the processes used to complete woodwork and millwork. The quality of preparation can affect the final appearance, so candidates should understand the relationship between fitting, sanding, surface condition, and the finished result.

Hand Tools accounts for three questions. Finish carpentry depends on tools used for measuring, marking, cutting, fitting, shaping, trimming, fastening, and adjusting finished components. Candidates should understand which tools are appropriate for different tasks and how they are used safely.

Machines accounts for two questions. Cabinet and finish-carpentry work can involve equipment used for cutting, drilling, shaping, sanding, and fabrication. Candidates should understand the general purpose of common woodworking machines and how they fit into the fabrication process.

Safety contributes three questions. Finish carpentry can involve portable power tools, woodworking machinery, ladders, nailers, saws, drills, routers, sanders, and material handling. Use 29 CFR Part 1926 during preparation to become familiar with applicable construction-safety requirements.

Plans and Estimating accounts for two questions. Candidates should practice interpreting dimensions and project information and determining quantities or measurements relevant to cabinets, flooring, countertops, trim, and other finish-carpentry work.
