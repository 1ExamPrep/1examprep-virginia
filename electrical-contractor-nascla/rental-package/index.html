Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. NASCLA offers standardized open-book electrical examinations that participating licensing agencies can recognize as part of their licensing processes.

Virginia is listed as a participating agency in the NASCLA electrical program through the Virginia Board for Contractors. The Electrical Contractor examination is intended for Master Electricians and Unlimited Electricians and covers advanced electrical knowledge associated with professional electrical work.

Preparation for the examination should include electrical theory, National Electrical Code application, jobsite and workplace safety, grounding and bonding, circuit protection, wiring methods, electrical equipment, special systems, project design and management, and energy-related subjects. Candidates should also be comfortable completing electrical calculations and working through questions that require multiple steps.

Technical knowledge and reference familiarity should be developed together. A wiring-method question may require a National Electrical Code lookup, while a construction-safety question may lead to OSHA Part 1926. A workplace electrical-safety question may require NFPA 70E, and a theory problem may require calculation resources or an electrical textbook.

Virginia contractor licensing requirements remain separate from the NASCLA trade examination. Virginia licenses contractor businesses as Class A, Class B, or Class C contractors, and each contractor license also includes classifications or specialties that determine the type of work the contractor may perform.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the Virginia contractor license you need. Virginia contractor firms are licensed as Class A, Class B, or Class C and must carry the appropriate specialty or classification for the work performed.
  2. Review the Electrical Contractors specialty requirements. Virginia requires the qualified individual for the Electrical Contractors specialty to hold a Master Electrician tradesman license issued by the Board for Contractors.
  3. Meet the qualified-individual experience requirement. The qualified individual must have the amount of experience required for the contractor license class being requested.
  4. Complete Virginia pre-license education. New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education.
  5. Complete the applicable examinations. Applicants must satisfy the examinations required for the contractor class, designated employee, individual tradesman credential, or other applicable licensing component.
  6. Use NASCLA examination results when applicable. Virginia participates in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and recognizes the Electrical Contractor examination through that program.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses business entities and sole proprietorships engaged in regulated contracting work. Contractor licenses consist of a license class and one or more classifications or specialties. The license class determines the monetary level of work the business is authorized to undertake, while the specialty determines the type of contracting work permitted.

Virginia requires a qualified individual for every classification and specialty listed on a contractor license. For the Electrical Contractors specialty, Virginia regulations require the qualified individual to hold a valid Master Electrician tradesman license issued by the Board for Contractors.

The qualified individual's required experience depends on the contractor class. Virginia requires at least one year of experience for a Class C qualified individual, at least three years for Class B, and at least five years for Class A.

New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the course.

Virginia's pre-license education is a business-focused requirement covering regulations, statutes, and general contractor responsibilities. It is separate from the technical electrical preparation associated with the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants also use a designated employee who satisfies the applicable contractor-business examination requirements. Virginia's contractor examinations evaluate general business knowledge and knowledge of Virginia laws and regulations governing contractors.

The Master Electrician credential is an individual tradesman license, while the contractor license applies to the business. Candidates pursuing electrical contracting in Virginia should therefore distinguish between the individual license held by the qualified individual and the Class A, B, or C contractor license held by the contracting entity.

Passing an accepted trade examination does not automatically create a Virginia contractor license. Applicants must complete the licensing process, meet the applicable experience and qualification requirements, satisfy the contractor-business requirements, and receive approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors.

How does the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam work?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
