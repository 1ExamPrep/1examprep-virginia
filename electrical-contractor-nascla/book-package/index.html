Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is intended for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians. The examination evaluates the technical and professional knowledge associated with performing and managing electrical work in accordance with applicable codes, standards, and industry practices.

  • Examination: NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors
  • Classification: Electrical Contractors / Master Electricians / Unlimited Electricians
  • Scored Questions: 100
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70
  • Time Allowed: 270 minutes
  • Exam Type: Open book

The examination content includes several major areas of electrical knowledge. Candidates should be prepared for questions involving electrical theory, personal and jobsite safety, project design and management, grounding and bonding, special systems and equipment, tools and test equipment, circuit protection, wiring methods, equipment for general use, and energy-efficiency concepts.

These subjects require candidates to work with multiple references. Some questions can be solved through electrical knowledge or calculation, while others require a precise Code lookup or information contained in a safety, project-management, or energy standard. Preparation should therefore involve both technical study and repeated use of the books.

The 270-minute testing period requires consistent pacing. Candidates should practice identifying which questions can be answered from knowledge and which require a reference search. Developing that judgment can reduce unnecessary lookup time and help preserve additional time for calculations or more difficult Code questions.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates may use approved reference materials during testing in accordance with current NASCLA and testing-center requirements.

An open-book format does not eliminate the need for focused preparation. The reference collection contains a large amount of technical information, and the examination operates under a fixed time limit. Candidates should know how their books are organized before arriving at the testing center.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors, operating through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, regulates contractor businesses and individual tradesmen engaged in electrical work. Virginia separates the individual master electrician credential from the contractor firm's business license.

Virginia is a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination. Passing the NASCLA exam can satisfy an accepted electrical examination component, but candidates must still complete Virginia's licensing process and meet the state's applicable qualifications.

For individual master electrician licensure, Virginia regulations establish experience pathways that include holding a valid journeyman license in the applicable trade for at least one year or documenting nine years of practical experience in the trade. Applicants must provide the documentation required by the Board for the qualification pathway they use.

Contractor licensing is separate. Virginia contractor licenses consist of a license class and a classification or specialty. The license class determines the monetary level of projects that a contractor may perform, while the classification or specialty determines the type of construction work the contractor may undertake.

Virginia contractor firms may hold Class A, Class B, or Class C licenses. Applicants for all three classes must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. For Class A and Class B applicants, the required pre-license education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the course.

Every Virginia contractor must designate a qualified individual for each classification or specialty held by the business. For the Electrical Contractors specialty, the qualified individual must hold a valid Virginia Master Electrician tradesman license issued through the Board for Contractors.

Virginia also establishes experience requirements for qualified individuals associated with contractor firms. The required experience depends on the contractor license class: one year for a Class C license, three years for a Class B license, and five years for a Class A license.

Passing the NASCLA examination alone does not issue a Virginia master electrician or contractor license. Candidates must complete the appropriate Virginia application process and satisfy all requirements associated with the individual credential and contractor business license they intend to obtain.

Test Information and Study Materials

A reference-book package becomes most useful when candidates work with the materials throughout their preparation. Waiting until exam day to become familiar with the books can make an open-book examination significantly more difficult.

Begin with the National Electrical Code. Review commonly tested electrical subjects such as branch circuits, feeders, services, conductor sizing, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, raceways, cables, boxes, motors, transformers, and general equipment requirements. Practice finding the actual controlling provisions rather than memorizing article numbers without context.

Electrical theory should be studied through active problem solving. Use the theory and calculation references to review voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and other principles. Work through calculations by identifying known values, choosing the correct formula, maintaining consistent units, and determining whether an NEC provision affects the final answer.

Safety should be studied through both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. Candidates should understand that these references address different aspects of construction and electrical workplace safety. Learning the organization of each book helps make safety lookups more efficient.

Project-management study should address planning, estimating, communication, coordination, risk, scheduling, and other responsibilities associated with professional electrical contracting. The NASCLA Contractors Guide and project-management reference help expand preparation beyond technical installation topics.

The ASHRAE/IES energy standard adds an energy-management component to the reference set. Candidates should become comfortable with its layout and terminology so that questions involving efficiency requirements can be researched more efficiently.

Use timed reference practice as the examination approaches. Choose questions from different subject areas, identify the appropriate book, locate the answer, and track the time required. If a particular subject repeatedly requires long searches, devote additional study time to learning that portion of the reference.

FAQ Section

Does Virginia accept the NASCLA Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination?

Yes. The Virginia Board for Contractors is a participating agency in the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program and accepts the Electrical Contractor/Master Electrician examination.

Is the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination open book?

Yes. The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is an open-book examination. Candidates should prepare by becoming familiar with the approved references before testing.

How many questions are on the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination contains 100 scored questions.

What score is required to pass?

The minimum passing score for the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is 70.

How much time is allowed for the examination?

Candidates are allowed 270 minutes, or four hours and thirty minutes, to complete the NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination.

Is a Virginia Master Electrician license the same as a contractor license?

No. A Virginia Master Electrician license is an individual tradesman credential. A Virginia contractor license applies to the contracting business and includes a license class and classification or specialty.

What does a Virginia electrical contractor need for a qualified individual?

For the Virginia Electrical Contractors specialty, the contractor's qualified individual must hold a valid Virginia Master Electrician tradesman license issued through the Board for Contractors.

Does Virginia require contractor pre-license education?

Yes. Applicants for Virginia Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education according to the requirements associated with the applicable license class.

Does passing the NASCLA exam automatically issue a Virginia license?

No. Passing the NASCLA examination can satisfy an accepted examination requirement, but candidates must still meet Virginia's experience, application, qualified-individual, and contractor licensing requirements.

Why are the reference books important for an open-book electrical exam?

The examination requires candidates to work across a broad range of electrical subjects. Practicing with the books helps candidates learn where Code provisions, formulas, safety requirements, project-management concepts, and other technical information are located.

How should I use this book package to prepare?

Study each major examination subject while actively using the appropriate references. Practice calculations, Code lookups, safety searches, and timed navigation so the books become familiar tools instead of unfamiliar resources on exam day.

How does the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam work?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
