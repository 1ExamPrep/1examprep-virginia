Exam Details

The NASCLA Accredited Trade Examination for Electrical Contractors is part of the NASCLA Accredited Electrical Examination Program. The program offers an open-book examination for Electrical Contractors, Master Electricians, and Unlimited Electricians that is recognized by participating licensing agencies.

The Virginia Board for Contractors is a participating agency in the NASCLA electrical program. A passing NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination result can be used in connection with participating-state licensing requirements, but the NASCLA examination itself is not a Virginia license.

Virginia contractor licensing is structured around both a contractor license class and a classification or specialty. The contractor license class determines the monetary level of work the business may perform, while the classification or specialty determines the type of contracting work authorized. Electrical contracting is identified under the Electrical specialty.

The Qualified Individual for the Virginia Electrical specialty must hold a valid Virginia Master Electrician tradesman license. This means candidates preparing for the NASCLA electrical examination should distinguish between the individual Master Electrician credential and the contractor business license issued to the company or sole proprietorship.

Preparation for the NASCLA electrical examination should include electrical theory, National Electrical Code application, calculations, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, overcurrent protection, electrical equipment, safety, project design and management, energy requirements, and supporting technical concepts.

The full reference set is important because not every question is answered through the NEC. Safety questions may involve OSHA Part 1926 or NFPA 70E. Electrical theory and calculation questions may require supporting technical references. Project-management and business topics may lead to the NASCLA contractor guide, while Virginia-specific contractor preparation can involve the Virginia business-law and building-code references.

Open Book Test

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination is an open-book test. Approved reference materials may be used during testing according to the applicable NASCLA and examination-provider rules.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity a major part of preparation. Electrical books contain extensive information, so candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question before opening a reference. Knowing whether the question involves Code requirements, electrical theory, safety, energy standards, or project management can significantly narrow the search.

The 2023 National Electrical Code should receive substantial navigation practice. Candidates should become comfortable using the NEC index, definitions, articles, tables, exceptions, and cross-references. Preparation should include branch circuits, feeders, services, conductors, ampacity, overcurrent protection, grounding and bonding, wiring methods, motors, transformers, equipment, special occupancies, and special systems.

Electrical theory should be reinforced through calculations and practical problems. Candidates should practice voltage, current, resistance, power, circuit relationships, motors, transformers, and related electrical concepts. A consistent calculation process can help candidates identify the values provided, determine the quantity being requested, select the proper formula, complete the calculation, and then check whether an NEC rule affects the result.

Safety preparation should involve both OSHA Part 1926 and NFPA 70E. OSHA Part 1926 focuses on construction-safety requirements, while NFPA 70E addresses electrical safety in the workplace. Candidates should become familiar with the organization and purpose of both references rather than treating safety as a single subject.

Licensing Steps

  1. Determine the Virginia contractor license class you need. Virginia contractor businesses are licensed as Class A, Class B, or Class C based on the scope and monetary level of work authorized.
  2. Select the Electrical specialty. The contractor business must hold the appropriate specialty for the work it intends to perform.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. Every licensed contractor must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on the license. For the Electrical specialty, the Qualified Individual must hold a valid Virginia Master Electrician tradesman license.
  4. Meet the experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirement associated with the contractor license class.
  5. Complete Virginia pre-license education. New Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education through the required responsible-management or designated-employee pathway.
  6. Complete the applicable contractor-business examinations. Class A and Class B applicants have designated-employee examination requirements covering Virginia contractor laws and general business knowledge, with an additional advanced component for Class A.

  12. Receive Board approval. Passing examinations does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The Board for Contractors must approve the completed application.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses business entities and sole proprietorships engaged in regulated contracting work. Virginia contractor licenses consist of two primary components: the license class and the classification or specialty. The class is Class A, Class B, or Class C, while the specialty defines the type of construction work authorized.

Electrical contracting is regulated under the Electrical specialty. Every contractor business applying for an Electrical specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who satisfies the Board's requirements for that specialty.

The Qualified Individual for the Electrical specialty must hold a current Virginia Master Electrician tradesman license. This individual credential is separate from the contractor business license and is issued through the Virginia Board for Contractors' Tradesmen Program.

Virginia also establishes experience requirements for Qualified Individuals based on the contractor license class. Current Board materials require at least one year of appropriate experience for a Class C Qualified Individual, at least three years for Class B, and at least five years for Class A.

New contractor applicants must complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the course.

The Virginia contractor pre-license course is a business-focused course covering statutes, regulations, and responsibilities relevant to operating a contracting business. It is separate from the technical electrical examination and separate from the Class A and Class B designated-employee examinations.

Class A and Class B businesses must declare a Designated Employee who satisfies the applicable examination requirement. The Class B contractor-business examination contains a Virginia portion and a general portion. Class A includes those components plus an advanced portion addressing higher-level administrative and business knowledge.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants must also satisfy Virginia's applicable financial-verification requirement. The Board provides financial-statement and surety-bond options for applicants subject to this requirement.

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor examination may satisfy an accepted trade-examination component through Virginia's participation in the NASCLA electrical program, but it does not replace the Virginia Master Electrician credential required for the Electrical Qualified Individual or the contractor-business requirements associated with Class A, B, or C licensure.

Candidates should therefore view the licensing pathway as a combination of individual electrical qualifications and business licensing requirements. The technical electrical examination addresses trade knowledge, while Virginia separately regulates the business entity, Qualified Individual, Designated Employee, pre-license education, experience, and financial requirements.

How does the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam work?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

  • Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
  • The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
  • Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
  • Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
