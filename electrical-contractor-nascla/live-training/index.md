### State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors, operating through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation, regulates contractor businesses and individual tradesmen in the electrical trade. Virginia distinguishes between a contractor-business license and an individual Master Electrician tradesman credential.

Virginia contractor firms are licensed as Class A, Class B, or Class C contractors. These licenses are issued to business entities or sole proprietorships rather than serving as individual electrical tradesman licenses. The contractor license also includes classifications or specialties that define the work the business is authorized to perform.

Every contractor business must identify a qualified individual for each classification or specialty on the license. Virginia's current Board for Contractors regulations identify a Master Electrician tradesman license issued by the Board for Contractors as the prerequisite for the Electrical Contractors specialty.

The qualified individual must also have the amount of experience applicable to the contractor class being requested. Virginia's current contractor application lists a minimum of one year of qualifying experience for a Class C license, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applications, the course must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management must complete the course.

The contractor pre-license education requirement is a business-focused course covering Virginia regulations, statutes, and contractor responsibilities. It is separate from electrical trade preparation and separate from the technical examination material covered in this NASCLA-focused training.

For Class A and Class B contractor licenses, Virginia also has designated-employee examination requirements relating to contractor business knowledge and Virginia laws and regulations. These requirements are distinct from the individual Master Electrician credential and from an electrical trade examination.

Virginia regulates individual Master Electricians through its Tradesmen Program. The Board for Contractors requires continuing education for Journeyman and Master Electricians as part of the credential-renewal process. Electrical tradesmen currently complete three hours of continuing education covering the applicable electrical Code.

Passing an electrical trade examination is only one component of the licensing process. Applicants must complete the applicable Virginia application, meet experience and qualification requirements, satisfy contractor-business requirements when relevant, and receive approval before performing work under the requested license.

## How does the NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam work?

The NASCLA Electrical Contractor exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
