### Exam Details

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination is a Qualified Individual specialty examination administered through PSI for the Virginia Board for Contractors. Candidates must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the examination.

The REF examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates receive **120 minutes** to complete the examination and must achieve a minimum score of **70%, or 35 correct answers**.

The current examination content outline is divided into the following subject areas:

- **General Knowledge and Requirements:** 16 questions
- **Refrigeration Piping:** 10 questions
- **Refrigerants:** 5 questions
- **Refrigeration Systems and Controls:** 12 questions
- **Testing and Inspection:** 7 questions

General Knowledge and Requirements represents the largest single area of the examination. Refrigeration Systems and Controls is another major category, followed by Refrigeration Piping. These three subjects account for a substantial portion of the test and should receive significant attention in a study plan.

Refrigerants and Testing and Inspection contain fewer questions but remain important. A candidate preparing only for the largest categories could still leave a meaningful number of points unaddressed. A complete exam-preparation strategy should cover all five subject areas.

Virginia uses PSI as the examination vendor for contractor Qualified Individual specialty examinations. The current Virginia Board for Contractors information lists the Qualified Individual specialty exam fee as **$85**, paid directly to PSI. The exam fee is separate from the online exam prep course.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because the testing center does not supply the books.

For ICC code-based examination questions, Virginia currently uses the **2021 edition** of the applicable International Codes. The International Mechanical Code, 2021 Edition is therefore an important part of preparation for the Refrigeration Contracting examination.

Open-book testing makes reference familiarity an important exam skill. Candidates should know how each book is organized, where major chapters are located, how to use the index, and how to distinguish between technical refrigeration information and mechanical code requirements.

Permanent tabs are permitted under the current examination rules. Removable tabs are not allowed. Candidates are also prohibited from writing in the references during the examination. Reference books should therefore be prepared before test day in accordance with the examination rules.

With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. ### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor licenses combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialties.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia licenses contractors as Class A, Class B, or Class C, with different requirements applying to each class.
2. **Select Refrigeration Contracting.** The Virginia specialty abbreviation is REF.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** Every contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must document the required experience for the contractor class being pursued.
5. **Receive eligibility approval for the specialty examination.** Candidates must complete the applicable approval process before scheduling the REF exam with PSI.
6. **Prepare for and pass the REF examination.** The online exam prep course is designed to support study for this technical specialty exam.
7. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor license applicants must complete the applicable eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
8. **Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable.** Class A and Class B applicants have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must provide the information required for its class, Qualified Individual, specialty, Responsible Management, and other applicable requirements.

The REF exam is only one part of the Virginia contractor licensing process. Candidates should prepare for the specialty examination while also making sure the contractor business satisfies the separate requirements associated with its intended license class.

### State Requirements

Virginia requires every contractor business to have a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty listed on the license. The Qualified Individual must meet the experience and examination requirements associated with the requested specialty.

The amount of required experience depends on the contractor license class. A Qualified Individual for a Class C contractor must have at least **one year of experience** in the applicable specialty. Class B requires at least **three years of experience**, while Class A requires at least **five years of experience**.

New contractor applicants must also complete **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education**. The pre-license course is a general contractor business requirement and is separate from technical preparation for the REF specialty examination.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants also have Designated Employee examination requirements. These business examination components are separate from the Qualified Individual Refrigeration Contracting technical exam.

The Virginia REF specialty is limited to refrigeration work. Its defined scope includes installation, repair, or removal of refrigeration equipment, excluding HVAC equipment. Electrical, plumbing, gas-fitting, and HVAC functions are not included under this specialty.

Understanding the scope is important for both examination preparation and business licensing. Candidates should make sure the REF specialty matches the type of work the contractor intends to perform before building a licensing strategy around this designation.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A productive study plan should follow the official exam content outline. Begin with **General Knowledge and Requirements**, which contains 16 questions. Review refrigeration fundamentals, system operation, major components, terminology, installation concepts, and general refrigeration practices.

Next, devote significant time to **Refrigeration Systems and Controls**. With 12 questions, this area requires candidates to understand how refrigeration systems operate as complete systems. Study compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, controls, pressure and temperature relationships, and the interaction among system components.

**Refrigeration Piping** contains 10 questions. Review piping arrangements, materials, fittings, installation principles, sizing concepts, and relevant code requirements. Use both the technical textbook and mechanical code where appropriate so that you understand both practical principles and enforceable requirements.

**Testing and Inspection** contains seven questions. Candidates should review system testing procedures, inspection concepts, operating checks, performance verification, and troubleshooting principles. Practical field experience can be especially useful here, but written exam questions may require precise terminology or reference-based information.

**Refrigerants** represents five questions. Review refrigerant characteristics, system applications, operating relationships, safe handling concepts, and the role refrigerants play within the refrigeration cycle.

Practice questions should be treated as learning exercises rather than simply a score. When a question is missed, determine why it was missed and locate the supporting information in the appropriate reference. This strengthens both subject knowledge and book-navigation skills.

Timed study sessions can also improve preparation. Work through a group of practice questions while limiting the amount of time available for book lookups. Practice recognizing when you know an answer, when a reference search is necessary, and when it may be better to move forward and return to a question later.

Learning the indexes and chapter structures in both books is especially important. An open-book examination rewards candidates who can move directly toward the likely location of an answer rather than scanning large sections of a book without a plan.

The online course helps organize these activities into a repeatable study process. Candidates can review a technical subject, work through related practice material, identify weaknesses, and then use the reference books to reinforce the underlying concepts.
