Open Book Test

The Virginia contractor specialty examination uses open-book testing with permitted references. Candidates are responsible for bringing the references they intend to use because books are not supplied at the examination center.

The open-book format makes reference familiarity an important part of preparation. Candidates should understand how Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning is organized and become comfortable with the chapter structure, index, diagrams, tables, and technical terminology. The International Mechanical Code should also become a familiar working reference rather than a book opened for the first time on examination day.

Virginia's PSI examination rules allow reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Temporary or removable tabs are not permitted, and references may not contain handwritten notes. Candidates also may not write in the references during the examination session.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialties. The business must satisfy the requirements associated with both the selected class and the requested specialty.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses, with different qualification requirements applying to each class.
  2. Select Refrigeration Contracting. The Virginia specialty abbreviation is REF.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. Each contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty requested.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must document the required experience for the contractor class being pursued.
  5. Complete the Qualified Individual examination process. REF is one of the specialties requiring pre-approval and successful completion of an applicable Board-approved technical examination.
  6. Complete contractor pre-license education. New contractor firms must satisfy Virginia's required pre-license education before licensing.
  7. Complete applicable business examinations. Class A and Class B contractor businesses have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
  8. Complete the contractor application. The business must provide the required information regarding Responsible Management, the Qualified Individual, license class, requested specialty, and other applicable qualifications.
  9. Submit the completed licensing materials. The Virginia Board for Contractors reviews the application and supporting documentation before licensure is issued.

State Requirements

Virginia requires every licensed contractor business to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on its license. The Qualified Individual for the REF specialty must satisfy the applicable experience and technical examination requirements.

The Qualified Individual must be either a full-time employee of the business, working at least the required number of hours, or a member of Responsible Management. Virginia's contractor application currently states that the Qualified Individual must have at least one year of applicable experience for Class C, three years for Class B, or five years for Class A.

REF is listed among the contractor specialties requiring the Qualified Individual to receive pre-approval and pass an examination. An Experience Verification Form is also required for the applicable specialty qualification process.

Virginia requires new contractor applicants to complete eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This contractor business education requirement is separate from preparation for the REF technical specialty examination.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants have additional requirements involving a Designated Employee and applicable business examination portions. These business requirements are separate from the REF technical examination completed by the Qualified Individual.

Contractor applicants should therefore view the licensing process as a combination of trade qualification and business qualification. Technical refrigeration preparation addresses the specialty, while business education, applicable business examinations, company information, and the contractor application address the broader licensing requirements.
