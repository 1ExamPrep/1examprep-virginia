Virginia requires an appropriately qualified individual for every classification or specialty appearing on a contractor license, and Refrigeration Contracting is among the specialties requiring the applicable technical examination.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors. A contractor license consists of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialties. Candidates pursuing Refrigeration Contracting should prepare for both the technical qualification requirements and the broader business licensing process.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses, with different qualification requirements applying to each class.
  2. Select Refrigeration Contracting. The applicable specialty abbreviation is REF.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must have a Qualified Individual for every requested classification or specialty.
  4. Meet applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must document the required experience for the contractor class being pursued.
  5. Obtain eligibility for the REF examination. The applicable approval process must be completed before scheduling the Qualified Individual specialty examination.
  6. Prepare for the technical examination. Use the refrigeration and mechanical references to strengthen trade knowledge and open-book navigation skills.
  7. Complete required contractor pre-license education. New contractor businesses must satisfy Virginia's applicable eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
  8. Complete contractor business examinations when applicable. Class A and Class B businesses have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. The business must satisfy the application requirements associated with its license class, specialty, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, and other applicable qualifications.

The REF technical examination and the contractor business examination requirements are separate parts of the licensing process. This rental package includes references that can support preparation across both areas.

State Requirements

Virginia requires every contractor business to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on the license. The Qualified Individual must have the appropriate experience in the classification or specialty and must maintain the required relationship with the contractor business.

The Qualified Individual must be either a full-time employee or a member of Responsible Management for the business seeking licensure. Experience requirements vary according to whether the business is applying for a Class A, Class B, or Class C contractor license.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new contractor license applicants. This course focuses on general contractor business and regulatory requirements and is separate from technical preparation for the Refrigeration Contracting examination.

Class A and Class B applicants have additional requirements involving a Designated Employee and applicable contractor business examinations. The NASCLA Contractor’s Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Virginia 11th Edition and Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code, 2021 Edition can support preparation for applicable business and state regulatory subjects.

The Refrigeration Contracting specialty itself is focused specifically on refrigeration equipment. Virginia defines REF as installation, repair, or removal of refrigeration equipment other than HVAC equipment. No electrical, plumbing, gas-fitting, or HVAC functions are included within the specialty.

Understanding this scope is important because a business intending to perform additional types of work may require different or additional classifications, specialties, or appropriately qualified individuals.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive refrigeration study plan should begin with the fundamentals of the refrigeration cycle. Candidates should understand how major components work together, including the compressor, condenser, metering device, and evaporator. Understanding pressure, temperature, heat transfer, refrigerant condition, and system operation can create a stronger foundation for more detailed technical topics.

Refrigeration piping should receive focused study as well. Review piping arrangements, materials, connections, supports, installation principles, insulation, and troubleshooting concepts. When a piping subject involves a precise code requirement, practice finding the applicable information in the International Mechanical Code.

System controls are another important area. Candidates should understand how controls interact with refrigeration equipment and how system operation changes in response to temperature, pressure, demand, and safety conditions. Technical study should focus on understanding how components relate to each other instead of memorizing isolated terms.

Refrigerant study should include the role of refrigerants within the cycle, refrigerant characteristics, system applications, and safe operating concepts. Candidates should also be prepared to work with technical terminology used in professional refrigeration service and installation.

Testing, inspection, troubleshooting, and system operation should be incorporated into preparation. Experienced refrigeration technicians may already perform many of these tasks in daily work, but written examinations can present familiar concepts using technical language or reference-based questions. Reviewing the textbook can help connect field experience with exam terminology.

The contractor business portion of preparation should be approached separately. Use the NASCLA Virginia guide to study applicable business, law, project management, and contracting concepts. Use the Virginia Uniform Statewide Building Code for applicable state regulatory and code topics.

When using highlighted and tabbed books, review the tabs before working through practice questions. Learn which subjects each tab identifies, and make sure you understand the surrounding material rather than relying only on highlighted lines.
