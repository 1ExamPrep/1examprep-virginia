### Exam Details

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination is a Qualified Individual specialty examination administered through PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Candidates must receive the appropriate approval before scheduling the specialty examination.

The Virginia Refrigeration Contractor exam contains **50 questions**. Candidates are allowed **120 minutes** to complete the examination and must earn a minimum score of **70%, or 35 correct answers**.

The examination content is divided into the following areas:

- **General Knowledge and Requirements:** 16 questions
- **Refrigeration Piping:** 10 questions
- **Refrigerants:** 5 questions
- **Refrigeration Systems and Controls:** 12 questions
- **Testing and Inspection:** 7 questions

General Knowledge and Requirements is the largest individual subject area, followed by Refrigeration Systems and Controls. Refrigeration Piping also represents a significant portion of the examination. Candidates should develop a study schedule that reflects this distribution while still reviewing refrigerants and testing and inspection.

The exam can include questions derived from listed references as well as trade knowledge and general industry practices. Practical refrigeration experience is therefore valuable, but candidates should also become familiar with the terminology, calculations, system components, code provisions, and technical concepts encountered in written contractor examinations.

The Virginia Board for Contractors currently identifies PSI as the exam vendor for contractor Qualified Individual specialty examinations. Successful completion of the applicable technical examination is required before applying for contractor licensure when the specialty requires a Qualified Individual technical examination.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the test center.

The current Virginia examination information specifies the **2021 edition of the International Mechanical Code** for ICC code-based questions. Candidates preparing with the International Mechanical Code, 2021 Edition should become familiar with its chapter organization, tables, definitions, equipment requirements, refrigeration provisions, and other sections relevant to the examination.

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed, while removable tabs such as temporary flags are not permitted. Reference materials containing writing are not allowed into the examination, and candidates may not write in their references during the testing session.

Loose papers and unbound reference materials are not permitted. Candidates using eligible downloaded materials must follow the examination provider's requirements for binding those materials before bringing them into the testing room.

A useful preparation strategy is to practice identifying the category of a question before opening a book. Repeating that decision-making process during study can make open-book navigation more efficient.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor licenses combine a **license class** with one or more **classifications or specialties**. The license class establishes the applicable level of contracting authority, while the specialty defines the type of work the business may perform.

1. **Determine the contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses, with different requirements applying to each class.
2. **Select Refrigeration Contracting.** Applicants seeking this specialty use the REF designation.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** Every contractor business must have a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on the license.
4. **Meet the experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must document the required amount of experience for the contractor class being requested.
5. **Obtain approval for the REF technical examination.** Refrigeration is one of the specialties requiring the Qualified Individual to successfully complete a Board-approved technical examination.
6. **Prepare for and complete the Refrigeration Contracting examination.** Candidates can use the technical and code references in this package while developing the knowledge and navigation skills needed for the exam.
7. **Complete Virginia pre-license education.** New contractor license applicants must satisfy the Board's eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
8. **Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable.** Class A and Class B contractor applicants have Designated Employee examination requirements separate from the REF specialty examination.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must provide the required information for its class, Responsible Management, Qualified Individual, specialty, and other applicable qualifications.

Passing the REF examination is one part of the overall Virginia contractor licensing process. Candidates should prepare for the specialty examination while also planning for the business, pre-license education, application, and contractor-class requirements that apply to their licensing path.

### State Requirements

Virginia requires every contractor business to identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested on the license. The Qualified Individual must be at least 18 years old and must be a full-time employee of the firm or a member of its Responsible Management.

The minimum experience required for a Qualified Individual depends on the contractor license class. A Qualified Individual for a **Class C** contractor must have at least **one year of experience** in the applicable classification or specialty. A **Class B** Qualified Individual needs at least **three years of experience**, and a **Class A** Qualified Individual needs at least **five years of experience**.

For specialties such as Refrigeration that do not use another individual master tradesman license or certification as the qualifying credential, the Qualified Individual must successfully complete the applicable Board-approved technical examination. Candidates must receive the required exam approval before scheduling the test.

Virginia also requires **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education** for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. The pre-license course is a general contractor business course and is separate from preparation for the Refrigeration Contracting technical examination.

The REF scope is specifically focused on refrigeration equipment. It includes installation, repair, and removal of refrigeration equipment, excluding HVAC equipment, and does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, gas-fitting, or HVAC functions. Examples identified within the Virginia scope include coolers, refrigerated casework, ice-making machines, drinking fountains, cold-room equipment, and similar hermetic refrigeration equipment.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A strong study plan should follow the examination content outline rather than treating every refrigeration topic equally. Start with General Knowledge and Requirements because it contains 16 questions. Review refrigeration fundamentals, equipment operation, commonly used components, system relationships, installation principles, and technical terminology.

Next, devote substantial attention to **Refrigeration Systems and Controls**, which accounts for 12 questions. Candidates should understand how refrigeration systems operate as complete systems rather than memorizing isolated components. Review the relationships among compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, controls, and other system components.

**Refrigeration Piping** accounts for 10 questions. Study piping principles, system layout, installation considerations, sizing concepts, materials, and the code and technical requirements associated with refrigeration piping. Use the International Mechanical Code when studying code-driven piping requirements and the refrigeration textbook for broader technical understanding.

**Testing and Inspection** represents seven questions. Candidates should review procedures and principles associated with system testing, inspection, operation, diagnosis, and safe verification of refrigeration systems. Practical experience can provide a useful foundation, but written questions may require precise terminology or reference-based information.

**Refrigerants** accounts for five questions. Review refrigerant characteristics, system applications, safe handling principles, operating considerations, and related technical knowledge. Although this is the smallest exam category, five questions can still make a meaningful difference in the final score.

Reference-navigation practice should be part of every study session. Select a technical question, identify its subject area, choose the most appropriate book, and locate the relevant information. Use the table of contents or index before scanning individual pages. This approach reinforces both the subject and the location of supporting information.

Candidates can also benefit from timed practice. Since the examination provides 120 minutes for 50 questions, practice answering questions while managing lookup time. Some questions may be answered directly from knowledge, while others may warrant confirmation in a reference. Learning when to search and when to continue can help build an efficient testing strategy.

Do not limit preparation to memorizing practice-question answers. When a question is missed, study the underlying concept and locate the supporting material in the applicable book. Understanding why an answer is correct provides better preparation for a similar question presented in a different form.
