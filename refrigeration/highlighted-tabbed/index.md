### Exam Details

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination is a contractor specialty examination for the Qualified Individual associated with the REF specialty. Virginia uses PSI as the examination vendor for contractor specialty examinations, and candidates must satisfy the applicable eligibility and approval requirements before scheduling the examination.

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates receive **120 minutes** to complete the examination and must achieve a minimum score of **70%, or 35 correct answers**.

The examination content is organized into the following subject areas:

- **General Knowledge and Requirements:** 16 questions
- **Refrigeration Piping:** 10 questions
- **Refrigerants:** 5 questions
- **Refrigeration Systems and Controls:** 12 questions
- **Testing and Inspection:** 7 questions

General Knowledge and Requirements represents the largest individual portion of the examination. Refrigeration Systems and Controls is another major area, followed by Refrigeration Piping. These subject areas should receive substantial attention when building a study schedule.

Refrigerants and Testing and Inspection contain fewer questions but remain important. A complete preparation strategy should address every examination category rather than concentrating only on the largest sections.

The examination may include questions based on the listed references as well as questions involving trade knowledge and general industry practices. Practical experience can therefore be valuable, but candidates should also be comfortable with written technical terminology, mechanical code provisions, refrigeration components, system operation, piping concepts, and inspection procedures.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor businesses are licensed according to a class and one or more classifications or specialties.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia offers Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. Qualification requirements vary by class.
2. **Select Refrigeration Contracting.** The applicable specialty abbreviation is REF.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must have the experience required for the selected contractor class.
5. **Obtain approval for the specialty examination.** Candidates must satisfy the applicable Virginia eligibility process before scheduling the REF examination.
6. **Prepare for and pass the Refrigeration Contracting examination.** The highlighted and tabbed references in this package support preparation for the technical specialty exam.
7. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour pre-license education requirement.
8. **Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable.** Class A and Class B contractor applicants have separate Designated Employee examination requirements.
9. **Submit the contractor license application.** The contractor business must provide the required information for its class, specialty, Qualified Individual, Responsible Management, and other applicable qualifications.

The REF examination is only one part of the overall contractor licensing process. Candidates should prepare for the specialty exam while also making sure the business satisfies the separate requirements associated with its intended contractor license class.

### State Requirements

Virginia requires each contractor business to designate a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty on its license. The Qualified Individual is responsible for meeting the experience and examination qualifications associated with that area of work.

The experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. A Qualified Individual for a Class C contractor must have at least **one year of experience** in the applicable classification or specialty. Class B requires at least **three years of experience**, while Class A requires at least **five years of experience**.

Virginia also requires new contractor license applicants to complete **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education**. This business-focused education requirement is separate from technical preparation for the Refrigeration Contracting examination.

The REF specialty is defined specifically around refrigeration work. It covers installation, repair, and removal of refrigeration equipment other than HVAC equipment. Examples include coolers, refrigerated casework, ice-making machines, drinking fountains, cold-room equipment, and similar hermetic refrigeration equipment.

The specialty does not authorize electrical, plumbing, gas-fitting, or HVAC functions. Candidates and contractor businesses should understand this distinction when determining whether the REF specialty matches the work they intend to perform.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A practical preparation plan should follow the official examination categories. Begin with **General Knowledge and Requirements**, which contains 16 questions. Build a solid understanding of refrigeration fundamentals, system operation, components, terminology, installation concepts, and general trade practices.

Next, spend significant time on **Refrigeration Systems and Controls**. This category contains 12 questions and requires candidates to understand how refrigeration systems operate as complete systems. Review compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, pressure and temperature relationships, system controls, and the interaction between major components.

**Refrigeration Piping** accounts for 10 questions. Candidates should review piping materials, system arrangements, installation considerations, sizing principles, fittings, connections, and code requirements associated with refrigeration piping. The highlighted International Mechanical Code can help candidates practice locating relevant code provisions, while Modern Refrigeration and Air Conditioning provides broader technical context.

**Testing and Inspection** accounts for seven questions. Preparation should include system testing concepts, inspection practices, equipment operation, troubleshooting principles, and methods used to confirm proper refrigeration system performance.

**Refrigerants** represents five questions. Candidates should understand refrigerant characteristics, applications, system relationships, handling concepts, and the role refrigerants play throughout the refrigeration cycle.
