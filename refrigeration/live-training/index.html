Exam Details

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination for the Qualified Individual associated with the REF specialty. The Virginia Board for Contractors requires Qualified Individuals for Refrigeration Contracting to successfully complete the applicable Board-approved technical examination.

The examination contains 50 questions. Candidates receive 120 minutes to complete the test and must earn a minimum score of 70%, or 35 correct answers.

The examination content is organized into the following subject areas:

  • General Knowledge and Requirements: 16 questions
  • Refrigeration Piping: 10 questions
  • Refrigerants: 5 questions
  • Refrigeration Systems and Controls: 12 questions
  • Testing and Inspection: 7 questions

General Knowledge and Requirements represents the largest individual exam category, followed by Refrigeration Systems and Controls and Refrigeration Piping. These subjects should receive substantial study time. Refrigerants and Testing and Inspection contain fewer questions but remain important parts of a complete preparation plan.

The examination can test information contained in the reference materials as well as general trade knowledge and accepted industry practices. Candidates should therefore combine book study with an understanding of refrigeration theory, components, system operation, installation principles, controls, piping, refrigerants, testing, and troubleshooting concepts.

Virginia uses PSI for Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. Candidates must receive the appropriate approval before scheduling the REF examination.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Refrigeration Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because the examination center does not supply the books.

Open-book testing rewards candidates who understand both the trade and their references. With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. Attempting to search an unfamiliar book from beginning to end for every answer can consume valuable examination time.

A stronger strategy is to understand the refrigeration material well enough to recognize the subject being tested and then use the references strategically. Candidates should practice deciding whether a question is primarily technical or code-based and select the appropriate book accordingly.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed in accordance with applicable examination rules. Permanent tabs may be used, while removable tabs are not permitted. Candidates should prepare their books before the examination and become familiar with the current reference-material requirements.

During Live Virtual instruction, candidates can build familiarity with technical subjects and the organization of the references. During Self Study preparation, students can reinforce those skills by repeatedly locating information and practicing book navigation under timed conditions.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors. Contractor businesses are licensed under a class and one or more classifications or specialties. The class establishes the applicable level of contracting authority, while the specialty identifies the type of work the business is qualified to perform.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Refrigeration Contracting. The Virginia specialty abbreviation for Refrigeration Contracting is REF.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. Required Qualified Individual experience depends on the contractor class being pursued.
  5. Receive approval for the technical examination. Qualified Individuals for the REF specialty must receive the applicable approval before taking the examination.
  6. Prepare for and complete the Refrigeration Contracting examination. Live Virtual and Self Study preparation can be used together to build technical knowledge and reference-navigation skills.
  7. Complete required pre-license education. New contractor applicants must satisfy Virginia's eight-hour contractor pre-license education requirement.
  8. Complete additional business examination requirements when applicable. Class A and Class B contractor applicants have Designated Employee examination requirements separate from the REF specialty exam.
  9. Submit the contractor license application. The business must complete the requirements for its license class, Qualified Individual, specialty, Responsible Management, and other applicable qualifications.

Passing the REF technical examination is one part of the overall Virginia contractor licensing process. Candidates should prepare for the specialty examination while also planning for the separate business and licensing requirements associated with the contractor class they intend to pursue.

State Requirements

Virginia requires a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty listed on a contractor license. For the Refrigeration Contracting specialty, the Qualified Individual must satisfy the required experience criteria and successfully complete the applicable technical examination.

The minimum experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. A Qualified Individual for a Class C business must have at least one year of experience in the applicable specialty. A Qualified Individual for a Class B business must have at least three years of experience, while a Class A Qualified Individual must have at least five years of experience.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. This pre-license education is a basic business course and is separate from the technical REF examination.

The Refrigeration Contracting specialty itself is limited to refrigeration work. Virginia defines the specialty around the installation, repair, or removal of refrigeration equipment other than HVAC equipment. Electrical, plumbing, gas-fitting, and HVAC functions are not included within the REF specialty.

Understanding the scope is important before beginning the licensing process. Contractors planning to perform work outside the REF specialty may need additional classifications, specialties, licenses, or appropriately qualified individuals depending on the services offered.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive preparation plan should be organized around the official examination categories. Begin with General Knowledge and Requirements, the largest area of the exam. Review refrigeration theory, components, system operation, installation concepts, terminology, and general refrigeration practices.

Refrigeration Systems and Controls is another major area. Candidates should understand how compressors, condensers, evaporators, metering devices, controls, and other components function together as a complete refrigeration system. Understanding system relationships is more useful than simply memorizing isolated component definitions.

Refrigeration Piping should include review of piping arrangements, materials, installation considerations, fittings, sizing principles, and applicable code provisions. Candidates can use the refrigeration textbook for technical understanding and the International Mechanical Code for relevant code requirements.

Refrigerants preparation should address refrigerant characteristics, system relationships, handling concepts, and the role of refrigerants in the refrigeration cycle. Candidates should be comfortable recognizing how refrigerant conditions change as the system operates.

Testing and Inspection preparation should include system testing, operating checks, inspection concepts, troubleshooting principles, and methods used to evaluate refrigeration system performance. Practical field experience can be particularly useful in this area when combined with exam-focused terminology and reference study.

Reference navigation should be incorporated into every stage of preparation. When working through a question, identify the subject first. Then select the appropriate reference and use the table of contents, index, chapter headings, code sections, and other navigation tools to locate the relevant information.

Timed practice can help candidates develop a realistic testing rhythm. Work through groups of questions and limit the amount of time spent on each lookup. Practice recognizing when you know an answer, when reference confirmation is useful, and when it may be better to move forward and return to a difficult question later.

When an answer is missed during practice, use it as a study opportunity. Locate the supporting information, review the surrounding material, and understand why the correct response is appropriate. This approach builds transferable knowledge instead of relying only on memorizing individual answers.
