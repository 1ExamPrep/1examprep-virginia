Exam Details

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. The specialty is identified by the abbreviation FIC, and the examination is administered through PSI.

  • Exam Name: Farm Improvement Contracting Examination
  • Specialty Abbreviation: FIC
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Allowed: 90 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 28 correct answers
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The current published examination outline divides the 40 questions among seven technical subject areas:

  • Excavating and Site Work: 5 questions
  • General Concrete: 6 questions
  • Masonry: 5 questions
  • Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing: 8 questions
  • Manufactured Trusses: 5 questions
  • Roofing and Siding: 6 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual exam category with eight questions. General Concrete and Roofing and Siding each account for six questions, while Excavating and Site Work, Masonry, Manufactured Trusses, and Safety each account for five questions.

This distribution means candidates should prepare across the full range of farm-building construction rather than concentrating on one trade. Farm improvement work can involve site preparation, foundations, concrete placement, masonry walls, framing, engineered trusses, roofing, exterior coverings, and safe construction practices.

The examination may also include questions based on trade knowledge and general industry practices. Candidates should therefore focus on understanding construction concepts as well as locating information in their references.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because books are not provided at the testing center.

Published examination rules permit references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and reference materials containing written notes are prohibited. Candidates may not write in their references during the examination session.

The books in this package are pre-highlighted and permanently tabbed to support efficient navigation within those rules. Candidates should still become familiar with every reference before examination day so they understand which book applies to each type of technical question.

With 40 questions and 90 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient book navigation can help preserve time for construction calculations, code questions, and technical scenarios that require careful review.

For example, excavation questions may point toward Pipe and Excavation Contracting. Concrete questions may require Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Masonry questions may lead to Modern Masonry, while framing topics may require Carpentry and Building Construction. Manufactured truss questions may point toward the BCSI reference, and safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class. Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
  2. Select the Farm Improvement Contracting specialty. Virginia identifies this specialty with the abbreviation FIC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for the specialty designation.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the requested contractor license class.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
  6. Complete the applicable qualification and examination process. The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the required FIC technical examination.
  7. Complete any additional examinations associated with the selected contractor license class.
  8. Prepare the contractor license application and supporting documentation.
  9. Submit the applicable licensing materials to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

Successful completion of the Farm Improvement Contracting examination is one component of the contractor licensing process. Passing the exam does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Farm Improvement Contracting as work involving the installation, repair, or improvement of a nonresidential farm building or structure, a nonresidential farm accessory-use structure, or additions to those buildings or structures.

The Farm Improvement Contracting specialty does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting functions. Those regulated services require the appropriate contractor classification, specialty, or individual trade credentials when applicable.

The Virginia Commercial Building Contractor classification also provides authority for Farm Improvement Contracting work within its scope.

The contractor business must satisfy the requirements associated with its selected license class and specialty. This includes the applicable Qualified Individual, experience, pre-license education, examination, business, and application requirements.

Farm Improvement Contracting involves construction of nonresidential agricultural structures, so candidates should develop broad knowledge of building methods rather than focusing only on traditional farm terminology. The scope can include construction activities that closely resemble other forms of building work while remaining within the defined nonresidential farm context.

Test Information and Study Materials

Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual content area on the examination. Candidates should become familiar with framing terminology, floor and wall systems, roof framing, structural members, construction layout, connections, and common building methods.

Manufactured Trusses should be studied separately from general carpentry. Metal plate connected wood trusses involve specific handling, installation, restraint, and bracing practices. Use the BCSI reference to become familiar with these procedures and the terminology associated with engineered truss systems.

General Concrete accounts for six questions. Study concrete materials, mixture properties, water-cement relationships, placement, consolidation, curing, strength, durability, and basic construction practices. Understanding concrete behavior can help candidates evaluate practical questions rather than relying only on memorized facts.

Masonry preparation should include brick, block, stone, mortar, masonry layout, construction techniques, and material properties. Work with the masonry reference regularly so you can locate information efficiently when a question uses specific trade terminology.

Excavating and Site Work includes five questions. Candidates should review excavation methods, site preparation, grading, earthmoving, trenches, equipment, and related field procedures. Excavation safety should also be connected with applicable OSHA requirements.

Roofing and Siding accounts for six questions. Candidates should understand roof framing relationships, roof coverings, siding applications, moisture considerations, and other exterior building systems relevant to nonresidential farm structures.

Safety accounts for five questions. Construction of farm structures can involve excavation, elevated work, ladders, scaffolding, tools, equipment, material handling, framing, and other jobsite hazards. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of 29 CFR Part 1926 and practice locating applicable requirements.

Code navigation is another important preparation skill. Applicable ICC code questions are based on the 2021 editions. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the International Building Code, 2021 and the International Residential Code, 2021, paying particular attention to construction topics relevant to foundations, framing, structural systems, roofs, and materials.

A productive study routine should combine technical review, reference navigation, and practice-oriented questions. After studying a subject, work through related questions and locate the supporting material in the appropriate reference. This helps reinforce both understanding and book familiarity.

When a practice question is missed, determine whether the issue involved trade knowledge, terminology, a construction calculation, code navigation, or selecting the wrong reference. Then return to the book and locate the information supporting the correct answer.

The pre-highlighted and permanently tabbed format helps make this process more organized. Candidates can repeatedly return to important areas during preparation so the tabs become familiar landmarks rather than unfamiliar labels first encountered on examination day.
