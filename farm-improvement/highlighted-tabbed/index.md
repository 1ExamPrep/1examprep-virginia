### Exam Details

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. The specialty is identified by the abbreviation **FIC**, and the examination is administered through PSI.

- **Exam Name:** Farm Improvement Contracting Examination
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** FIC
- **Number of Questions:** 40
- **Time Allowed:** 90 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 28 correct answers
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

The current published examination outline divides the 40 questions among seven technical subject areas:

- **Excavating and Site Work:** 5 questions
- **General Concrete:** 6 questions
- **Masonry:** 5 questions
- **Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing:** 8 questions
- **Manufactured Trusses:** 5 questions
- **Roofing and Siding:** 6 questions
- **Safety:** 5 questions

Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual exam category with eight questions. General Concrete and Roofing and Siding each account for six questions, while Excavating and Site Work, Masonry, Manufactured Trusses, and Safety each account for five questions.

This distribution means candidates should prepare across the full range of farm-building construction rather than concentrating on one trade. Farm improvement work can involve site preparation, foundations, concrete placement, masonry walls, framing, engineered trusses, roofing, exterior coverings, and safe construction practices.

The examination may also include questions based on trade knowledge and general industry practices. Candidates should therefore focus on understanding construction concepts as well as locating information in their references.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because books are not provided at the testing center.

Published examination rules permit references to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and reference materials containing written notes are prohibited. Candidates may not write in their references during the examination session.

The books in this package are pre-highlighted and permanently tabbed to support efficient navigation within those rules. Candidates should still become familiar with every reference before examination day so they understand which book applies to each type of technical question.

With 40 questions and 90 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient book navigation can help preserve time for construction calculations, code questions, and technical scenarios that require careful review.

For example, excavation questions may point toward *Pipe and Excavation Contracting*. Concrete questions may require *Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures*. Masonry questions may lead to *Modern Masonry*, while framing topics may require *Carpentry and Building Construction*. Manufactured truss questions may point toward the BCSI reference, and safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate Virginia contractor license class.** Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
2. **Select the Farm Improvement Contracting specialty.** Virginia identifies this specialty with the abbreviation FIC.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must provide a Qualified Individual for the specialty designation.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirements.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the requested contractor license class.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
6. **Complete the applicable qualification and examination process.** The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the required FIC technical examination.
7. **Complete any additional examinations associated with the selected contractor license class.**
8. **Prepare the contractor license application and supporting documentation.**
9. **Submit the applicable licensing materials to the Virginia Board for Contractors.**

Successful completion of the Farm Improvement Contracting examination is one component of the contractor licensing process. Passing the exam does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license.

## How does the Farm Improvement Contractor exam work?

The Farm Improvement Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
