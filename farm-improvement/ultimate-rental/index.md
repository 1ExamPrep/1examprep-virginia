### Exam Details

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination for the FIC designation under the Virginia Board for Contractors. The examination is administered through PSI.

- **Exam Name:** Farm Improvement Contracting Examination
- **Specialty Abbreviation:** FIC
- **Number of Questions:** 40
- **Time Allowed:** 90 minutes
- **Minimum Passing Score:** 70%, or 28 correct answers
- **Exam Format:** Multiple choice
- **Exam Administrator:** PSI

The published examination outline divides the 40 questions among seven construction subject areas:

- **Excavating and Site Work:** 5 questions
- **General Concrete:** 6 questions
- **Masonry:** 5 questions
- **Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing:** 8 questions
- **Manufactured Trusses:** 5 questions
- **Roofing and Siding:** 6 questions
- **Safety:** 5 questions

Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual examination category, with eight questions. General Concrete and Roofing and Siding each account for six questions. Excavating and Site Work, Masonry, Manufactured Trusses, and Safety each contain five questions.

This distribution makes broad technical preparation important. Candidates should be prepared to move from excavation and foundation questions to framing, trusses, roofing, siding, and safety without relying on one reference for every answer.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must provide their own permitted reference materials because the testing center does not supply the books.

Permitted reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are allowed. Removable tabs are not permitted, and references containing written notes are not allowed under the examination rules.

The rental books in this Ultimate package are highlighted and permanently tabbed to support efficient navigation. Manufactured truss questions may require the BCSI guide, safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926, and code-based questions may require the 2021 International Building Code or 2021 International Residential Code. Developing these associations during study can make open-book navigation more efficient.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses consist of a license class together with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
2. **Select the Farm Improvement Contracting specialty.** The Virginia specialty abbreviation is FIC.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable requirements for the specialty.
4. **Document applicable experience.** Qualified Individuals for the FIC designation must provide the required experience verification.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must complete the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
6. **Complete the FIC technical examination.** The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the Farm Improvement Contracting examination.
7. **Complete any additional examinations required for the selected contractor license class.**
8. **Prepare the contractor license application and supporting documentation.**
9. **Submit the required licensing materials through the Virginia Board for Contractors process.Application Service is included** with this Ultimate package, providing support with the contractor licensing application process. Passing the technical examination remains one part of licensing and does not by itself result in issuance of a Virginia contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Farm Improvement Contracting** as the service that provides for the installation, repair, or improvement of a nonresidential farm building or structure, a nonresidential farm accessory-use structure, or additions to those buildings or structures.

Virginia also recognizes the Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications as providing authority for Farm Improvement Contracting functions within their respective scopes.

The FIC specialty does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting functions. Contractors performing separately regulated work must have the appropriate authority for those services.

The Virginia Board for Contractors licenses business entities and sole proprietors. Contractor licenses include both a class and a classification or specialty. The class determines the applicable contracting level, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the contractor is authorized to perform.

Farm Improvement Contracting covers a broad collection of construction activities within its defined agricultural scope. A project may involve excavation, grading, foundations, concrete, masonry, framing, manufactured trusses, roofing, siding, and other building systems. This variety is reflected in the technical examination.

## How does the Farm Improvement Contractor exam work?

The Farm Improvement Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
- Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
- Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
- The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
