Exam Details

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the FIC designation under the Virginia Board for Contractors. The examination is administered by PSI.

  • Exam Name: Farm Improvement Contracting Examination
  • Specialty Abbreviation: FIC
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Allowed: 90 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 28 correct answers
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The published examination content is divided among seven technical areas:

  • Excavating and Site Work: 5 questions
  • General Concrete: 6 questions
  • Masonry: 5 questions
  • Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing: 8 questions
  • Manufactured Trusses: 5 questions
  • Roofing and Siding: 6 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual exam category, accounting for eight questions. General Concrete and Roofing and Siding each account for six questions. Excavating and Site Work, Masonry, Manufactured Trusses, and Safety each contribute five questions.

This distribution makes balanced preparation important. Candidates should be ready to move from a question about site excavation to one involving concrete, masonry, framing, truss bracing, roofing, or construction safety. Understanding how these subjects connect within a complete building project can make exam questions easier to interpret.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted reference materials to the examination because the books are not provided at the testing center.

Approved reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs are not allowed, and references containing written notes are prohibited under the testing rules.

Open-book status does not mean every answer can be found quickly without preparation. Candidates have 90 minutes to answer 40 questions, providing an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Spending several minutes searching an unfamiliar reference can create unnecessary time pressure later in the examination.

A useful strategy is to classify the question before reaching for a book. Excavation and site-work questions may lead to Pipe and Excavation Contracting. Concrete questions may require Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Masonry questions may point toward Modern Masonry, while framing questions may require Carpentry and Building Construction.

Questions involving metal plate connected wood trusses may require the BCSI reference. Safety questions may point toward 29 CFR Part 1926. Code-related questions may require the 2021 International Building Code or 2021 International Residential Code.

The goal is to build a repeatable process: identify the subject, choose the appropriate reference, locate the relevant section using the index or table of contents, and then evaluate the answer choices using the technical information found there.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select the Farm Improvement Contracting specialty. Virginia identifies this specialty with the abbreviation FIC.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate an individual who meets the applicable requirements for the specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must satisfy the experience requirements associated with the selected license class and specialty.
  5. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must complete the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
  6. Complete the required FIC technical examination.
  7. Complete any additional examinations associated with the selected contractor license class.
  8. Prepare the contractor license application and supporting documentation.
  9. Submit the required materials through the Virginia Board for Contractors licensing process.

Passing the Farm Improvement Contracting examination is one component of Virginia contractor licensing. Successful completion of the technical examination does not by itself issue a contractor license.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Farm Improvement Contracting as the service that provides for the installation, repair, or improvement of a nonresidential farm building or structure, a nonresidential farm accessory-use structure, or additions to those buildings or structures.

The Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications also provide authority for Farm Improvement Contracting work within their applicable scopes.

The FIC specialty does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting functions. Those types of regulated work require the applicable classification, specialty, or individual trade credential when required.

The broad scope of FIC work explains the variety of subjects included on the technical examination. A nonresidential agricultural building may require site preparation and excavation, concrete foundations, masonry, conventional or metal framing, manufactured trusses, roofing, siding, and multiple construction-safety practices.

Candidates should therefore approach Farm Improvement Contracting as a building-construction specialty with an agricultural application rather than as a single-trade examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive FIC study plan should follow the published examination categories. Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual section, so candidates should become comfortable with building layout, wall framing, floor systems, roof framing, structural members, connections, and related construction practices.

Manufactured Trusses should be treated as a distinct study subject. Metal plate connected wood trusses require specific handling, installation, restraint, and bracing procedures. The BCSI reference should be used regularly so candidates become familiar with the terminology and procedures associated with engineered truss systems.

General Concrete represents six questions. Preparation should include cementitious materials, aggregates, water-cement relationships, mixture properties, strength, durability, placement, consolidation, curing, and testing. Understanding why concrete behaves a certain way can help with practical questions that require more than simple memorization.

Masonry preparation should include brick, concrete masonry units, stone, mortar, layout, materials, tools, and basic construction methods. Candidates should become comfortable with masonry terminology so they can recognize the subject quickly and locate supporting information efficiently.

Excavating and Site Work accounts for five questions. Candidates should review site preparation, excavation methods, grading, trenching, soil movement, construction equipment, and other earthwork activities. These subjects may also connect with OSHA requirements related to excavation safety.

Roofing and Siding accounts for six questions. Study roofing materials, roof systems, exterior coverings, siding applications, weather protection, and the relationship between the roof or wall framing and the exterior building envelope.

Safety contributes five questions and should be incorporated throughout the study schedule rather than treated as a final topic. Farm-building construction may involve excavations, ladders, scaffolds, elevated work, material handling, power tools, framing, lifting, and other common construction hazards.

Code navigation should also receive dedicated practice. The 2021 International Building Code and 2021 International Residential Code are extensive references, so candidates should become familiar with their chapter structures, indexes, tables, definitions, and common terminology.

A useful preparation routine combines technical review with practice-oriented questions. Study one subject at a time, such as concrete or framing, and then work through related questions. After answering, use the appropriate reference to locate the information supporting the correct response.

When a practice question is missed, identify the reason. Determine whether the problem involved a construction concept, calculation, terminology, code navigation, or choosing the wrong reference. Then return to the relevant source and review the material associated with the question.

Timed practice can help candidates become comfortable working within the 90-minute test period. Not every question should require the same amount of time. Straightforward trade-knowledge questions may be answered quickly, leaving additional time for code questions or calculations that require careful reference work.

The objective is not to memorize all eight references. A more practical goal is to understand the major concepts, recognize which book supports each subject, and develop enough familiarity with the references to locate detailed information efficiently when needed.

FAQ Section

How many questions are on the Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting exam?

The Virginia FIC examination contains 40 questions.

How much time is allowed for the FIC examination?

Candidates have 90 minutes to complete the examination.

What score is required to pass?

The minimum passing score is 70%, corresponding to 28 correct answers out of 40 questions.

Is the Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting exam open book?

Yes. The FIC examination is open book, and candidates must bring their own permitted references to the testing center.

What subjects are covered on the FIC examination?

The published content areas include Excavating and Site Work, General Concrete, Masonry, Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing, Manufactured Trusses, Roofing and Siding, and Safety.

What is the largest section of the examination?

Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual section, with eight questions.

What books should I study for the Virginia FIC exam?

The listed references include OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926, the 2021 IBC, the 2021 IRC, Carpentry and Building Construction, Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures, Modern Masonry, Pipe and Excavation Contracting, and the 2025 BCSI guide for metal plate connected wood trusses.

What does Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting cover?

The specialty covers installation, repair, and improvement of nonresidential farm buildings and structures, nonresidential farm accessory-use structures, and additions to those structures.

Can a Virginia FIC contractor perform electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting work?

The FIC specialty does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting functions. Those services require the applicable authority when separately regulated.

Why is reference navigation important for the FIC exam?

The examination is open book but timed. Candidates who can quickly identify which reference applies to excavation, concrete, masonry, framing, trusses, roofing, or safety can reduce unnecessary searching and use their examination time more efficiently.

Does passing the FIC examination automatically issue a Virginia contractor license?

No. Passing the Farm Improvement Contracting technical examination is one component of the licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable requirements for the contractor license class, Qualified Individual, experience, education, business, and application.

How does the Farm Improvement Contractor exam work?

The Farm Improvement Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
