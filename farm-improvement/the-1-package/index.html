Exam Details

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is the technical specialty examination associated with the FIC designation under the Virginia Board for Contractors. PSI administers the specialty examination for Qualified Individuals.

  • Exam Name: Farm Improvement Contracting Examination
  • Specialty Abbreviation: FIC
  • Number of Questions: 40
  • Time Allowed: 90 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 70%, or 28 correct answers
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The published examination outline divides the test among several construction disciplines:

  • Excavating and Site Work: 5 questions
  • General Concrete: 6 questions
  • Masonry: 5 questions
  • Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing: 8 questions
  • Manufactured Trusses: 5 questions
  • Roofing and Siding: 6 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing is the largest individual examination category. General Concrete and Roofing and Siding also represent significant portions of the test. Candidates should nevertheless prepare for every subject because questions can move quickly between site work, concrete, masonry, framing, trusses, roofing, siding, and safety.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Farm Improvement Contracting examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because testing centers do not provide the books.

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted. Removable tabs and references containing written notes are not permitted under the published examination rules.

The included rental books are highlighted and permanently tabbed to support organized navigation. Candidates should still use the books regularly throughout their 1 year of course access so they become familiar with each reference and understand where important subjects are located.

The exam allows 90 minutes for 40 questions, providing an average of approximately two minutes and fifteen seconds per question. Efficient reference selection can help candidates preserve time for calculations, technical interpretation, and detailed code questions.

For example, excavation questions may point toward Pipe and Excavation Contracting. Concrete questions may require Design and Control of Concrete Mixtures. Masonry questions may lead to Modern Masonry, while framing questions may require Carpentry and Building Construction. Manufactured truss questions may require the BCSI reference, and safety questions may require 29 CFR Part 1926.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. A contractor license combines a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

  1. Establish the business entity. The 1 Package includes Business Formation assistance for an LLC or Corporation.
  2. Obtain an EIN. EIN Filing with the IRS is included to help establish the federal identification used for common business and tax purposes.
  3. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia licenses contractors as Class A, Class B, or Class C.
  4. Select the Farm Improvement Contracting specialty. Virginia identifies the specialty using the abbreviation FIC.
  5. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable requirements for the FIC specialty.
  6. Meet the applicable experience requirements. The Qualified Individual must document qualifying experience associated with the requested contractor license class and specialty.
  7. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable eight-hour approved pre-license education requirement.
  8. Complete the FIC technical examination. The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the Board-approved technical examination.
  9. Complete any additional examination requirements applicable to the selected contractor license class.
  10. Prepare and submit the contractor license application. Application Service is included with The 1 Package.
  11. Address applicable compliance requirements. Contractor Compliance Guidance is included to support understanding of Virginia contractor requirements relevant to operating the business.

Passing the FIC technical examination is one part of the Virginia contractor licensing process. The business must also satisfy the applicable licensing requirements before the requested contractor license and specialty are issued.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Farm Improvement Contracting as the installation, repair, or improvement of a nonresidential farm building or structure, a nonresidential farm accessory-use structure, or additions to those structures.

The Virginia Commercial Building Contractor and Residential Building Contractor classifications also provide authority for Farm Improvement Contracting work within their respective scopes.

The FIC specialty does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, HVAC, or gas-fitting functions. Contractors performing separately regulated trade work must have the applicable authority for those services.

The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for the FIC specialty who meets the experience and technical examination requirements applicable to that designation. Virginia also requires the contractor business to satisfy the requirements associated with its selected Class A, Class B, or Class C license.

Virginia requires successful completion of the applicable examination before a contractor applies for licensure. The technical examination is therefore an important milestone, but it remains part of a broader licensing process involving the business entity, Qualified Individual, education, examinations, documentation, and contractor application.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong FIC preparation plan should follow the exam content outline. Rough Carpentry and Metal Framing deserves substantial attention because it is the largest individual section. Candidates should review layout, structural members, floor systems, wall framing, roof framing, connections, and general building practices.

Manufactured Trusses should be studied separately from conventional framing. Metal plate connected wood trusses involve specific handling, installation, restraint, and bracing procedures. The BCSI reference provides dedicated technical guidance for this area.

General Concrete preparation should cover concrete ingredients, mixture proportions, water-cement relationships, strength, durability, placement, consolidation, curing, and testing. Candidates should focus on understanding how concrete behaves under practical construction conditions.

Masonry study should include brick, block, stone, mortar, layout, tools, materials, and construction methods. Familiarity with the terminology in Modern Masonry can make both technical interpretation and reference navigation more efficient.

Excavating and Site Work requires knowledge of site preparation, grading, excavation methods, trenching, soil movement, and equipment. These subjects may also connect directly with construction safety requirements in 29 CFR Part 1926.

Roofing and Siding should include roof components, roofing systems, exterior coverings, siding, weather protection, and the relationship between structural framing and the building envelope.

Safety should remain part of the study schedule throughout preparation. Nonresidential farm-building construction can involve excavation, scaffolds, ladders, elevated work, framing, power tools, material handling, and equipment hazards.
