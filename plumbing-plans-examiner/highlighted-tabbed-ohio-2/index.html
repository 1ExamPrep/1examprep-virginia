Exam Details

The ICC Plumbing Plans Examiner (P3) exam is a certification exam for candidates who review plumbing construction documents for code compliance. The exam measures the ability to evaluate proposed plumbing systems, identify required plan information, apply plumbing code provisions, and determine whether submitted drawings and specifications demonstrate compliance with applicable requirements.

The exam commonly includes 60 multiple-choice questions with a 2.5-hour time limit. It is an open-book exam. Candidates preparing for the 2021 exam version should study from the International Plumbing Code, 2021 and the International Fuel Gas Code, 2021. ICC A117.1-2017 is included in this package to support accessibility-related plumbing plan review preparation.

Major study areas include plumbing plan review procedures, administration, construction documents, fixtures and fittings, water supply and distribution, sanitary drainage, venting, storm drainage, and special systems. Candidates should be prepared to answer questions that require code navigation, table usage, plan review judgment, and careful reading of technical details.

Administration and plan review questions may involve scope, permits, construction document requirements, approvals, alternative materials, inspection coordination, plan submission information, code applicability, and incomplete or unclear plan details. Plans examiner candidates should understand how the code establishes the review process and how submitted documents must show enough information to demonstrate compliance.

Fixture and fitting questions may involve minimum plumbing fixture counts, fixture types, toilet rooms, drinking fountains, service sinks, employee facilities, public facilities, occupancy-related fixture requirements, and accessible plumbing fixtures. Candidates should be ready to use both the International Plumbing Code and ICC A117.1-2017 when questions involve accessibility, fixture approach, clear floor space, turning space, or usable plumbing facilities.

Water supply and distribution questions may involve water service, water distribution piping, fixture units, pipe sizing concepts, pressure requirements, water heaters, hot water supply, valves, backflow protection, potable water protection, and system design information shown on plans. Candidates should know where to locate the water supply provisions and how to use tables when a question requires sizing or plan review evaluation.

Sanitary drainage questions may involve drainage fixture units, horizontal and vertical drainage piping, pipe sizing, slope, cleanouts, offsets, indirect waste, special waste, traps, interceptors, separators, and system layout. Plans examiner candidates should become comfortable reading scenarios that describe drainage conditions and matching those details to the correct code section.

Venting questions may involve individual vents, common vents, wet vents, circuit vents, vent stacks, stack vents, branch vents, vent termination, vent sizing, air admittance valves where permitted by the code, and required vent connections. Venting is an important study area because small differences in system layout can change the correct answer.

Storm drainage questions may involve roof drains, secondary drainage, overflow scuppers, leaders, conductors, storm pipe sizing, rainfall rates, drainage area, and roof drainage design information. Candidates should study how storm drainage provisions are organized and practice locating the tables used for sizing and review.

Special systems and fuel gas coordination may involve indirect waste, special waste, interceptors, medical gas coordination, fuel gas piping, gas appliances, appliance venting, combustion air, and coordinated building systems shown on construction drawings. The International Fuel Gas Code, 2021 is an important reference when plan review questions involve gas piping systems or fuel-burning appliances.

How does the Plumbing Plans Examiner (P3) exam work?

The Plumbing Plans Examiner (P3) exam is a code official certification. It is taken after the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class and leads to DHCD certification.

  • Virginia code officials are certified by the Department of Housing and Community Development (DHCD), not by DPOR.
  • The path is the Virginia Building Code Academy Core class, the classes for your certification, then the required exam, usually an ICC exam.
  • Building, fire and property maintenance officials apply within 12 months of hire; technical assistants have 18 months.
  • Certified officials complete 16 hours of DHCD-approved continuing education every 2 years.
