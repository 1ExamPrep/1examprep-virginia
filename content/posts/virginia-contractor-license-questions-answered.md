Virginia requires a contractor license for any job of $1,000 or more. The Board for Contractors at DPOR issues three classes by job size: Class C, Class B and Class A. Every applicant takes 8 hours of pre-license education, and Class A and B firms also pass open-book PSI exams. Below are answers to the 15 questions Virginians search most.

## Do you have to have a contractor license in Virginia?

Yes, for any job worth $1,000 or more. The Virginia Board for Contractors, part of the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation (DPOR), licenses the business that signs the contract. The class you need depends on the size of a single job and your yearly volume, not on whether the work is residential or commercial. Below $1,000 a job, no state contractor license is needed, although a trade license may still apply if the work is electrical, plumbing, HVAC or gas fitting.

## When do you need a contractors license in Virginia?

You need one the moment a single contract reaches $1,000, or when your yearly total crosses the limit of your class. The three classes break down like this:

| Class | Single job | Yearly total | Exams |
|---|---|---|---|
| Class C | $1,000 to under $10,000 | under $150,000 | none, pre-license course only |
| Class B | $10,000 to under $120,000 | under $750,000 | business and law, plus trade exam where required |
| Class A | no limit | no limit | business and law, plus trade exam where required |

If you outgrow your class mid-year, apply for the higher class before you sign the bigger contract.

## How do I get a contractor license in Virginia?

Pick your class and your specialty classification, such as [Home Improvement](/home-improvement/) or Commercial Building. Form the business and line up any insurance your classification needs. Complete the 8-hour [pre-license course](/pre-license-education/). For Class A and B, name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the PSI exams. Then send the application to DPOR. Class C needs no exam, only the course and the application.

## How much is a contractor license in Virginia?

The exam is the cheapest part. PSI charges $40 for one exam portion, $72 for two and $85 for three when you schedule them together. DPOR charges its own application fee by class, listed on the current application form, and the 8-hour pre-license course is priced by the provider you choose. Add your study books: because every Virginia contractor exam is open book, most candidates buy the reference set for their classification, often highlighted and tabbed.

## How long does it take to get a contractor license in Virginia?

Plan on 4 to 8 weeks from start to license for a Class B or A. The pre-license course takes one day. Studying for the business and law portion usually takes 2 to 4 weeks, and the trade portion adds time if your classification has one. DPOR then reviews the application. A Class C, with no exam, is the fastest route; its timing depends mostly on DPOR review once the course is done.

## How hard is it to pass the Virginia contractor exam?

The exams are open book, so the challenge is speed with the references rather than memory. The business and law portion is written to the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Virginia edition. Candidates who know where every topic lives in that book pass comfortably. You may bring your own references highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed in the room.

## Can a contractor work in Virginia without a license?

Only on jobs under $1,000. Above that, contracting without the right class of license is a violation that DPOR can penalize, and an unlicensed contractor may have trouble enforcing payment on the contract. Working above your class limit counts the same as working unlicensed for the part that exceeds it, so a Class C contractor cannot take a $12,000 job.

## What is a Virginia contractor license number?

A Virginia contractor license number is the number DPOR assigns to your firm's license, printed on the license and listed in DPOR's public license lookup. Clients, building departments and suppliers use it to confirm your class and specialties. Virginia expects the number on contracts and certain advertising, so keep it handy once the license is issued.

## How do I renew my Virginia contractor license?

Renew through DPOR before the expiration date printed on the license, and keep your firm's information and designated employee current. Tradesman licenses carry continuing education: 3 hours for electrical, plumbing and HVAC, and 1 hour for gas fitting, before each renewal. A license that lapses past the reinstatement window may mean applying again, so set a reminder well ahead of the date.

## When does a contractors license expire in Virginia?

On the date printed on the license, which DPOR also shows in its lookup. DPOR sends a renewal notice, but the responsibility is yours. Working after expiration is the same as working unlicensed, so file the renewal early.

## Does Virginia have contractor license reciprocity?

Virginia does not have broad reciprocity for contractor licenses. Each Virginia license is issued on Virginia's own requirements, including the 8-hour pre-license course. Contractors licensed elsewhere should check DPOR for any agreement covering their trade. Many find that passing the NASCLA business guide material is quick if they already know it from another state.

## Can a Virginia contractor work in Maryland or DC?

Not on a Virginia license alone. Maryland and the District of Columbia issue their own contractor and home improvement licenses, with their own exams and insurance rules. A Virginia contractor who wants work across the river applies there too. The business and law knowledge carries over, so the second license is usually faster to earn than the first.

## Do electricians and plumbers need a separate license?

Yes. Electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting workers hold an individual tradesman license from the same Board for Contractors, separate from the firm's contractor license. The board approves you before PSI lets you schedule, and the exam fee is $100 for [journeyman](/journeyman-electrician/) and $125 for [master](/master-electrician/). Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses were added on April 1, 2025, and need less experience than a journeyman license.

## Should contractors be licensed in Virginia?

Yes, for the client's protection and your own. A license shows the firm passed the required exams and education, and it gives the homeowner access to the state's complaint process. For you, it is what lets you pull permits, bid public work and collect on contracts above $1,000 without legal risk.

## What books do I need for the Virginia contractor exam?

For Class A and B, the core book is the NASCLA Contractors Guide to Business, Law and Project Management, Virginia edition, plus the references listed in the PSI bulletin for your trade classification. Because the exam is open book, a highlighted and tabbed set saves the most time. See the [business and law](/business-and-law/) page for the current packages.
