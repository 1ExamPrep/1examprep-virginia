Exam Details

The Virginia Vessel Construction Contracting examination is administered through PSI for the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Virginia identifies Vessel Construction Contracting with the abbreviation VCC.

The VCC specialty requires a qualified individual to receive the applicable approval and pass the specialty examination. Examination preparation should focus on the technical knowledge required to construct, repair, improve, and remove nonresidential vessels, tanks, and associated piping systems within the scope of the specialty.

The examination covers subjects associated with underground tanks and installation, aboveground tanks and installation, sitework and excavation, welding, cathodic protection, hoisting and rigging, and safety. These areas reflect the different phases of work involved in storage-system and vessel construction.

Tank installation requires more than knowing how a vessel is assembled. Candidates should understand how sites are prepared, how excavations are performed, how tanks and related components are handled, how loads are rigged and lifted, how piping connections are made, and how installations are protected and completed safely.

Welding and rigging are particularly important because vessel construction often involves heavy components and permanent connections that must be completed using appropriate procedures. Underground and aboveground storage systems also involve specialized installation practices addressed by the RP100 and RP200 references.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Vessel Construction Contracting examination is an open-book examination. Candidates may use approved reference materials that comply with the current PSI examination rules.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Books must otherwise remain unmarked, and written notes are not permitted. References may be indexed with permanent tabs. Removable tabs and temporary flags are not allowed.

The open-book format makes advance familiarity with the references especially important. A candidate who has never worked with the books may spend unnecessary time trying to determine which reference contains the information needed for a particular question.

During preparation, practice recognizing the subject before opening a book. Questions involving underground tank installation may lead toward RP100, while aboveground storage-system questions may require RP200. Excavation questions may involve Pipe and Excavation Contracting or OSHA depending on whether the question focuses on construction procedures or safety requirements.

Welding questions may require Modern Welding, while lifting and load-handling questions may point toward the Handbook of Rigging. Construction safety questions can require 29 CFR Part 1926. Learning these distinctions helps candidates make more efficient use of their reference materials.

Open-book exams reward a combination of knowledge and reference navigation. Candidates should know enough about the trade to identify the likely source before beginning a detailed search. Practice using tables of contents, indexes, chapter headings, diagrams, charts, and terminology until the books become familiar working tools.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation through the Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to businesses and consist of a license class together with the applicable classification or specialty.

Applicants pursuing Vessel Construction Contracting must identify a qualified individual for the VCC specialty. Virginia requires qualified individuals for VCC to meet the applicable experience requirements and receive approval to take the specialty examination.

The minimum experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. Current Virginia contractor application requirements call for at least one year of applicable experience for a Class C qualified individual, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A.

The qualified individual must also have the required relationship with the contracting business. Virginia requires the qualified individual to be a full-time employee working at least 30 hours per week or a member of the business's responsible management.

After the applicable approval is obtained, the qualified individual completes the Vessel Construction Contracting examination through PSI. Successful completion of the required specialty examination satisfies the technical examination requirement associated with the VCC designation.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management completes the required education.

Class A and Class B contractor applicants also have business examination requirements associated with the designated employee. These requirements are separate from the VCC specialty examination.

After the examination, education, experience, and other applicable requirements are satisfied, the contractor license application is completed and submitted to the Virginia Board for Contractors for review.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Vessel Construction Contracting (VCC) as the service that provides for construction, repair, improvement, or removal of nonresidential vessels, tanks, or piping that hold or convey fluids other than sanitary, storm, waste, or potable water supplies.

This specialty can apply to storage tanks, vessels, and related piping systems used in commercial, industrial, fueling, and similar nonresidential applications when the work falls within the scope established by Virginia contractor regulations.

The Highway/Heavy classification also provides for Vessel Construction Contracting. A contractor seeking the specialty designation directly must satisfy the qualified-individual and examination requirements associated with VCC.

Virginia contractor licenses are issued in Class A, Class B, and Class C categories. The license class determines the monetary level of projects and contracts the business may undertake, while the classification or specialty determines what type of work the business is authorized to perform.

Each contractor must identify a qualified individual for each applicable classification or specialty. For VCC, the qualified individual must satisfy the required experience criteria and pass the approved specialty examination.

Applicants should select the license class and specialty that correspond with the projects the business intends to perform. Test Information and Study Materials

A productive Virginia Vessel Construction Contractor study plan should begin by dividing preparation into the major technical areas of the trade. Start with underground and aboveground storage systems, then study excavation, welding, rigging, piping, cathodic protection, and construction safety.

For underground tank preparation, use RP100 to study site preparation, tank handling, excavation, placement, backfill, components, piping, and installation procedures. Pay attention to how the various parts of the underground storage system work together rather than memorizing isolated requirements.

For aboveground storage systems, use RP200 to become familiar with placement, foundations, system components, fueling applications, piping, and installation practices. Learn the terminology used throughout the reference so exam questions involving aboveground systems are easier to identify.

Excavation preparation should combine practical methods with OSHA safety. Pipe and Excavation Contracting can support understanding of earthmoving, trenching, pipe placement, backfill, and compaction, while 29 CFR Part 1926 covers the safety requirements that apply to excavation and construction operations.

Welding preparation should include common welding processes, equipment, joint types, materials, preparation, safety, and inspection concepts. Vessel and piping construction can involve substantial welding, so candidates should understand the practical purpose behind different welding methods.

Rigging preparation should focus on safe lifting practices, sling configurations, rigging hardware, load limits, wire rope, chains, hoists, and load calculations. Tank and vessel components can be large and heavy, making safe handling and proper rigging a critical trade subject.

After studying each subject independently, move into mixed-reference practice. Read a question, identify the primary trade area, choose the applicable reference, and locate supporting information. This develops both technical understanding and the open-book navigation skills needed for the exam.

What does VCC stand for in Virginia contractor licensing?

Is the Virginia Vessel Construction Contractor exam open book?

What subjects should I study for the VCC exam?

Why are RP100 and RP200 included?

RP100 addresses recommended installation practices for underground liquid storage systems, while RP200 addresses recommended installation practices for aboveground storage systems used for motor vehicle fueling.

Why is the Handbook of Rigging included?

Vessel and tank construction can involve heavy lifts and large components. The Handbook of Rigging supports preparation involving slings, wire rope, rigging hardware, load handling, hoisting practices, and lifting calculations.

Can the examination books be highlighted and tabbed?

Virginia PSI rules permit approved reference materials to be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before testing. Permanent tabs are allowed, while removable tabs are not permitted.

Does a Virginia VCC qualified individual need experience?

Yes. The applicable experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. Virginia requires at least one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A qualified individuals.

Is Virginia contractor pre-license education required?

Yes. New Virginia contractor licenses require eight hours of approved pre-license education completed by the applicable member of responsible management or designated employee based on the license class.
