### State Requirements

Virginia regulates individual gas fitters through the Board for Contractors Tradesmen Program. Applicants for the Journeyman Gasfitter examination must satisfy an approved combination of education or formal vocational training and practical experience.

Current Virginia regulations provide several Journeyman Gasfitter qualification pathways, including:

- **Bachelor's degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade** plus **1 year of practical experience**.
- **Associate degree or certificate from at least a two-year tradesman-related program** plus **2 years of practical experience**.
- **120 hours of formal vocational training** plus **4 years of practical experience**.
- **80 hours of formal vocational training** plus **5 years of practical experience**.
- **40 hours of formal vocational training** plus **6 years of practical experience**.
- **No formal vocational training** plus **8 years of practical experience**.

Candidates should submit the required documentation for the qualification pathway they are using. Practical experience must relate to gas fitting work, and qualifying education or vocational training must be documented where applicable.

The Journeyman Gasfitter credential is issued to an individual and should not be confused with a Virginia contractor business license. A business performing gas-fitting contracting work may have separate licensing obligations through the Board for Contractors.

Virginia also requires continuing education for licensed gas fitters as part of the renewal process. Gas-fitting continuing education must be Board approved and is separate from initial Journeyman Gasfitter examination preparation.

### Test Information and Study Materials

A strong Journeyman Gasfitter study strategy should combine technical understanding with repeated code navigation. Live Virtual instruction can provide a structured path through the material, while Self Study preparation allows candidates to spend additional time on calculations, tables, and difficult technical topics.

**Natural and LP-Gas Piping** preparation should include approved materials, fittings, joints, system routing, piping protection, pressure considerations, installation practices, shutoff requirements, and other provisions affecting gas piping systems.

**Pipe Sizing** should receive repeated practice. Candidates should become comfortable identifying system demand, developed length, available pressure, pipe type, and the applicable sizing table. Candidates should also practice reading table headings and notes carefully because a small difference in conditions can change the correct answer.

**LP-Gas Tanks and Containers** should be studied directly from NFPA 58. Candidates should review placement, clearances, protection, installation, container requirements, piping connections, and safety provisions associated with LP-Gas systems.

**Gas Piping Controls** preparation should include regulators, valves, shutoff devices, and other components used to control fuel flow safely through a gas system.

**Testing** preparation should include pressure testing, test conditions, procedures, system verification, and requirements for confirming that piping is suitable for service.

**Virginia Regulations** should be reviewed as their own subject. Candidates should study Standards of Practice, Standards of Conduct, qualifications, renewal, reinstatement, definitions, and other rules governing licensed tradesmen.

Live Virtual instruction can help candidates organize these subjects and establish a consistent study schedule. Self Study preparation then gives candidates time to reinforce the material between sessions and spend additional time on the topics that require more practice.

Reference drills can improve open-book performance. Select a practice question, determine which book applies, locate the appropriate chapter or table, and confirm the answer. Repeat the process until the reference layout becomes familiar.

When a practice question is missed, candidates should review the full supporting provision rather than memorizing a single response. Gas codes frequently include conditions, exceptions, table notes, or definitions that affect how a requirement applies.

Pipe-sizing practice should include problems under different conditions. Repetition helps candidates develop a consistent process for selecting the correct table and avoiding errors caused by using the wrong pressure, length, demand, or system configuration.

Candidates should also practice managing difficult questions. If a lookup or calculation is taking too long, it may be more effective to mark the question, continue through the examination, and return after completing questions that can be answered more efficiently.
