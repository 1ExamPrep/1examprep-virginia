### State Requirements

Virginia's current regulations establish several education-and-experience pathways for examination approval as a journeyman gas fitter. An applicant may qualify with a bachelor's degree in an engineering curriculum related to the trade and one year of practical experience, or with an associate degree or certificate from at least a two-year tradesman-related program and two years of practical experience.

Other pathways combine formal vocational training with practical trade experience. For journeyman gas fitter applicants, the regulations provide pathways that include 120 hours of formal vocational training with four years of practical experience, 80 hours of formal vocational training with five years of practical experience, or 40 hours of formal vocational training with six years of practical experience. An applicant with no formal vocational training may qualify with eight years of practical experience in the trade.

Applicants must also be at least 18 years of age and submit the documentation required by the Board. Experience is generally verified through qualifying references such as current or former employers, contractors, building officials, inspectors, engineers, architects, or clients who can attest to the applicant's work in the trade. Educational qualifications may be documented through transcripts or diplomas from the applicable institution.

Virginia defines gas fitting-related work to include work involving industrial gas, fuel gas, gaseous hydrogen, liquefied petroleum gas or natural gas piping, tanks, chimneys and vents, direct vents, equipment, and appliances annexed to real property within the scope established by Virginia law and regulation.

Virginia gas fitter licenses are also subject to continuing education requirements for renewal. Gas fitters must complete the applicable Board-approved continuing education relating to building code changes as required during the licensing cycle.

## How does the Journeyman Gas Fitter (LP) exam work?

The Journeyman Gas Fitter (LP) exam is a tradesman exam: one person is licensed, and a contracting firm in that trade also needs its own Board for Contractors license.

- Tradesman licenses are issued by the Board for Contractors through its Tradesmen program, for electrical, plumbing, HVAC and gas fitting.
- The board has to approve you before PSI lets you schedule. The exam fee is $100 for journeyman and $125 for master.
- Pre-license education is required, and renewals need 3 hours of continuing education for electrical, plumbing and HVAC and 1 hour for gas fitting.
- Residential plumbing and residential HVAC mechanic licenses, added on April 1, 2025, need less experience than a journeyman license.
