### Exam Details

The Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contractor examination contains **50 questions**. Candidates are given **120 minutes** to complete the test. The minimum passing score is **70%, or 35 correct answers**.

The examination content is divided into four major subject areas:

- **General Knowledge:** 16 questions
- **Materials and Application:** 18 questions
- **Wall Coverings:** 6 questions
- **Safety and Employee Protection:** 10 questions

Materials and Application represents the largest portion of the examination, followed closely by General Knowledge. These areas deserve substantial study time because together they make up more than half of the test. Safety and Employee Protection is another important category, particularly because professional painting and decorating work can involve ladders, scaffolds, personal protective equipment, hazardous conditions, tools, and other construction-related safety concerns.

Wall Coverings represents a smaller portion of the examination but should still be included in a complete study plan. Candidates should be comfortable with the basic knowledge associated with surface coverings, preparation, materials, installation practices, and related trade concepts.

The examination may include questions based on the listed reference materials as well as general trade knowledge and accepted industry practices. For that reason, candidates should combine book study with their practical experience and a structured exam preparation program.

### Open Book Test

The Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contractor examination is an **open-book test**. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials because the books are not provided at the examination center.

Open-book testing requires a different study strategy from a closed-book examination. Candidates should not expect to look up every answer from the beginning. With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, there is an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. A candidate who spends several minutes searching through a book for every answer can quickly fall behind.

Candidates preparing for the test should learn where important chapters, sections, tables, safety standards, and trade subjects are located so they can move quickly through the books.

### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Board for Contractors through the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. Contractor licenses are made up of two important components: a license class and a classification or specialty. The license class determines the level of contracting activity permitted, while the classification or specialty identifies the type of work the contractor may perform.

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia contractor licenses are issued as Class A, Class B, or Class C. Each class has its own qualification requirements.
2. **Select the Painting and Wallcovering Contracting specialty.** Painting and Wallcovering Contracting is identified by the abbreviation PTC.
3. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** The contractor business must identify a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested on the license.
4. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must satisfy the minimum experience requirement associated with the selected license class.
5. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor applicants must meet the state's pre-license education requirement.
6. **Receive approval for the applicable trade examination.** Painting and Wallcovering Contracting is among the specialties requiring examination approval and successful completion of the applicable specialty exam.
7. **Pass the required examination.** Candidates must successfully complete the required examination before proceeding through the applicable licensing process.
8. **Submit the contractor license application.** The business must provide the information and documentation required for its license class, specialty, Qualified Individual, responsible management, and other applicable licensing requirements.

The Painting and Wall Covering trade exam is one component of contractor licensing. Class A and Class B applicants may have additional examination requirements involving a Designated Employee. Candidates should treat trade exam preparation and contractor licensing as related but separate parts of the overall process.

### State Requirements

Virginia requires a contractor business to identify a Qualified Individual for every classification or specialty appearing on its license. For Painting and Wallcovering Contracting, the Qualified Individual must satisfy the requirements associated with the PTC specialty and the selected contractor license class.

The Qualified Individual must have at least **one year of experience for a Class C license**, **three years of experience for a Class B license**, or **five years of experience for a Class A license** in the classification or specialty for which the business is applying.

The Qualified Individual must also have an appropriate relationship with the business. Virginia requires the individual to be either a full-time employee working the required number of hours with the business or a member of Responsible Management, as applicable under the licensing requirements.

New contractor licenses also require **eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education**. This pre-license education is a business-focused requirement and is separate from preparation for the Painting and Wall Covering specialty examination.

The PTC specialty itself includes applying materials common to the painting and decorating industry for protective or decorative purposes, installing surface coverings such as vinyl, wallpaper, and cloth fabrics, and performing associated preparation including caulking, sanding, and cleaning. The specialty applies to interior and exterior surfaces.

Candidates should understand that successfully completing the trade examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. The business must satisfy all applicable Board requirements before the license is granted.
