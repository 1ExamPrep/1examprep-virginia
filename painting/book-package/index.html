Exam Details

The Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contracting examination is administered as a specialty contractor examination. The examination contains 50 questions, provides 120 minutes for completion, and requires a minimum score of 70%, or 35 correct answers.

The examination measures knowledge across multiple parts of the painting and wall covering trade. Candidates should prepare for questions involving general trade knowledge, materials, application methods, tools and equipment, safety, surface preparation, wall coverings, and estimating. Surface preparation and application represent especially important areas of study because successful painting and wall covering work depends heavily on correctly preparing substrates and selecting and applying appropriate materials.

The examination content is divided among the following subject areas:

  • General Knowledge: 7 questions
  • Materials: 4 questions
  • Application: 10 questions
  • Tools and Equipment: 4 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions
  • Surface Preparation: 13 questions
  • Wall Covering: 3 questions
  • Estimating: 4 questions

A productive study plan should account for this distribution rather than treating every topic equally. Candidates can devote additional attention to surface preparation and application while still developing enough familiarity with safety, materials, tools, estimating, wall coverings, and general painting knowledge to work confidently through the entire exam.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their permitted reference materials to the examination site because the references are not supplied at the testing location.

Open-book testing rewards organization and familiarity. With 50 questions and 120 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately 2.4 minutes per question. That makes efficient reference navigation important. Searching a book from the beginning for every answer can consume valuable examination time. A stronger approach is to become familiar with the table of contents, index, chapter arrangement, terminology, safety sections, and major trade subjects before the test.

Reference materials may be highlighted, underlined, or indexed before the examination according to the testing rules. Permanent tabs may also be used. Removable tabs such as temporary flags are not permitted. Reference materials containing writing are not permitted under the examination rules, and candidates may not write in their reference materials during the examination session.

Downloaded reference material must also meet the examination site's requirements for bound material. Loose papers and unbound references are not permitted. Candidates preparing books for the test should organize them well before examination day so that their study system is already familiar when they arrive at the testing center.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered through the Virginia Board for Contractors within the Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation. A Virginia contractor license combines a license class with a classification or specialty. The license class determines the level of contracting activity permitted under the license, while the classification or specialty determines the type of work the business is authorized to perform.

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia contractor applicants apply for Class A, Class B, or Class C licensing based on the level of contracting authority being sought and the applicable state requirements.
  2. Select Painting and Wallcovering Contracting as the specialty. The Virginia specialty abbreviation is PTC.
  3. Identify the qualified individual. A contractor business must identify a qualified individual for each classification or specialty for which the firm seeks licensure. Experience requirements vary according to the class of license.
  4. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor licenses require eight hours of Board-approved pre-license education. This basic business course is separate from preparation for the PTC trade examination.
  5. Complete the applicable examination requirements. Painting and Wallcovering Contracting is a specialty for which the qualified individual must satisfy the applicable Board examination requirements. Class A and Class B licensing also has designated-employee examination requirements separate from the trade specialty examination.
  6. Complete the contractor license application. The business must provide the information and documentation required for its selected class, responsible management, qualified individual, designated employee when applicable, and requested specialty.
  7. Submit the application through the Virginia licensing process. The Board reviews the application and supporting qualifications before a contractor license is issued.

The book package focuses specifically on preparation for the Painting and Wall Covering trade examination. Pre-license education, business examination requirements that may apply to Class A or Class B applicants, application documentation, and other licensing obligations remain separate parts of the Virginia licensing process.

State Requirements

Virginia requires contractor applicants to satisfy requirements associated with both the selected license class and the requested specialty. The qualified individual's required experience increases with the contractor license class. A qualified individual for a Class C license must have at least one year of experience in the classification or specialty. Class B requires at least three years of experience, and Class A requires at least five years of experience.

The qualified individual must be at least 18 years old and must have the appropriate relationship to the licensed firm as required by Virginia regulations. The individual must also satisfy the applicable examination requirement for the classification or specialty.

Virginia also requires eight hours of Board-approved contractor pre-license education for new Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the pre-license education must be completed by a member of responsible management or the designated employee. For Class C applicants, a member of responsible management completes the required course.

Class A and Class B licenses include additional requirements involving a designated employee. Financial qualification requirements also apply to these license classes under Virginia contractor regulations. Applicants should prepare for licensing as a complete process rather than treating the PTC trade examination as the only requirement.

The Painting and Wallcovering Contracting specialty itself includes the application of materials used for protective and decorative painting, installation of surface coverings such as vinyl, wallpaper, and cloth fabrics, and associated preparation activities including caulking, sanding, and cleaning. Both interior and exterior surfaces are included within the specialty's scope.

Test Information and Study Materials

A strong preparation plan should combine content review with timed reference practice. Begin by learning the structure of each book. Review tables of contents, indexes, chapter headings, major safety sections, glossaries, charts, and other features that help you locate information quickly.

Next, organize your study around the official examination subjects. Surface preparation deserves significant attention because it represents 13 of the 50 exam questions. Review concepts related to preparing different substrates, cleaning, sanding, caulking, correcting surface conditions, and creating a suitable surface for coatings or coverings. Application is another major area, with 10 questions, so candidates should be comfortable with painting procedures, application techniques, materials, and the appropriate use of trade tools and equipment.

Do not overlook the smaller categories. Questions involving estimating, wall coverings, materials, safety, and general knowledge can make the difference between a passing and unsuccessful score. Study how wall coverings are prepared and installed, understand commonly used materials, become familiar with trade terminology, and review the OSHA material relevant to construction activities encountered by painting contractors.

Practice locating information rather than relying only on memorization. Select a trade question, determine which reference is most appropriate, locate the answer, and note how long the search takes. Repeating this exercise helps turn an unfamiliar set of books into a usable exam system.

Permanent tabs can also support faster navigation when they are prepared according to testing rules. Useful tab categories may correspond to major chapters, safety subjects, surface preparation sections, application topics, materials, or other high-value sections already present in the references. The goal is not to over-tab every page, but to create a straightforward structure you can recognize immediately under exam conditions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What books are included in the Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contractor Complete Book Package?

The package includes Code of Federal Regulations - 29 CFR Part 1926 (OSHA), Painting & Decorating Craftsman's Manual and Textbook, Eighth Edition, 1995, and Paint Contractor's Manual by Dave Matis and Jobe H. Toole.

Is the Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contracting examination is open book. Candidates must bring their permitted references because the testing site does not provide them.

How many questions are on the Virginia Painting and Wall Covering Contractor exam?

The exam contains 50 questions. Candidates are given 120 minutes to complete the examination and need a score of 70%, equivalent to 35 correct answers, to meet the stated passing score.

What subjects should I study for the PTC exam?

The examination covers general knowledge, materials, application, tools and equipment, safety, surface preparation, wall covering, and estimating. Surface preparation and application account for a substantial portion of the examination.

Can I highlight and tab my books for the exam?

References may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed before the examination. Permanent tabs are permitted under the examination rules, while removable tabs are not. Candidates should prepare their references before test day and follow the testing provider's current reference-material rules.

Can I write notes in the reference books?

No. Reference materials containing writing are not allowed under the examination rules, and candidates may not write in the references during the examination session. Highlighting, underlining, indexing, and permitted permanent tabs are different from handwritten notes.

What does Virginia Painting and Wallcovering Contracting cover?

The PTC specialty includes applying materials commonly used for protective or decorative painting, installing surface coverings such as vinyl, wallpaper, and cloth fabrics, and performing related preparation such as caulking, sanding, and cleaning. The specialty includes both interior and exterior surfaces.

Does passing the trade exam automatically give me a Virginia contractor license?

No. The PTC examination is one part of the licensing process. Virginia contractor applicants must also satisfy the requirements associated with their selected license class, complete required pre-license education, provide the required qualified-individual and business information, and complete the applicable contractor license application process.

How should I use these books to prepare for an open-book exam?

Study the examination subject areas while becoming familiar with each reference's organization. Practice identifying the correct book for a question, using the index or table of contents, and locating answers under timed conditions. Efficient reference navigation can be just as important as reviewing the underlying trade knowledge.

How does the Painting and Wall Covering Contractor exam work?

The Painting and Wall Covering Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
  • The exams are open book. You bring your own references, which may be highlighted and permanently tabbed; removable flags and loose notes are not allowed.
