### Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the **Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors**. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors and combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

For the Alternative Energy Systems specialty, the Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience requirements and receive approval to take the specialty examination. A practical overview of the licensing path includes the following steps:

1. **Determine the appropriate contractor license class.** Virginia contractor licenses are divided into Class A, Class B, and Class C.
2. **Identify the Qualified Individual.** Every business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested on the license.
3. **Meet the applicable experience requirement.** The Qualified Individual must document the required experience for the license class being pursued.
4. **Complete required pre-license education.** New Virginia contractor licenses require eight hours of approved pre-license education. The person who must complete the education depends on the license class.
5. **Obtain approval for the AES specialty examination.** Alternative Energy Systems is one of the specialty designations that requires examination pre-approval.
6. **Schedule and take the examination through PSI after approval.**
7. **Complete the contractor license application requirements.** Business, financial, examination, Qualified Individual, and other applicable requirements depend on the class and structure of the applicant.
8. **Submit the appropriate licensing materials to the Virginia Board for Contractors.**

The specialty examination is one component of the contractor licensing process. Passing the Alternative Energy Systems examination by itself does not constitute issuance of a Virginia contractor license.

### State Requirements

Virginia defines **Alternative Energy System Contracting (AES)** as the service that provides for the installation, repair, or improvement, from the customer's meter, of alternative energy generation systems, supplemental energy systems, and associated equipment annexed to real property. The specialty does not include installation of emergency generators powered by fossil fuels.

The Virginia regulations also state that the AES specialty does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, gas fitting, or HVAC functions. Contractors must hold the appropriate classifications, specialties, or credentials when work extends into regulated functions outside the scope of the AES specialty.

Every contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on its license. The Qualified Individual must either be a member of Responsible Management or a full-time employee of the business meeting Virginia's requirements.

The minimum experience associated with the Qualified Individual depends on the contractor license class:

- **Class C:** Minimum of 1 year of qualifying experience
- **Class B:** Minimum of 3 years of qualifying experience
- **Class A:** Minimum of 5 years of qualifying experience

Alternative Energy Systems is one of the Virginia contractor specialties for which the Qualified Individual is required to receive pre-approval and successfully complete an approved technical examination. Experience documentation is part of the qualification process for specialties requiring examination approval.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the pre-license education must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of Responsible Management must complete the education. This business-focused pre-license education is separate from the Alternative Energy Systems technical examination.

## How does the Alternative Energy Systems Contractor exam work?

The Alternative Energy Systems Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

- Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
