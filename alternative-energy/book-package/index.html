Exam Details

The Virginia Alternative Energy Systems Contracting examination is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors. The examination is administered through PSI for qualified candidates who have received the required approval.

  • Examination: Alternative Energy System Contracting
  • Number of Questions: 60
  • Time Allowed: 150 minutes
  • Minimum Passing Score: 72%, or 43 correct answers
  • Exam Administrator: PSI

The published examination content outline divides the 60 questions among five principal subject areas:

  • Photovoltaics: 23 questions
  • Estimating, Calculations, and Designs: 13 questions
  • Electrical Theory and Knowledge: 9 questions
  • Wind Generated Power: 10 questions
  • Safety: 5 questions

Photovoltaics represents the largest portion of the examination, making solar-energy concepts an important area of preparation. Candidates should also devote substantial study time to estimating, calculations, system design, electrical theory, and wind-generated power. Safety remains a distinct tested category and should be treated as an important component of a complete study plan.

The examination outline also notes that questions may be based on trade knowledge and general industry practices in addition to published reference material. For that reason, candidates benefit from understanding concepts rather than relying only on locating isolated answers in a book.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Alternative Energy Systems Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates are responsible for bringing their own permitted reference materials because references are not supplied at the testing site.

An open-book exam still requires strong preparation. With 60 questions and 150 minutes available, candidates have an average of approximately two and a half minutes per question. Spending too much time searching through an unfamiliar book can quickly reduce the amount of time available for calculations, technical questions, and final review.

A productive study approach is to become familiar with the organization of every reference before examination day. Learn where major chapters, tables, indexes, definitions, electrical information, safety provisions, and renewable-energy topics are located. Practice identifying the correct reference first, then narrowing the search using the index, table of contents, headings, and familiar terminology.

The examination program publishes specific rules governing reference materials, tabs, markings, and materials permitted at the test center. Candidates should follow the current PSI examination instructions when preparing their books for testing.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing is administered by the Virginia Department of Professional and Occupational Regulation Board for Contractors. Contractor licenses are issued to business entities and sole proprietors and combine a license class with one or more classifications or specialty designations.

For the Alternative Energy Systems specialty, the Qualified Individual must satisfy the applicable experience requirements and receive approval to take the specialty examination. A practical overview of the licensing path includes the following steps:

  1. Determine the appropriate contractor license class. Virginia contractor licenses are divided into Class A, Class B, and Class C.
  2. Identify the Qualified Individual. Every business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty requested on the license.
  3. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must document the required experience for the license class being pursued.
  4. Complete required pre-license education. New Virginia contractor licenses require eight hours of approved pre-license education. The person who must complete the education depends on the license class.
  5. Obtain approval for the AES specialty examination. Alternative Energy Systems is one of the specialty designations that requires examination pre-approval.
  6. Schedule and take the examination through PSI after approval.
  7. Complete the contractor license application requirements. Business, financial, examination, Qualified Individual, and other applicable requirements depend on the class and structure of the applicant.
  8. Submit the appropriate licensing materials to the Virginia Board for Contractors.

The specialty examination is one component of the contractor licensing process. Passing the Alternative Energy Systems examination by itself does not constitute issuance of a Virginia contractor license.

State Requirements

Virginia defines Alternative Energy System Contracting (AES) as the service that provides for the installation, repair, or improvement, from the customer's meter, of alternative energy generation systems, supplemental energy systems, and associated equipment annexed to real property. The specialty does not include installation of emergency generators powered by fossil fuels.

The Virginia regulations also state that the AES specialty does not provide authority for electrical, plumbing, gas fitting, or HVAC functions. Contractors must hold the appropriate classifications, specialties, or credentials when work extends into regulated functions outside the scope of the AES specialty.

Every contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for each classification or specialty on its license. The Qualified Individual must either be a member of Responsible Management or a full-time employee of the business meeting Virginia's requirements.

The minimum experience associated with the Qualified Individual depends on the contractor license class:

  • Class C: Minimum of 1 year of qualifying experience
  • Class B: Minimum of 3 years of qualifying experience
  • Class A: Minimum of 5 years of qualifying experience

Alternative Energy Systems is one of the Virginia contractor specialties for which the Qualified Individual is required to receive pre-approval and successfully complete an approved technical examination. Experience documentation is part of the qualification process for specialties requiring examination approval.

Virginia also requires eight hours of approved pre-license education for new contractor licenses. For Class A and Class B applicants, the pre-license education must be completed by a member of Responsible Management or the Designated Employee. For Class C applicants, a member of Responsible Management must complete the education. This business-focused pre-license education is separate from the Alternative Energy Systems technical examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

The exam content distribution can help candidates organize study time efficiently. With 23 photovoltaic questions, photovoltaic systems account for more examination questions than any other single subject area. Candidates should be comfortable with terminology, components, system relationships, technical concepts, and information retrieval within photovoltaic references.

The 13 questions covering estimating, calculations, and designs make calculation practice another major part of preparation. Rather than simply reading formulas, practice identifying the information provided in a problem, determining what must be calculated, selecting the correct formula, and checking whether the result is reasonable.

Electrical theory and knowledge account for nine questions. Study should include familiarity with electrical terminology, formulas, relationships, measurements, and practical reference information. Candidates who can recognize the type of electrical problem being presented are better positioned to determine whether they should solve it directly or locate supporting information in a reference.

Wind-generated power accounts for 10 questions. This represents a significant portion of the exam and should not be treated as a minor topic. Candidates should allocate dedicated study time to wind-energy principles and terminology rather than concentrating exclusively on photovoltaics.

The five safety questions make OSHA-related preparation part of a complete study plan. Learn how the construction safety reference is arranged and practice locating relevant provisions by subject. Familiarity with safety terminology can reduce lookup time during the exam.

Because this is an open-book examination, reference-navigation practice should be part of every study session. Build the habit of using the index and table of contents before flipping through pages randomly. Practice with timed questions. When you miss a question, identify both the correct concept and where that information can be found in the applicable reference.

Another useful strategy is to divide preparation into three activities: concept review, calculation practice, and book navigation. Concept review builds understanding. Calculation practice improves speed and accuracy. Book navigation teaches you how to retrieve information efficiently under exam conditions. Combining all three creates a more balanced preparation routine than simply reading each book from beginning to end.

What is the Virginia Alternative Energy Systems Contractor exam?

It is a technical specialty examination associated with the Virginia Board for Contractors' Alternative Energy Systems specialty designation. The examination evaluates knowledge related to photovoltaics, estimating and calculations, electrical theory, wind-generated power, and safety.

How many questions are on the Virginia Alternative Energy Systems exam?

The examination contains 60 questions.

How much time do I have to complete the exam?

Candidates are allowed 150 minutes to complete the Virginia Alternative Energy Systems Contracting examination.

What score is required to pass?

The published minimum passing score is 72%, which corresponds to 43 correct answers out of 60 questions.

Is the Virginia Alternative Energy Systems Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not provided at the test site.

What subjects are covered on the exam?

The examination covers photovoltaics, estimating and calculations and designs, electrical theory and knowledge, wind-generated power, and safety. Photovoltaics is the largest individual subject area on the published examination outline.

Who administers the Virginia Alternative Energy Systems examination?

PSI administers the examination for the Virginia contractor licensing program. Candidates requiring the AES technical examination must receive the applicable approval before scheduling the specialty exam.

Does passing the AES exam automatically give me a Virginia contractor license?

No. The technical examination is one part of the Virginia contractor licensing process. Applicants must also satisfy the applicable business, experience, education, Qualified Individual, license-class, and application requirements established by the Virginia Board for Contractors.

What experience does a Qualified Individual need?

The minimum experience requirement depends on the contractor license class. Virginia identifies one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A Qualified Individuals, along with the other applicable qualification requirements.

Why is book navigation important for an open-book exam?

An open-book format does not provide unlimited time. Candidates have 150 minutes for 60 questions, so repeatedly searching unfamiliar references can consume valuable testing time. Practicing with the books beforehand helps candidates recognize where information is located and choose the appropriate reference more efficiently.

How does the Alternative Energy Systems Contractor exam work?

The Alternative Energy Systems Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
