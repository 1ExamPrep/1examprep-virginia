About this Steel Erection Contractor package

Prepare for the Virginia Steel Erection Contractor examination with a complete reference package focused on structural steel erection, rigging, welding, reinforcing steel, steel decking, joists, girders, and construction safety. The Virginia Steel Erection Contractor Exam Complete Book Package brings together the principal technical references candidates can use while preparing for Virginia's Steel Erection Contracting (STL) specialty examination.

Virginia defines Steel Erection Contracting as work involving the fabrication and erection of structural steel shapes and plates, regardless of shape or size, when those materials are used as structural members or tanks. The specialty includes related riveting, welding, and rigging. It also includes the fabrication, placement, and tying of reinforcing steel bars and rods, as well as post-tensioning used to reinforce concrete buildings and structures.

Because steel erection combines several specialized skills, exam preparation requires more than knowledge of structural steel alone. Candidates may need to work with lifting and rigging practices, welding procedures, reinforcing bar placement, steel deck installation, joist and girder handling, fall protection, construction safety standards, and other practical steel erection principles.

This complete book package gives candidates six focused references covering those major areas. Instead of relying on a single textbook, candidates can prepare with dedicated materials for OSHA construction safety, rigging operations, reinforcing steel, welding, steel deck construction, and the handling and erection of steel joists and joist girders.

The Virginia Steel Erection Contractor examination is open book, which makes familiarity with the references an important part of preparation. Candidates should not simply bring the books to the testing center. Effective preparation includes learning which reference is most useful for each subject and practicing how to locate information efficiently under examination conditions.

Exam Details

The Virginia Steel Erection Contractor examination is a Qualified Individual specialty examination associated with the STL designation. Steel Erection Contracting is one of the Virginia contractor specialties for which the Qualified Individual must receive the applicable examination approval and successfully complete the required specialty examination.

Virginia uses PSI as the examination vendor for Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations. Candidates must receive the applicable approval from the Virginia Board for Contractors before scheduling the examination.

The current Qualified Individual specialty examination fee listed by the Virginia Board for Contractors is $85, paid directly to PSI. The testing fee is separate from the purchase of this book package.

The examination is based on the minimum knowledge expected of a contractor professional working within the steel erection specialty. Questions can involve information from the listed references as well as trade knowledge and accepted industry practices.

Major areas candidates should be prepared to study include:

  • Structural steel erection methods and sequencing
  • Rigging, hoisting, and load-handling practices
  • Construction safety and OSHA requirements
  • Welding principles and procedures
  • Reinforcing bar placement and tying
  • Steel deck installation and handling
  • Steel joist and joist girder handling and erection
  • Fall protection and worker safety
  • Material handling and lifting equipment
  • Temporary stability and erection-related precautions

Field experience is valuable, but candidates should also prepare to answer written questions involving terminology, procedures, safety requirements, reference information, and technical details that may not arise frequently in everyday work.

Open Book Test

The Virginia Steel Erection Contractor examination is an open-book test. Candidates must bring their own permitted references because books are not supplied at the testing center.

The open-book format makes reference navigation an important component of exam preparation. A candidate may understand steel erection work extremely well but still lose valuable testing time if unfamiliar with the organization of the approved books.

Permitted references may be highlighted, underlined, and indexed according to applicable PSI testing rules. Permanent index tabs are allowed, while removable tabs are not permitted. Loose papers and unbound reference materials are not allowed.

Candidates should practice identifying the subject of a question before selecting a reference. A fall-protection or jobsite-safety question may lead to OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926. A load-handling question may require the Handbook of Rigging. A reinforcing steel question can lead to Placing Reinforcing Bars, while a welding question may require Modern Welding.

Steel deck questions are supported by the SDI Manual of Construction with Steel Deck, while questions involving joists and joist girders can require Technical Digest No. 9.

The goal is not to search every reference for every answer. Candidates should develop enough trade knowledge to recognize the subject quickly and then use the appropriate book when a detailed procedure, safety requirement, or technical fact needs to be confirmed.

Licensing Steps

Virginia contractor licensing combines a contractor license class with one or more classifications or specialties. Steel Erection Contracting is identified by the specialty abbreviation STL.

  1. Determine the contractor license class. Virginia issues Class A, Class B, and Class C contractor licenses.
  2. Select Steel Erection Contracting. The applicable specialty designation is STL.
  3. Identify the Qualified Individual. The contractor business must designate a Qualified Individual for the Steel Erection specialty.
  4. Meet the applicable experience requirement. The Qualified Individual must document experience relevant to the specialty and license class.
  5. Submit the required experience verification. Steel Erection is among the specialties requiring examination pre-approval and experience documentation.
  6. Receive approval for the STL examination. The candidate must be approved before scheduling the Qualified Individual specialty exam.
  7. Prepare for the examination. Study steel erection, rigging, welding, reinforcing steel, steel decking, joists, girders, OSHA safety, and reference navigation.
  8. Complete the required examination. The Qualified Individual must successfully complete the applicable Steel Erection specialty examination.
  9. Complete contractor pre-license requirements. New contractor applicants must satisfy the applicable Virginia pre-license education and business requirements.
  10. Submit the contractor license application. The business must complete the licensing process for the selected class and STL specialty.

Passing the Steel Erection examination does not by itself issue a Virginia contractor license. Examination completion is one component of the overall process, which also includes the business application, license class requirements, Qualified Individual qualifications, and other applicable Board requirements.

State Requirements

The Virginia Board for Contractors regulates contractor businesses and establishes the qualifications for each contractor classification and specialty. A business requesting the STL specialty must identify a Qualified Individual who satisfies the applicable requirements.

The Qualified Individual must be a full-time employee of the business or a member of Responsible Management. Current Virginia contractor application requirements establish minimum experience of one year for Class C, three years for Class B, and five years for Class A for the Qualified Individual.

An Experience Verification Form is required for a Qualified Individual requesting a designation that requires pre-approval for an examination. Steel Erection Contracting is among the specialty designations subject to that examination requirement.

Virginia's definition of the STL specialty includes fabrication and erection of structural steel shapes and plates used as structural members or tanks, together with related riveting, welding, and rigging. It also includes fabrication, placement, and tying of reinforcing steel bars and rods and post-tensioning used to reinforce concrete buildings and structures.

Other Virginia contractor classifications can also provide authority for steel erection work within their defined scopes. Candidates seeking the dedicated STL specialty should understand that the specialty identifies steel erection as the qualifying area for the business.

New contractor applicants must also address Virginia's required pre-license education. Class A and Class B applicants have additional Designated Employee business examination requirements that are separate from the STL Qualified Individual technical examination.

Test Information and Study Materials

A productive steel erection study strategy should begin by dividing preparation into technical categories. Candidates can study safety, rigging, welding, reinforcing steel, decking, and joist erection separately before combining those subjects through practice questions and reference-navigation drills.

Safety should receive consistent attention throughout preparation. Steel erection involves working at elevation, suspended loads, cranes, temporary structural conditions, welding hazards, material handling, and other significant risks. Candidates should be familiar with applicable OSHA requirements and understand how safety rules relate to actual erection operations.

Rigging and lifting are central to steel erection. Review sling configurations, load handling, wire rope, hooks, shackles, lifting equipment, inspection principles, center of gravity, load control, signaling, and safe hoisting practices. The Handbook of Rigging should become a familiar reference during study.

Welding preparation should include terminology, welding processes, electrodes, joint designs, equipment, preparation, safe work practices, and quality considerations. Candidates should understand both practical welding concepts and how to locate detailed information within Modern Welding.

Reinforcing steel should not be overlooked. The Virginia STL scope specifically includes fabrication, placement, and tying of reinforcing bars and rods, as well as post-tensioning used to reinforce concrete buildings and structures.

Steel deck preparation should include handling, placement, fastening, support, construction sequencing, and safe installation practices. Candidates should become familiar with the organization of the SDI manual and where common construction topics are located.

Steel joists and joist girders require careful attention to handling, erection stability, temporary conditions, bridging, and sequencing. Technical Digest No. 9 provides focused information that candidates should review alongside practical field knowledge.

Open-book practice should be incorporated into every study session. When answering a practice question, first determine which reference should contain the answer. Then use the index, table of contents, headings, diagrams, or tables to locate the supporting information.

When an answer is missed, locate the correct information in the appropriate reference and review the surrounding material. This strengthens both technical understanding and the ability to use the books efficiently.

Timed navigation practice can also help candidates avoid spending excessive time on a single lookup. Become comfortable moving on from difficult questions and returning to them after completing easier items.

What books are included in the Virginia Steel Erection Contractor Exam Complete Book Package?

Is the Virginia Steel Erection Contractor exam open book?

Yes. The Virginia Steel Erection Contractor specialty examination is open book. Candidates must bring their own permitted reference materials to the testing center.

What does the Virginia STL specialty cover?

The specialty includes fabrication and erection of structural steel shapes and plates used as structural members or tanks, including related riveting, welding, and rigging. It also includes reinforcing steel placement and post-tensioning within its defined scope.

Who administers the Virginia Steel Erection exam?

PSI administers Qualified Individual contractor specialty examinations for the Virginia Board for Contractors.

Does the candidate need approval before scheduling the exam?

Yes. Steel Erection is one of the Virginia contractor specialties requiring the Qualified Individual to receive the applicable examination pre-approval.

Why is OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 included?

Steel erection involves significant construction safety requirements. OSHA 29 CFR Part 1926 supports preparation involving steel erection safety, fall protection, material handling, personal protective equipment, rigging, and other jobsite requirements.

Why is the Handbook of Rigging included?

Rigging and lifting are fundamental parts of structural steel erection. The handbook supports study of slings, wire rope, load handling, cranes, hooks, shackles, signaling, and other lifting practices.

Does the STL specialty include reinforcing bars?

Yes. Virginia's Steel Erection Contracting scope includes fabrication, placement, and tying of reinforcing bars and rods and post-tensioning used to reinforce concrete buildings and structures.

Is welding part of the Steel Erection specialty?

Yes. Virginia's definition of Steel Erection Contracting includes related welding, along with riveting and rigging, as part of the specialty scope.

Does passing the STL examination automatically issue a Virginia contractor license?

No. The Steel Erection examination is one part of the contractor licensing process. The business must also satisfy the applicable contractor class, Qualified Individual, pre-license education, application, and other licensing requirements.

How does the Steel Erection Contractor exam work?

The Steel Erection Contractor exam is the technical portion of the license for that classification. A Class A or B firm usually also needs the business and law portion, and every applicant needs the 8-hour pre-license course.

  • Virginia issues contractor licenses in three classes. Class C covers single jobs of $1,000 to under $10,000 and under $150,000 a year; Class B covers single jobs of $10,000 to under $120,000 and under $750,000 a year; Class A has no upper limit.
  • Every applicant completes 8 hours of pre-license education before the license is issued.
  • Class A and B firms name a designated employee or member of responsible management who passes the exams. PSI runs them, and the fee is $40 for one portion, $72 for two and $85 for three.
